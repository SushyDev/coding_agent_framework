# F-192: a person's claim is closed by its healer's restart

F-190 left two gaps around a person's answer "act" to a `remedy` prompt: a healer cut between
the person's claim and its act left the row `claimed` until the framework's next start
(new-2), and the design did not say how a person's act reads (new-1, new-4). This unit closes
the first and writes the second into the design.

## What was built

- **`Acts.claim/6` takes `healer:`**, the actor of the process that takes a person's act for
  them. It is written in the row's `detail` as `healer`, beside a map `detail:` (the limit's
  press keeps its `by`). **`Acts.interrupt/2`** closes the rows still `claimed` whose actor is
  the one given or whose `detail` names it as `healer`; it reads the detail only where it is
  JSON, since a row's detail may be plain text (a pane). It now answers the ids sorted.
- **Each healer names itself on a person's claim**: `AbsentMains` (resume), `StuckBackgrounds`
  (end), `Recoveries` (reopen, end) and `Limits` (`press_stop`, `go_on`). Their `init`s call
  `Acts.interrupt/2` as before, which now closes those claims too, and each asks of a cut row
  as before: for a person's state the `Watch` refuses the ask (the state is `answered`), so the
  person's answer is the one prompt and the row's `interrupted` is its outcome.
- **`Heal.tla`**: the header says a person's claim names its healer, so the restart settles it
  as its own; `SettleCut` takes a guard that only `Break = "CutOwnOnly"` uses, and
  `Break_Heal_CutOwnOnly.cfg` (only the healer's own cut claims settle) fails `ClaimsEnd`.
- **Tests**: a doctest of `interrupt/2`; an `Acts` test of its rows' details (a pane, plain
  words, a map beside the healer, another healer's name); an `AbsentMains` test with the healer
  killed between sam's claim and its resume, whose restart closes sam's claim `interrupted`,
  and whose next check resumes nothing.
- **The design** ("Metrics and healing", "A person's act, read"): an `act` answer's outcome is
  its row in `Acts`, and the inbox shows the prompt answered and, once it settles, the act's row
  (not built: new-1); a person's claim names its healer and that healer's restart closes it;
  a person's act that fails its verify narrows the list as the healer's does.

## What I ran into

- `Acts.acted/4` writes `detail` over the claim's (a pane, a group's tasks), so the `healer`
  stays on a row only while it is `claimed`. That is the only status `interrupt/2` closes, so
  nothing is lost for it; it is why the detail cannot carry a link from the act to its prompt
  (new-1).
- SQLite gives no order to `RETURNING`, so `interrupt/2` sorts the ids it answers, as
  `clear/3` already did.

## Findings

### new-1. The inbox's reading of a person's act is not yet one function of the rows

What happened: the brief asks for the reading in `Prompts` if it is one function of the rows.
It is not, for three reasons. `Prompts` reads no `Acts` file. `Limits`' press claims a state of
its own (the symptom's state with the menu's line and count), so a person's press row is not
found by its symptom's key, where the other healers' are. And `Acts.acted/4` writes over the
claim's `detail`, so no link to the symptom survives the act. When an answered prompt leaves
the inbox once its act's row has settled is not said either. Why: each healer keyed its claims
for its own acts. What I did: wrote the reading into the design as decided, marked it not
built, and pointed to `acts(caf, …)` for now. What the design should change: a person's claim
takes its symptom's state as its state in every healer (the limit's press for a person by the
symptom's state; the line and menu matter only to the framework's own presses); then `Prompts`
reads `Acts`' file (`acts_db:`, the `acts` cue) and shows a `remedy` prompt answered "act" as
`answering` until the row on `(target, state)` ends, and closes it then, the outcome read from
`Acts` (a fault in its fault ledger).

### new-2. A person's press is recorded `by: "framework"`

What happened: `Limits` writes `detail: %{by: …}` on each press from the limit prompt's
answer, and a press a person's "act" took has none, so its row's actor is the person and its
detail says `framework`. Why: `by/1` reads only the `limit` prompt's answer. What I did:
nothing; the actor is right. What the design should change: nothing, if the code makes `by`
the claim's actor when no limit prompt's answer names one.
