# F-122: R6, `Delivery.tla`; `Move.tla` rewritten

The remodel's sixth unit (`docs/design.md`, "Delivery", "Delivery into a main session", "Ending a
session", "The model", R6; F-115's section 3; F-116's restart policies; F-118's new-2; F-119's
new-2 and new-8). Specs, configurations and `model/tla/READING.md` only; no Elixir, and
`bin/check-model` is unchanged.

## What was built

1. **`Delivery.tla`** (734 lines with its header and its own spec): `Mailbox`'s rows with one
   mark and one settle over the transports `stream`, `waiter`, `typed` and `call`.
   - A row has a sender, an addressee, a kind (`msg`, `brief`, `resume`, `reply`, `notice`), a
     key (`re`), and a state: due, sending (with its transport and the process it is for),
     delivered, ended, or kept (an answer for the operator). `First` picks the oldest due row
     per sender and addressee, a row made due again first, one in flight per addressee.
   - `Mark` only while the addressee's process runs, its turn has ended (a call answers inside
     the turn that called) and its row is not stopping; `Handed` re-checks the process and the
     stopping row (`Open`); `Refused` undoes the mark; `Unknown` (the `Inlet`'s) keeps it.
   - Each transport's evidence and restart policy, as the design's table says: `SettleReplay`
     and `SettleExit` (stream: the replay, else the transcript once the process is gone;
     undelivered is due again, first), `SettleSeen` (waiter and typed: the transcript once the
     turn that took it has ended), and the call's `Written` (three outcomes), `Told`,
     `CallerSeen` and `SettleCut` (due again, for the waiter, recorded `unsure` when the write
     may have reached the agent). `Reclaim` claims a stream line again after a restart.
   - Replies and notices, one per key; a brief whose turn its process's end cut gets a row to go
     on under the brief's key (`GoOn`), so the report is the reply to the brief across a resume.
   - Alone: senders, interactive and headless sessions, a person's prompts, turns a session
     starts by itself, crashes and resumes, a stop by SIGTERM after a separate idle read that
     waits for no row sending, a session's end for good, `run_delegate` calls, restarts.
   - Properties: `AtMostOnce`, `OnlyIntoEndedTurn`, `NotCutByStop`, `OnlyToItsProcess`,
     `InOrder`, `DeliveredWasTaken`, `EndedNotDelivered`, `ReplyOnce`, `ReplyOrNotice`,
     `ReplyNamesItsRow`, `AnswersTheBrief`, and the liveness `SendingSettles`, `Arrives`,
     `AnswerComes`. 21 Breaks, each with a `Break_Delivery_*` configuration that fails.
   - Configurations: `Delivery_Interactive` (safety), `Delivery_Turns` (its liveness),
     `Delivery_Stream`, `Delivery_Replies` (safety, with turns the session starts by itself),
     `Delivery_Answers` (liveness across a resume), `Delivery_Calls`; `slow/Delivery_Large`.
2. **`Move.tla`** rewritten (347 to 439 lines). The stop is `Sessions`': `StopAsked` writes the
   row stopping, `IdleRead` (the turn ended, no row sending) keeps the pid in memory, `Term` is
   the SIGTERM as a step of its own. A person's prompt may start a turn between them; the
   SIGTERM cuts it and the copy after the exit keeps it (`TranscriptKept`). A framework restart
   at any step; the Mover's phase is a row. Rows to the session (a delegate's report, a
   message) are `Delivery`'s, by the waiter or the typed write, settled against the transcript
   of the account the session's row names, so after the resume against the copy. The old
   process may crash once its stop is asked, so a row can be in flight across the end. New
   properties `OnceAcrossMove`, `NotCutByEnd`, `OnlyToItsProcess`, `OnlyIntoEndedTurn`,
   `DeliveredIsKept`, `RowsArrive`. Breaks: `NoTurnWait` is now `NoIdleRead`;
   `ReportDuringMove` goes (Delivery's mark needs a running process, and no Break removes
   that); new `MarkWhileEnding`, `StopWhileSending`, `SettleOnTarget`.
3. **`Delegate.tla`** (686 to 516 lines) hands the report to `Mailbox` once (`Report`, which
   stores row `d`) and reads `Delivery`'s state. `report`, `sending`, `got`, `wrong`,
   `DeliveryAsked`, `DeliveryTyped`, `DeliveryRefused`, `DeliveryMarked`, `SendingResolved`
   and the whole answer path (`caller`, `callerUp`, `watched`, `heard`, `Taken`, `Told`,
   `TellLost`, `CallerSeen`, the start's hold) go; the call is `Delivery`'s `call`
   transport. `DelegateEnds` waits for the row delivered. A waiter leaves only with its process
   or a restart here (its own deadline is `Delivery`'s), to keep the state small.
4. **`Team.tla`** (664 to 542 lines): messages, replies and notices are `Delivery`'s rows; a
   process number per agent (`life`, a resume starts a new one) gives `Delivery` the process.
   `msgs`, `msend`, `inbox`, `fold`, `answers`, `rmem`, `last` and their steps go. Main sessions
   take the typed write here and delegates the stream; the waiter is `Delivery`'s and
   `Delegate`'s. `ReplyToMain` is `ReplyToSender`: a reply to the operator's message is kept
   for it, as the design says. `Team_RepliesTwo` moves to `slow/`; `Team_Replies` checks the
   invariants only (the answers' liveness is in `Team_Delivery` and `Delivery_Answers`);
   `Team_Faults` and `Team_Messages` drop the second project's delegate.
5. **`Stream.tla`** (691 to 727 lines): a message's row is `Delivery`'s (`row`), and `Mark` is
   `D!Mark` with the stream; `Handle` and `ExitSeen` settle it with `Delivery`'s values.
   Stream keeps the offset, the spool and claim, the FIFO per process and the requests. Its
   state counts did not change.
6. `model/tla/READING.md`: a `Delivery.tla` section, and the Move, Delegate, Stream and Team
   text.

## What I ran into

- TLC's liveness checking, not its search, is the cost. `Delivery_Interactive` finds 264,929
  states in 5 s and then spent minutes on the tableau with every fairness condition. Each row
  needs strong fairness for its mark, its hand-on, Claude Code's take and its settle, since a
  person's prompt can take the turn each time (TLC found each of these starvations as a
  counterexample). So the liveness configurations are separate and smaller than the safety
  ones, and the per-row steps after the hand-on share one fairness condition.
- An instance's variables can be substituted by expressions (`pr <- [a \in Agents |-> …]`), so
  the addressee's state is the instancing spec's own. That only works because `Delivery`'s
  steps never assign the addressee's variables; the instancing spec's step does, beside the
  module's (`TurnEnds(a)` is `proc' = …` and `D!Result(a)`). A variable a module step assigns
  must be a real variable of the instancing spec, or an expression that the step leaves
  unchanged; that is why Stream declares `row`, `hand` and `dwrong` and gives constants for the
  rest.
- A cfg cannot write `1..n`, so `Delivery`'s configurations list their row ids (`{1, 2, 3}`).

## Findings

### new-1. The stop's idle read must wait for no row sending, not only refuse a new mark

What happened: with only F-118's rule (`Mailbox` marks no row for a session whose row is
stopping), TLC found a row marked before `StopAsked`, handed to the waiter after it, and taken
after the idle read; the SIGTERM then cut the turn it started (`Break_Delivery_StopWhileSending`,
`Break_Move_StopWhileSending`). A first version also found a row marked before the stop and
handed on after it (the first counterexample of `Delivery_Interactive`).
Why: the mark and the hand-on are two steps, and a waiter's answer takes a moment to become a
turn; the idle read sees the turn ended while an answer is on its way.
What I did: the model has `Mailbox` re-check the stopping row at the hand-on (`Open`) as well as
at the mark, and `Sessions`' idle read wait until no row to the session is sending, as the
headless end already does ("Ending": no row to the delegate sending or due). Both are
`Delivery`'s and `Move`'s design; each has a Break that fails.
What the design should change: "Ending a session" and the Mover's step 3 should say that the
idle read waits until no `Mailbox` row to the session is sending, and that `Mailbox` neither
marks nor hands on a row for a session whose row is stopping. Please confirm this reading;
otherwise `NotCutByStop` does not hold, and the guard is one line in each spec.

### new-2. A waiter's or typed row cannot be made due again from the transcript alone

What happened: "settled from the transcript once that turn has ended: … due again if not"
(the waiter's restart policy) double-delivers when the answer was handed to the hook but the
turn it starts has not begun yet: the transcript lacks the line, the session reads idle, and
the row goes again (`Break_Delivery_SettleEarly`). This is the gap F-115 named.
Why: nothing the framework reads tells "not yet" from "never" between the waiter's answer and
the line in the transcript. A restart that cut the waiter's connection before the answer went
out leaves the same picture, and there the row must go again, or it waits forever.
What I did: the model settles "due again" only once nothing of the row is on its way to a turn
(`wire[i] = 0`), or the process it was for is gone. In the code that is a bound: the time after
the waiter's answer (or the typed write) by which Claude Code has started the turn or never
will. The header names it as the one piece of time the spec reads.
What the design should change: the waiter's and typed rows in "Delivery"'s table should name
that bound: due again only once the bound past the hand-on has passed, or the process is gone;
the bound is checked against each pinned release, with the transcript line's text.

### new-3. Where a resumed delegate's row to go on stands in the order

What happened: in `Delivery.tla` alone a message due before a delegate's resume goes in before
the row the resumed process gets to go on with its brief, since the two have different senders
and the go-on row is newer.
Why: "a row made due again goes first" covers a resend, not a new row the resume makes.
What I did: nothing; no property needs the order, and `Delegate.tla` models the go-on as
`Stream`'s mark, not a row.
What the design should change: "The delegate protocol" should say whether the go-on row goes
before other due rows to the delegate (I recommend it does, as a resend does, so the resumed
process first goes on with its brief).

### new-4. Whether the brief is a `Mailbox` row

What happened: `Delivery.tla` makes the brief and the go-on row rows (the report is the reply
under the brief's key); `Delegate.tla` still writes them through `Stream`'s own mark under the
uuid `Delegates` chose, as the design's "What Delegates holds" says.
Why: "Delivery" lists a message, a reply, a notice, a report and the app's word, not the brief.
What I did: modeled both as they are; `AnswersTheBrief` and `Break_Delivery_ResumeOwnKey`
check the keying in `Delivery`.
What the design should change: R7 should say whether the brief and the go-on row become
`Mailbox` rows (I recommend they do: one mark for everything a session is handed, and
`Delegate.tla`'s `marked` and `pipe` then go too).

### new-5. R6 grows the model by about 570 lines, where the design says about −50

What happened: the specs grew from 6,166 to 6,736 lines (+1,492, −922). `Delivery.tla` is 734
lines where the design said about 450; `Delegate.tla` lost 170 and `Team.tla` 122, while
`Move.tla` gained 92 (the stop, the restart, the rows, a crash) and `Stream.tla` 36 (the
instance and its header).
Why: as F-119's new-8 found for R4, the module carries its own alone-spec, configurations and
Breaks, and each instancing spec still names the module's steps and its own frame. Four
transports with their restart policies, the call's three outcomes and the answers are more
than the 450 lines guessed.
What I did: nothing; each mechanism is now written once.
What the design should change: R6's size in "Order of the first units" is about +1,500 −900, and
"The model stays about 5,400 lines" is now about 6,700.

### new-6. The fast set needs smaller liveness configurations

What happened: with `Delivery`'s fairness, the old configurations that checked liveness ran for
minutes (`Team_Delivery` 200 s, `Delegate_Design` over 10 min, `Move_Design` over 10 min).
Why: see "What I ran into".
What I did: split safety and liveness (`Delegate_Design`, `Move_Design`, `Team_Replies` check
the invariants; `Move_Liveness`, `Delivery_Turns`, `Delivery_Answers` and the one-delegate
Delegate configurations check the liveness), made `Team_Faults` and `Team_Messages` smaller,
and moved `Team_RepliesTwo` (111 s) to `slow/`.
What the design should change: nothing; "The model" already says a configuration over the
bound moves to `slow/` or gets smaller bounds.
