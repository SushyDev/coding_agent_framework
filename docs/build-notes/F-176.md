# F-176: every written topic subscribable; a subscription that knows its tree; small cleanups

Six small decided items from the findings of F-171 to F-174, in one unit.

## What was built

- **Every written topic subscribable** (F-174's new-1). Each part that cues declares what it
  writes, as data beside `reads/0`: `writes/0`, each topic's whole shape with `*` for each word
  the part fills in (`Acts.writes/0` is `["acts:*:*"]`). Fifteen parts declare it: `Acts`,
  `Copies`, `Delegates`, `Intake`, `Jobs`, `Mailbox`, `Mains`, `Registry`, `TmuxSampler`,
  `Scheduler`, `Services`, `Sessions`, `Stream`, `Transcripts`, `Usage`. The tree's
  `CodingAgentFramework.writes/0` lists them by part, `Cues` takes them as `writes:`, and
  `Cues.topics/2` and the app's `topics/1` are the union of reads and writes. A subscription is
  declared when a declared pattern matches it, or it begins one (`"acts"` and `"acts:ws"` are
  begun by `"acts:*:*"`). `acts`, `intake`, `scheduler`, `services` and `copies` can now be
  subscribed to, and `subscribe/2`'s moduledoc has the table of every topic's cue shape.
- **The check in tests.** `Cues.notify/3` makes a part's `notify:`; with `:raise` a cue whose
  topic no part declares it writes (`Cues.written?/2`) raises in the part that wrote it. The
  config's new `undeclared_cues` (`:route` by default, `:raise`) sets it for the tree, and the
  surface's tests set `:raise`. Checked by hand: with `copies` taken out of `writes/0`, the
  landing test fails on `a cue on "copies", a topic no part declares it writes`.
- **A subscription that knows its tree** (F-174's new-2). `subscribe/2` answers `{:ok, %{ref:
  ref, tree: pid}}`, the tree's supervisor, kept as the meta of the instance's `Registry`
  (`meta: [tree: self()]`, set in the supervisor, where each child's start runs). The moduledoc
  says a subscription lives and dies with the tree and shows an app's process that monitors the
  tree and subscribes again once it is back. A new test stops the tree, waits for the `:DOWN`,
  and finds the restarted tree holding no subscription of the old one's until the app
  subscribes again.
- **The instance's name** (F-174's new-3): the design's app surface says it is unique per app
  and 1 to 100 bytes with no control character, as `Config.new/1` checks it.
- **Operator-only tools listed to no agent** (F-172's new-5). `MCP`'s `@operator`
  (`answer_startup`, `end_main`, `withdraw`) is left out of `tools/2` and of `reload`'s answer
  for every caller but the operator; `MCP.operate/3` still serves them, and an agent's call is
  still refused `:operator_only`.
- **`run` removed** (F-171's new-3): the tool, its act, its class and lease, `MCP.caller/3`,
  `MCP.Table.caller/2`, the tree's late-end routing to `Mailbox`, and `MCP`'s `jobs:`,
  `lease:` and `instance:` options. The tree's `Jobs` now takes a late end as stored once it is
  in the job's row, which `Jobs` marks told and cues on `jobs`. The tests moved: the gang lease
  of a landing is now the surface's test of the app's own `Jobs.run/6` with
  `job_units(c, :landing, "app")`; the late end is the surface's test of the row told and its
  `jobs` cue reaching a subscriber; the cap is tested on `run_delegate`. `Jobs`' own tests
  already cover a run's answers, its classes and its late ends. `caf_mcp` no longer uses
  `caf_jobs` or `exqlite`.
- **`bin/caf-login` sets `USER`** (F-173's new-2) from `id -un`, devenv's (`CAF_ID`, new in
  `devenv.nix`), so the login names the Keychain item a held launch names whatever the shell's
  `USER` was. A test runs it with `USER` set to another name and unset.
- Docs: the design's `Cues` row, "The order in which the system settles", the app's surface,
  the agent's surface, Hosts and the order of units; `README.md` and `docs/e2e.md` (step 6 now
  runs a heavy command through `Bash`, with `scheduler.heavy` in the e2e config).

## What I ran into

- The size: about 570 lines added and 530 removed over 35 files, past the 500 a unit aims
  for. `run`'s removal is most of the removed lines; the fifteen `writes/0` and the cue table
  most of the added.
- A written shape is longer than most subscriptions, so the check reads a declared pattern by
  as many words as the topic has. It also accepts a topic longer than a written shape
  (`"acts:ws:a:b"`), as it always accepted one longer than a read pattern; such a subscription
  matches nothing.
- `Supervisor.stop/1` of the tree under the test's supervisor restarts it (`:permanent`), so the
  restart test needs no restart of its own.

## Findings

### new-1. A part's own tests do not check its writes

What happened: the brief wants a part that writes an undeclared topic to fail its tests. The
check is in the tree's `notify` (`Cues.notify/3`), so it holds in the surface's tests, which
start the whole tree, but not in a part's own tests, which hand the part a `notify` of their
own.
Why: the parts do not use `caf_cues`, and their tests build their `notify` by hand.
What I did: the tree's check, a config key to set it (`undeclared_cues`), and the surface's
tests with it set. A write only a part's own tests reach is not checked.
What the design should change: either nothing (the tree's tests cover the writes the tree
makes), or each part's test `notify` goes through `Cues.notify(fn -> nil end, [p:
Part.writes()], :raise)`, with `caf_cues` `only: :test` in each part.

### new-2. A cue without ids

What happened: the design says `subscribe/2` gives "one stream of `{topic, ids}`". `Copies`
cues `%{topic: "copies"}` with no ids (a copy's key is three columns), and the `Scheduler`
cues `%{topic: "scheduler:<host>", pressure: level}` at each change of the pressure level.
Why: both predate the app's subscribers.
What I did: the table in `subscribe/2`'s doc names both shapes.
What the design should change: say that a cue may carry no ids (re-read the whole table), or
give `Copies` one id per copy and the pressure a row of its own.

### new-3. The tree's `on_late_end` is now a constant

What happened: with `run` gone, no agent's call waits on a job, so the tree's `Jobs` answers
every late end `:ok`: the end is in the job's row, cued on `jobs`. `Jobs` still requires the
option.
Why: `Jobs` was built for a caller the embedding app routes to.
What I did: `on_late_end: fn _late -> :ok end` in the tree, with a comment.
What the design should change: make `on_late_end` optional in `Jobs` (none: the row is the
end), or give the app's config a callback for the late ends of the jobs it runs.

### new-4. Nothing announces that the tree is back

What happened: after the tree's `:DOWN`, the app must subscribe again once the tree is back,
but no message says when; the moduledoc's example tries again on a timer while `subscribe/2`
answers `{:refused, :no_instance}`.
Why: the tree is the app's supervisor's child, and the framework cannot tell the app's
process about its own restart.
What I did: documented the retry, and the test waits on `subscribe/2` until it answers.
What the design should change: either recommend that the app start its subscribing
processes after the tree under a `:rest_for_one` supervisor of its own (they restart with the
tree and subscribe in `init`), or nothing.
