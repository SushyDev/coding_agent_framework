# F-241: a reload reaches a session whose waiter holds its turn

Branch `framework/f-241-waiter`, from `main` at 476cb05. F-239 and F-240 are not on `main` yet;
what will need merging with them is under "Merging".

The first real run of F-239's reload test (2.1.322, Haiku) had an idle main session answer
`Operator.reload_plugins` `{:refused, {:turn, :waiter}}` in 54 ms. Every idle session the
framework delivers to by the waiter has one held, so F-233's typed check refused exactly the
sessions a reload is for. F-233's tests ran a stand-in with no waiter.

## What was built

- **The model** (`model/tla/Reload.tla`, new). One session: Claude Code's turn, `Intake`'s
  waiter (`none`, `held`, `deciding` with text in flight), `Mailbox`'s rows in order with one in
  flight, the box, and the `Inlet`'s writes, keys then Enter. A text handed to the waiter wakes
  the session with a turn of its own, whatever is in the box. The reload is asked in one step of
  `Mailbox`: refused while a turn runs, refused `message_due` while a row is open with a waiter
  there for it, else the waiter is passed (`Hold.tla`'s `Passed`, by its decider) and the reload
  waits. It is typed once the turn is idle, no waiter holds and the box is empty, and is
  `unknown` at its bound (a step it may take while it waits). A row that comes meanwhile has no
  waiter, so it is typed in an `Inlet` step of its own. Properties: `IntoIdleTurn` (no Enter
  into a running turn), `OneWriteInBox` (no keys into another write's), `DueFirst` (the waiter
  is passed only with no row open), `Arrives`, `ReloadAnswered`. `Reload_Waiter.cfg` (two rows,
  a person's prompt, two reloads, a waiter that may expire) checks them all. Four breaks fail:
  `TypeWhileHeld` (no pass; TLC's trace is F-239's: the turn ends, the waiter holds, the reload's
  keys go, a row is handed to the waiter, it wakes a turn, the reload's Enter goes into it),
  `PassWithDue`, `NoBound` and `NoInletStep`. `Delivery.tla` is not changed (see "Merging").
- **Intake.** `Intake.pass_waiter/4` ends the session's waiter's hold with a pass, the
  response empty, as its deadline does: the program exits 0 and wakes nothing. Answers
  `{:ok, :passed}`, `{:refused, :no_waiter}`, or `{:refused, :in_flight}` for a waiter whose
  text is in flight, which goes on.
- **Mailbox.** `reload_plugins/3` reads the turn in its own step. When it reads ended with a
  waiter held (`{:refused, :waiter}`): with a row to the agent open (queued or sending) it
  answers `{:refused, :message_due}`; else it passes the waiter (`:in_flight` is `message_due`
  too; `:no_waiter`, a hold that ended in between, goes on). The check and the pass are one
  step of `Mailbox`, so no row is handed to that waiter in between. The task then calls
  `Inlet.reload_plugins/3`, and while it refuses for the turn or the screen reads again every
  100 ms for `hold:` ms (5 000 by default). Past it, nothing typed, it answers
  `{:unknown, {:held, refusal}}`. With no pass, every refusal is the answer at once, as before.
  The call's default timeout grew by `hold:`, to 25 s.
- **After the reload** no waiter holds until a turn of the session ends: the pass ended it and
  `/reload-plugins` is a local command that runs no turn (assumed, new-4). So the next row goes
  by the typed write, a short one as itself and a long one as its pointer line, and its turn's
  end brings the next waiter. A row too wide even as a pointer line is F-240's case: on `main`
  it waits for a waiter; with F-240 its wake line goes. The first test checks a short row.
- **Tests.** `MailboxReloadTest`, on a real `Sessions` with the waiter hook, `Intake` behind
  Bandit, `Registry`, `Inlet`, tmux and dtach, `ScreenStandIn` and `WaiterStandIn`. A waiter
  held: the reload answers `{:ok, :reloaded}`, the hook exits 0 with nothing on stderr,
  `/reload-plugins` is typed once, no waiter holds after it, and the next row is typed and
  delivered by its user line. With a stand-in `Intake` whose waiter never goes: a row sending
  to it refuses the reload `message_due`, with no pass and nothing typed; with no row, the
  reload is `{:unknown, {:held, {:turn, :waiter}}}` at a 500 ms bound, with nothing typed.
  `IntakeWaiterTest`: a pass ends a held waiter's response empty and leaves no waiter; a waiter
  with text in flight is refused `in_flight` and its text goes on. With `pass/3` cut out, the
  first test answers `{:refused, {:turn, :waiter}}`, the real run's answer.
- The design's waiter paragraph and `reload_plugins` row, `Intake`'s and `Mailbox`'s docs, and
  the README status line say so.

## What I ran into

- The waiter does not hold Claude Code's turn the way a blocking hook would: it is an
  `asyncRewake` Stop hook, so the turn has ended and the box takes keys while it runs (the
  design's "Delivery into a main session"). What the hold risks is a wake: a text handed to it
  starts a turn whatever is in the box. So the pass is what makes the reload safe, and the wait
  after it is a margin, not a wait for a turn to end (new-1).
- `Intake` had no way for a decider to end a waiter's hold but by a text. `Hold.tla` has the
  decider's `Passed`; `Intake.tla`'s waiter does not take it (new-3).
- I put the model in a spec of its own. `Delivery.tla` is where a typed write's guard is, but
  F-240 adds a constant to it that every `Delivery` configuration and the four specs that
  instance it must set; a second change there would conflict in about fifty files.
- `mix deps.get` was needed once in this worktree before the first compile.

## Findings

### new-1. The design says a waiter "holds the turn"; it holds a wake, not the box

What happened: the brief, and F-233's check (`Turn.interactive/3`'s `:ended`), treat a held
waiter as a turn not yet ended. Its hook is `asyncRewake`: the turn has ended and the box takes
keys. The danger is that a text handed to the waiter wakes a turn between a write's keys and
its Enter (`Reload.tla`'s `TypeWhileHeld` trace).
Why: `:ended` was named for "ended, and a waiter will carry the row", the typed write's reason
to stay out.
What I did: built the pass and a short wait after it.
What the design should change: the waiter paragraph says that a held waiter's turn has ended,
and that a typed write stays out because the waiter may wake the session, not because the box
is busy.

### new-2. `unknown` at the bound, with nothing typed

What happened: the brief asks for `unknown` when the hold never ends. Nothing was typed, so the
reload's outcome is known: not reloaded. What changed is the waiter, which the pass ended.
Why: the act changed the session (it lost its waiter), so it is not a clean refusal either.
What I did: answered `{:unknown, {:held, refusal}}`, which `Acts.answered/4` settles `unknown`.
What the design should change: decide it with F-240's new-2 (a row answered at a bound with
nothing sent). Recommended: `{:refused, {:held, refusal}}`, with the act's row noting the pass,
since the operator's next step is the same as for any refusal: try again.

### new-3. `Intake.tla`'s waiter has no decider's pass

What happened: `Hold.tla` lets a decider pass a held request. `Intake.tla`'s waiter instance
takes only its deadline, a newer waiter and a cut. `pass_waiter/4` is modeled in `Reload.tla`
only, abstractly.
Why: the brief named the reload's model; `Intake.tla` is large and its waiter configs are slow.
What I did: nothing in `Intake.tla`.
What the design should change: `Intake.tla` gains `WaiterPassed(cur)`, `HW!Passed` for the
session's waiter while not deciding, so `WaiterOneResponse` and `WaiterRequestsEnd` are checked
with it.

### new-4. Whether `/reload-plugins` starts a Stop hook is not known

What happened: the model and the code assume the command runs no turn, so no waiter comes back
after it.
Why: no capture on 2.1.322 (F-233's new-1).
What I did: nothing that depends on it. If one comes, the next reload passes it as the first did.
What the design should change: the real run's records say which (item 3 above), and the design
states it.

### new-5. A steady stream of rows can refuse every reload

What happened: a reload is refused `message_due` whenever a row to the session is open while its
waiter holds. A session that gets rows often could refuse each try.
Why: the brief sends a due row first by the waiter.
What I did: the refusal, and nothing more.
What the design should change: nothing unless real use shows it. If it does, `Mailbox` holds new
rows to a session from the pass until the reload is answered (F-233's new-5 names the same hold).
