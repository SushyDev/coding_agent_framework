# F-217: every bounded wait reads the clock

F-216's new-1: on this host a sleep wakes late in proportion to its length, so a wait bounded by
a count of sleeps overshoots its stated bound by several times, as SQLite's busy wait did.
Decided: every bounded wait in the library takes its deadline once from the clock and checks
against it, and a stated bound is a clock bound. This unit surveys every wait and turns the ones
bounded by a count into ones bounded by the clock, with the same stated bound.

## The survey

Every wait in `apps/*/lib` and in the test aids (`apps/caf_test/lib`): polls, retries, `receive
… after`, sleep loops, timers, and the loops of the shell scripts the parts run on a host. Paths
are under `apps/<app>/lib/coding_agent_framework/`. "Mono" is `System.monotonic_time`; "OS" is
`System.os_time` (or a part's `clock:`, by default the same).

## What was measured

On this macOS host, load average 3 to 5:

| wait | bound | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Holder.Process.await_dead/3` on a live program | 1.5 s | 3.9 s | 1.74 to 1.89 s (8 runs) |
| `caf-run` with no answer | 30 s (30 asks) | 34.0 s | not run at 30 s |
| `caf-run` with no answer, `unanswered: 2_000` | 2 s | | 2.43 to 2.56 s (4 runs) |

A shell loop of `sleep 0.1` while a 0.5 s timer lived ran 3 rounds, not 5: each round took
about 0.2 s, half of it the sleep's late wake. The count of 15 reads behind a 1.5 s wait took
3.9 s for the same reason, with `dtach -p` and `ps` in each round.

## What was built

**The shape.** A host's shell has no millisecond clock (POSIX `date` gives seconds, and not on
every system), so a shell wait runs one `sleep` of its whole bound in the background beside its
reads, and reads again only while `kill -0` finds that sleep alive. One sleep wakes late once,
not once per try. The wait ends within one round after its bound: the round's sleep, its reads,
and the timer's own late wake. At the end the timer is killed only while `kill -0` still finds
it: it is the shell's own child, and a pid is no other program's until its parent reaps it.

- **`Holder.Process`'s read** (`caf_holder`): `@read` takes the wait in seconds, not a count.
  One timer serves every name of the read, so a read of several names waits out one deadline,
  which the shell's own timeout (the holder's and the wait) already assumed; before, each name
  counted its own reads. A wait of 0 starts no timer. The docs of `read/3` and `await_dead/3`
  say the wait is by the host's clock. `Jobs`' `wait_end` and `Services`' `read_held` use this
  wait and needed no change.
- **`Services`' offset wait** (`caf_services`): 2 s by one timer, 0.1 s between reads.
- **`caf-run`** (`caf_runs`): the first ask with no answer starts one timer of the bound, and
  the wrapper asks again once a second while it lives; then it runs the command without a lease,
  as before. The timer starts at the first ask with no answer, not at the wrapper's start, so a
  long queued wait that the framework then drops is not counted, as the count did not count it.
  The timer is killed before the command runs (`exec` would keep it as the command's child) and
  before a refusal's exit. The bound is `prepare/5`'s new option `:unanswered` (ms, 30 s by
  default), written into the script with the paths; a bound that is not a positive integer is
  refused `:bad_unanswered`. The option is there so a test can state a bound of 2 s.
- **The design**: rule 5 says a stated bound is a bound by the clock, and how a shell takes it;
  the `Runs` paragraph states the 30 s. **The README** status line.

No receive was re-entered with the full timeout: every re-entered `receive` already waits the
time left.

## The model

No spec in `model/tla/` counts tries of these waits. `Schedule.tla`'s shape `Native` has the
wrapper ask and end, not ask 30 times; `Start.tla` reads a held program's record
(`Holder.Process`) as one step, with no count of reads. `Limit.tla`'s `BoundedRetries` bounds the
framework's go-ons at a usage limit, a count by design, not a wait. No spec changed.

## Tests

- `holder_process_test.exs`: `await_dead/3` on a live program, 1.5 s, answers the live reading
  no sooner than 1.5 s and within 1 s after it; then a read of two live sockets whose records
  never come, `wait: 1_500`, answers both without records in the same window, one deadline, not
  one each. Against the code before this unit the first part took 3.9 s and failed.
- `runs_test.exs`: the wrapper against a listener that hangs up on every ask, `unanswered:
  2_000`, runs the command without a lease and says so, no sooner than 2 s and within 1.5 s
  after it (the bound is seen at the next ask, a second later); and `prepare/5` refuses a bound
  of 0.

The stated slacks are wide because a round is a `sleep` and two programs, each slow under load;
the tests still fail the count's overshoot. They wait on the call's own answer, not a sleep.

## What I ran into

- Job specs (`kill %1`) are not portable: dash in a script ignores them, and bash keeps a
  finished job, so `kill -0 %1` answered alive forever. A pid and `kill -0` behaved the same in
  dash, macOS's `/bin/sh`, devenv's bash and zsh: each reaps a finished background child while
  it waits for the next foreground command.
- macOS's `/bin/sh` (bash 3.2) cannot parse `caf-run`'s `case` inside `$( )`; the wrapper runs
  under the `sh` from `Programs`, which is devenv's bash here. I measured the old wrapper under
  that one.
- The worktree's `deps` lagged the lock for `plug`; `mix deps.get` fixed it.

## Findings

### new-1. Some in-process deadlines are taken from OS time

What happened: `Holder.Process.dead_by/3` and `end_held/5`, `Jobs`' `retry`, `Copies.Held.settle/5`
and `Services.wait/4` take their deadlines from `System.os_time`, and the parts with a `clock:`
read OS time by default. Why: most of these deadlines are a row's (`start_until`, `deadline`)
and must outlive a restart, which the monotonic clock does not; the rest share the part's `now/0`.
What I did: nothing, since none counts tries: each is checked against a clock. What the design
should change: say which deadlines are a row's (OS time, kept across restarts) and which live
only in a call; a call's own (`end_held/5`'s grace and `@kill_wait`, `Services.wait/4`'s
`timeout`, `Jobs`' read retries within a call) could use the monotonic clock, so a step of the
wall clock does not stretch or cut them. `dead_by/3`'s public deadline is OS time, and
`Services` passes its own `now()` to it, so that change moves both together.

### new-2. `WaiterStandIn`'s hook run has no bound of its own

What happened: `WaiterStandIn.run/2` (`apps/caf_test`) waits in a `receive` with no `after` for
the hook's exit. Why: the test's `Task.await/2` (5 s by default) bounds the test, not the hook,
which may run on after the test fails. What I did: nothing. What the design should change: a
bound of the test's own `await` (5 s), after which the port is closed and the hook's group ended,
answering `{:timeout, …}`.

### new-3. One `caf-run` ask has no bound

What happened: `caf-run` runs `curl` with no `--max-time`. A framework that accepts the
connection and then stalls without closing it holds the wrapper, and so the agent's command,
for as long as it stalls. Why: a queued ask waits legitimately for as long as the `Runs.Plug`'s
`:wait`, so a short bound would cut real waits. What I did: nothing. What the design should
change: a `--max-time` of the plug's `:wait` and a slack (a few seconds), written into the
wrapper as `prepare/5` writes the no-answer bound, after which the ask counts as no answer.
