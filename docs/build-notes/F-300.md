# F-300: an app reads the host's pressure shares, and each queue's length

## What was built

- `Scheduler.Pressure` keeps `shares`: every line the last span checked, at every level, `ok`
  included. Each is a reading with the level it asks for, `%{file, line, share, threshold,
  asks, at}`, in the order the gate checks them: memory `full` (`asks: :severe`), then the
  `some` lines of cpu, memory and io (`asks: :high`). On macOS there are two entries of `file:
  :level`, the kernel's level held to `level_severe:` and to `level_high:`. `shares` is nil
  until a span has been read. The values come from the sample the `Scheduler` already takes.
  No read was added.
- `Scheduler.pressure/2` answers two more keys: `shares`, and `hosts`. `hosts` has one entry
  for each other host the `Scheduler` leases for (the hosts of its `{name, host}` resources).
  Each entry has the same shape as the answer: `level: :ok` (as the gates take it), `source:
  :none`, `at: nil`, `unread: :not_sampled`, `shares: nil`.
- `Scheduler.waiting/2` answers each queue's length: for every resource key it leases
  (`"seat"`, `"slot@mac"`, `"land:app"`), the number of queued requests that need some of it,
  0 when none does. It is a read of the in-memory queue, a call of its own. The hot path
  (request, grant, release) is unchanged.
- `CodingAgentFramework.holds/2` reads it, so each of its `resources` is `%{capacity, held,
  waiting}`. Its `pressure` is `Scheduler.pressure/2`'s answer, so the shares and the hosts'
  entries are there too.
- The shapes are in each function's `@doc` with an example, in `docs/design.md` ("The app's surface"
  and "A lease"'s gates), and in the README's status list.
- No model was written. This unit adds reads of state the `Scheduler` already holds. It adds no
  step to any protocol.

## What was measured

- `caf_scheduler`'s tests: 27 (8 doctests, 19 tests), three of them new or extended:
  - a stand-in PSI directory at `ok` answers the four lines' shares (0.0 each, since the totals
    do not grow) and thresholds (0.01, 0.25, 0.10, 0.20), and a `mac` host's entry as
    `unread: :not_sampled`;
  - a full seat with two requests waiting reads `"seat" => 2`, and the slot and the lock read 0;
    after one is granted, 1;
  - the macOS-shaped source at `severe` answers its two entries, level 4 held to 4 and to 2.
- `caf_surface`: the panel test reads `waiting: 0` on every resource, with both slots held. The
  tree's pressure test reads the app's own memory `full` threshold (0.05) in the panel's shares.
- The tests wait on the answer they check (`await`). They have no timing thresholds.
- The gates: `devenv test` passed, and `gate-test` passed in runs 1, 2 and 4. Run 3 failed once
  in `caf_delegates` (`delegates_interactive_test.exs:546`, a message answered just after a
  turn: `answered/2` timed out at `{:refused, :no_answer}`), at a load of about 39 on 12 cores.
  This unit does not touch `caf_delegates`. The test passed alone three times out of three.
  `bin/check-model` (fast configurations) passed.

## What I ran into

- The brief asks for "Linux PSI `some avg10` per resource". The gate does not read `avg10`. It
  reads the growth of each line's `total` over the span between two samples, and its
  thresholds are held to that share (design, "A lease", "Gates"). An `avg10` beside a threshold
  that is compared to something else would mislead a panel. So the shares answered are the
  span's, the numbers the level comes from. See new-1.
- The brief names CPU and memory. The answer has every line the gate checks: memory `full`,
  and the `some` lines of cpu, memory and io. A panel can drop what it does not show. If a
  line is missing from the answer, a level set by it could not be explained.
- A remote host's pressure: the `Scheduler` has no sample of it today. The design decided it is
  not read (F-292): another host's runs are taken as unpressured and bounded by their slots. So
  the host's entry is answered as unread, and nothing more was built. See new-2.

## Findings

### new-1. The panel's shares are the span's, not the kernel's `avg10`

- **What happened:** the brief asked for PSI `some avg10` per resource. The `Scheduler` never
  parses `avg10`. Its level comes from the growth of `total` over each 2 s span.
- **Why:** `avg10` is the kernel's own 10-second average. The thresholds (`cpu: 0.25`, and the
  others) are held to the span's share. Answering `avg10` with those thresholds would show a
  number that did not set the level.
- **What I did:** answered the span's share for each line the gate checks, beside its threshold
  and the level it asks for. I did not parse `avg10`.
- **What the design should change:** nothing, unless the app wants the kernel's averages as
  well. They are in the same file the sample reads, so `Pressure.parse/1` could keep them at
  no extra read. If they are wanted, name them apart (`avg10`), not as the share the gate reads.

### new-2. A remote host's pressure is not sampled, so its entry reads `unread: :not_sampled`

- **What happened:** the app wants a host's pressure once the host takes jobs (F-296).
- **Why:** F-292 decided that another host's runs are bounded by their slots alone. Reading
  that host's pressure would put an ssh on every 2 s sample.
- **What I did:** `Scheduler.pressure/2`'s `hosts` has an entry per other host it leases for,
  `level: :ok` and `unread: :not_sampled`. Nothing samples it.
- **What the design should change:** if a panel needs a remote host's pressure, read it on the
  host's own reads, not the `Scheduler`'s. For example, a job's wrapper could leave the host's
  `/proc/pressure` totals (or the macOS level) beside its log when it starts and ends, or
  `Hosts` could read them once per placement it already makes over ssh. Either one gives a
  sample with its own time, which a panel can show beside `at`. It would not gate anything, so
  F-292's rule stands. Gating on it would be a new decision with its own model.

### new-3. `waiting/2` is a second call from the panel

- **What happened:** the panel now makes two calls to the `Scheduler`, `pressure/2` and
  `waiting/2`, one after another.
- **Why:** the brief kept this unit to the read path, with no change to the messages the hot
  path handles. A call of its own is the smallest read.
- **What I did:** left it as two calls. A queue can change between them, so the panel's
  `waiting` and its `pressure` may be a few ms apart. Both are reads of memory.
- **What the design should change:** if F-299 or F-301 adds a panel read to the `Scheduler`,
  it could answer both in one call.
