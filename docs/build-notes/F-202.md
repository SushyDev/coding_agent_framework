# F-202: the facts the metrics could not read

The brief's seven items, from F-199's new-1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 and F-194's new-4: each fact F-199's
fold listed under `unread` is written once, when it happens, and the fold reads it. Built on
`main` at F-199 (a826bea). About 500 changed lines with tests and docs, this note included, so
the unit was not split. No test started the real Claude Code.

## What was built

- **How a report was taken** (item 1): `Delegates.Table` has a `taken_by` column, added to a
  file made before (`@added`). `Delegates` writes it with the report: `result` (a headless
  turn's `result`), `transcript` (the transcript's last reply, read once the process was gone:
  a headless delegate's `exited/4`, and an interactive one's reply found with its process
  gone), `turn_end` (an interactive turn read ended, its process still running). A failure's
  notice, the framework's own, leaves it NULL.
- **A hold's open time** (item 2): `Intake.Table` has `held_at`, written with the row when the
  event is held, never by a move or by a start's settle. `Prompts` reads a hold's `opened_at`
  from it, and from `written_at` for a row of an earlier build.
- **A kill seen** (item 3): `Delegates` records each kill in `Acts` (`Acts.record/7`, actor
  `delegates:<host>`, act `killed`, outcome `verified`, state `killed:<host>:<row id>:<resumes>`,
  detail the row and its resumes) before it decides between a resume and a failure, in
  `exited/4` and in `settle_interactive/3`. A record not written decides nothing; the next cue
  reads the kill again. It is recorded only once the row decides (its process gone, its agent
  row free or its one resume spent), so a cue that waits for `Sessions` writes nothing.
- **A delivery time** (item 4): `Mailbox.Table` has `delivered_at`, written in the same update
  as `delivered`, at both places a row is delivered.
- **A prompt's open** (item 5): the first read of `Prompts` that lists a prompt records its open
  in `Acts` (actor `prompts:<host>`, act `opened`, state `opened:<host>:<prompt id>`, target its
  agent or, with none, its id; detail its id, kind, agent and `opened_at`, the prompt's own or
  the read's time). A set in its state keeps it to one call per start, and the key keeps it to
  one row across starts.
- **The fold** (items 6 and 7): `stalled` counts a delegate whose row says `transcript`
  (`why: ["stop_lost"]`, beside `["stuck_background"]`); `late` (a) is the same fact, and (c)
  reads `delivered_at`; `killed` counts each kill (`count`) and the delegates killed
  (`delegates`) from the `killed` records, the excusing acts read since the kill before; a
  `killed` record is no recovery; `prompts` counts every hold by its `held_at` (one settled
  `answered` or `passed` by an actor was answered by it, at its settle) and every `opened`
  record. No metric has `unread` now. A row written before F-202 is read as F-199 read it: a
  kill from its resume's claim or the row's count, a delivery from `updated_at`, a hold still
  held from `written_at`, a prompt from its answer row. The periods (item 6) are in the fold's
  `@doc` and the design, confirmed.
- **Docs**: the fold's `@doc`; "Metrics and healing" (the cuts, the killed rule, the table's
  `stalled`, `late`, `killed` and `prompts` rows, and a paragraph on the facts the rules read);
  the module docs of `Delegates`, `Delegates.Table`, `Intake.Table`, `Mailbox.Table` and
  `Prompts`.

## What was measured

- `MetricsTest`, ten tests on fixed rows. Stalled: a row `taken_by` `transcript` counts as
  `stop_lost`, one taken by `result` does not. Late: (a) from `taken_by`; (c) from
  `delivered_at`, 61 s late and not 59 s whatever `updated_at` says, and from `updated_at` for
  a row with none. Killed from records: a kill whose resume was refused before its claim (no
  resume on the row), a delegate killed twice (two kills, one delegate), and one whose first
  kill the operator's `end` excuses but not its second; the resume after a kill is restarted,
  and no `killed` record is a recovery. Prompts: rows of an earlier build read as before; a hold
  by its `held_at`, the app's answer at its settle (`by: "app"`, 500 ms); an expired hold opened,
  never answered; a login and a limit prompt counted from their `opened` records; each in the
  period it opened.
- `DelegatesTest`: `taken_by` `result` on a finish, `transcript` on a kill after the last reply,
  NULL on a second kill's failure. With a real `Acts`: no kill is recorded while `Sessions`' row
  still runs; then the `killed` row, keyed by resumes 0, before the resume's claim; a second
  kill is a row of its own, keyed by resumes 1. `DelegatesInteractiveTest`: `turn_end`.
- `IntakeToolsTest`: `held_at` equals `written_at` while held, and stays when the deadline
  settles the row 50 ms or more later; it stays through a restart's settle to `lost`.
- `MailboxWaiterTest`: `delivered_at` written with `delivered`, and a later write of the row
  leaves it.
- `PromptsTest`: a permission prompt's open is one `opened` row with its id, kind, agent and
  `opened_at`, and a restart of the inbox writes none again; a login's open is a row whose
  target is the prompt's id, kept after the login is answered.
- Gates: `devenv test` passed; `bin/check-model` (in devenv) passed. `devenv shell -- gate-test`
  failed in each of three runs, every time on a test this unit does not touch: the Delegates
  test at `delegates_test.exs:239` (runs 1 and 2, new-4) and the Services test at
  `services_test.exs:463` (run 3, new-5). Both also fail on `main` at a826bea: three gate-test
  runs there failed once on the Services test, and the Delegates tests alone with seed 449221
  failed once on main as well as on this branch. Every other test passed in every run.
- No spec changed. `Delegate.tla` does not model the ledger; the kill record is a fact under
  its own key, `Claim.tla`'s claim and settle in one step, and it can only delay `KillSeen`, as
  the resume's claim already does. The other facts are columns written in the step that writes
  their row.

## What I ran into

- The brief does not say where a prompt's open is kept. `Prompts` has no row of its own, and
  `Acts.record/7` is the ledger's way to keep a fact a reader found, under a key, once. So it is
  a row in `Acts`, which the surface already reads; no table, migration or reader was added.
- "Killed, a delegate killed twice included": I count each kill, so a delegate killed twice is
  two, and give the number of delegates beside it (new-3).
- The `killed` records share `Acts`' unique `(target, state)` with the resume's claim, whose
  state is the JSON of the row and its resumes. So the kill's state is a key of its own that
  begins with its act, as the read facts' are; the fold reads its row and resumes from its
  `detail`.
- A `Stop` that reaches Claude Code's cap is held at its post and waits on a person only once
  the app's callback answered the cap's last block (`Intake`'s `take/5`). Its `held_at` is the
  post's time, so its prompt's open is now that time, not the cap's (new-1).

## Findings

### new-1. A `stop_cap` prompt opens at the Stop's hold, before it waits on a person

What happened: `Intake` holds a `Stop` at its post, asks the app's callback, and only when the
callback blocks at the cap's last does the row become a `stop_cap` prompt (`prompt` set, its
`written_at` moved). F-202's `held_at` is the post's time, and `Prompts` now reads a hold's open
from it, so a `stop_cap` prompt's `opened_at`, and its time to answer, include the callback's
time.
Why: one `held_at` per row, written at the hold, as the brief says; the cap is a later step.
What I did: read `held_at` for both kinds. The callback is the app's own, so its time is
usually small.
What the design should change: either accept it (a `stop_cap` prompt counts from the Stop's
hold), or `Intake` writes the cap's time too (`capped_at`, once, with `prompt: "stop_cap"`) and a
`stop_cap` prompt opens then.

### new-2. A login prompt's id is reused, so its second open is no row

What happened: a login prompt's id is `login:<account>`. Once the account is logged in again
and later refused again, the new prompt has the same id, and `Prompts`' `opened` record, one
row per id, is not written again. The fold counts that login prompt once.
Why: every other kind's id names its state (a dialog's start, a menu's read, a limit's reset, a
symptom's row); a login's names only its account.
What I did: nothing beyond the brief's one row per id.
What the design should change: a login prompt's id names its refusal (`login:<account>:<the
refused check's time>`), as a menu's names the read that found it.

### new-3. Confirm what `killed` counts

What happened: the brief says the fold counts killed from the records, "a delegate killed twice
included". That reads as either each kill (a delegate killed twice is two) or each delegate
(including one whose second kill F-199 had no fact of).
What I did: `count` is the kills and `delegates` the delegates killed, each kill in its
evidence with its resumes.
What the design should change: name which one the baseline compares, so the digest shows that
one.

### new-4. A message can be delivered between a background command's end and the turn it begins

What happened: `DelegatesTest`'s "a turn a background command's end begins is never a second
report or a reply" fails now and then under load, on `main` too: the parent's message is
`delivered` (via the stream, about 70 ms after it was queued) where the test expects it
`queued` through the turn the command's end begins.
Why: as far as I read it, `Mailbox`'s stream delivery waits for `Turn.headless/2` to read
`:ended`. Once the stream has the task's end but not yet the next turn's start, there are no
tasks and no running turn, so a read in that window delivers.
What I did: nothing; it is the stream reader after a background command, which F-204 builds.
What the design should change: the fold should read a task's end as a turn about to begin
until that turn's start or a bound passes, so no delivery falls between the two.

### new-5. A 64-letter service name's socket path can pass the host's limit

What happened: `ServicesTest`'s "a name is a file name on the host" failed with
`{:socket_path_too_long, "/tmp/caf-14029/t103106/ws/services/<64 letters>.sock"}`, on `main`
too.
Why: the path grows with the test VM's pid and the test's directory number, so a long name
passes the socket path limit on some runs only.
What I did: nothing.
What the design should change: the longest name a service may have should fit the socket path
of the longest framework home, or the socket should be named by something shorter than the
service's name.
