# F-189: the end-to-end runbook, current; the Linux pass's checks

A person runs `docs/e2e.md` next, on a fresh home on macOS and then on Linux. It was last
brought up to date at F-176; F-177 to F-186 landed since. This unit brings every step to the
code on `main`, adds the steps that exercise what an app will rely on, and adds the checks only
Linux can make, each with the record it leaves. Docs and one small script; no library code. No
step of it started the real Claude Code.

## What was built

- **Every step current** (`docs/e2e.md`, steps 0 to 9 and the old 10, now 14). Each prompt a
  step meets is read with `App.prompts("e2e", …)` and answered with `App.answer("e2e", id,
  choice, "me")`, the app's `prompts/2` and `answer/4`, by the config's instance: the trust
  dialogs (steps 2, 4, 7), a main session's and an interactive delegate's permission requests,
  the ask (step 5, with `{:refused, :settled}` for the second answer, where the console's
  `answer_ask` read `:answered`). The texts as the code has them: a delivery framed `From
  <sender> (<kind>):`, the pointer line in backticks after its frame, a delegate's prompt told
  to its parent once, the pick by room among usable accounts (F-181) in place of "the most
  allowance left". The config gains a third account never logged in (`three`), a cap of 6 and
  8 seats, since the run keeps up to six delegates until its end.
- **New steps**, each with what to do, see and record:
  - 10, the one inbox: an ask, a main session's permission request and an interactive
    delegate's trust dialog open at once, read whole and by `subtree:` and `kind:`, each
    answered once and refused `settled` the second time;
  - 11, usage: `Usage.readings/2` before and after a turn against the status line's file and
    Claude Code's `/usage`; `Logins.check/3` refusing `three`, its `login` prompt, `check`;
  - 12, a headless delegate with one background command: the stdin correction in its request,
    the report while the command runs, one notice of Claude Code's own turn, no second report,
    and the event log's order;
  - 13, an absent main: its pid from its agent row, checked against `ps` and its session file,
    ended by that pid; one resume in `Acts`; a second end within the bound a `remedy` prompt,
    answered `leave`, then the operator's resume;
  - 15, the exit dialog's capture with `bin/capture-screen`, the session file read at two
    states, the four new fixtures and their manifest entries for `2.1.322`.
- **`## On Linux`**: the whole run again, with what differs (the login's file, the session's
  `PATH` on NixOS, what Claude Code writes outside the home), and four checks: L1 the process
  tree of a headless session with a background command and a stdio MCP server, and the rule
  that decides `stuck_background`'s default; L2 `StuckBackgrounds.Groups.parse/2` over its own
  read of `/proc`, against `ps` and `CLK_TCK`, and the symptom; L3 one `system`/`init` per turn
  of Claude Code's own; L4 an interactive delegate's brief past `MAX_ARG_STRLEN`, by pointer.
- **"After the run"**: the records by name and step, what each settles, that they are scrubbed
  of the person's names before they enter this public repository, and where the fixtures go.
- **`bin/quiet-mcp`**: a stdio MCP server in plain bash that answers `initialize`,
  `tools/list` and any other request, offers no tool, and then waits on its stdin with no CPU
  time and no child: L1's server.

## What was measured

  with spaces in its JSON, each answered by its id; exit 0 at end of file. Not run under Claude

- The gates: see "Gates".

## What I ran into

- The example console offers none of the inbox's calls (new-1), so every step names the
  surface's functions and a few parts' through `Run.parts/1`, after one block of aliases in
  step 1 (typed again after step 8's restart).
- A headless delegate's permission request is not in the inbox (new-2), so step 3 keeps the
  console's `answer_request`, and records whether one shows anyway.
- The first draft of step 11 moved `demo-main` to `three` to see the login refused; the
  `Mover` would have begun that move (new-3). The step now warns against it.
- A headless brief goes in on the stream, not as an argument, so L4's check of the argument cap
  uses an interactive delegate.
- `jq` is not in devenv: step 12 reads the event log in the console.

## Findings

### new-1. The example console has no call for the inbox, the symptoms, usage or logins

What happened: the brief says each prompt is answered through `prompts/2` and `answer/4`
"where the app offers it". `caf_example`'s console offers `dialogs`, `requests`,
`answer_request`, `asks` and `answer_ask`, each the older mechanism's, and nothing for
`prompts/2`, `answer/4`, `symptoms/2`, `Usage.readings/2`, `Logins.check/3` or `Acts.rows/3`.
Why: the console predates F-179 and F-181 to F-186. What I did: the runbook calls the surface
by the instance's name (`App.prompts("e2e")`) and the parts through `Run.parts(caf)`, after
aliases. What the design should change: a small unit in `caf_example` adds `prompts(caf,
filter)`, `answer(caf, id, choice)` (as the config's operator), `symptoms(caf, filter)`,
`usage(caf)` and `acts(caf, filter)`, each printing a short reading as the others do; the
runbook then names them.

### new-2. A headless delegate's permission request is no prompt

What happened: `Prompts` reads `Intake`'s held `PermissionRequest`s. A headless delegate runs
with `--permission-prompts host`, so its request is Claude Code's `can_use_tool` over the
stream, held in `Stream`'s fold and answered by `Stream.answer/5`; the inbox does not list it,
and its parent is not told. Why: F-179 built the kinds from `Intake`'s holds. What I did: step 3
answers it from the console and records what the inbox read. What the design should change:
`Prompts` reads each fold's held requests as kind `permission` (id
`permission:stream:<session>:<request id>`), answered through `Stream.answer/5`, told to the
parent once as any delegate's prompt; unless step 3's record shows Claude Code also sends the
hook from a headless session.

### new-3. A move to a named account checks only that it is prepared

What happened: `Mover.move/4` with `to:` checks the account is known and `Accounts.ready/2`
(its files written), and begins the drain; `usable:` (`Logins.usable/3`) is read only when the
`Mover` picks the account itself. `prepare/3` writes every configured account's files at the
app's start, so a move to a logged-out account begins, ends the agent's process, and resumes it
on Claude Code's own login screen. Why: F-181 gave `usable:` to the pick. What I did: step 11
warns against such a move. What the design should change: a named target is checked by
`usable:` before the claim, and refused `{:account, to, reason}` with nothing begun, as a
resume that must leave its account is (one clause in `Mover`'s `target/3`).

### new-4. `StuckBackgrounds` reads only at a cue, and a quiet command sends none

What happened: `StuckBackgrounds` checks at its start and at each `stream` or `watch` cue. A
background command that hangs writes nothing to the stream, and `Stream` cues only after a
write that changed rows, so after the report's last events the healer may read no more, and
the quiet group's ten minutes pass with no read. `HooksSilent` reads at the tree's interval
for this reason. Why: F-184 gave the healer the cues of the readings it takes. What I did: L2
records whether the symptom shows by itself, and asks for one read with `check/2` if not.
What the design should change: `StuckBackgrounds` reads at the tree's `interval` too, as
`HooksSilent` does.

### new-5. No stdio MCP server reaches a delegate as built

What happened: F-184's new-1 left `stuck_background`'s remedy off by default because a quiet
stdio MCP server that a project configures might read as a stuck group. Every launch passes
`--mcp-config` with the account's `mcp.json` and the agent's own (`caf`, over HTTP) and
`--strict-mcp-config`, so a project's `.mcp.json` and the user's servers never load, and the
surface writes the account's `mcp.json` with no server. Why: the framework's servers are its
own. What I did: L1 writes `bin/quiet-mcp` into each account's `mcp.json` by hand, after the
app's start (the next start writes the file again), to record the group's shape anyway. What
the design should change: nothing before the record; but say whether an app may ever give its
agents a stdio server. If not, the risk does not arise, and the remedy may go on by default
whatever L1 shows.

### new-6. The healer's read of the groups has no call a person can make

What happened: `StuckBackgrounds.Groups.read/3` needs the tree's `Holder.Process`, which the
console cannot build, and its shell text is a module attribute. What I did: L2 runs the same
shell text with `System.cmd/2` and the module's own `parse/2`. The copy in the runbook can
drift from the module. What the design should change: nothing now; if the copy drifts, the
console's `groups(caf, agent)` (new-1's unit) calls `read/3` through the tree's own record.

### new-7. A `remedy` prompt past the bound has no answer that resumes

What happened: a second absence within the bound is asked with `approve` and `leave`.
`absent_main` is already on the list, so `approve` widens nothing, and either answer leaves the
main stopped. Why: F-182's new-3 made "act once" the operator's own act. What I did: step 13
answers `leave`, then resumes with `Operator.resume/2`. What the design should change: say what
`approve` means for a state asked for its bound (nothing, or the act once), and show the
operator's resume beside such a prompt.
