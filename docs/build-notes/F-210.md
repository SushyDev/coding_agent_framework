# F-210: a move's resume reads the app's flag again; a moved lease names its account

The brief's three items: F-209's new-1 and new-2 as the app decided them. No test started the
real Claude Code.

## What was built

- **The model first** (`Move.tla`). A move's resume now reads the app's flag on its target at
  the resume, not only at the pick (`TargetTaken`, a guard on `LaunchMarked`, `ResumedWith`
  and `ResumedBare`, as `Mover.resume/2` reads `usable:` before it marks the first row):
  - **`MoveBack`**: the target flagged since the pick, a move not at a limit takes its own
    account as its target (the `Mover`'s `back/3`, which writes `move_to` back to
    `move_from`), and the resume runs there by the same steps, read by the same guard;
  - **`Stranded`**: its own account flagged too, the phase is `failed` with no process, as
    the `Mover`'s `stranded/3` leaves the row down and records an act `move` `failed`
    `stranded`. A limited session's move with a flagged target takes `Back`, as before;
  - **`NoResumeOnRefused`** has a second clause: the step from `stopping` or `ending` to
    `moved` never puts the row on an account the app flags. It reads the flag only, since a
    move's windows are read at its pick (`Usage.pick/4`) and the code's resume reads
    `usable:` (the login and the app), not the windows;
  - **`Break_Move_PickOnly`** reads the flag at the pick alone and fails the property: the
    trace flags the target while the stop waits for the turn, and the resume runs there;
  - **`Move_LimitFlags.cfg`** (new): `Move_Limit`'s bounds with `Flags = TRUE` and
    `NoResumeOnRefused`, the limited branch.
  - Fairness has `MoveBack` and `Stranded`; the header's `Mover` and app entries say it.
- **The code** (`Mover.resume/2`): `s.usable.(row.move_to)` in place of
  `Accounts.ready(s.accounts, row.move_to)`. The tree gives the `Mover` the same `usable/3`
  as `Delegates` (`Logins`, then the app's `Usable.check/3`), and by default it is
  `Accounts.ready/2`, as before. A refusal takes the path a refused resume already took
  (`answered/4`): `back/3` to the own account, whose resume reads `usable:` the same way, and
  with that refused too `stranded/3`; a limited agent's is `left_gone/3`. An `{:unknown, …}`
  waits for the next cue, as the launch's unknowns do. The moduledoc's `resuming` step, its
  failure list and `start_link/1`'s `usable:` say it.
- **The lease's account**: read, and left as it is. The `Scheduler` reads a request's
  `account` once, in its budget gate at the grant (`spent/2`; `Schedule.tla`'s
  `NoStartWithoutBudget`), and the telemetry of a grant or a refusal names it then. After the
  grant, what the `Scheduler` counts is per resource (`Table.held/1` sums `units`), never per
  account, and no other part reads a lease row's account to decide anything: `Jobs`, `Runs`,
  `Mains` and `Delegates` read a lease's id and holder. Two displays show it: the `Tracker`'s
  `leases` rows (every column, `Scheduler.Table.meta/0`) and the example's `Console.leases/2`
  (`lease 3: builder for app on work, …`). So the design says it, in "A lease": the account is
  read once by the grant's gate, and a moved delegate's lease names the account the grant was
  read against; the agent row names the one it runs on. Nothing else changed (new-1 below).
- **Docs**: the design's `resuming` step and failure list in "How `Mover` does it", "A lease",
  and `Move.tla`'s entry in "The model".

## What I ran into

- The `Mover` is the process that calls `usable:`, in its own `handle_call` and steps, as its
  pick already did; the tree's `usable/3` is a `Logins` call (bounded by its own timeout) and
  the app's function.
- `Stranded` waits, as `Back` does, until no row to the session is `sending`. The code refuses
  by `usable:` before it marks a first row, so it is not held by a row a restart left
  `sending`; the model leaves that difference out, as it leaves the row's deadline out.
- `mix test` failed at first on plug's lock (F-209 moved it); `mix deps.get` and a `--force`
  compile in both environments, as the brief says.

## Findings

### new-1. The lease's displays name the start's account after a move

What happened: a delegate that a resume (F-209) or the `Mover` puts on `spare` keeps its
start's lease, whose row says `work`. The `Tracker`'s `leases` rows and the example's
`Console.leases/2` (`… on work, seat 1: held by delegate …`) show `work` while it runs on
`spare`.
Why: the lease's account is what its grant was read against; no part reads it after the grant,
so the brief's rule changed nothing, and the design now says what the column means.
What I did: the design's "A lease" says it; nothing else.
What the design should change: either nothing (a person reads the account from the agent row),
or the console's line says `asked on work` so it does not read as where the work runs. A write
of the new account on the lease would need `Boxes.tla` or `Schedule.tla` first, for a display.

### new-2. A `usable:` that keeps answering unknown holds a move's resume with no bound

What happened: `Logins.usable/3` answers `{:unknown, …}` when `claude auth status` cannot
tell. At a move's resume that leaves the row `resuming`, its process gone, asked again at each
cue. `Accounts.ready/2`, the old check, never answered unknown.
Why: the move's deadline bounds its drain and its stop, not its resume, and an unknown decides
nothing (the rule `Delegates`' resume follows, F-209). The launch's own unknowns already wait
the same way.
What I did: nothing; the brief names a refusal.
What the design should change: decide whether a move's resume has a deadline of its own after
which an unknown is taken as a refusal (back, or stranded), or whether a person reads it from
the row's `resuming` phase as today.
