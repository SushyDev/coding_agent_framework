# F-182: the healer on an allow-list (B8) and the symptom record (B7's start)

Several remedies are due at once (F-180's hung background command and absent main, F-181's
usage-limit remedies), and each must have one shape. This unit builds that shape once: the
contract, the record, the list, the cap and the `remedy` prompt, with today's two acts as its
first two entries. It runs well past 1,000 lines, so, as the brief says, it lands items 1 to 3
(and item 5, one clause) and stops before item 4's remedies. No test or step of it started the
real Claude Code.

## What was built

- **`model/tla/Heal.tla`** (item 1), instancing `Claim` as `C`. A symptom is a fact of a target
  in a state (`on`, `st`); the healer's steps are `Detect` (the state recorded first), `Claim`
  (a recorded symptom of a kind on the list, under the cap: the `Ledger`'s row first, counted in
  the bound), `Act` (its answer ok, refused or unknown; what it does is the instancing spec's
  `Effect`), `Verify` (an ok read again: gone is recovered, still there is a failed verify, and
  the kind leaves the list at once), `Ask` (a `remedy` prompt once per symptom state: off the
  list, past the cap, refused, unknown, failed or cut by a restart), `Restart`, and a person's
  `Answer` (leave it, or approve: the only widening). Properties: `OncePerState`,
  `RecordedFirst`, `UnknownNotRetried`, `CapToPrompt`, `NarrowsAlone`, `OnlyApproved`,
  `OnlyOnSymptom`, and liveness `ActedOrAsked` and `ClaimsEnd`. Ten `Break_Heal_*`
  configurations, each failing: `InMemory` (`RecordedFirst`), `TwoClaims` (`OncePerState`),
  `RetryUnknown` (`UnknownNotRetried`), `NoCap` and `CountInMemory` (`CapToPrompt`),
  `WidenOnRestart` and `NoNarrow` (`NarrowsAlone`), `Unlisted` (`OnlyApproved`),
  `StaleSymptom` (`OnlyOnSymptom`), `SilentCap` (`ActedOrAsked`).
- **`Boxes.tla` composes it** as `H`, the stuck turn its one kind: `on <- stuck`, `st <- ep`,
  the `Ledger`'s variables its own, a cap of one, no bound passing and no person's answer. The
  healer's steps are `H`'s (`Detect`, `HealClaim`, `HealAct` with an interrupt as the effect,
  `HealVerify`, `HealAsk`, `H!Restart` inside `RestartAll`); the parent's fix is `ParentAct`.
  `NeverWorking` is now `H!OnlyOnSymptom`, and `Boxes_Safety` and `Boxes_Limit` check
  `OnlyApproved`, `RecordedFirst` and `NarrowsAlone` too. The healer's in-memory `seen` became
  the durable record `facts`. Every `Break_Boxes_*` still fails as before.
- **`Watch`** (items 2 and 3; a new app, `caf_watch`, one process per instance). Two tables:
  `symptoms`, one row per symptom state under a unique `(kind, target, state)` (`record/3`
  answers the row recorded before, so a state is one row), with `since`, `evidence`, `source`
  and a status `open`, `asked`, `answered` or `passed`; and `remedy_acts`, the acts on the
  allow-list (the healer's `drop`, the operator's `revoke` and `approve`). The list is the
  config's (`remedies`: kind to `%{remedy, cap}`, and `remedy_bound`), then those acts in order,
  so a restart reads it as left (`Heal.tla`'s `Break_Heal_WidenOnRestart`). `ask/4` makes a
  state `asked` once, with the remedy proposed and why; `answer/5` takes `"approve"` or
  `"leave"` once; `pass/4` passes an `open` state no longer read, and an `asked` one only when
  its target shows the kind no more. A kind whose remedy is nil is shown and never asked; an
  approve by the name `healer` is refused. Every write cues `watch`.
- **`Recoveries` is the healer** (item 2). Its two acts are the default list's two entries
  (`lost_viewer` by `reopen`, `end_owed` by `end`, 10 per hour). For each state it reads (a lost
  viewer two samples agree on, an end owed) it records the symptom first; a state with a
  non-skipped row in `Acts` is decided by that row; a kind off the list or past its cap (a
  count of its own rows of that act in the bound, read from `Acts`) is asked; else it claims and
  acts as before. A refused open, a third loss in the window, an unknown end, an unknown
  verify and a claim a restart cut (closed at `init`) are asked; a failed verify
  (`not_viewed`) drops `lost_viewer` from the list with its reason and is asked. Each check
  passes the states it no longer reads. `watch:` is a required option.
- **The `remedy` kind in `Prompts`**: the `Watch`'s `asked` rows, read from its file
  (`Watch.Table.asked/1`), id `remedy:<symptom id>`, options `approve` and `leave`, answered
  through `Watch.answer/5`, cued on `watch`. It is not told to the agent's parent: it is the
  `Watch`'s wait, not the agent's.
- **The surface**: `Watch` in the tree before `Recoveries` and named in the instance's
  `Registry`; `CodingAgentFramework.symptoms/2` and `remedies/2` (`:list`, the operator's
  `approve` and `revoke`; a `drop` from the app is refused); the config's `remedies` and
  `remedy_bound`, checked whole; the topic `watch` in `writes/0` and the cue table.
- **An asked background command corrected** (item 5, F-180's new-4): `Intake` writes
  `{:ask, updated_input: …, additional_context: …}` as `permissionDecision: "ask"` beside
  `updatedInput`, and `Intake.Background` corrects an answer of `:ask` (or `{:ask, opts}`) as
  it corrects a pass or an allow: one clause each, with a test through `Intake`.
- **Docs** (item 6): `docs/design.md` ("Metrics and healing": `Heal.tla`'s properties, "One
  shape for every remedy", what is built, the first remedies' table, and the line that a
  usage-limit remedy is an instance of this healer; "Usage limits" item 7; the `remedy` prompt;
  a `partial` report in "The delegate protocol", decided for `stuck_background`'s unit; the
  parts and cues tables; "Intercepted, not added"; the app's surface; B7 and B8), the README.

## What I ran into

- The design's list of `Heal.tla`'s properties (`OnlyApproved`, `OncePerState`, `CapHolds`,
  `AskedWhenCapped`, `NeverWorking`, `ActedOrAsked`) and the brief's (`OncePerState`,
  `RecordedFirst`, `UnknownNotRetried`, `CapToPrompt`, `NarrowsAlone`) differ. The spec checks
  both: `CapHolds` and `AskedWhenCapped` are `CapToPrompt` with `ActedOrAsked`, and
  `NeverWorking` is `OnlyOnSymptom` in `Boxes.tla`. The design's text now names what is checked.
- A lost viewer's state key moves when the healer opens it (its `since` is the open), so a
  failed verify leaves the target in a new state. The first `pass` passed the asked state with
  it and the person's prompt went; an asked state now passes only when its target shows the
  kind no more. The same fact made `Heal.tla`'s `Verify` read the symptom, not its state, as
  `Recoveries`' rule does (new-4).
- A per-app `mix test` in `caf_prompts` and `caf_intake` stops on the umbrella's `:only`
  mismatch for `caf_stream`; the tests run from the root with their paths.

## Findings

### new-1. The healer acts directly, not through `Team.operate/4`

What happened: B8 says the healer acts through `Team.operate/4` as an actor with the operator's
checks and claims. `operate/4` is B5's and not built, so `Recoveries` still opens a viewer
through `Sessions` and ends a process through `Holder.Process`, under its own claim in `Acts`.
Why: B8 lands before B5. What I did: the shape (record, list, cap, claim, verify, ask) is the
healer's own, and each act is one function call, so moving it is local. What the design should
change: nothing now; B5 routes the healer's two acts through `operate/4` as the actor `healer`,
and every remedy after it takes the same path.

### new-2. Two states still wait for `Recoveries.clear/2` as well as their prompt

What happened: a viewer lost a third time in its window and an end whose outcome is unknown
were already states that wait for a person, made new by `Recoveries.clear/2`. They are now also
`remedy` prompts, but answering the prompt does not clear them: the next loss of that socket is
not acted on until `clear/2`. Why: the `Watch` writes no `Acts` row and calls no healer, and
the clear is `Acts`' `cleared_at` that `Recoveries` keys its next state by. What I did: both
remain, and the design says so. What the design should change: one clear, `Watch.clear/3` (the
interface's own): the answer of a `remedy` prompt clears its state, and the healer reads the
answer's time as it reads `cleared_at` today; then `Recoveries.clear/2` goes.

### new-3. "Act once" is not an answer of the `remedy` prompt

What happened: the design lists three answers, "act once", "add to the allow-list" and "leave
it". The healer acts once per state, and the asked state is one it did not, or may already have,
acted on; acting on it again under the healer's claim breaks `OncePerState`, and under
`UnknownNotRetried` an unknown one must never be tried again by the healer. What I did: the
prompt offers `approve` (the list widened) and `leave`; the design's text says "act once" is
the operator's own act through `Team.operate/4`. What the design should change: confirm that
reading; when B5 builds `operate/4`, the app offers the operator's act beside the prompt, under
the operator's own claim of a new state.

### new-4. A verify reads the symptom, not its state

What happened: `Recoveries` decides a reopen by the first sample after it: viewed is recovered,
not viewed is failed, even when the viewer opened and was lost again before the sample (a new
state, in the model's terms). `Heal.tla` first read a failed verify as "still there in the same
state", under which that case is recovered and the list stays wide. Why: the code cannot tell a
viewer that never attached from one that attached and died. What I did: `Verify` reads the
symptom alone (`still == on[s]`), which only narrows the list more often; every property and
break holds as before. What the design should change: nothing, unless a failed verify that
drops a remedy too often shows in a run; then the verify should wait for two samples, as the
detection does.

### new-5. The cap and the bound are placeholders

What happened: the default list caps each act at 10 per hour, where the old rule was three
losses of one socket in 60 s (kept). Why: nothing measured how often a workstation loses
viewers or owes ends. What I did: the values are the config's, per kind. What the design should
change: B7's folds count `lost_viewer` and `end_owed` per hour in a real run, and the defaults
follow.
