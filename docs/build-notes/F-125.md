# F-125: R7 part 1, `Mailbox` delivers a delegate's brief, its word to go on and its report

The first part of R7 (`docs/design.md`, "Delivery", "The delegate protocol", R7), against
`model/tla/Delivery.tla`, with F-122's new-1 to new-4 and F-123's new-1 and new-3 decided by
the brief and folded into the design. The unit ran well past its size, so it stops at a clean
point: the transports `stream` and `call` are built, and `waiter` is the second part (new-1).

## What was built

- **One row per delivery.** `Mailbox.Table` gains `kind` (`msg`, `brief`, `resume`, `reply`,
  `notice`), `via` (the transport), `at` (the process the row was marked for) and `again`
  (made due again, so it goes first), each added to a file an earlier build made, and a unique
  index on `uuid` beside the one on `reply_to`: one row per uuid, one answer per row.
  `store/2` is the one insert. Open rows come a row made due again first, then oldest first.
- **The mark and the settle, once.** Per addressee, while no row to it is `sending`, the first
  open row is marked `sending` with its transport and process, then handed on; a refusal undoes
  the mark, an unknown keeps it. The settle of a `sending` row is its transport's, one module
  each in `Mailbox.Transport`: `Transport.Stream` (the fold's replay, or at the exit the
  transcript the fold records; undelivered or never marked is due again, first, same uuid) and
  `Transport.Call` (a row whose call is not in memory any more is due again: a restart or the
  caller's end cut it). `waiter` and `typed` will be one module each beside them.
- **The addressee.** `Mailbox` no longer reads `Delegates`' table. It reads `Sessions`' row of
  the addressee (its role, session id and status) and `Stream`'s fold of that session: a
  headless delegate takes a row only into an ended turn of its current process, with its row
  `running` (not `stopping`). Anything else keeps its rows `queued`, `:no_delivery`, until the
  waiter's part; a waiting call can still take a reply or a notice to it.
- **The brief and the word to go on are rows.** `Delegates` writes its row first, as before,
  then `Mailbox.brief/5` under the brief's uuid, then calls `Sessions`; `Mailbox` delivers it.
  A resume whose brief had gone in stores `Mailbox.go_on/4` (from `framework`, `again`, so it
  goes before every other row due to the delegate) before it counts the resume; one whose brief
  had not gone in leaves the brief's own row, which `Mailbox` made due again, first. A refused
  start has its rows settled `ended` (`Mailbox.ended/3`). `Delegates` no longer writes to
  `Stream` for a turn's message; `Turn.next/2` answers `{:finished, result}`, `:exited` or
  `:waiting`.
- **The report is the reply to the brief.** `Delegates` hands it with `Mailbox.report/5` under
  the brief's uuid: a reply from the delegate for a finish (from the result or the transcript's
  last reply), or a notice from `framework` for a second kill or a refused resume. Its row's
  `report` is `held`, with its text, until `Mailbox` takes it, then `taken`; a cue hands a held
  one again. `held/3`, `await_report/4`, `answered/2`, `release/2`, `deliver/2`, the waiters and
  the watched callers go from `Delegates`. A finished delegate is `ended` once `Mailbox` has
  its report delivered and `Sessions`' row of it ended; `stop` needs the report delivered and no
  row to the delegate open (`{:refused, {:report, status}}`, `{:refused, {:open, ids}}`).
- **The call transport.** `Mailbox.await/4` takes the answer to a key for a waiting call, only
  before its deadline, only when it is first among its addressee's rows from its sender and no
  row to the addressee is `sending`, and marks it `sending` by `call` in the same step. It
  watches the caller: its end makes the row due again. `told/3` settles it: `:written`
  delivered, anything else due again. A wait of 0 ms takes only a row it can take now and never
  waits. `MCP`'s `run_delegate` awaits the brief's uuid and tells `Mailbox` how the endpoint's
  write went; its answer's `status` is `finished` for a reply and `failed` for a notice.
- **Replies.** A delegate's reply to a message goes to the message's sender; to its parent (the
  sender of its brief) for a message from `framework`, as F-102's new-4 says. Only `msg` rows
  are answered by `Mailbox`; a brief's and a word's answers are `Delegates`' report.
- **Smaller pieces.** `ClaudeCLI.wrapper/4` no longer writes `<stream dir>/pid`, and
  `Stream`'s test holds through `Holder.Process` and ends a process by its record (F-118's
  new-4). `Transcripts.read/3`'s failures are `{account, session_id, reason}` (F-120's new-4).
- **Wiring.** `caf_delegates` uses `caf_mailbox`; `caf_mailbox` uses `caf_sessions` and no
  longer `caf_delegates`. The example app starts `Mailbox` before `Delegates`, hands it to
  `Delegates`, and routes `sessions` cues to `Mailbox` and `mailbox` cues to `Delegates`.
  README's "The apps" chart and status follow.
- **The design.** "Concepts" and "Delivery" list the brief and the word to go on; "The delegate
  protocol" step 4 and "What `Delegates` holds" say they are `Mailbox` rows and the word goes
  first (F-122's new-3, new-4); "Moves" step 3 and "Ending a session" say the idle read waits
  for no row `sending` and `Mailbox` neither marks nor hands on a row to a `stopping` session
  (new-1); "Delivery" names the take bound for `waiter` and `typed` rows (new-2); "Delivery into
  a main session" says `{:ok, :sent}` keeps the row `sending` (F-123's new-1) and that
  `Intake` cues a waiter's coming and going (F-123's new-3); the wrapper's text; R7's two parts.

## What I ran into

- `Mailbox` and `Delegates` cannot use each other: `Delegates` hands `Mailbox` the brief and the
  report, so `Mailbox` gives up `Delegates`' table and reads the addressee from `Sessions`' row
  and `Stream`'s fold. What it needed from `Delegates` was a parent (now the sender of the
  answered row, or of the brief), the session (now `Sessions`'), and whether the delegate ended
  (the fold's end, or `Mailbox.ended/3` for a refused start). `Mailbox`'s tests start a
  delegate's session through `Sessions` directly; the tests of `Delegates` with `Mailbox` live
  in `caf_delegates`, the higher app.
- The undelivered line needed a real one. The stand-in reads every line at once, so the test
  stops it (`SIGSTOP`), lets the claim write the line into its FIFO, then kills it: the
  transcript lacks the uuid, the fold says undelivered, and the row is due again.
- A test's 0 ms `await` raced a waiting caller for the key, as `Delegates.await_report/4`'s did
  not: that one answered a 0 ms wait before it registered. `Mailbox.await/4` does the same now.
  The first gate run then found a real bug under load: the 0 ms wait's timer, canceled too late,
  arrived after the caller's wait on the same key had registered and ended that one with a
  timeout. Each wait's timer message now names the wait (a ref), and a stale one is dropped.
- The same gate run found two waits in my tests that took the stand-in's taking of the brief
  for the end of its turn; they now wait for the session to be idle too.
- A report handed again after `Mailbox` did not take it is handed with its text only: the
  result's `subtype` and `is_error` are not on `Delegates`' row. It is rare (a `Mailbox` that
  did not answer), and the text is the report.
- A word to go on is stored before the resume is counted. If `Mailbox` answers unknown but
  stored it, the next cue stores another under a new uuid, and the resumed process gets two.

## Findings

### new-1. R7 is two units, and its first part alone is about +1,300 −900

What happened: the brief, the word to go on and the report as rows, the stream and call
transports, `Delegates` cut to `taken`, and the tests that moved between apps came to +1,300
−888 (library +720 −481), where R7 was estimated at +350 −350 in all.
Why: the report's old path (`held`, `answering`, the watched caller) and its tests leave
`Delegates`, and their counterparts arrive in `Mailbox`; `Mailbox`'s tests, which started a real
`Delegates`, now start sessions through `Sessions`; and `MCP`, the example app and three apps'
dependencies follow. Each test that waits on a delegate now cues `Mailbox` too.
What I did: stopped at a clean point, as the brief allows. Part 2 is the `waiter` transport:
the take bound and the transcript's settle, `Intake`'s waiter cue, the waiter's hook in a main
session's start (which needs F-123's new-2 decided: an overlay's `hooks` replace the account's),
and `Sessions`' idle read and delegate stop reading `Mailbox` (F-118's new-3, F-122's new-1 in
code). The design's R7 entry says so.
What the design should change: R7's size, and a unit for its second part before R8.

### new-2. `Sessions` reading `Mailbox` turns the layers around

What happened: the brief has `Sessions`' stop of a delegate read `Mailbox` and drop `stop/4`'s
word, and F-122's new-1 has `Sessions`' idle read wait until no row to the session is
`sending`. `Mailbox` now reads `Sessions`' row of its addressee, so `caf_sessions` cannot use
`caf_mailbox` (a cycle), and the layers put `Sessions` below the parts built later.
Why: the check belongs to the stop (it must be read in the same step as the idle read), but the
rows are a higher part's.
What I did: kept `stop/4`'s word for this part. `Delegates` asks `Mailbox` (the report
delivered, nothing open to the delegate) before it calls `Sessions.stop/4` with
`:report_delivered`.
What the design should change: say how `Sessions` reads it. I recommend a function the
embedding app hands `Sessions` at its start, as it hands `Delegates` `mcp:` (say `open_rows:
fn name -> [ids] end`, `Mailbox.open_to/3`), read in the idle read's step; the report's
delivery is then `Delegates`' check before it asks, as now, and `stop/4` loses its word.

### new-3. `Stream`'s fold still keeps a record per uuid, which also guards its writes

What happened: the brief says `Stream` keeps its line claim and no message state of its own.
`Mailbox`'s row is now the only mark: no other part writes a message to a delegate, and nothing
but `Mailbox` decides whether a row is due, sending or delivered. But `Stream.Fold` still keeps,
per uuid, its claim's record and its evidence (`writing` until a replay, `delivered`,
`undelivered` at the exit by the transcript, the result, `exited`), and its `check/2` still
refuses a message while another is `writing` or found `undelivered`, and a uuid used before.
Why: those are the evidence `Mailbox` settles by (`Stream.tla`'s `Handle`, `ExitSeen`) and the
claim's own guard against writing a uuid twice; `Stream.tla` names its `OneWaiting` and the end's
guards as defense in depth.
What I did: kept them, renamed nothing, and documented them as the claim and its evidence.
What the design should change: confirm that reading, or say that `Fold.check/2`'s message
rules (`message_waiting`, `{:undelivered, uuid}`) go, leaving only the duplicate uuid and the
turn check, which `Mailbox`'s order already makes true.

### new-4. The report's own mark is gone; the waiter's text needs the row's

What happened: `Delegates` gave each report a mark (`[report <uuid>]`) so the parent's
transcript would show it. The report is now a `Mailbox` row, and the row's uuid is its mark, but
the waiter's text is what reaches the transcript, so a mark only helps if the waiter's
transport writes it into the text.
What I did: dropped `mark` from new rows (the column stays for rows an earlier build wrote).
What the design should change: part 2 should say the waiter's and the typed write's text
carries the row's uuid, in a form the transcript keeps, so the settle finds the row's line and
not a look-alike.

### new-5. A delegate whose report an earlier build answered never ends

What happened: a `finished` row an earlier build left with `report` `answering`, `answered` or
`delivered` has no answer in `Mailbox`, so `Delegates` never finds the report delivered and the
row stays `finished`, holding its parent's cap.
Why: the report's state moved tables, and there is no migration.
What I did: nothing; no app has rows of that build that are still running.
What the design should change: nothing, unless an app has; then `Delegates` reads `answered` and
`delivered` as delivered for a row with no answer in `Mailbox`.
