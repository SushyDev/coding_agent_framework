# F-216: the store's busy wait is bounded by the clock

F-215's new-2: SQLite's busy wait ran about four times its bound. `Store` opened every
connection with a busy timeout of 5 s, and a write that met another connection's write lock
waited about 19 s before it failed. Every wait in this framework is bounded by the bound that is
written down. This unit makes a busy store answer within its stated bound by the monotonic
clock, and makes the answer explicit.

## What was measured first

The test aid is `against_held/2` in `apps/caf_store/test/store_test.exs`: it takes the file's
write lock from a second connection with `Store.hold/1`, runs a write, and answers what the
write answered or raised and how long it took by the monotonic clock. Run against the code
before this unit (busy timeout 5 s, macOS, a host with a load average about 10 on 15 cores):

| write | waited | then |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Store.insert/4` | about 18.5 s | raised `MatchError` on `{:error, "Database busy"}` |
| `Store.transaction/2` | 17.7 s | raised `MatchError` on `{:error, "database is locked"}` |
| `Store.write/3` | 18.2 s | raised `MatchError`, not `{:refused, …}` |

The same against exqlite directly, `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` on a held file, two runs each:

| busy timeout | waited |
| --- | --- |
| 1 ms | 7, 9 ms |
| 3 ms | 25, 27 ms |
| 50 ms | 321, 401 ms |
| 300 ms | 1.57, 1.61 s |
| 1,000 ms | 4.0, 4.1 s |
| 5,000 ms | 18.7, 19.2 s |

Linux: to be filled in on that pass.

**Why it overshoots.** exqlite's busy handler (`deps/exqlite/c_src/sqlite3_nif.c`,
`exqlite_busy_handler`) counts the delays it asked for, by the retry count, and never reads a
clock: it stops when its table of delays (1, 2, 5, … 50 ms, then 50 ms each) sums to the
timeout. On this host a sleep wakes late, and every late wake adds to the wait. A C program with
`usleep` on this host: a 1 ms sleep woke 5.4 ms late on average, a 5 ms one 32 ms, a 50 ms one
127 ms. A 5 s timeout is 106 sleeps, and 106 late wakes of about 130 ms are the 14 s over.

The BEAM's own timers wake late too, though less: `Process.sleep(25)` woke up to 70 ms late at
the 99th percentile, `Process.sleep(5)` up to 33 ms, `Process.sleep(1)` up to 22 ms.

## The bound: what I chose, and what I ruled out

**Chosen: SQLite's wait off (0), and `Store` waits itself against a deadline.** Every connection
`Store` opens has a busy timeout of 0, so SQLite answers busy at once. A statement that is
refused busy is tried again, sleeping 1 ms, then twice as long each time up to 5 ms, until 2 s
(`Store.busy_bound/0`) have passed on the monotonic clock since its first try; the last sleep
ends at the deadline. Then the statement is busy:

- `open/3`, `write/3`, `read_only/4` and `snapshot/2` answer `{:refused, {:sqlite, :busy}}`.
  `open/3` and `write/3` run several statements (the journal mode, the schema, each added
  column, the transaction) under one deadline, so the whole call waits at most the bound.
- `query/3`, `transaction/2` and the functions on them (`insert`, `update`, `upsert`, `select`,
  `rows_by_id`, `convert`) raise `Store.Busy`, with `waited` in ms, as any statement's error
  raises in this module. A writer that meets it is restarted by its supervisor, as before. A
  busy `transaction/2` has begun nothing.

The stated slack is 100 ms. The sleeps are short (at most 5 ms) because a longer sleep wakes
later: with sleeps of up to 25 ms, 30 busy answers from ten files at once came up to 69 ms after
the bound, and 15 from one file at a time up to 104 ms after it, on this host; with 5 ms, 30
busy answers from ten files at once came 0 to 24 ms after it. Two
thousand ms in sleeps of 5 ms is about 400 tries, each a few microseconds.

Every statement in `Store` goes through the wait, not only writes: a reader of a WAL file can be
refused busy while another connection changes the journal mode or recovers the WAL index, and
before this unit it waited for that under SQLite's handler.

**Ruled out:**

- **A busy timeout SQLite honors exactly.** exqlite's handler is the only one its API sets a
  timeout on, and it counts nominal delays, so no timeout it takes is a bound by the clock. A
  smaller timeout tuned to the overshoot would hold only on the host it was tuned on: the
  overshoot was 7× at 1 ms and 4× at 5 s here, and depends on the host's timer slack.
- **`PRAGMA busy_timeout`.** It installs SQLite's own handler in place of exqlite's (exqlite's
  doc says so), which drops exqlite's cancel and its check that the caller still lives, and
  SQLite's handler sleeps through the same `usleep` by the same kind of table.
- **A change to exqlite** (a handler that reads the clock): the brief rules out patching a
  dependency, and the wait in `Store` needs none.
- **Keeping 5 s as the bound.** Most parts' call timeouts are 5 s (below), so a bound of 5 s and
  its slack sit above them. 2 s and its slack fit twice under 5 s, so a call that runs two
  writes that each wait still answers before its caller's timeout.

## The model

No spec in `model/tla/` models a busy wait on the store. The specs that write rows treat a
store write as one atomic, instant step (for one, `Delivery.tla`'s `StoreRow`). I left them so.

## What was built

- **`Store`** (`caf_store`): the busy timeout of every connection is 0; `execute/3`,
  `until_free/3` and `busy!/2` (private) are the one wait, against a deadline from
  `System.monotonic_time/1`; `busy_bound/0` names the bound; `Store.Busy` is the exception. The
  moduledoc says how the wait runs, its bound and slack, and what each function answers.
  `hold/1` keeps its own busy timeout of 0 and its one try.
- **The design's `Store` row** and **the README status line** state the bound.

## Tests

Four new tests in `store_test.exs`, and `busy_bound/0`'s doctest. Each busy case asserts, by
the monotonic clock, that the answer came after the bound and no later than the bound and 100
ms:

- an insert against a held lock raises `Store.Busy` (its `waited` within the time measured), and
  after the let-go the same insert runs;
- a transaction against a held lock raises `Store.Busy`, its function never ran, and after the
  let-go a transaction writes;
- `write/3` against a held lock is refused busy, and `open/3` with two columns to add (two
  statements that each meet the lock) is refused busy within one bound; after the let-go both
  succeed;
- a write while another process holds the lock, which it lets go 200 ms later, runs after the
  let-go and before the bound.

## What I ran into

- A connection that nothing refers to is collected and closes, and its lock goes with it
  (F-215 ran into it too): my first measurement script held nothing until it used the holder's
  connection again after the timed writes.
- `ExUnit`'s `async: true` runs modules, not tests, side by side, so the four busy tests run one
  after another, about 8 s of `caf_store`'s tests.

## Findings

### new-1. A sleep on this host wakes late, in proportion to its length

What happened: a 50 ms `usleep` woke about 127 ms late on average, and the BEAM's 25 ms
`Process.sleep` up to 70 ms late, on a macOS host with a load average about 10. Why: most likely
macOS's timer coalescing for a process that is not in the foreground; I did not narrow it. What
I did: `Store`'s wait sleeps at most 5 ms between tries, and its slack (100 ms) is measured
against the clock. What the design should change: any wait elsewhere that counts sleeps rather
than reading the clock overshoots the same way; a bound in this framework should be checked
against `System.monotonic_time`, and a test that asserts a bound should state its slack.
