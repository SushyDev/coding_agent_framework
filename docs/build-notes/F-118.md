# F-118: R3, `Holder.Process`, and the end of a session by SIGTERM

R3 of the remodel ("Order of the first units"): the model first, then one module for a held
program's record, and every part that holds a program moved onto it.

## What was built

**`Start.tla`.** `StopAsked` is SIGTERM to the row's process by its pid and start time, sent
only once the session file of that process reads idle. A process's turn is `busy` (`TurnStarts`
and `TurnEnds`, while its row is running). One operator, `Signal`, is the only way the spec
reaches a process: the stop's TERM, and `Kill`, which is now `Recoveries`' end of an orphan or
of a late stop, by the row's record, TERM then KILL in one step. Every hold writes its record
(`pidFile`) before the program runs, so an orphan of a live socket has one too. An orphan now
settles: `stopped` at a cue once its process is gone and its socket is dead, so its name is
free again. New checks: `TermsOnlyIdle` (invariant), `OrphanEnds` and `LateStopEnds`
(liveness). New Breaks, each found by TLC: `StopWhileBusy` (`TermsOnlyIdle`), `TermByPid`
(`KillsOwnOnly`), `OrphanKept` (`OrphanEnds`). Every earlier Break still finds its bug.

**`Holder.Process`** (`caf_holder`). A held program's record: its pid and start time, written
by a wrapper before it execs the program (`<dir>/<name>.pid`), and its socket.

- `hold/4` clears an earlier hold's record only while that socket is not live, then holds
  under the wrapper.
- `read/3` reads several names in one shell: the socket's state, the record, and whether that
  pid runs with that start time.
- `dead?/1`: the process not live and the socket absent or stale.
- `signal/4` and `push/5` check the pid and start time, and act on that process, in one shell.
- `end_held/5`: TERM, a wait, then KILL.
- `await_dead/3` waits on the host, in one shell.
- `liveness/3` is the batch `ps` that was `Registry.Liveness`, now used by `Registry` and
  `Sessions`.

Start times are compared with their runs of spaces made one.

**`Sessions`.**

- It holds through `Holder.Process`.
- An interactive stop reads the account's `Registry` and sends TERM by the row's pid and start
  time only when that process's session file says `idle`. Otherwise the stop answers
  `{:refused, {:not_idle, status}}` and sends nothing. The `turn` word is no longer read for an
  interactive session, so no `/exit` is typed and `Control` is no longer called for a stop.
- A headless delegate is confirmed and found unheld by its record, so a delegate's orphan now
  keeps a pid and start time. That closes F-64's new-3.
- An orphan of a live socket takes the held program's record.
- Orphans settle to `stopped{ended}`.
- `ends_owed/2` names the orphans and the late stops that have a record.
- The headless pid-file parser is gone.

**`Recoveries`** ends each owed process once per state, under a row of kind `end`, keyed by
name, pid and start time. The row is claimed first, then the process is ended with
`Holder.Process.end_held/5`, and the row ends `recovered` or `unknown`. An `end` row a restart
left `claimed` ends `unknown{interrupted}` at start. `Recoveries` now takes `programs:` and
`end_grace:`. Lost viewers are counted and verified on their own kind only.

**`Jobs`, `Copies.Held`, `Services` and the forward** hold through `Holder.Process`.

- `Jobs` reads dead by the record and waits for its end on the host (`await_dead/3`), then reads
  the exit file. Its own dtach probe is gone.
- `Services`' four `ps -o lstart=` scripts and its pid-file parsers are gone. The start's log
  offset is a file of its own (`<name>.from`). An end is `end_held/5` to the group, and input
  goes through `push/5`.
- The forward holds its ssh under the same record, reads it, and checks with `lsof` that its
  pid listens.

**The stand-in** writes `status` in its session file (`busy` and `idle` lines switch it), exits
143 on SIGTERM, and ignores SIGTERM after the line `deaf`.

## What was measured

TLC, the four fast `Start` configurations, before and after. The workstation's load average was
about 80 on 15 cores throughout, so the seconds are not an idle machine's:

| Configuration | distinct states before → after | seconds before → after (loaded) |
|---|---|---|
| `Start_Restarts` | 35,104 → 40,593 | 13 → 23 |
| `Start_SharedTree` | 64,083 → 76,199 | 29 → 63 |
| `Start_TwoSessions` | 198,724 → 244,379 | 119 → 337 |
| `Start_Worktrees` | 34,450 → 41,152 | 10 → 25 |

Every one of the 24 `Break_Start_*` configurations exits 12 or 13 with its violation. The slow
`Start_Large` was not run: R4 adds its parse to the gate, and by hand it is not the fast set.

The change is about 1,380 lines added and 360 removed across 30 files. Of that, about 430 is
`Holder.Process` with its docs and the 80 lines moved from `Registry.Liveness`, about 210 is new
tests, and 210 is `Start.tla` and its configurations. This is over the 500 lines the brief and
F-115 estimated for R3 (+200, −300): the record took one module where the code had six copies,
and its tests are new.

## What I ran into

- Each of `Services`' scripts and `Registry.Liveness` collapsed a start time's spaces in a
  different way: `echo $t`, `String.trim`, or none. `ps` pads a one-digit day. `Holder.Process`
  makes runs of spaces one on both sides of every comparison.
- `Services`' wrapper wrote `pid offset` in one line. With the record written first by
  `Holder.Process`'s wrapper, the offset comes a moment later from the service's own wrapper,
  so its read waits for it, for at most 2 s.
- "Dead" for a service, a forward and a job was the socket alone. It is now `Holder.Process`'s:
  the process gone as well. Nothing in the tests depended on the difference.

## Findings

### new-1. `Start_TwoSessions` is over the 30 s bound under load, and could not be timed idle

What happened: it took 119 s before this change and 337 s after, with a load average of about
80. The rule says the next unit that changes a spec times the fast set on an idle machine.
Why: other agents were running on the workstation the whole time.
What I did: I recorded the loaded times above and kept the configuration in the fast set. In
the full `devenv test` it took 147 s, and `gate-model` as a whole took 1,058 s.
What the design should change: time the fast set on an idle machine before R4. If
`Start_TwoSessions` is still over 30 s, move it to `slow/` and keep a smaller one, for example
with `MaxRestarts = 0`, in the fast set.

### new-2. The idle read and the SIGTERM are two steps in the code, and a delivery can come between them

What happened: `Start.tla` reads the session file and sends the signal as one step. In the
code, `Sessions` writes `stopping`, reads the `Registry`, then signals. A waiter's answer or a
typed write that starts a turn in that gap would be cut by the TERM.
Why: the stop and a delivery are different parts, and nothing orders them yet.
What I did: the spec's header names the gap and leaves it to `Delivery.tla`.
What the design should change: `Delivery.tla` (R6) should model that `Mailbox` never marks a
row for a session whose agent row is `stopping`, and that `Sessions` writes `stopping` before
its idle read (it does). That makes the read and the signal one step for any delivery.

### new-3. `Sessions.stop/4`'s word is read only for a delegate

What happened: an interactive stop is decided by the session file, so its `word` argument is
not read. A delegate's is still the caller's `:report_delivered`.
What I did: I documented it. The callers pass `:idle`.
What the design should change: R9 or R7, once `Delegates` keeps only `taken` and `Mailbox` the
report, could let `Sessions` read the report state itself and drop the argument.

### new-4. `ClaudeCLI.wrapper/4` still writes `<stream dir>/pid`

What happened: `Sessions` no longer reads it, since the record holds the same pid with its
start time. `Stream`'s tests read it.
What I did: I left it, since it is `ClaudeCLI`'s and `Stream`'s.
What the design should change: remove the line and move `Stream`'s test onto the record, in a
unit that touches `Stream` (R7).

### new-5. An end that fails is never tried again within its state

What happened: `Recoveries` acts once per state. An end whose process outlives the KILL ends
`unknown`, and the process runs on until its state changes, for example when its row's record
changes.
What I did: nothing more. `Acts` (R10) takes these rows, and the fault ledger shows them.
What the design should change: say in "The parts" that a failed end waits for a person, as a
repeating lost viewer does, or name the next state that takes it again.

### new-6. The record is one `echo`, not a rename

What happened: the wrapper writes `<pid> <start>` with one `echo`. A reader that ran in the
middle of the write could see half of it.
Why: a rename needs `mv` on every host's list of programs.
What I did: I relied on one short write being seen whole, and a reader treats a record it
cannot parse as unknown, not as dead.
What the design should change: nothing unless a half record is ever seen. Then add `mv` to the
wrapper.
