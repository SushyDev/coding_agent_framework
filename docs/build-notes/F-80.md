# F-80: `Team.tla` models a delegate's reply and the notice of a turn with no result

A model unit: `model/tla/Team.tla`, its configurations and its part of `model/tla/READING.md`.
No code. It models the paragraph of "Messages" on a message into a delegate and its reply
(F-77, F-79), as F-79's new-1 asked.

## What was built

- **The fold.** `fold[i]` is `Stream`'s fold of a delegate's log for message `i`: `"none"`,
  `"turn"` once typed, `"result"` once a result names it, `"exited"` once the process ended
  during its turn with no result. It is read again from the log after a restart, so
  `FrameworkDies` keeps it. Only messages to a delegate have entries.
- **Turns.** `Result(a)` ends a turn with a result that names the message in turn, or none
  (`OwnTurn`: a brief, a resume, a turn the delegate started by itself). `TurnEnds` and an
  interrupt that helps (its result is `error_during_execution`) both use it. A `Faults` that
  kills the process mid-turn marks the message `"exited"`; one that sticks it leaves the turn
  open. `SelfStarts(d)` is a turn the delegate starts by itself, under a new constant
  `SelfTurns`.
- **Answers.** `answers` is the table of replies and notices, `[re, kind, to, src]`, keyed by
  the answered message (`reply_to`). `ReplyStored(i)` stores the reply from the delegate to its
  main session once the row is `"delivered"` and the fold keeps a result on `i`;
  `NoticeStored(i)` stores the framework's notice once the fold says `"exited"`. Both refuse
  when the key already has a row. Both are weakly fair.
- **Properties.** `ReplyOnce` (a reply stored at most once, across restarts), `ReplyOrNotice`
  (never both), `ReplyToMain`, `ReplyNamesMessage` (a reply's source is the result that names
  its message), and the liveness `ReplyOrNoticeComes` (a message to a delegate whose turn ends,
  with a result or the process's end, is answered).
- **Breaks**, each failing on its property:
  - `ReplyInMemory`: the once-check is a set in memory (`rmem`). A restart forgets it and
    the message gets a second reply. Fails `ReplyOnce`.
  - `ReplyAndNotice`: the notice is read from the process's end after the message, not the
    fold, and checks only for notices. A turn that answered and then died gets both. Fails
    `ReplyOrNotice`.
  - `SelfReply`: the reply is the last result since the message was typed, whatever it names
    (`last`). A self-started turn's result becomes the reply. Fails `ReplyNamesMessage`.
  - `NoNotice`: no notice. A message whose turn ended with the process never gets an answer.
    Fails `ReplyOrNoticeComes`.
- **Configurations.** `Team_Replies` (one message, a fault, a restart, self-started turns, with
  liveness) and `Team_RepliesTwo` (two messages, safety only), both on one project with a
  delegate and a second main session alone (`{{p, d1}, {q}}`). The six existing `Team_*`
  configurations check the new invariants and, where they check liveness,
  `ReplyOrNoticeComes`, with `SelfTurns = FALSE` (see new-2).
- The spec's header and `READING.md` name the fold and the reply table, and say why no other
  result is ever a delegate's report.

The breaks took 3 to 4 s each. Their traces:

- `ReplyInMemory`: delivered, turn ends, `ReplyStored`, restart, `ReplyStored` again.
- `ReplyAndNotice`: delivered, turn ends with the result, `Faults` kills the delegate,
  `ReplyStored`, `NoticeStored`.
- `SelfReply`: delivered, turn ends with the result, `SelfStarts`, its turn ends,
  `ReplyStored` takes the self-started result.
- `NoNotice`: delivered, `Faults` kills the delegate mid-turn; nothing is ever stored.

Seconds vary with the machine's load: in the same full run `Start_TwoSessions`, which this unit
does not touch, took 67 s.

## What I ran into

- With `SelfStarts` on in every configuration, all six existing designs passed but took 23 to
  73 s (`Team_Claims` 3,375,241 states, 73 s). Self-started turns let every idle delegate
  cycle. Hence `SelfTurns` (new-2).
- A set of records hides a second identical reply, so "stored twice" is the ghost
  `"reply twice"` in `wrong`, set when a reply row already exists for the message.
- No `Break` for `ReplyToMain`: the brief named none, and the answer's `to` is `Main(d)` by
  construction, as `Mailbox` takes it from the `Delegates` row's `parent`.
- The report half of "a result that names no message is never taken as a reply or a report"
  is argued, not checked: a delegate is idle only after a result, and its first is its
  brief's (or, after a death, the resume's, which Delegates also counts), so no other turn's
  result can come before its report. `Delegate.tla` models the report.

## Findings

### new-1. A notice must carry the message's `reply_to`

What happened: the model refuses a reply after a notice, and a notice after a reply, by one key
on the answered message. `ReplyAndNotice` shows what a notice keyed apart allows. Why: "in
place of the reply" implies one row per message, but F-79's `reply_to` index is only on
replies. What I did: modeled the notice under the same key. What the design should change:
say the notice is stored with `reply_to` set to the message's uuid, under the same unique
index, so the table, not the order of reads, keeps "never both".

### new-2. `SelfTurns` is off in the configurations that do not check replies

What happened: self-started turns roughly tripled the existing `Team` designs, past the fast
limit. Why: any idle delegate may start a turn at any time. What I did: added the constant,
off in the six existing designs and on in `Team_Replies`, `Team_RepliesTwo` and
`Break_Team_SelfReply`. What the design should change: nothing, unless a later property needs
self-started turns together with claims or services; then a slow configuration.

### new-3. The fold needs an outcome for "delivered, then the process ended with no result"

What happened: the notice reads the fold's `"exited"`: the message was typed, its turn
started, and the process's exit came with no result naming it. F-79's `Stream.Fold.outcome/2`
answers `:undelivered` for a message found undelivered at the exit, but has no answer for one
delivered whose turn the exit cut. Why: the notice was not built. What I did: modeled it as
its own state. What the design should change: when the notice is built, `outcome/2` answers a
distinct value (for example `:exited`) for that case, and `Mailbox` stores the notice from it
only, never from the process's end alone.
