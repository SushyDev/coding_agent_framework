# F-206: every onboarding menu of a start reaches a person; a person's leave is their clear

Two findings of F-203, decided in the brief: new-2 (a second menu in one start reaches no
person) and new-3 (a waited resume a person left is asked again). Both matter on a new host,
where every account is new and Claude Code's onboarding may show several menus in one start.

## What was built

- **Each menu of a start is its own read** (item 1). `Dialog.tla` kept each answer by its
  kind, so a second dialog of one kind could open no prompt. The model came first: a start's
  dialogs are now reads, `Dialogs[i]` read `i`, each its own prompt (Prompt.tla's row), its own
  press and its own clear; `Dialog_Menus` (the trust dialog, then two menus) checks every
  property, and `Break_Dialog_ByKind` (answers by kind, F-203's code) breaks
  `KeptAcrossRestart`: the second menu has no prompt waiting. The spec says what it leaves
  abstract: the dialogs shown one after another differ (a start's menus by title), so each draw
  is one read, as in `Menu.tla`.
  In the code, `Sessions.Dialog.seen/3` gives a start's menu its read's `at` (wall-clock ms):
  kept while the pane shows the same menu (title and options) and its answer is not sent; any
  other menu, or one read after its answer was sent, is a new read, its `at` later than the
  last even on a clock that has not moved. A start's menu's answer holds its `at`, and
  `answers/1` drops one whose `at` is not the row's read, so every caller that reads the
  answers by kind (the check, the callback's decide, the clear, `Prompts`) sees the shown
  menu's answer alone. The act's state names the read (`read: at`), so `Acts` keeps one claim
  per menu. The prompt id is `dialog:<agent>:<at>:start_menu`, the shape of a running menu's;
  `Prompts` hands its `at` to `Sessions.answer_dialog/5`'s new `at:` option, and `Sessions`
  refuses `{:read, at}` an answer for a menu that has gone (`Prompts` reads it `settled`), so
  one meant for the first menu never presses the second. The trust and imports dialogs keep
  their keys: each shows once a start.
- **A person's leave is their clear** (item 2). `Heal.tla` has `SettledLeft`: an outcome that
  came later read as a person's leave settles refused, and the state is asked and closed in one
  step. `Remedies.tla`'s `SettleWait` takes it for a wait whose end is `left`; its new invariant
  `LeftNotAsked` (no prompt open for a left wait's state) holds in `Remedies_Wait` and
  `slow/Remedies_Large`, and `Break_Remedies_AskLeft` (the leave not copied) breaks it.
  `ActedOrAsked` still holds: the state was asked. In the code, `AbsentMains`' settle of a wait
  whose row is `refused` with reason `left` asks the absence, then clears it with
  `Watch.clear/3` as the row's `waited` (the person who answered `leave`). A pick refused at the
  reset is no one's answer and is asked as before.
- **Docs** (item 3). The design's "Startup dialogs" says a start's menus are keyed by their
  read; "Prompts" gives the new id and `at:`; "One clear" and the usage-limit section say a
  person's leave is their clear; the model chapter names the new configurations.
- **Tests.** `SessionsDialogTest`: a stand-in shows two menus in one start; each is answered in
  turn by its read, an answer naming the first read is refused on the second, each press is its
  own act, and the row runs after the second. `PromptsTest`: the same start through the inbox,
  two prompts, the first `settled` once the second shows. `AbsentMainsTest`: a left wait opens
  no `remedy` prompt, its state answered `leave` by the person, and a later check asks nothing;
  a wait whose pick was refused is still asked. The screen stand-in has `:menu2`, the menu
  fixture with another title (a second onboarding step), and `menu_answers/1`.

## What I ran into

- Two menus with the same title and options one after the other are one read to the code
  unless the first's press was sent, as for a running menu: a press whose outcome is unknown
  leaves the next identical menu `unknown`, for a person at the viewer. The stand-in's second
  menu has its own title for that reason, and the spec says the dialogs it models differ.
- A clock that does not move (a test's) would give two reads one `at`, and the second menu the
  first's answer; a new read's `at` is now at least the last one plus one.
- `mix test` from inside `apps/caf_prompts` fails on the umbrella's `:only` options; the
  tests run from the root.

## Findings

### new-1. The copy of a person's leave is two calls, with a prompt open between them

What happened: `Watch.clear/3` answers only an `asked` row, so the settle of a left wait asks
the absence and then clears it. Between the two calls the row is `asked`, its cue goes, and
`Prompts` may show the `remedy` prompt for that moment. Why: the `Watch` has no call that asks
and answers in one write. What I did: the two calls, one after the other in the healer's one
process; a person who answers in that moment answers once, and the clear is then refused
`settled`. `Remedies.tla` takes the copy as one step and its header says so. What the design
should change: if no flash of that prompt is wanted, a `Watch` call that writes a state asked
and answered in one write (the reason and the person's answer together) would make it one step
in the code too.

### new-2. `Remedies_Design` is past the fast gate's 30 s

What happened: `Remedies_Design` has 143,081 distinct states and took 76 s here (the machine
loaded near 25). F-204's note measured the same 143,081 states and 53 s; F-203's 59,700 and
27 s were measured without F-204's states. This unit does not change it: its steps for a wait
are behind `MayWait`, which that configuration turns off. Why: F-204's delegate message states
(`msg`, `joined`) multiplied the design configuration. What I did: nothing; it passes. What the design should change: give the delegate part its own fast
configuration and keep `Remedies_Design` to one kind at a time, or move it to `slow/`.
