# F-199: one reading of the metrics, by the baseline's rules

The brief's four items, from F-194's new-1 and new-2: `metrics/2`, a pure fold over the rows the
surface reads once (item 1); a test per metric on fixed rows (item 2); a viewer attached read by
`TmuxSampler` (item 3); and the design's metrics table (item 4). Built on `main` at F-194
(b2b5d20), rebased onto F-200 (ea50801) with no conflict. All four came to about 1,250 changed lines with tests, docs and this note (the fold
about 430 and its tests about 290), past the brief's 1,000. Item 4 was 40 lines of the design
once the rest was built, so I did not split it off. No test started the real Claude Code.

## What was built

- **`Watch.Metrics.fold/2`** (`caf_watch`): a pure fold of `%{acts, delegates, messages,
  symptoms, holds}` within `since:` and `until:` (ms), cut `by: :day | :week | :all` in UTC,
  weeks ISO, Monday to Sunday. It answers one map per cut, oldest first, with each metric of
  F-194's new-1 table, each with its evidence. A metric with a time also has its median and max.
  `cuts/3` is public, with doctests. The rules are written in the `@doc`, and where a rule
  names a fact no row holds, the metric says so in `unread` (new-1, new-2). The metrics are
  `started`, `reported`, `stalled`, `late`, `killed`, `restarted`, `dropped`, `limited`,
  `moves`, `prompts`, `recoveries` and `interventions`.
- **`CodingAgentFramework.metrics(config, since:, until:, by:)`**: takes the config, as
  `agent_rows/1` does, so it reads whether the tree runs or not. It reads the rows in one
  read-only snapshot of `caf.db` (`Store.snapshot/2`: one read transaction, and the names of
  the tables made, so a table not made reads as no rows) and hands them to the fold with the
  config's `operator`. It keeps no table. New readers for it: `Mailbox.Table.rows/1` and
  `Intake.Table.holds/2` (every hold that could wait on a person, decided ones too).
- **A viewer attached** (item 3): `Tmux.list_clients/1`, a new callback, gives each client's
  `tty`, `session` and `created` (`list-clients -F`). It is implemented in the CLI
  (`client_info/1` parses a line, doctested), in the fake (`attach/3` and `detach/2` as test
  aids), and in the `Inlet` test's probe. Given `acts:`, `TmuxSampler` reads the clients at each
  good sample with the server up. A client not there at the last sample is one `Acts.record/7`
  row: act `attach`, actor `person:`, target the agent whose tagged pane its session shows,
  `detail` the client. Its key is `attach:<host>:<server>:<tty>:<created>`, so a restart writes
  none twice. The sample's answer gains `attached`. The tree hands the sampler `Acts` and the
  name `Interventions` writes a person's acts by (`parts.interventions.person`).
  `caf_sampler` now declares `caf_acts`, and `caf_surface` declares `caf_store`.
- **Docs**: "Metrics and healing" gains `attach` among a person's interventions, the rules and
  the metrics table: each metric, its rows and its rule. The `Interventions` and `TmuxSampler`
  module docs say who reads an attach.

## What was measured

- `MetricsTest`, nine tests on fixed rows. A delegate on each side of Monday 2026-11-23 00:00
  UTC, which is a day cut and an ISO week cut at once (`2026-11-22` / `2026-11-23`, `2026-W47` /
  `2026-W48`); its report counts in its start's period. Stalled once per delegate, by its own
  row, even across two rows of one name; a `hooks_silent` delegate and one with no symptom (a
  stall that cleared first) are not counted. Late (b) and (c), one on time at 59 s, one not
  relayed yet, and (a) `unread`. Killed with no act; not killed with the operator's `end_main`
  first; killed when the stop came after the resume; the resumes, counted in the period they
  acted. Dropped, limited with its time past the reset, moves, prompts by kind (an ask
  answered and one open, a remedy prompt, an open hold, a dialog and a hold known only from
  their answer rows, a refused answer leaving its prompt open, a hold decided by the app not
  counted). Recoveries by outcome and asked, and the interventions, where a parent's answer
  and interrupt through `MCP` and the operator's interrupt are not counted. Refusals.
- `SurfaceTest`: `metrics/2` on a real home's `caf.db` with real `Acts`, `Delegates` and
  `Mailbox` tables, with no `Watch` or `Intake` table made.
- `TmuxSamplerTest`: on the fake server with real `Acts` and `Holder`. One attach is one row
  (actor, target, detail). A second sample writes none. A terminal on a session with no
  agent's pane writes none. Detached and attached again is a new row. Two restarts of the
  sampler write none twice. `TmuxContractTest` (CLI and fake): no clients on a fresh server,
  and `:no_server` when it is down.
- The fold on synthetic rows (2,000 delegates and reports, 900 asks and answers, 20,000 acts,
  2,000 symptoms; `bench.exs` in the session's scratchpad, not kept): see "What I ran into".
- No spec changed. Item 3 writes a fact a reader found, as `Interventions` does, under a key
  of its own: `Claim.tla`'s claim and settle in one step, once per state. The fold acts on
  nothing.

## What I ran into

- The design names `Watch.metrics/2`. What reads the rows is the surface, which opens the
  store; `caf_watch` holds only the pure fold. I named the fold `Watch.Metrics.fold/2` and the
  box's `metrics/2` is `CodingAgentFramework.metrics/2`. The design's line now says so.
- A person's Esc and a parent's interrupt through `MCP` are both act `interrupt` in `Acts`. The
  fold tells them apart by the fact's own key: `Interventions` records under
  `interrupt:<agent>:<uuid>`, while a fix's state is its JSON. `typed` and `attach` are read
  the same way.
- A real tmux client needs a terminal. The contract test reads the empty list and a server
  that is down, and the CLI's line parsing is doctested. The line of a real attach has not
  been measured (new-7).

## Findings

### new-1. How a report was taken is in no row: late (a) and stalled's `Stop` never came

What happened: rule 1's first symptom and late's (a) are a report read by a recovery's path
because its `Stop` (a headless delegate's `result`) never came. `Delegates` takes such a report
in `exited/4`, from the transcript's last reply once the process is gone. It writes the same
`finished` row and the same `Mailbox` reply as a report taken from a `result`. The reply's
`subtype` is nil then, but a `result` may carry no subtype either, so that is no fact.
What I did: the fold counts nothing for them and says `unread: ["stop_lost"]` and `["a"]`.
What the design should change: `Delegates` records how it took each report, either a column on
its row (`taken_by`: `result`, `transcript` or `turn_end`) or a field of the reply's row in
`Mailbox`, written with the report. The fold then reads it.

### new-2. A hold decided by anyone but a person has lost its open time

What happened: rule 4 counts a prompt in the period it opened. `Intake` writes a held row's
`written_at` again when it settles. A hold decided by the app's callback, a pause or its
deadline therefore has no open time, and only an answer through `answer/4` keeps one, in its
answer row (F-194's new-4).
What I did: such holds are not counted (`prompts.unread: ["hold_opened_at"]`). Holds still
open count from their `written_at`.
What the design should change: as F-194's new-4 said, `Intake` keeps `held_at`, written once at
the hold.

### new-3. A dialog, login or limit prompt counts only once it is answered

What happened: those prompts are read from rows that change as they open and close (an agent
row's dialog, a login's refusal, an agent row's limit). No row keeps one that opened and was
never answered.
What I did: the fold counts each one that an answer row names, with the open time that row
carries.
What the design should change: if the comparison needs unanswered prompts of every kind,
`Prompts` records each prompt's open as a fact (one row per prompt id, its kind and
`opened_at`) when it first lists it.

### new-4. Rule 4 names three periods; two more were needed

What happened: the cuts' rule names a delegate's, a recovery's and a prompt's period. A
person's intervention and a usage limit are neither.
What I did: a person's act counts in the period it was claimed, and a usage limit in the
period it began (its symptom's `since`).
What the design should change: confirm both, or name others.

### new-5. Relay time (c) is read from `updated_at`

What happened: `Mailbox` keeps no delivery time. Late's (c) reads a report's `updated_at`, the
time of its last write, which is `delivered` and is written once.
What I did: as above, and the doc says so.
What the design should change: `Mailbox` writes `delivered_at` once with `delivered`, so a
later write to the row (none today) could not move it.

### new-6. A kill whose resume was refused before its claim leaves no fact of the kill

What happened: a kill is read from the row's `resumes` or from `Delegates`' resume claim in
`Acts`. When the resume is refused before the claim (an unknown project, an account not ready,
the word to go on not stored), the row is `failed` with `resumes` 0 and no claim. Only its
notice's text says it was killed.
What I did: it is not counted killed. I did not read the notice's text.
What the design should change: `Delegates` records the kill it saw (`Acts.record/7`, act
`killed`, keyed by the row and its resumes) before it decides between a resume and a failure.
That one fact serves both `killed` and the first kill of a delegate killed twice.

### new-7. A real client's `list-clients` line is not measured

What happened: no test makes a tmux client, which needs a terminal.
What I did: the CLI asks for `#{client_tty}`, `#{client_session}` and `#{client_created}`,
tab-separated, and keeps any other line as `{:malformed, line}`.
What the design should change: the next end-to-end run (`docs/e2e.md`) attaches a terminal to
a main session's viewer and checks one `attach` row, keeping the line as a fixture.
