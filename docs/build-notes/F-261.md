# F-261: a fix answers on a busy host

Built by the main session.

## What happened

On a live tree at load 30 to 60 (15 cores), every parent's `stop` of a finished interactive
delegate answered `{:unknown, {:timeout, 15000}}` and claimed no act. An operator's stop through
another path got in early in the day at 11 to 18 s, and later not at all. With the stops failing,
finished delegates kept their places, and nothing new could start.

`Delegates.fix_state/3` is a call bounded at 15 s (`3 * @step`). Its own work is small: the
latest rows, a `Stream.read` bounded at 5 s, and one intake query. So the 15 s went by in the
`Delegates` process's mailbox. Two reads of the running process, taken about 5 s apart, showed:

- `Delegates`, with 2 to 4 messages queued, inside `settle_row` → `own_turns` → `ended_own` →
  `turn_of` → `turn_from` → `Reader.call`, waiting on `Transcripts`;
- `Interventions` (11, then 31 queued) and `HooksSilent` (7, then 23 queued), both waiting on
  `Reader.call`, their queues growing;
- `Transcripts` in `open_port` (each tail read starts a program), and the tracker in SQLite.

## Why

- **Each cue ran a whole settle.** `Delegates.cue/2` is a cast. A settle walks every open row,
  and a cue that arrives during one waits for its own settle. A fix's call waits behind all of
  them.
- **A finished delegate's transcript was read in full at every cue.** For an interactive
  delegate whose report is taken and which is not ended, `ended_own/3` read its transcript from
  the report's marked line to the end twice per cue: once in `turn_of/3` and once in
  `report_turn/3`. Each read starts a program. A delegate that takes more turns after its report,
  such as a builder given a second round, makes that range grow. More finished delegates that
  could not be stopped made every settle slower, so stops failed more and more through the day.
- **`Interventions` and `HooksSilent` ran one read per `:check`.** On a busy host the checks
  arrived faster than the reads ended.

## What was built

- `Transcripts.offset/4`: the session's fold offset, from memory, with no file read.
- `Delegates`: `handle_cast(:cue)` drains every `:cue` already queued, then settles once. An
  interactive delegate's own turns are kept per row with the reader's offset at their read
  (`own`), and read again only once that offset moves. The entry is dropped when the delegate
  ends.
- `Interventions` and `HooksSilent`: `handle_info(:check)` drains every queued `:check`, then
  checks once.
- No spec changes. A cue only triggers a read of the current state, and a settle or check reads
  everything afresh, so one settle for many queued cues takes the same steps the specs allow.
  The cached turns are exactly what a read at the same reader offset would find. A turn written
  after the reader's last read is read once the reader takes it, at its next read of the file.
  It is told then, a reader's interval later, as before.

## Tests

`delegates_interactive_test.exs`: "queued cues settle once, and a finished delegate's unchanged
transcript is not read". A finished delegate whose turn of its own was told. With `Delegates`
suspended, 50 cues are sent, and after it resumes, call tracing counts one `settle` and no
`Transcripts.text`. Then the transcript grows, and its next turn of its own is told, once.

## Findings

### new-1. Tracker reads wait on the database

The tracker was seen in `Store.until_free/3`, inside SQLite, while the checks queued behind it,
with about 19 000 `intake_events` rows. F-253 sweeps old tool rows. Other events are still
unbounded, and `until_free` waits for the database to be free. The design should bound the
intake's other rows and say how long a reader may wait for the database, and measure where the
wait comes from (another writer, or a long read).

### new-2. A transcript read starts a program

Each `Transcripts` tail read opens a port to `tail`. On a loaded host that costs much more than
reading the file in the VM. The design should say why a program is used (a file on another
host?), and read local files directly if nothing needs the program.

### new-3. Stops that settled `not_verified`

Two operator stops (acts 439 and 445) settled `not_verified` although the process ended. That is
not looked at here. A unit of its own should read how a stop is verified, and when.
