## What was built

- **The model first** (`Move.tla`). A constant `Flags` and a variable `flagged`: the app's
  `usable` flags an account and lifts the flag at any time (`AppFlags`), and `Refused(a)` is now
  its windows or its flag. The resume after a process ended by itself (`Resumes`) was a main
  session's; its header now names `Delegates`' `resume/4` and `went_on/4` too, which choose the
  account the same way (by `usable` alone: a delegate's windows are `Limit.tla`'s). Its first
  line stays `Delegate.tla`'s, so `Resumes` is still played by an interactive session
  (`ASSUME ~(Headless /\ Resumes)` kept). A move's pick (`Drain`, `LimitMove`) also leaves out a
  flagged account, as `Mover.target/3` does through `usable:`.
  - **`NoResumeOnRefused`**, an action property: the step that leaves `stopped` never chooses a
    refused account, its own (`ResumeHere`) or the copy's target (`CopyAsked`). With none usable
    no step is enabled: the resume is refused and the row stays down. The property is about the
    choice: a flag the app sets after it is the app's own, as one set after the launch would be.
  - **`Break_Move_ResumeInPlace`** drops `ResumeHere`'s guard and fails `NoResumeOnRefused`.
  - **`Move_Flags.cfg`**, the resume with the flag moving under it; `Move_Resume.cfg` keeps its
    old bounds, `Flags = FALSE`, and checks the new property by the windows alone. Every other
    Move configuration has `Flags = FALSE`.
- **The resume** (`Delegates`). `resume/4` and `went_on/4` ask `resume_account/2`, which reads
  `usable:` (the tree's `usable/3`: `Logins`, then the app) for the agent row's own account:
  - taken: the resume runs there, as before;
  - refused: `Usage.pick/4` among the other accounts; the account it finds is the resume's, and
    the resume's task first copies the conversation there with `Transcripts.Copy.copy/4`, the
    `Mover`'s and `Mains`' copy, recorded as an act `copy` in `Acts` (actor `delegates:<host>`,
    target `agent:<name>`, detail `from`, `to`, `session_id`, `because`), then calls
    `Sessions.resume/6` on it, which writes the agent row's account. A copy refused is the
    resume's refusal `{:copy, reason}`, and `Sessions` is not called;
  - refused, and no other account usable (`none_usable`, or no other account): the resume is
    refused with its own account's reason, `{:refused, {:app, reason}}` for the app's flag. For
    `resume/4` that is a refused resume as before: the row `failed`, the refusal the notice to
    its parent. For `went_on/4`, `go_on/5` answers it and the row stays `running` at its limit;
  - the others usable but each spent: the pick's `{:refused, {:allowance, at}}`, as above;

## Findings

### new-1. A move's resume does not read `usable:` again

What happened: the `Mover` picks a move's account by `usable:` when the move begins, and its
resume (`Mover.resume/2`) reads only `Accounts.ready/2` of that account. A drain and its stop may
take up to the move's `timeout` (30 minutes); an account the app flags in that time still takes
the move's resume.
Why: the app's flag came after the `Mover` (F-207), and its resume never had a check of its own.
`Move.tla`'s `NoResumeOnRefused` is about the resume after a process ended by itself; the
model's move picks its target once, as the code does.
What I did: nothing; the brief names `Delegates`' resumes.
What the design should change: either say that a move's account is decided at its pick, or have
the `Mover`'s resume read `usable:` of `move_to` and, refused, take the way a refused resume on
the other account takes today (`back/3`: its own account, or left gone at a limit). I recommend
the second, since the app's flag says no work runs there.

### new-2. A delegate moved by its resume keeps its start's lease, which names the old account

What happened: a delegate's resume runs under its start's lease (`Delegates`' moduledoc), so a
resume that moves to `side` runs under a lease whose `account` is `work`. A move by the `Mover`
does the same (`Boxes.tla`: a move is a stop and a resume under one lease).
Why: the lease is the start's; the brief's path is the `Mover`'s, which keeps it.
What I did: nothing.
What the design should change: nothing, if a lease's account is only what its grant was read
against. If the `Scheduler`'s gate reads a running lease's account later (room per account),
both a move and a moved resume should name the new account on the lease.

### new-3. More keys look read only by an agent's part

What happened: by the same reading as the four keys refused here, `heavy` (read only in the
tree's agents' wiring, `Runs.new/3`), `mains`, `main_model`, `lease`, `viewer`, `remedies`,
`remedy_bound`, `git` and `margin` are read by the tree only where it wires a part that an
agentless tree does not start. I did not check each one through every part.
Why: `Config.new/1` checks each key's shape, and F-207's new-3 named four.
What I did: refused the four the brief names.
What the design should change: decide whether the refusal covers every key no part of an
agentless tree reads (then a list kept beside `agentless/0`, checked by a test that starts the
agentless tree), or only the keys an app would read as a guard, as `usable` is.
