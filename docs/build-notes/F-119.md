# F-119: R4, `Claim.tla` and `Hold.tla`, instanced by the specs that repeat them

The remodel's fourth unit (`docs/design.md`, "The model" and "Order of the first units", R4;
F-115's section 3, new-1, new-9 and new-13; F-116's new-1). Specs, configurations and
`bin/check-model` only; no Elixir.

## What was built

1. **`Claim.tla`** (169 lines, with its header and its own spec): a claim on a target's
   state before an act. `Take` writes the row, and no second claim is taken on a state that has
   one; the call holds the claim in memory; `Act` runs only while the claim's state is the
   target's (`Current`), and acts on the target as it is now; `LetGo` ends a claim with no act;
   `Restart` cuts the calls and keeps the table; `Settle` ends a row whose call was cut;
   `Passes(T)` is for a spec that does not number its states. Properties `OneClaimPerState`,
   `OneActPerState`, `NoActOnCutOffClaim`, and the liveness `ClaimsEnd`. Breaks `TwoClaims`,
   `Stale`, `InMemory` (a claim with no row), each with a `Break_Claim_*` configuration that
   fails; `Claim_Design` checks the design alone (two targets whose states move, two actors,
   restarts at any step).
2. **`Hold.tla`** (188 lines, the same): a request held open with a deadline. `Opens`, `Held` (the
   row), `Asked(m, h)` (a decision meant for `m` takes the hold it names; the row says
   `"deciding"` before anything goes out), `Decides` (the response; the request closes),
   `Marked` (the row says answered), `Expires` and `Passed` (an empty response), `Cut(R)` (the
   holders of `R` die; a restart is all of them), `Settles` (a cut row: held is lost, deciding
   unknown). Properties `OneResponse`, `LateDropped`, `OnlyNamed`, `RequestsEnd`, and a fifth,
   `AnsweredWasSent` (new-4). Breaks `NotNamed`, `NoDeadline`, `TwoResponses`, `LateSent`,
   `TrustDeciding`, each with a `Break_Hold_*` that fails; `Hold_Design` checks two requests.
3. **`Agent.tla`** instances `Claim` for Recoveries (`RC`). The loss is not numbered, so a
   reading that names a new loss passes the old one (`RC!Passes(new)`), as `claimed \ new` did.
   `claimed`, the `[agent, old]` records of `acting`, `reopened` per agent and the action
   property `NoActOnCutOffClaim` are gone; `OneReopenPerLoss` is `RC!OneActPerState` and
   `NoActOnCutOffClaim` is `RC!NoActOnCutOffClaim`, now an invariant (two configurations move it
   to `INVARIANTS`). The claim of a third loss is for the end failed{repeating}, which its act
   writes, as the code's `act/4` does. The `/exit` comment on `AgentExits` goes.
4. **`Team.tla`** instances `Claim` as `Cl(b)`, with the bug `b` a Break puts in one actor's
   claims (`ClaimBreak`), so `OperatorTwoClaims` and `OperatorStaleFix` stay the operator's
   alone. A claim is `[by, t, s, act, n]` (`ep` and `kind` renamed); `fixed`, `done` and `calls`
   are Claim's; `twice` and `cut` are new ghosts. `OneClaimPerState`, `OneFixPerState`,
   `ClaimEnds` are Claim's, and `NoActOnCutOffClaim` is new in `Team_Claims` and
   `Team_OperatorClaims`. The `/exit` comment goes.
5. **`Copy.tla`** instances `Claim` for the forward's reopen (`RF`), on the state `Key(s)`:
   `fclaims`, `facting` (now a set of claims), `fdone`, `reopened` (a set of states), `twice`,
   `fcut`. `OneReopenPerState` is `RF!OneActPerState`. `NoClaim` goes.
   `Break_Copy_RetryFailed` now checks `ForwardReopened` (new-1).
6. **`Intake.tla`** instances `Hold` twice: `HP` for `PermissionRequest`, `HT` for
   `PreToolUse`. The permission hold keeps its steps and state counts; `"answering"` is
   `"deciding"`, and `sent` holds the responses. The tool hold gets a row per request (`trow`),
   a mark after the decision goes out (`ToolMarked`), a settle after a restart
   (`ToolSettled`), and a step of its own for the callback's decision taking the hold
   (`DecisionTaken`); `entry` and `ToolAnswered` go (new-3). `SentAtMostOnce`,
   `OnlyNamedDialog`, `HoldSettles`, `OneResponse`, `LateDropped` and `ToolRequestsEnd` are
   Hold's; `ToolRequestsEnd` now also says the row settles.
7. **`Stream.tla`** keeps its requests; its header says why (new-2).
8. **`bin/check-model`** parses every slow configuration in `all` (the gate): TLC reads the spec
   and the configuration and takes the initial state alone (`-simulate num=1 -depth 1`), so a
   constant left unset fails there (exit 151). `slow/Intake_Large.cfg` sets `MaxCalls = 0`
   (new-5).
9. `model/tla/READING.md`: a section on the modules and `INSTANCE`, and the Agent, Team, Intake
   and Copy text for the instanced claims and holds.

## What I ran into

- An instance cannot be named by a definition (`ClaimsOf(by) == Cl(ClaimBreak(by))` is
  "incomplete" to SANY); `Team.tla` writes `Cl(ClaimBreak(c))!Take(…)` at each use.
- A module variable substituted by a constant (Agent's `now <- [a \in Agents |-> 0]`) works as
  long as no step the instancing spec takes primes it; `Claim`'s `Moves` is only in its own
  `Next`.
- With the module's row on each state, two Copy Breaks would have been hidden behind it:
  `ReopenInMemory` maps to Claim's `InMemory` (a claim with no row), which is what it means, and
  `RetryFailed` is found by a different property (new-1).
- My first `Claim_Design` (three states per target) had 978,121 states; two states per target
  has 10,000 and finds the same Breaks.

## Findings

### new-1. `RetryFailed` is now refused by the claim's row, and found by `ForwardReopened`

What happened: under `RetryFailed` a failed reopen is taken as a new loss with the open count
unchanged, so the second claim lands on the state the first claimed. Claim's row refuses it, and
`OneReopenPerState`, which `Break_Copy_RetryFailed` checked, holds. The forward then stays
dropped with its row saying open and no person told, which `ForwardReopened` finds.
Why: the table's one claim per state is a second guard against a retry of one state, behind
the forward row's `"failed"`.
What I did: `Break_Copy_RetryFailed` checks `ForwardReopened`; Copy's header names the claim's
row as defense in depth behind the forward's row.
What the design should change: nothing; "The model" may note that a guard a module adds can
move a spec's Break to another property.

### new-2. `Stream`'s requests do not fit `Hold`

What happened: F-116's new-1 asks R4 to instance `Hold` for `Stream`'s `can_use_tool` requests
unless the state count grows too much. The reason they stay is not the state count. Claude Code
holds those requests, not the framework: a restart cuts none, the row follows the log (a
request, an answer's replay, a cancel) rather than a connection the framework holds, there is no
deadline, and the answer is a spooled line whose outcome is read back from its replay. `Hold`'s
cut at a restart and its mark in memory are wrong for that shape, and a withdraw (the cancel)
has no place in it.
What I did: `Stream.tla` keeps its requests, and its header says why. I did not build a second
shape into `Hold`.
What the design should change: the table in "The model" says `Stream.tla` keeps its requests,
held by the requester, as a different shape from `Hold`'s. The other choice, a "held by the
requester" kind in `Hold` (no cut at a restart, the row moved by the requester's events, a
withdraw), would serve one spec, so I do not recommend it.

### new-3. The tool hold becomes the permission hold's shape

What happened: `PreToolUse`'s hold kept its entry in memory, wrote a row only for a decision,
and wrote that row in the step that handed the decision to the plug, which wrote it as a later
step. As one `Hold` with the permission hold, a tool call has a row from the start
(`"held"`), the row says `"deciding"` before the decision is handed out, it is marked answered
after, and a cut row settles lost or unknown after a restart. The plug's write is one step with
the hand.
Why: R5 makes them one structure, and the model comes first.
What I did: modeled it so; every tool property holds, and `DecidedHasRow` reads a row
`"deciding"`, `"answered"` or `"unknown"`.
What the design should change: R5's brief says so: a `PreToolUse` request gets its row when
held, the decision's mark before its response, and the settle after a restart.

### new-4. `Hold` checks a fifth property, `AnsweredWasSent`

What happened: Intake's `TrustAnswering` (a row still deciding after a restart taken as
answered) is a bug in `Hold`'s settle, and none of the four properties the design names finds
it: the row settles, and nothing was sent twice.
What I did: `AnsweredWasSent` (a row says answered only when a decision went out), with
`Break_Hold_TrustDeciding`. Intake still checks its own `AnsweredWasHeard`.
What the design should change: the table in "The model" lists `AnsweredWasSent` with `Hold.tla`.

### new-5. `slow/Intake_Large` sets `MaxCalls = 0`

What happened: F-115's new-1. The configuration predates the tool calls and checks the events
and the dialogs at larger bounds.
What I did: `MaxCalls = 0`, said in its comment; `Intake_Tools` checks the calls. The gate's
parse of the slow set would have caught it.
What the design should change: nothing.

### new-6. Recoveries' claim of a passed loss is let go, not acted on

What happened: `Agent.tla` acted on a claim whose loss a later reading had passed (`old`),
without counting it as a reopen. With `Claim`, an act runs only while its claim is current, so
`Passes` lets such a claim go. In the code the claim and the act are one call (`act_on/3`), and
no reading falls between them, so the model's reading between the two steps stands for nothing
the code does; either choice keeps every code behavior in the model.
What I did: modeled it with `Passes`; every Agent property and Break holds as before, with
fewer states.
What the design should change: nothing now; if `Recoveries` ever acts in a later call than its
claim, it checks that the loss is still current, as `Claim`'s `Act` says.

### new-7. `Start_TwoSessions` is over the 30 s bound, and F-118 owns its spec

What happened: 118 s before and 61 s after, on a loaded machine; every other fast configuration
finished within 25 s.
Why: its state space (198,724 states at depth 24) is the largest of `Start.tla`'s.
What I did: nothing; F-118 changes `Start.tla` and its configurations at the same time.
After F-118 landed and this unit was rebased on it, it took 218 s (875,981 states, 244,379
distinct, depth 25) at a load of 30, so it moved to `slow/`, where the gate parses it and a
person runs it by hand. The other `Start` configurations stay fast.
What the design should change: nothing.

### new-8. R4 grows the model by about 420 lines, where the design says about −300

What happened: `Claim.tla` and `Hold.tla` are 357 lines; Agent, Team, Copy and Intake each
changed about as many lines as they shed (+343, −291 in all), and Stream gained its header note.
Why: each spec's claim or hold was 20 to 60 lines of steps, and most of what instancing removes
is replaced by the steps that call the module and by the module's own spec, configurations and
fifth property. The `UNCHANGED` frames shrink little: each spec still names the variables a
module step leaves alone. The −300 estimate counted the specs' encodings, not the modules' own
checks.
What I did: nothing; each mechanism is now written once, which is what R4 is for.
What the design should change: R4's size in "Order of the first units" says about +400 for the
model; R6's estimate is worth a second look on the same grounds.
