# F-47: the claim guard of `Recoveries` gets a check that fails without it

`RecoveryClaims` in `Agent.tla` has two guards, `a \notin claimed` and `a \notin opened`. F-41's
new-2 found that deleting either one alone fails no configuration. This unit gives
`a \notin claimed` a check that fails without it, and names `a \notin opened` as defense in depth.
Only `model/tla/` and this note change; no code changes.

## What was built

In `Agent.tla`:

- **`cutOff`**, a history variable in `rec` (so in `vars`), starting `{}` and typed in `TypeOK`.
  `RecoveriesRestart` adds the agents in `claimed` to it; no other step adds to it.
- **`NoActOnCutOffClaim`**, a step property: no `RecoveryActs` step acts on an agent in `cutOff`.
  It is written as `[][\A a \in cutOff : ~(\E c \in acting : c.agent = a /\ RecoveryActs(c))]_vars`,
  the form `Next` uses for the act.
- **`Break = "ClaimTwice"`**, which drops `a \notin claimed` from `RecoveryClaims` and nothing else.
- The header says the cut-off claim is never acted on again as `NoActOnCutOffClaim`, and names
  `a \notin opened` as defense in depth behind `a \notin claimed` for `OneReopenPerLoss`.

In the configurations:

- **`Break_Agent_ClaimTwice.cfg`**: one agent, one start, one viewer death, one restart of
  `Recoveries`, no writes. It checks only `NoActOnCutOffClaim`, and TLC finds it violated.
- **`Agent_Claims.cfg`** now checks `NoActOnCutOffClaim`. It is the only configuration that is not
  a `Break_` one and allows a restart of `Recoveries`: the other fast ones and all three slow ones
  set `MaxRecoveryRestarts = 0`, so `cutOff` stays empty there and the property is vacuous. So no
  other configuration lists it.
- `READING.md` names the property, the break and the guard.

One line of `Agent.tla` goes beyond the brief's step 1, as its text allows ("except as the step
property below needs"): `Reading` takes the agents it names a new loss for out of `cutOff`
(new-1).

About 47 changed lines in the spec, `Agent_Claims.cfg` and `READING.md`, 14 in the new
configuration, apart from this note.

## What I ran into

- The brief's `cutOff` (the agents in `claimed` at a restart) made the property fail in the
  design, until `Reading` cleared it (new-1).
- With several workers TLC reports a counterexample that is not the shortest (new-2).
- `gate-test` failed twice on a 60 s property timeout in `caf_registry`, under load (new-3).

## Findings

### new-1. `claimed` keeps an acted claim, so `cutOff` holds it and must end with the loss

What happened: `claimed` keeps an agent from its claim until a reading names a new loss for it,
acted on or not. So `cutOff = cutOff \cup claimed` takes claims that were acted on before the
restart as well as claims cut off between claim and act. With a `cutOff` that only grows,
`NoActOnCutOffClaim` failed in the design (`Agent_Claims`, 12 states): claim, act, the server
dies, `Recoveries` restarts (the acted claim joins `cutOff`), a reading names a new loss, and its
new claim and act hit an agent in `cutOff`.

Why: the header's rule is about one loss: a claim cut off by a restart is never acted on again.
A new loss is a new claim, which the spec says in `Reading` (`claimed \ new`, `reopened \ new`).
The history variable has to end with the loss too.

What I did: `Reading` takes the agents it names a new loss for out of `cutOff`, with a comment, as
it does for `claimed` and `reopened`. No other step changes `cutOff` but `RecoveriesRestart`.

What the design should change: nothing in `design.md`. The unit that builds `Recoveries`' table
should know the rule is per loss: a claim row survives a restart, blocks a second claim of its
loss, and ends when a sample names a new loss. A reading of the brief that takes only the claims
with an act pending (the agents in `acting`) at the restart is also possible; it passes the same
way, and checks a little less (no claim acted on before the restart). I kept the brief's.

### new-2. A `Break` configuration's trace under `-workers auto` is not the shortest

What happened: `Break_Agent_ClaimTwice` found its counterexample at depth 16 and 17 in two runs
with `-workers auto`, and at depth 11 with one worker. The longer traces had a process exit and
a reading before the act that the act did not need.

Why: TLC stops at the first violation any worker finds, and the workers do not explore breadth
first in step, so the trace need not be the shortest. The state counts differ for the same reason.

What I did: recorded the one-worker numbers beside the gate's, and picked the bounds by the
one-worker depth.

What the design should change: "The model" could say that a counterexample is for reading with
`TLC_WORKERS=1`, and that a `Break_*` configuration's state count and depth vary between runs. A
note's table of a break should give the one-worker numbers.

### new-3. A `caf_registry` property takes longer than ExUnit's 60 s under load

What happened: `gate-test` exited 2 on two runs in a row, each with the same property of
`caf_registry` timing out at 60 s. Alone it takes 73.5 s at a load average near 110. F-41 recorded
42 s for the whole suite at a load of 34 to 53.

Why: not found beyond the load. The property reads generated session files, which is file calls,
and "Tests" in `design.md` already says file calls are what slow under load.

What I did: nothing to the test or the gate. I ran the suite with `--timeout 900000` and it
passed, and said so under Gates.

What the design should change: "Calibrate load gates on the machine that runs them" could say the
same of a test's timeout: a property whose time grows with load names its own `@tag timeout:`,
or its generated size shrinks, and a gate that fails on a timeout under load is rerun at a lower
load before it is called red. This is for a unit of its own; it is not this one.

### new-4. F-41's new-2 is done

`a \notin claimed` has its break (`ClaimTwice`) and its property (`NoActOnCutOffClaim`).
`a \notin opened` is named in the header as defense in depth behind it, so it needs no break.
