# F-196: a start refused at a limit waits as its owner's row; an answered act shows in the inbox

The brief's three items, from F-193's new-1 and new-2 (and F-192's new-1), decided: the
`limit` prompt of a start refused at a usage limit is the start's owner's row, answered `wait`
or `leave`. Item 1 models it in `Limit.tla`, item 2 builds the row in `Delegates` and `Mains`
and its reading in `Prompts`, and item 3 keeps a `remedy` answered `act` in the inbox until its
act's row ends. Built on `main` at 5cfaeec (F-194, F-198 and F-200 in). No test started the real
Claude Code, and nothing was measured by hand.

## What was built

- **The model first** (`Limit.tla`, constant `Starts`): a start of new work (`StartAsked`)
  picks its account on `Usage`'s record (`Pick`): another usable account with room takes it
  (`there`), this one only while its record says it is not limited (`granted`), and refused at
  a limit the row is written `waiting` for the soonest reset with its `limit` prompt open
  (StartRefusedAtLimit). `AnswerWait` closes the prompt, `AnswerLeave` refuses the row `left`,
  `WaitDefault` takes the wait at the deadline, and `StartAtReset` picks again under the same
  row, which may wait again for a newer limit. A restart keeps the row (`again` goes back to
  `waiting`; a start whose row was not yet written is `cut`). Invariant `StartPromptIsRow`,
  liveness `StartWaitEnds` (a waiting start is granted, here or on another account, or left);
  `NoStartOnLimited` holds unchanged. Configurations `Limit_Start` (a headless agent whose
  record limits the account, two limits, a restart) and `Limit_StartMove` (another account);
  `Break_Limit_NoRepick` (the start goes at its reset on the pick it was refused by) fails
  `NoStartOnLimited`. Every earlier configuration sets `Starts = FALSE`.
- **`Delegates`** (item 2): a start whose pick refuses `{:allowance, until}` writes its row
  `waiting` (`Delegates.Table.waiting/2`: `until`, `deadline` the reset plus `limit_margin:`,
  default 2 min, the start's request kept as a term in `start`, `tell` its parent) and answers
  at once `{:ok, %{id, name, brief_uuid, session_id: nil, account: nil, waiting: until}}`,
  whatever `wait:`. At its deadline (a wake set at the write and at each start) the row picks
  again: room, it becomes `starting` and asks for its lease under its own request id through
  the same step a start takes (`leased/5`, shared now); a newer limit, it waits for that reset
  with `waited` cleared; another refusal refuses it; an unread reading decides nothing. Its
  parent is told once it leaves `waiting`, as a queued start's is, the notice naming the reset
  it waited for. `answer_wait/4`: `wait` (`waited` names the person) or `leave` (refused
  `left`). A second start of the name is refused `{:waiting, name}` (`Turn.again/3`), the row
  holds its place in its parent's cap, and a fix of it is `{:not_started, "waiting"}`.
- **`Mains`** (item 2): `start_main/5` and `resume_main/3` the same way, on `Mains.Starts`
  (`waiting/2`, `account` empty while none is picked, `tell` the start's sender; a resume tells
  no one). A resume waits when its own account is refused at a limit and no other usable one has
  room, for the sooner of the resets. At the deadline a start declares its agent row and a
  resume reads its agent row again (`resumable`, the same session id) before its lease.
  `answer_wait/4`; while it waits a start, resume or end of the name is refused
  `{:waiting, name}`. The sender's notice is a `framework` notice under
  `start:main:<host>:<row>:<created_at>`.
- **The pick's clock**: `Delegates` and `Mains` give `Usage.pick/4` their own clock (`clock:`,
  wall-clock ms), the one the row's deadline is read by, in place of `System.os_time/1`.
- **`Prompts`** (items 2 and 3): `delegates:`/`delegates_db:`, `mains:`/`mains_db:` and
  `acts_db:`, and the cues `delegates`, `mains` and `acts`. A waiting row is kind `limit`, id
  `limit:start:delegate:<row>` or `limit:start:main:<row>`, choices `wait` and `leave`, its
  deadline the row's, its parent the delegate's parent; open until answered for this reset. A
  `subtree:` filter also takes a prompt whose parent is in the tree (a waiting delegate has no
  agent row). A `remedy` answered `act` is `answering` while the person's act has no row in
  `Acts` (actor the person, target the symptom's, detail's `symptom` the state) or that row is
  open, and leaves once it has ended.
- **The app** starts `Prompts` after `Delegates` and `Mains`, its new owners, with the files
  and pids. `AbsentMains` settles a resume that waits `verified` with answer `{waiting, until}`,
  so a wait is no failed verify and keeps the remedy on the list. The MCP tools pass `waiting`
  through (`start_delegate`'s and `start_main`'s answers).
- **Docs**: "Prompts" (the kinds' table's `limit` row, the rule of when an answered prompt
  leaves, what F-196 added), "Usage limits" (the start's prompt, built in F-196), "The model"
  (`Limit.tla`'s start).

## What was measured

- TLC: `Limit_Start` 87,961 distinct states, depth 34, 13 s; `Limit_StartMove` 87,806, depth
  27, 11 s; `Break_Limit_NoRepick` fails `NoStartOnLimited` in 9,299 distinct states, depth 18.
  `Limit_Headless` 5,678 states, as before the change. A check that a second wait is reached
  (an invariant `~(sr = "waiting" /\ sAt = 2)` added for one run, then removed) failed in 921
  states: the newer-limit path is in the state space, so `StartWaitEnds` is not vacuous there.
- `DelegatesTest`, two new tests on the real parts: a start at a limit waits as its row (the
  answer, the row, the second start and the cap refused, `wait` answered once, a restart keeping
  it, a newer limit at the deadline making it wait again, then past that reset the start runs
  under its own row to its report, its parent told once); and a start a person leaves.
- `MainsTest`: a main's start at a limit waits, refuses a second start and an end, takes `wait`
  once, keeps its row across a restart of `Mains`, and at its deadline starts on `work` under its
  own row and lease, through its trust dialog, its sender told once. The resume test now finds
  `work` spent and `spare` logged out a wait, which a person leaves, in place of a refusal.
- `PromptsTest`: a real `Delegates` whose start waits is a `limit:start:delegate:<row>` prompt
  with its deadline, parent and project, in its parent's subtree, answered `leave` once (an
  answer row in `Acts`); the remedy test's `act` stays `answering`, is not taken for another
  state's act, and leaves once its act's row fails, which the fault ledger shows.
- About 1,320 changed lines with the tests and configurations, before this note.

## What I ran into

- A re-pick first stamped the row's `called_at` with the test's moved clock, so `Mains`'
  `settle` never read the agent row's start as since the call: `called_at` is the wall clock,
  as `Starts.starting/2` and `Table.starting/2` stamp it; the deadline is the owner's clock.
- `Usage.pick/4` inside the owners read `System.os_time/1`, so a test could not pass a reset
  without waiting for it; the owners give it their clock now.
- `main_starts.account` is `NOT NULL`: a waiting row writes it empty.
- In `Prompts`, a `for` filter `act = person_act(…)` drops a nil act (no act yet); a
  one-element generator keeps it.

## Findings

### new-1. An `act` whose state passes before the healer takes it stays `answering`

What happened: a `remedy` answered `act` leaves the inbox when its act's row ends. The healer
takes the act at its next check of that state. If the state is no longer read by then (the
viewer came back, the main resumed by itself), the healer never claims it, and `Watch.pass/4`
passes only `open` and `asked` rows, so the answered row stays and the prompt stays
`answering`. Why: an answered row was the end of the prompt before this unit. What I did:
nothing; the rule in "Prompts" says when it leaves. What the design should change: `pass/4`
passes an `answered` `act` row whose state is no longer read, by the `open` row's rule, and the
inbox drops it then, its outcome "passed before its act".

### new-2. The start's prompt id names its owner

What happened: the brief names the id `limit:start:<row>`. `Delegates`' and `Mains`' rows are
numbered by two tables, each from 1, so one number can be both. Why: two owners of one kind.
What I did: `limit:start:delegate:<row>` and `limit:start:main:<row>`. What the design should
change: nothing, unless the id must stay one word after `start`.

### new-3. `run_delegate` waits for a report a waiting start cannot give before its reset

What happened: a start at a limit answers at once with `waiting`, whatever `wait:`.
`run_delegate` then awaits the report up to its timeout, which a reset hours away outlasts, and
answers `{:unknown, %{reason: :timeout}}`. Why: the tool's await is the start's report. What I
did: `start_delegate` and `start_main` pass `waiting` through; `run_delegate` is unchanged.
What the design should change: `run_delegate` answers `%{delegate: name, waiting: until}` at
once for a start that waits, the report coming later as for `start_delegate`.

### new-4. An absent main's resume that waits is a verified act with no outcome after

What happened: `AbsentMains` settles a resume that waits `verified` (answer `waiting`). The row
resumes the main at its reset, or a person leaves it, or its pick is refused then; none of
these is a row in `Acts`, so the fault ledger never shows a waited resume that failed later.
Why: the act's answer is the row's, which outlives the healer's check. What I did: the settle
above, so the remedy is not dropped from the list for a wait. What the design should change:
either the healer's claim stays open (`acted`) until the waiting row ends and is settled from
it, or the owner records its waited start's end as an act.

### new-5. The unit ran past its size

What happened: about 1,320 changed lines with tests and configurations, over the brief's
1,000. Why: the row, its timer, its re-pick, its answer and its notice are built twice, in
`Delegates` and in `Mains`, whose start paths share nothing. What I did: everything, in one
commit per item (the model; `Delegates`; `Mains`; `Prompts`; the docs), so it can be landed
in parts. What the design should change: nothing; a later unit may give the two owners one
`waiting` row module.
