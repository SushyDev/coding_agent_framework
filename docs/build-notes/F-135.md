# F-135: the `Scheduler`

The lease ledger in front of every agent start, heavy run and landing (`docs/design.md`, "A
lease"), against `model/tla/Schedule.tla`, with F-134's three findings modeled first. The unit
ran well past 1,000 lines, so, as the brief allows, it lands the ledger, the queue and the gates
with `Delegates`' start; `Jobs`' start and a landing's gang lease ask it in the next unit.

## What was built

- **The model first** (`Schedule.tla`):
  - F-134's new-1: the lease's holder is the owner's row of the work it admits, a variable
    `work` (`starting`, `live`, `ended`) that outlives a restart, in place of the requester's
    process. The owner writes it `starting` before it asks, starts the work only once its
    requester was told `granted` (`WorkStarts`), and settles it `ended` at any step after it
    asked (`WorkEnds`: live work's end, or a start that never ran). `Release` and `Reclaim` read
    it; a starting row is live. `ReleaseOnce` and `QueuedIsAnswered` read `work`. Break
    `StartingIsEnded` (a reclaimer that takes a starting row as ended) fails `ReleaseOnce`.
  - F-134's new-2: the hold. `Fits` adds `HigherNeed(x, c)`, the units the waiting requests of a
    higher class need; `ClassOrder` gains its second conjunct, "what the grant leaves free
    covers every such request's needs". Break `NoHold` drops the reservation. A new shape,
    `Hold` (two landings and a heavy run, two slots and one lock), with `Schedule_Hold.cfg`.
  - F-134's new-3: `NoHeavyUnderHigh` and Break `NoHighGate`.
- **`caf_scheduler`**, a new app over `caf_store` (and the `telemetry` package):
  - `Scheduler`, one process per instance, the one writer of `leases`. `request/3` answers
    `{:ok, lease}`, `{:queued, %{id, position}}` then one message `{:lease, id, answer}`, or
    `{:refused, {reason, retry_at}}` with `queue_full`, `deadline`, `pressure` or `allowance`.
    The lease is looked up first; a request asked again while queued takes the new requester's
    process. A requester's process that ends leaves the queue. The monitor asks the holders'
    owners (`holders:`, a function per kind) about the leases it holds at each cue
    (`reads/0`: `sessions`, `delegates`, `usage`) and on `interval:`; the reclaimer asks about
    every unreleased row at start, and monitors the live ones. An unknown answer decides
    nothing. Telemetry `[:coding_agent_framework, :scheduler, :grant | :refusal]`.
  - `Scheduler.Table`: `leases` with `AUTOINCREMENT` ids (the fencing token), a partial unique
    index on `request_id` among granted rows, the grant as one immediate transaction that sums
    the unreleased rows' units, the release as `UPDATE … WHERE id = ? AND released_at IS NULL
    RETURNING id`. Refused requests are rows too (`outcome`, `reason`, `retry_at`), and the
    table's `meta/0` is a `Tracker` source (`scheduler:<host>`, kind `:leases`).
  - `Scheduler.Queue`, pure: classes in order, the hold, aging inside a class (a request that
    waited `age` ms goes first, oldest first), else the turns of the modules after the one
    served last, and one module's requests in order.
  - `Scheduler.Pressure`, pure: `/proc/pressure/{cpu,memory,io}` read as deltas of `total`
    over the span, or a level function for macOS; `severe` at once, any other change once asked
    for a whole `window`. Every threshold an option with a placeholder default.
- **`Usage`** reads `rate_limits.five_hour` beside `seven_day` (two added columns; a file of an
  earlier build gains them), and `Usage.Allowance` answers `:room` or `{:spent, retry_at}` for
  both windows, through a connection of the caller's own.
- **`Delegates`**: with `scheduler:`, a start writes its row `starting`, then asks for a lease
  (class `:builder`, module the project, units `lease:`), the row as holder; a queued start
  waits in `Delegates`' process with its caller and counts against its parent's cap; a refusal
  is the row `refused` with `{:lease, {reason, retry_at}}`; the `Scheduler`'s end refuses the
  waiting starts. A granted start resets `called_at` and the deadline, since its call of
  `Sessions` starts then. `Delegates.Work` answers for a delegate's row: live while it may
  start, run or be resumed; ended once refused, or done with its agent row free, or when a newer
  start owns its name.
- **`caf_example`**: the `Scheduler` before `Delegates`, with `resources` (`seat` 4, `slot` 2),
  `lease` and `pressure` in the config (stall times where `/proc/pressure` exists, none
  elsewhere), its declaration in `Cues`, and its table on the `Tracker`.
- **Docs**: the design's `Scheduler` and `Usage` rows, the cue table, "A lease", the model's
  paragraph and the unit order; README's Status and the apps chart.

| `Schedule_Hold` (new) | 103,350 | 18 | 6 s |
| `Schedule_Mains` | 17,088 (was 16,050) | 15 | 8 s, then 3 s |
| `Schedule_Liveness` | 17,088 (was 16,050) | 15 | 25 s, then 10 s |
| `Break_Schedule_*` (17) | each fails as it should | | 4 to 10 s each |

A probe (in the scratch directory, not kept) kept `HigherWaits` and dropped only the reservation
on the `Safety` shape: TLC found no error in 70,590 states. With one slot, a landing that can be
held for is a landing that fits, so `HigherWaits` alone already serves it. That is why `Hold`
has two slots: the reservation is checked only where the higher request waits on another
resource. `Break_Schedule_NoClassOrder` moved to `Hold` for the same reason: on `Safety` the
reservation now catches what it used to.

Tests: `caf_scheduler` 15 (6 doctests, 9 tests) in about 1 to 4 s; `caf_usage` 11; the four new
`Delegates` lease tests among its 14 in about 25 s; `caf_example` 10 in about 40 s, one of them
following a delegate's lease through a resume to its release by the monitor and onto the
`Tracker`.

## What I ran into

- The brief named the outcomes `{:granted, lease}`, `{:queued, position}` and `{:refused,
  reason, retry_at}`; the design names `{:ok, lease}`, `{:queued, request_id}` and `{:refused,
  {reason, retry_at}}`, which is the repository's outcome rule. I followed the design, and the
  queued answer carries both the id and the position (new-1).
- `Scheduler` and `Delegates` call each other in a cycle if the owner's answer were a call:
  `Delegates` waits on `request/3` in its own process while the `Scheduler` asks about a
  delegate's row. So the owner's answer (`Delegates.Work`) and the allowance
  (`Usage.Allowance`) are pure modules over connections of the caller's own, opened by the
  embedding app as it opens `Sessions.Agents`.
- `Delegates`' settle refuses a `starting` row with no call under way at its deadline; a
  queued start had no call under way, so it would have been refused while it waited. Waiting
  starts sit in `pending` beside the calls, which keeps them out of the settle and in the cap.
- The `Scheduler` reads `jobs` in no cue yet: no job asks for a lease until `Jobs` is wired,
  so its declaration names `sessions`, `delegates` and `usage` only.
- `erl_child_setup: failed with error 32` printed during `caf_example`'s tests, as earlier notes
  record; no test failed with it.

## Findings

### new-1. The brief and the design name the outcomes differently

What happened: the brief's `{:granted, lease}`, `{:queued, position}`, `{:refused, reason,
retry_at}` against the design's `{:ok, lease}`, `{:queued, request_id}`, `{:refused, {reason,
retry_at}}`.
Why: the brief took the research report's names; the design follows the repository's rule that
every outcome is `{:ok, …}`, `{:refused, reason}` or `{:unknown, reason}`.
What I did: the design's shapes, with `{:queued, %{id: request_id, position: n}}`; the design's
text says so.
What the design should change: nothing, if the operator agrees; a later brief should use these.

### new-2. The macOS memory-pressure level needs a program `Programs` does not know

What happened: the level is a `sysctl` read, and `Shell` is the only module that starts
programs, from the paths `Programs` lists, which has no `sysctl`.
Why: the design names the level as macOS's signal, but not how it is read.
What I did: the `Scheduler` takes `{:level, read}`, a function the embedding app gives;
`caf_example` reads no pressure where `/proc/pressure` is absent, so the laptop runs on the
static capacities alone.
What the design should change: either add `sysctl` to `Programs` on macOS, so the example reads
the level, or decide that the laptop keeps the static count only until the workstation move, as
the research suggested.

### new-3. An account with no reading has room

What happened: "a request is admitted only while both have room" does not say what an unread
window is.
Why: an account has no reading until a session on it draws its status line; refusing on no
reading would refuse the first start on every fresh account, forever.
What I did: no reading, or a window the reading lacks (it drops once its reset passed), counts
as room; a spent reading stays spent until its reset, however old, since a window's share only
grows. The design's "A lease" says so.
What the design should change: confirm it.

### new-4. The account as a resource is not wired

What happened: the design says the account is a resource with several entries, chosen when a
start is admitted. `Delegates` still picks the account (`Usage.pick/2`) before it asks, and the
example declares `seat` and `slot` only.
Why: a per-account session count needs a capacity per account, which no measurement or
decision gives yet, and the pick by allowance already chooses the account.
What I did: nothing beyond the allowance gate on the picked account.
What the design should change: decide whether an account is a capacity (`account:<name>` units
in each start) or only a gate, and say so in "Accounts".

### new-5. No resource checks the fencing token yet

What happened: the design says a resource the lease guards (a heavy slot's script, a lock file)
carries the lease id and refuses a holder whose id is not current. No such resource exists in
the framework yet: a delegate's seat is counted, not locked.
What I did: the id grows strictly (`AUTOINCREMENT`) and is handed in the lease.
What the design should change: name the first fenced resource with `Jobs`' wiring (a heavy run's
slot), and model the check there if it is a protocol.
