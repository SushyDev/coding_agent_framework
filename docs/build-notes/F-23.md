# F-23: `Recoveries`, its core and its first recovery: a lost viewer

## What was built

- **`Agent.tla`, before the code**: the reopen is now two steps of `Recoveries`, as the code
  takes them. `RecoveryClaims(a)` writes a claim on the loss the sampler read, only when that
  loss is not claimed yet and the reading came after `Recoveries`' own last open of `a`'s
  viewer. `RecoveryActs(c)` opens the viewer, and starts the server when it is down.
  `RecoveriesRestart` drops what `Recoveries` held in memory; its table stays. `SamplerReads`
  names a new loss when an agent was not unviewed in the last reading, or when a viewer was
  opened for it since. New invariant `OneReopenPerLoss`, new `Break` `ClaimInMemory` (claims
  kept in memory: a restarted `Recoveries` opens a second viewer for one loss), new
  configuration `Agent_Claims` (restarts of `Recoveries`). `ServerRestarts` lost its fairness:
  only the reopen starts the server again, as in the code. The old `ViewerReopened` and
  `ViewerReopenRefused`, which spent a reading, are gone.
- **`Recoveries`**: a `GenServer` the caller's tree starts per host, with no name, given the
  host's `TmuxSampler`, `Control`, `Holder` and tmux server. `check/2` (or `cue/1`) reads the
  sampler's last good sample, ends the open states it verifies, then claims and acts on new
  ones. A lost viewer is a held socket, live, read `:unviewed` or `:no_server`. Its key is
  `lost_viewer:<socket>:<generation>:<since>`, where `since` is when the sampler's row took that
  status, or when `Recoveries`' last open of that socket answered with a pane, whichever is
  later (`lost_viewers/2`, pure). The row is written as `claimed` before the act. The act is
  `Sessions.viewer/4`. A refused open ends `failed`; otherwise the first sample whose reads
  began after the act decides: `recovered`, `failed{not_viewed}`, or `unknown` (socket gone or
  stale, an unknown open, or `interrupted` for a claim with no act). It does nothing while the
  newest sample failed.
- **`Recoveries.Table`** (`recoveries`, unique per `{host, server, key}`: the claim) and
  **`Tracker.RecoveriesSource`** (kind `:recoveries`, under the sampler's scope).
- **`Sessions.viewer/4`**: the viewer open (one row short, then grown) made public, so a
  start and a reopen share it. A pane that opened but did not grow answers
  `{:unknown, {:resize, pane, why}}`.
- **`TmuxSampler`**: `read_at` is now taken before the reads, so a sample stamped after an act
  was read after it.
- **Tests** (`test/recoveries_test.exs`, real tmux 3.8 and dtach, `cat` held): a killed viewer
  is reopened once, the same reading claims nothing more, the next sample verifies it, and a
  second loss is a new state, with a `Tracker` view of both rows; a killed server comes back
  with the viewer at `%0` of generation 1; a claim left by a `Recoveries` that ended before
  its act is never acted on and ends `unknown{interrupted}`; an open refused (the host's
  `Control` gone) leaves a `failed` row and the agent live; a stale socket is not reopened.

## What I ran into

- The first spec change made `Agent_Viewers` take 551 s and `Agent_Writes` pass 27 million
  states. The loss counters and a set of claims multiplied the states. Flags relative to the
  current loss (claimed, reopened, and an in-memory claim marked `old` once a newer loss is
  read) say the same thing in far fewer states. `PaneLookedUp` now runs only while a write is
  left to ask, so a configuration with no writes does not vary `kept`. `Agent_Viewers` runs
  with no writes (writes are `Agent_Writes`' job), and `Agent_Writes` with two starts; F-1's
  bounds moved to `slow/Agent_WritesLarge`.
- In an Elixir `for`, `x = expr` is a filter as well as a binding: a `nil` dropped every state.

## Findings

### new-1. A loss is not named by the socket and the server's generation alone

What happened: a viewer killed twice within one server generation gives the same socket and
generation both times, so the second loss would read as already claimed and never be reopened.

What I did: the key adds `since`, the time the sampler's row took its status (its
`written_at`), or `Recoveries`' own last open of that socket when later. A viewer opened and
lost again before the next sample leaves the row unchanged; the open is what tells the two
losses apart. The model says the same in `SamplerReads`.

What the design should change: name a loss as "the socket, from the sample where its row
took that status, or from the last open answered with a pane".

### new-2. A viewer that opens and dies at once is reopened once per sample, with no bound

What happened: an open that answers with a pane proves a viewer existed, so the next unviewed
reading is a new loss. If the viewer dies at once every time (a viewer command that exits as it
starts), each sample brings one more open.

What I did: nothing beyond the rule; the rows show each attempt.

What the design should change: bound the losses per socket in a window (for example, a third
loss of one socket within a minute ends `failed{repeating}` and waits for a person).

### new-3. A claim with no act is never acted on, so the liveness needs no restart there

What happened: a `Recoveries` that ends between its claim and its act leaves that loss claimed.
Acting on it after the restart could act twice, so it ends `unknown{interrupted}` and the agent
stays unviewed until its next loss. `LostViewerReopened` fails under such a restart.

What I did: `Agent_Viewers` checks the liveness with no restart of `Recoveries`;
`Agent_Claims` checks `OneReopenPerLoss` with two. The spec's header says so.

What the design should change: say that an interrupted recovery ends `unknown` and is shown to
a person, or that the act is made idempotent (tmux refuses a second session of the agent's
name, which would let a restarted `Recoveries` act once more on a claim with no recorded act).

### new-4. `Recoveries` acts on the last good sample whatever its age

What happened: `TmuxSampler.rows/2` answers the last good sample with its `age`. `Recoveries`
skips a check while the newest sample failed, but acts on an old one when the sampler simply
stopped sampling.

What the design should change: give a recovery a maximum age of the sample it acts on.

### new-5. The code names states more finely than the model

What happened: a row going from `:no_server` to `:unviewed` (another part started the server
with no viewer for this agent) is a new state in the code, and one more open; the model's
`unviewed` merges the two.

What I did: noted it in the spec's header.

What the design should change: nothing, unless a person wants a server start by another part
to count as the same loss.

### new-6. `READING.md` does not describe the new steps

What happened: this unit was to touch only `Agent.tla` and its configurations in `model/`.

What the design should change: `model/tla/READING.md` should name `RecoveryClaims`,
`RecoveryActs` and `RecoveriesRestart`, and `Agent_Claims`.

## Second commit: two samples agree, repeating losses, a sample's age, naming a loss

The main agent decided four things after the first commit; this commit adds them, model first.

### What was built

- **`Agent.tla`**: a reading is now the panes, then the sockets (`Reading`). `SamplerReads` has
  nothing between the two reads; `SamplerReadsAround(a)` has a start's viewer of `a` open
  between them, so the reading is wrong for `a`. A start now opens its agent's viewer
  (`Sessions`): in the hold's step, or, up to the new constant `MaxLateViewers`, later in
  `StartOpensViewer`. `RecoveryClaims` claims only a loss the last two readings read
  (`agreed`); a restart of `Recoveries` forgets its last reading (`fresh`). The third claimed
  loss since a person's clear goes to `repeating` with no act, until `PersonClears`.
  `LostViewerReopened` now allows "waits for a person": the bound changes the reopen's
  fairness, and the header says so. New invariant `NoActOnOneWrongReading`, new `Break`
  `OneReading` (acts on one reading and reopens a viewer that was not lost), new configuration
  `Agent_LateViewers`.
- **`Recoveries`**: acts on a state only when the last two samples it checked (two `read_at`s)
  both read it. Options `max_age:` (30 s), `window:` (60 s) and `clock:`. A sample older than
  `max_age` claims nothing and writes a `skipped` row with `reason` `{:sample_too_old, age}`; a
  later claim takes the row over. A third loss of one socket in the window, counted since the
  last clear, ends `failed{repeating}` with no act, and that socket's losses are not claimed
  until `Recoveries.clear/2`. An act refused because the session exists ends
  `unknown{session_exists}` and counts as an open for naming the next loss (new-7).
- **`Recoveries.Table`**: `cleared_at`; `skip/4`, `losses/5`, `repeating/3`, `clear/5`;
  `last_opened/3` also takes `exists` opens and clears.
- **`TmuxSampler`**: a `clock:` option, so a test shares one clock with `Recoveries`.
- **Docs**: `design.md` names a loss, the maximum age and the bound, in "Multiple hosts"
  (new-1). `READING.md` has an `Agent.tla` section (new-6).
- **Tests**, real tmux and dtach, `cat` held, a counter clock that ticks one ms per reading and
  that the test jumps: one sample alone is not acted on; a viewer opened after two samples
  agree ends the state `unknown{session_exists}`, and its loss is then a new state; a third loss
  in the window waits for a person, with two losses before a jump of 61 s not counted, and
  `clear/2` lets the next loss be reopened; a sample 31 s old is not acted on and its row says
  why. The older tests now take two samples to claim.

### Findings

### new-7. Two samples in a row can both read a loss a start's viewer ends

What happened: TLC found it in `Agent_Viewers`. A start holds its agent; the first sample reads
it unviewed (true: the viewer is not open yet); the start's viewer opens between the second
sample's two reads, so it reads unviewed too. The two agree, `Recoveries` claims, and tmux
refuses the open (the session exists). If that viewer then dies before the next sample, the
sampler's row never changed, so the loss keeps its key, stays claimed, and the agent is never
reopened: `LostViewerReopened` fails.

Why: two samples agreeing removes one wrong reading, not a true reading followed by a wrong
one.

What I did: an open refused because the session exists ends `unknown{session_exists}` and
counts as an open in naming the next loss (`open` is `exists`; `last_opened/3` takes it). The
model's `RecoveryActs` adds the agent to `opened` whether or not it opens a pane. This is a
small mechanism beyond the four decisions; without it the liveness gate fails. Revert it if the
design wants another way.

What the design should change: say that an act refused because the session exists counts as an
open (it is in the loss's name in "Multiple hosts" now), or have a start's viewer open mark the
loss itself.

### new-8. "The row says why" for a sample too old

What happened: a recovery's row is its claim, unique per key, so a row written for an old
sample would claim the state and block a later act.

What I did: the row is `skipped`, with `reason` `{:sample_too_old, age}`, and `claim/3` takes
over a `skipped` row. A check of an old sample answers `{:refused, {:sample_too_old, age}}`.

What the design should change: name the `skipped` status in the `Recoveries` row, or say the
reason belongs elsewhere.

### new-9. `Agent_Writes` is near the 30 s line

What happened: the new variables raised it from 895,180 states and 18 s to 1,199,123 and 25 s.

What the design should change: nothing yet; the next change to `Agent.tla` should move it to
`slow/` or cut its bounds.

### new-10. The model counts every loss within the window

What happened: time is left out, so a loss is never old enough to leave the window.

What I did: the header says so; this is the case where the bound stops the reopen, which is
what the liveness must allow for. The window's passing is in the tests only.

What the design should change: nothing.

### new-11. `slow/Agent_Restarts` does not finish at its bounds

What happened: two agents, three starts, two server deaths, two writes and the liveness check,
with this commit's variables (`agreed`, `losses`, `wrongOnce`, `starting`), passed 3.9 million
states in 29 minutes with its queue still growing. It was not run in the first commit either.

What the design should change: cut its bounds (two starts, or no writes, since
`slow/Agent_WritesLarge` checks the writes), or split it into a safety run and a liveness run
with one agent. I did not choose.
