## Findings

### new-1. A person's act is taken at the healer's next check, not in the answer's call

What happened: `Watch.answer/5` records `act` and the healer acts when its next check reads
it. So `answer(caf, id, "act")` answers `{:ok, :answered}` before the act, and its outcome is
read from `Acts` (`acts(caf, …)`), not from the answer. Why: the healer owns each remedy's
act, its precondition and its verify; a call from `Prompts` into four healers would have been
four new entry points. What I did: the asynchronous way, which `Heal.tla` models as one step
(a cut between answer and claim leaves no act). What the design should change: say that the
answer's outcome is the act's row in `Acts`, and whether the inbox should show it.

### new-2. A healer cut between a person's claim and its act leaves the row open until the next start

What happened: each healer's `init` closes the claims a restart cut with `Acts.interrupt/2`,
by its own actor. A person's claim has the person's actor, so a crash of the healer process
alone, between that claim and its act, leaves the row `claimed` until the next start of the
framework closes every other start's rows. Why: `Acts` interrupts by actor only. What I did:
nothing. What the design should change: name the healer on a person's claim (its `detail`) and
let `interrupt/2` close by it, or accept the gap within one start.

### new-3. The frame's account makes a short typed row go by a file sooner

What happened: `Typed.short?/2` counts the framed line, so `on <account>` moves a row near
the cut to the pointer path. Why: the cut is on the whole line typed. What I did: nothing; the
pointer path is correct. What the design should change: nothing, unless the cut should count
the text alone.

### new-4. A person's failed verify narrows the list as the healer's does

What happened: `Recoveries.acted/4` hands the person's acted rows to the healer's verify, so
a person's reopen or end that fails its verify drops the kind from the list, as `Heal.tla`'s
`Verify` does for any claim. Why: one verify per act, whoever claimed it. What I did: kept it,
since narrowing is always allowed. What the design should change: say so, or that a person's
act is never verified.
