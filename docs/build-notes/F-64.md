# F-64: a socket apart from its process

## What was built

F-61's new-1 and new-3. In `Start.tla` a socket ended in the same step as its process, so the
socket's half of `RunningSettled` could not fail, a stop judged by its pid alone looked right,
and the model leaned on fairness for a settle the code takes only at a cue.

- **`Start.tla`**, in three commits' worth of one unit:
  - A socket of its own, `sock`. `ProcExits` leaves it live; `DtachExits` (weakly fair) ends it
    once its process has; `DtachKilled` (`MaxHardKills` of them) leaves it stale while the
    process runs on, unheld. A hard kill is assumed to come only once the session file names the
    process (new-2). `Confirms` is the file naming the live process (`Named`) while the socket is
    live. The hold needs a dead socket and no process of the name.
  - `RunningSettled` has a `Break` for each half: `SettleRunningBySocket` (the socket alone) and
    `SettleRunningByLiveness` (pid and start time alone). `NoHeldWithoutRow` now says that no
    process of a name, held or unheld, and no live socket, is without a row that has not ended;
    TLC fails it for each.
  - `StopSettled` reads the socket too (`StopByLiveness` drops it). `StopTimedOut` also ends a
    stop whose process is gone while its socket lives, and marks it late only when its process
    lives.
  - `ReRead` and `Adopt`: a dead socket while the file names a live process is an orphan with
    the file's pid and start time, the design's place for a live process with no confirmed start
    (`ReReadBySocket`, `AdoptBySocket` take it as gone). It is the smallest answer I found that
    keeps every invariant: `running` would leave a row no `/exit` can reach (its viewer needs the
    socket), and waiting would leave the row unsettled while the process runs.
  - Cues (new-3): `cued` is a cue `Sessions` has not handled. The Registry cues at `ProcExits`
    (when a file named the process) and `Kill`, the sampler at `DtachExits` and `DtachKilled`,
    and `FrameworkStarts` settles every row. `RunningSettled` and `StopSettled` with no stop
    under way run only at a cue; `CueIdle` handles one they cannot act on. `NoSocketCue` (the
    sampler sends none) fails `RunningSettles` and the new `StoppingSettles`: a cue met a live
    socket and nothing came after. A cue for a row that is neither running nor stopping, or that
    has a call under way, settles nothing in the model and is dropped (`Cueable`); without that
    `Start_TwoSessions` reached 365 872 distinct states, not 198 724.
  - Two properties read a held start now: `NotGivenUpEarly` (the new unheld orphan may have
    written its file on time) and `RunningOnlyIfLive`, which names the file's live process
    (`Named`), as its text always said: a stopping row whose dtach was killed is running again
    after a restart while its process lives.
  - Six new `Break_Start_*` configurations; every earlier one sets `MaxHardKills = 0` and fails
    as before. The design configurations add `StoppingSettles`; `Start_Restarts`,
    `Start_Worktrees` and `slow/Start_Large` allow one hard kill.
- **`docs/design.md`**: the sampler's job in the parts table; "The delegate protocol" says a
  socket ends apart from its process, what a stop, `ReRead` and `Adopt` do with it, and who
  cues the settles; "The model" lists the new properties. `READING.md` says the same for the
  new steps.
- **`Sessions`**:
  - A stop is done only when its process is gone by pid and start time and its socket is read
    as dead. At its deadline with the process gone and the socket not read as dead, it answers
    `{:unknown, {:socket_live, typed}}` and leaves the row `stopping`, reason `socket_live`.
  - A stopping row with no stop under way settles `stopped` on the same two reads.
  - `confirm/3` reads the Registry for a dead socket too: a file that names a live process is
    `{:unheld, file}`, an orphan with reason `unheld` and the file's pid and start time, for a
    starting row past its deadline and for an unknown row. A delegate's is the pid its wrapper
    recorded, if `ps` lists it, and its orphan keeps no pid (new-3). A dead socket with no such
    file is dead, as before, but only once that read succeeds.
  - A `tmux:<host>:<server>` cue, the one the `TmuxSampler` already sends when its rows change
    (a held socket that goes away or goes stale is such a change), settles every row of the
    host with no call under way. The sampler's code does not change; its moduledoc says the host
    app hands the cue to `Sessions` as well.
- **Tests**, on the stand-ins and real dtach sockets, each killing only pids its stand-ins
  recorded. A wrapper stand-in (`linger/2`) runs the stand-in as a child and, when it exits,
  writes `exited` and keeps the socket live while a file `hold` exists (at most 20 s):
  - "a stop is done only when its socket has ended too": the stop waits past the stand-in's
    exit (announced by `exited`), answers `socket_live` at its deadline, a cue while the socket
    lives settles nothing, and one after it ends settles `stopped`; the name then resumes.
  - "the sampler's cue settles a running row whose first settle met a live socket": the
    stand-in is killed, the Registry's cue meets a live socket and the row stays `running`; the
    socket ends, a real `TmuxSampler` samples, and its cue settles the row `stopped{exited}`.
  - "ReRead and Adopt make an orphan of a dead socket whose file names a live process": rows
    whose sockets are not there while their files name live processes become `orphan{unheld}`
    with those pids after a restart.
  - `new-4` (F-25) now gives its reused-pid row a socket that is not there: with its socket live
    the row rightly stays `stopping`.

## Findings

### new-1. `Adopt` and `ReRead` still lean on fairness, and a cue during a start's call is lost

What happened: the brief asked for the cue to carry `RunningSettles` and the stop's settle. In
the code `Adopt` and `ReRead` also run only at a settle (start, a starting row's deadline, a
cue). In a scratch copy of the spec (not committed) I gated `ReRead` on cues too, with the
Registry cueing at `FileWritten`, and `Start_Restarts` without hard kills and with
`UnknownSettles` alone failed it in 9 steps: a hold answers unknown with nothing held, the start
answers unknown at its deadline, and then no socket ever lives and no file comes, so no reader's
rows change and no cue comes. The code has the same gap today: the row stays `unknown` until a
restart or an unrelated change on the host. A cue that comes while a call is under way is lost
the same way (`settling/3` passes over a row with a call), as when a process exits before its
file and its socket ends while the start waits.

What I did: kept `Adopt` and `ReRead` under the weak fairness of F-17, and said so in the header.

What the design should change: say who settles a row once its call ends. I recommend that a
start or stop that answers without settling its row (`unknown`, or `socket_live`) has `Sessions`
handle one cue for that row at once, a message to itself in `done/4`, and that `Start.tla` gates
`ReRead` and `Adopt` on cues as it now gates `RunningSettled`.

### new-2. A dtach killed before the session file is written leaves a process no reader finds

What happened: dtach does not report the pid it holds, and an interactive session's pid comes
only from its session file. If dtach is killed hard before Claude Code writes that file and the
process runs on, nothing the framework reads names it: `Adopt` refuses the row and `ReRead`
stops it while it runs. `NoHeldWithoutRow` fails in such a model.

What I did: the model assumes a hard kill comes only once the file names the process, and the
header says so.

What the design should change: either check, against each pinned release, that Claude Code ends
when its terminal goes away (SIGHUP, or reads that fail), which makes the assumption hold, and say
so; or hold an interactive session under a wrapper that records its pid before `exec`, as a
delegate's does, so a pid is known from the hold on.

### new-3. A delegate's `pid` file names no start time

What happened: with a dead socket, a delegate's only reading of its process is the pid its wrapper
recorded and the start time `ps` gives for that pid now. After the delegate exited, that pid may
be another program's. The orphan then keeps no pid, since `Recoveries` kills only by pid and
start time and this start time may be the other program's. And such a reuse makes an orphan of a
name whose process is gone, so the name waits for a person. For a resume whose wrapper never ran,
the file still names the previous process.

What I did: a delegate with a dead socket and a pid `ps` lists is `orphan{unheld}` with no pid,
the side that never takes a live process for gone.

What the design should change: the wrapper also records its own start time before `exec`
(`ps -o lstart= -p $$`; `exec` keeps the process and its start time), so the file names the
process exactly, as a session file does, and the orphan can keep the pid and start time.

### new-4. The sampler cues only on a change it saw

What happened: the sampler cues when a sample differs from its last one. A socket it never
sampled live, as of a session that lived less than one sample interval, brings no cue when it
goes. The model's sampler sees every socket that goes away.

What I did: nothing more; the host app's interval bounds it.

What the design should change: name the bound in "Multiple hosts": a settle that met a live
socket is taken again at the sampler's next sample only if that socket was in an earlier one. With
new-1's cue at the end of a call, a start or stop would not depend on it.

### new-5. Test VMs leave held stand-ins behind, and the host ran out of pseudo-terminals

What happened: the second `gate-test` run failed in most apps with tmux's `create window failed:
fork failed: Device not configured`. The host had 527 pseudo-terminals open against
`kern.tty.ptmx_max` 511, and 468 `dtach -n` processes alive under 219 test VMs' directories,
the oldest from the evening before this unit began, 15 to 22 per VM. They hold the `:never` stand-ins of existing tests (`gamma`, `taken`, `blind` in `SessionsTest`, `gone` in
`SessionsHeadlessTest`), none of this unit's. For one of them, the VM's directory, and with it the
`pids` the stand-in recorded, was gone while the stand-in ran on. So `on_exit`'s `kill_all/1` did
not reach them, and nothing can now end them by a recorded pid.

What I did: nothing to them. I cannot tell which VMs were mine, the records are gone, and the
rules forbid a kill by pattern.

What the design should change: the interactive stand-in should end itself after a bounded time,
as "Tests" asks of every stand-in ("sleeps a bounded time"): it reads its input with no bound
today. And the temporary directory of a test VM should outlive its `on_exit` callbacks, or the
stand-ins record their pids where the VM's cleanup reads them before it removes the directory.
