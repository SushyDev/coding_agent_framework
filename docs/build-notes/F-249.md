# F-249: a press's wait in the `Inlet`'s queue is bounded

## What was built

F-245's new-1. The `Inlet` ran every call on its host in its own process, one at a time. A
press queued behind another call waited up to that call's bound, and no bound counted that
wait. Its caller could give up first, with `{:unknown, {:timeout, ms}}` from the call
wrapper, while the queued press could still start later, on a screen that had moved on.
`Sessions`' ask for the press bound at start waited in the same queue.

### The model first: `model/tla/Inlet.tla`

Two callers, a long call and a short one, into one `Inlet`. Each call carries its deadline
and has its own bound. A clock ticks only while nothing in the `Inlet` is due: a run at its
bound must end, a free `Inlet` with a queue must start or refuse its oldest call, and a queued
call at its last start must be answered busy. A run ends at any time up to its bound.

| Configuration | What it checks | States (distinct) | Depth | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Inlet_Tight` | deadlines one tick over each bound: `TypeOK`, `OneAtATime`, `StartsInTime`, `AnsweredByDeadline` | 1 930 | 17 | 3 s |
| `Inlet_Loose` | the same, two ticks over | 2 052 | 17 | 3 s |
| `Break_Inlet_StartLate` | a call starts when its turn comes, whatever its deadline: `StartsInTime` fails | 460 | 15 | 2 s |
| `Break_Inlet_InProcess` | the `Inlet` runs a call in its own process and answers nothing meanwhile: `AnsweredByDeadline` fails | 551 | 12 | 2 s |

The times are at a load of about 28 on 15 cores.

`InProcess` is the reason for the code's one structural change: with the call run in the
`Inlet`'s own process, a queued call's busy answer can only come after the running call ends,
which may be past the queued call's deadline.

### The `Inlet`'s queue

- `call/4`, the wrapper, sets the deadline to the caller's `timeout:` less 1 000 (`@answer`,
  the reply's way back) and waits the whole `timeout:`. So the `Inlet`'s answer, busy or the
  run's, comes before the caller would give up.
- The `Inlet`'s process keeps the queue in its state and runs the oldest call in a task, one at
  a time. The task is linked, as a call run in the process was: a call that crashes ends the
  `Inlet`, and its caller answers the exit unknown, as before.
- Each queued call has a timer at its last start, the deadline less its own bound. A call still
  queued then is answered `{:refused, :busy}` and dropped. A call whose turn comes after its
  last start is answered the same. Neither touches the screen. `:busy` replaces F-217's
  `:expired`, which no caller could see: its caller had already given up.
- A press's own bound is `press_bound_at/2` of the instance's `see:` and its server's
  `Tmux.bound/1`. The other calls' bounds are not counted (new-1), so each starts while its
  deadline has not passed.
- `Inlet.press_timeout/1` is the `timeout:` a press goes with: its bound, 10 000 in the queue
  (`@queue`), and the 1 000 for the answer. `Sessions` keeps it at start, and
  `Sessions.press_wait/1` adds its 5 000 to it.

### `Sessions`' ask at start

`press_bound/1` is answered by the `Inlet`'s own process, at once, outside the queue, so the
ask never waits behind a press. It stays a call, not a pure read. Reading it without a call
would need `Sessions` to hold the `Inlet`'s `see:` and its tmux server's bound, the second
copy of `see:` F-245 removed. With the process free while a call runs, the call costs no wait,
and the one owner stays.

### Tests

On the stand-in, on a real tmux server (`apps/caf_inlet/test/inlet_test.exs`):

- "a press queued behind a long press is busy at its deadline, and never runs": the long
  press's program stopped for 3 s, a short press queued behind it with 1 000 left in the queue.
  The short one answers `{:refused, :busy}` after 1 000 or more and before the long one ends.
  Its pane's dialog reads as before, with the same option selected, and nothing was trusted.
  The long press answers `{:ok, :sent}`. While it runs, `press_bound/1` answers at once: the
  ask `Sessions` makes at start.
- "a write queued behind a short press still runs": a typed write called while a press runs
  waits for it and is typed.

The test probe names the `Inlet` whose task made a write, so the existing tests still check
that each write comes from the `Inlet`. `sessions_dialog_test.exs` checks the new chain of
bounds at 10 s and 2 s.

## Findings

### new-1. The bounds of a typed write, an interrupt, a reload and a look are not counted

What happened: only a press has a counted bound (F-245). The `Inlet` gates a typed write, an
interrupt, a reload and a look by their deadline alone, so one that starts just before its
deadline can still run past it, and its caller then answers `{:unknown, {:timeout, ms}}`.
Why: counted the way a press is, each needs more than its caller gives. A look's two tmux
commands at 6 500 each and the reply's 1 000 come to 14 000, against `Sessions`' 5 000. A
typed write at the tree's 10 s `see:` comes to 43 520, against `Mailbox`'s 10 000. An interrupt
comes to 32 020, against `Delegates`' 30 000. With those bounds in the gate, every such call
would be refused busy. What I did: gated them by their deadline alone, said so in the module
doc, and left their callers' timeouts as they are. What the design should change: count each
kind's bound as F-245 counted a press's, and derive each caller's `timeout:` from it. A look
run in `Sessions`' own process for 14 s would then hold its other calls longer than its 5 000
margin, so those looks may need to move into tasks too.

### new-2. The queue's 10 000 is not measured

What happened: a press may wait 10 000 in the queue before it is answered busy. Why: the calls
ahead of it are normally short (a look, a typed line, a short press), each well under a second
on a host that answers, but none is measured under load. What I did: named it `@queue` in the
`Inlet`, with that reason. What the design should change: measure a busy host's queue, or make
a press's queue allowance a press bound, so one press ahead always fits, at the cost of a
caller wait twice as long.
