# F-276: a lease waits for its caller's deadline, and a request barred by pressure keeps its place

## What was built

- **The model first** (`Schedule.tla`). A new shape, `Barred`: two heavy runs of one module on
  one slot. In it a caller may go at any step while it waits (`CallerGone`: the deadline its
  request carries passes, or its process dies). The grant reads that (`Insert` grants none to a
  caller gone), and the timer or the monitor tells the scheduler later (`Expire` refuses it and
  frees its place). Pressure no longer refuses a request at `Receive`: the request queues,
  keeps its place, and is served in order once pressure eases, or refused at its deadline. Two
  new properties: `NoGrantToGone` (no new lease to a request whose caller is gone) and
  `KeepsPlace` (no grant to a request while an earlier one of its class and module still waits,
  read until the first restart, since a restart loses the queue's order by design). A ghost,
  `before`, keeps who waited when each request first asked. `QueuedIsAnswered` also ends when
  the caller is gone. Two breaks, each failing as it should:
  - `Break_Schedule_GrantAfterCaller`: the grant does not read the caller. TLC's trace: the
    first run takes the slot, the second queues, its caller goes, the slot frees, and the
    second is granted.
  - `Break_Schedule_BarredSkipsQueue`: a barred request is turned away at once and its
    requester asks again later, as an app's poll does. TLC's trace: under `severe` the second
    request asks and is turned away; pressure eases; the first asks and takes the slot while the
    second still waits.
  Only shape `Barred` lets a caller go, so the other shapes keep their state spaces.
  `slow/Schedule_BarredRestart.cfg` is `Barred` with one restart, run by hand.
- **The Scheduler.** A request may carry `wait:` (ms its caller waits for the answer). A queued
  entry's deadline is its class's `max_wait`, or the caller's `wait` when that ends first; the
  first is refused `:deadline` (or `{:pressure, level, reading}` if pressure bars the class
  then), the second `:caller_gone`. A caller that dies while it waits is refused `:caller_gone`
  with a row, where before it left the queue with no row. Before each grant, `serve/1` refuses
  any entry past its deadline (on monotonic time) or whose caller's process is dead, so a grant
  never goes past either while the timer or the monitor message is still unread. Pressure
  refuses nothing at once: a barred request queues, and `serve/1` already skipped barred
  entries. The queued answer is `%{id, position, barred}`, `barred` nil or the pressure that
  bars the class now. `wait:` that is not a positive integer is `{:bad, :wait}`.
- **The callers that wait with a bound** pass it: `Runs.Plug` its `:wait`, `Jobs` its
  `:lease_wait`. `Delegates` and `Mains` wait in their own processes with no bound of their own
  and are unchanged.
- **`caf-run`** prints the whole reason. The plug's refusal line is `refused <word> <time>`, and
  for pressure it goes on with the level and the reading: `refused pressure <time> severe:
  memory full 2.00% over 1.00%` (`level 4 at 4` for a level source). The wrapper prints
  `caf-run: not run: the lease was refused (pressure severe: memory full 2.00% over 1.00%); try
  again after <time>`. A barred request's queued line says why it waits: `caf-run: queued
  (position 1; pressure severe: level 4 at 4)`.
- **Tests.** The Scheduler: a barred request waits, keeps its place against a later one, and is
  granted when pressure eases; a barred request whose caller dies is refused `caller_gone` with
  a row, and the next request takes position 1; a request past its deadline, the caller's or
  its class's, is never granted though its timer has not been read (the Scheduler is suspended
  until both timers are in its mailbox behind the cue that frees the seat); the pressure test now
  reads the barred answer and the pressure refusal at the deadline. `caf-run` prints the queued
  line and the refusal with the level and reading. `Jobs`' test of a caller that ends while
  queued now finds its `caller_gone` row.

## What I ran into

- The first run of the Scheduler's tests crashed `request/3` (`Queue.position/5` found no
  entry). On a loaded host the step that receives a request took longer than a class's 50 ms
  `max_wait`, so the new deadline check in `serve/1` refused the very entry being received, and
  the reply then looked for it. The entry being received has no requester yet; its caller is in
  the call, so it is now skipped by that check, and its timer refuses it later.
- The deadline check first compared the injected wall clock (`clock:`). The design keeps a
  deadline that lives in one process on monotonic time, so a step of the OS clock cannot refuse
  every queued request at once; it is now monotonic, and the test suspends the Scheduler and
  waits for its timers in its mailbox, instead of moving a clock.
- Every request gets a deadline timer, a granted one too, so the test waits for three timers,
  not two.

## Findings

### new-1. `caller_gone` keeps the shape `{reason, retry_at}`

What happened: the brief names the refusal `{:refused, :caller_gone}`. Every other refusal the
Scheduler gives is `{:refused, {reason, retry_at}}`, the design says so, and the callers
(`Runs.Plug`, `Jobs`, `Delegates`, `Mains`) match on that. Why: a bare atom would be the only
refusal of another shape. What I did: `{:refused, {:caller_gone, retry_at}}`, `retry_at` now.
What the design should change: nothing, if the brief meant the reason; else say which shape.

### new-2. The only pressure refusal left is at a barred request's deadline

What happened: once pressure refuses nothing at once, a `{:pressure, level, reading}` refusal
would never be given, and `caf-run` would never show F-273's reading. Why: item 2 and item 3 of
the brief pull against each other. What I did: a request refused at its class's deadline while
pressure still bars its class is refused `{:pressure, level, reading}` with `retry_at` now plus
the window; otherwise `:deadline`. The queued answer names the pressure too. What the design
should change: confirm this, or name the pressure in a `deadline` refusal another way.

### new-3. A caller's deadline equal to the plug's wait races the plug

What happened: `Runs.Plug` passes its own `:wait` as the request's `wait`, and counts its own
wait from just after the call returns. So the Scheduler's `caller_gone` comes a little before
the plug gives up, and the wrapper exits 75 where before it ran without a lease (`unknown
no_answer`). With the defaults (`:wait` 600 000 ms, every `max_wait` 240 000 ms) the class's
deadline comes first and nothing changes. Why: one bound read in two processes. What I did:
nothing more; a refusal is the safer of the two. What the design should change: say whether a
plug whose wait passes should refuse, as now, or run without a lease; if the second, the plug
should pass a `wait` a little past its own.

### new-4. `Delegates` and `Mains` carry no caller's deadline

What happened: both wait for a queued lease in their own long-lived processes, with no bound of
their own; their callers' calls have bounds that the request does not carry. Why: a delegate's
start that is answered after its caller gave up already makes nothing (F-166), so a grant there
is released by its owner. What I did: left them as they are. What the design should change: if a
grant there should also never come after the caller's bound, `Delegates` and `Mains` would
pass the bound of the call that started the wait.
