# F-215: a home belongs to one tree at a time

F-214's new-1: nothing kept two trees from starting on one home at once. `start_link/1` refused
only an instance name already running in the same VM, so two VMs, or two instances that share a
home in one VM, would share `caf.db`, the tmux and dtach sockets, the leases and the tool home.
This unit makes a home one tree's at a time: a tree takes the home before it opens anything in
it and holds it for its life, a second start on a held home is refused and touches nothing, and
a holder's death gives the home back with no person's step. No test started the real Claude
Code.

## The model

`model/tla/HomeLock.tla`, whose header names `start_link/1`, `Home.Lock` and `Store.hold/1`.
Trees start on one home at any time and as often as they like, die at any step, stop, and are
started again. A death does not give the lock back in the same step: the operating system does,
in a later one (`Released`), and a dead tree may be started again before it has. The lock's file
stays after a death.

- Invariants: `OneHolder` (at most one live tree holds the home), `HolderHasLock` and
  `OpenOnlyHeld` (no tree opens the home's database without holding its lock). Liveness:
  `TakenAgain` (once the holder is dead, some starter takes the home).
- `HomeLock_Design.cfg`, three trees: 57 distinct states, 3 s.
- `Break_HomeLock_CheckThenTake.cfg`: the take looks, then takes in a second step. Fails
  `OneHolder` (two trees take it), 3 s.
- `Break_HomeLock_StaleBlocks.cfg`: the lock is a file a death leaves behind, and a file there
  is held. Fails `TakenAgain` (take, die, restart, and the home is held for good), 3 s.
- `Break_HomeLock_OpenFirst.cfg`: a tree opens the database before it takes the lock. Fails
  `OpenOnlyHeld`, 2 s.

My first draft let a restarted tree orphan its old life's lock: the release was a step of the
dead tree, so a restart disabled it and the design itself failed `TakenAgain`. The lock's death
now belongs to the holding process (`gone`), not to the tree.

## The lock: what I chose, and what I ruled out

**Chosen: SQLite's write lock on `<home>/home.lock`**, an empty file of its own, taken by `BEGIN
IMMEDIATE` and never committed (`Store.hold/1`), and given back by closing the connection
(`Store.let_go/1`). SQLite takes the lock with `fcntl`, so:

- the OS gives it back when the VM dies, and SQLite closes it when the connection is closed or
  collected, so a crash leaves nothing a person must clear;
- it is atomic between two takers in two VMs (the kernel's `fcntl`) and in one VM (SQLite keeps
  the locks of one process's connections apart itself, since `fcntl` does not);
- it is on macOS and Linux alike, and needs no program: SQLite is already the framework's store,
  and `Store` stays the one module that names `Exqlite`.

The take is one try. With a busy wait of 0, a held file answers SQLITE_BUSY at once, which
`hold/1` answers `{:refused, :held}`. No statement writes the file, so it stays empty, and no
journal is made. An immediate transaction only takes the RESERVED lock, which only one
connection holds at a time and which a second taker's SHARED lock does not block, so two takers
at one instant give one holder and one refusal; an exclusive transaction could refuse both.

Ruled out:

- **`flock` (devenv's) held by a port for the tree's life.** It meets the rule, but it adds an
  OS process beside each VM, and its lock is given back only once that process has read end of
  file and exited, after the tree's own `:DOWN`. Its release on a normal stop is not in step
  with the tree's end unless the holder waits for the process's exit, and every config's
  `programs` would need `flock` and `sh`.
- **A file made with `O_EXCL`** (`File.open(path, [:exclusive])`), or a pid file: a death leaves
  it, and it blocks for good (`Break_HomeLock_StaleBlocks`). Clearing a stale one is a look at
  the pid and then a take, two steps (`Break_HomeLock_CheckThenTake`).
- **A Unix socket bound in the home**: a dead holder's socket file blocks the next bind in the
  same way. Linux's abstract sockets, which leave no file, are not on macOS.
- **A TCP port picked from the home's path**: the OS gives it back on a death, but two homes can
  pick one port, and any other program can hold it.
- **`:global` or a registered name**: only within one VM or one connected cluster; two VMs that
  are not connected meet nothing.
- **SQLite's busy wait for the take**: measured, it overshoots its bound about fourfold (new-2),
  and a take that waits is a hidden wait. The one wait is the existing `restart_wait`.

## What was built

- **`Store.hold/1` and `Store.let_go/1`** (`caf_store`), with a doctest: the take in one VM, the
  refusal, the release, and a second take.
- **`CodingAgentFramework.Home.Lock`** (`caf_surface`): the holder, a `GenServer` registered under
  a name of its instance's own (`Elixir.CodingAgentFramework.Home.Lock.<instance>`, apart from
  the instance's `Registry` name). Its `init` makes the home's directory, takes the lock and
  writes its record, so the connection lives in its process only. `take/1` answers `{:ok, pid}`,
  `{:refused, {:home_held, holder}}`, `{:refused, {:instance_running, name}}`, `{:refused,
  {:sqlite, reason}}` or `{:unknown, reason}`. It traps exits, so a tree's exit reaches it as a
  message and `terminate/2` removes the record and closes the file before it ends.
  - **The record**, `<home>/home.holder`: `instance`, `os_pid`, `node` and `taken_at`, one `key
    value` per line, written whole and renamed into place. A refusal reads it into a map, or
    `:unknown`. A normal stop removes it; a death leaves it, and the next holder writes over it.
  - **Its life.** Until a tree adopts it, it monitors the start that took it and ends with it.
    The tree's first child is `%{id: :home, start: {Home.Lock, :adopt, [lock]}, restart:
    :temporary, significant: true}` under `auto_shutdown: :any_significant`: `adopt/1` runs in
    the tree's supervisor and links the holder to it. As the first child it stops last, after
    every part, and when it ends for any reason the tree ends, since the home is no longer held.
    `parts/1` names it `:home`.
- **`start_link/1`**: `unique/1`, then `Home.Lock.take/1`, then what it did before (`mkdirs/1`,
  `Home.mark/1`, the programs, …) in `start_tree/2`. A start that took the home and is then
  refused, or whose supervisor does not start, gives it back (`Home.Lock.release/1`). A refused
  take has opened and written nothing: the home's directory is only made when it is missing, and
  a held home has one.
- **`unique/1`** waits for the instance's old holder as it waits for its old `Registry`, both
  within one `restart_wait`. A tree killed whole ends its holder by an exit signal, which the
  holder reads after the parent may already have started the tree again; without this wait that
  restart would be refused `{:home_held, …}` by its own old life.
- **`home_kind/1`** is unchanged and takes no lock; its doc says so.
- **Docs**: `start_link/1`, the module's "Order", `home_kind/1`, `Home`'s module doc (F-214's
  new-3, and that the mark now runs under the home's lock), the design's surface section and
  "Built so far" (the rule, and F-214's new-3), and the README status line.

F-214's new-2 ("database is locked" from two first opens of one new `caf.db`) cannot come from
two starts now: only the holder of the home opens `caf.db`. The race test below checks it.

## What was measured

- Before the code, a prototype in two VMs: a second VM's `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` on a held file was
  refused, `lsof` showed the holder's VM with the file open, and after the holder's VM ended the
  file was free. In one VM: two connections refuse each other, and a closed one frees it.
- A holder process killed with `:kill`, which runs no `terminate`: in 2,000 tries, the lock was
  free every time the killer's `:DOWN` arrived (0 of 2,000 late). The connection is closed when
  the dead process's heap is freed. The holder's normal paths close it explicitly.
- SQLite's busy wait in one VM against a held lock (new-2): a timeout of 50 ms waited 398 ms, 100
  ms 754 ms, 300 ms about 1.55 s, 1,000 ms 4.1 s, and 2,000 ms 7.8 s.
- The new tests: 7 (six in `home_lock_test.exs` and `Store.hold/1`'s doctest). Six runs of the
  lock and home tests, no failure. With `hold/1`'s `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` replaced by `SELECT 1` (no
  lock), the three tests that expect a refusal fail (one VM, a second VM, the race); the three
  that expect a take after a stop or a death pass, as they do with no lock at all.
  - A second start on a held home in one VM: refused at once (under `restart_wait`), the record
    names the holder, no file under the home changed, b's name runs nothing, and `home_kind/1`
    answers while the home is held.
  - A normal stop removes the record and leaves the empty lock file, and another start takes it.
  - A tree killed whole and started again at once by its own name takes the home.
  - A holder killed alone ends its tree (`:shutdown`), its record stays, and another start
    takes the home.
  - A second VM (`:peer`) takes the home as a start's first step does; a start here is refused
    and its record names that VM's OS pid; after `kill -KILL` of that VM and its pid gone, a
    start here takes the home.
  - 25 new homes, each with two starts sent at one instant: each time one tree and one
    `{:home_held, _}`, never "database is locked".
- Gates: see below.

## What I ran into

- A connection the holder does not keep is collected, and with it the lock: in the first
  prototype a VM that "held" the file held nothing, because the connection was bound to an
  unused variable and collected before the sleep ended. So the holder keeps the connection in
  its state, and no other process gets a copy of it.
- The surface tests that list every part's key now list `:home`.
- Run alone, the race test (25 trees started and stopped just before its VM ends) makes the
  VM's `erl_child_setup` print "failed with error 32 on line 284" a few times, most likely a
  stopped tree's reaper watcher (`sh`) still exiting as the VM goes. The gate runs print the
  same line 6 to 14 times, from the `caf_mains`, `caf_sessions`, `caf_mailbox` and
  `caf_delegates` tests, which this unit does not touch, and once from `caf_surface`. No test
  fails on it. I did not run the gate on `main` to count it there.
- The kill of a whole tree logs its `Registry`'s exit, as the earlier kill tests do; the new
  test captures it.

## Findings

### new-1. A refusal in the instant after a take can name the holder before

What happened: the holder writes its record right after its take, and there is no way to read
the lock's holder from the lock itself. A start refused in between reads the record a dead
holder left, or none (`:unknown`). Why: the lock is the OS's, which keeps no name, and the
record cannot be written before the take without a loser writing over the winner's. What I
did: documented it in `Home.Lock`'s module doc and the model's header; a normal stop removes
its record, so only a death leaves a stale one. What the design should change: nothing, unless
a refusal's holder is used for more than a person's reading. If it is, the record could carry
the holder's OS pid and the reader could check that it lives.

### new-2. SQLite's busy wait runs about four times its bound

What happened: with exqlite's busy handler, a busy timeout of 300 ms waited about 1.55 s and one
of 2,000 ms 7.8 s, in one VM against a lock held by another connection. `Store.open/3` sets 5
s for every writer, so a writer that meets a held lock may wait about 20 s before its
`{:refused, …}` or its raise. Why: I did not narrow it; exqlite's handler sums its own table of
delays by the retry count, and the time it sleeps is longer than the table says. What I did:
the home's take uses no busy wait. What the design should change: decide whether `Store`'s busy
timeout is a bound the design relies on (a part's call timeout under it, say). If it is, measure
it on Linux too and bound the wait by the clock rather than by SQLite's handler.
