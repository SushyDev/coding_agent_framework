# F-245: a press's bounds have one owner

## What was built

F-242's new-2, new-3 and new-4. After F-242, `Sessions` took `press_see:` and the `Inlet`
took `see:`, separately. A caller of a press (`answer_dialog/5`, `answer_menu/5`,
`press_limit/4`) used a fixed 25 s unless it passed a timeout. And `Inlet.press_bound/1` gave
the tmux commands 16 s, though six of them at 5 s each can take 30 s.

### The `Inlet` owns `see:`

`Sessions` no longer takes `press_see:`. As it starts, it asks its `Inlet` once for its press
bound (`Inlet.press_bound/1`, a call), keeps `[timeout: bound]`, and passes only that with
each press. A press takes no `see:` of its own any more (`Inlet.choose/5` dropped it), so the
reads and the bound both come from the `Inlet`'s one `see:`. `choose/5` now requires
`timeout:`, so no press runs under the library's 15 s default. A `Sessions` that cannot read
its `Inlet`'s bound stops with `{:inlet, reason}`. With no `Inlet`, no press goes.

The inbox (`Prompts`) dropped `press_see:` and its `press` bound. The tree passes
`Config.press_see` to the `Inlet` alone, and `Limits` gets `Sessions.press_limit/3` with no
timeout.

Why ask, not be given the same value at start. Being given it means `Sessions` keeps an option
(`press_see:`, or a bound) that an app can set apart from the `Inlet`'s. That is the pair the
brief says must not stay configurable. Asking keeps one value, in one process, set by one
option. Neither choice uses global state: the answer comes from the `Inlet` instance that
`Sessions` was handed, so two trees in one VM each read their own. The cost is one call at
start, which the next section's queue caveat also applies to (new-2 below).

### Derived all the way out

Each bound that waits on a press is the one below it plus a stated margin. None of them is
set apart from `see`:

| Bound | Derived as | At `see` 10 000 (default) | At `see` 2 000 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| one tmux command, `Tmux.bound/1` (CLI: `Shell.bound/1` of the server's 5 000 timeout) | timeout + 500 (TERM to KILL) + 1 000 (the killed program's exit) | 6 500 | 6 500 |
| one read of a press | `see` + 20 (the pause between captures) + one command (a capture started just before the deadline) | 16 520 | 8 520 |
| the `Inlet`'s press, `Inlet.press_bound_at/2`, the `timeout:` `Sessions` passes | 2 reads + 4 commands outside them + 1 000 (its own work and reply) | 60 040 | 44 040 |
| `Sessions`' call, `Sessions.press_wait/1`, asked through `press_call/2` | the `Inlet`'s + 5 000 (calls ahead in `Sessions`, and the reply) | 65 040 | 49 040 |
| the inbox (`Prompts.answer/5`), `CodingAgentFramework.answer/4`, the operator's `answer_startup` | the call above: they run in the caller's process and add no bound | 65 040 | 49 040 |

The operator's MCP tool call has the client's tool timeout above all of these (an hour by
default). The old numbers were 36 000 and 41 000 at 10 s, and 20 000 and 25 000 at 2 s.

Each caller of a press asks `Sessions.press_call/2` first, a read bounded by 5 000, and then
waits what it answers. `Sessions` answers that read while a press runs, since a press goes in
a task. The `timeout:` options of `answer_dialog/5` and `answer_menu/5` are gone, and
`press_limit/4` is now `press_limit/3`, so no caller can name a second number.

### The tmux commands counted

In `Inlet`'s `act/3` for a choice: `pane/2` lists the panes (1), `dialog/5` captures (1),
`move/4` sends the keys (1, or none when the option is already selected), `selected/7` reads
until the marker is on the label, `enter/3` sends Enter (1), and `left/6` reads until the
dialog leaves. A read checks its deadline after each capture, so the last capture can start
just before the deadline and take a whole command: each read is `see` + 20 + one command. That
is six commands. An Escape's press runs four (the list, the capture, Escape and one read).
Each command is one `Shell.run/4`. Its worst case is its timeout plus `Shell`'s 500 ms between
TERM and KILL and its 1 000 ms wait for the exit status, so `Shell.bound/1` names that and
`Tmux.CLI` answers it. `Tmux.bound/1` is a new callback; the fake answers its own call timeout.

### Tests

- `sessions_dialog_test.exs`, "the bounds that wait on a press derive from the Inlet's see, up
  to the caller": the table's numbers at 10 s and 2 s, each from the one below plus its margin,
  and a live `Sessions` over an `Inlet` at 10 s answering `press_call/2` 65 040. With no
  `Inlet`, 5 000.
- "a press past the old fixed caller bound is answered within the instance's": an `Inlet` at
  `see: 30_000`, the held program stopped for 26 s, past the old 25 s caller bound.
  `answer_dialog/5` with no options answers `{:ok, :sent}` after 26 s or more. Before, the
  default 25 s would have answered `{:unknown, {:timeout, 25000}}`.
- "a press past all its bounds is unknown from the Inlet's read, not a caller's": an `Inlet` at
  `see: 500`, the program stopped for 3 s. The answer is the innermost bound's,
  `{:unknown, {:not_selected, …}}`, the mark `unknown`, and nothing trusted.
- F-242's test that gave `Sessions` its own `press_see:` is gone with the option.
- `inlet_test.exs`: the two late-redraw tests start an `Inlet` at their `see:` and pass its own
  `press_bound/1`; every `choose/5` passes `timeout:`. The doctests of `press_bound_at/2`,
  `press_bound/1` (51 040 over the fake at 10 s: 2 × 10 020 + 6 × 5 000 + 1 000) and
  `Shell.bound/1`.

## Findings

### new-1. The `Inlet`'s queue is in no bound

What happened: the `Inlet` serializes every write and press on its host in its own process.
A press queued behind another press, or behind a typed write, waits up to that one's bound
before its own work starts. Neither the press bound nor `Sessions`' 5 000 margin counts that
wait. Why: the queue belongs to the `Inlet`, and its length is not known when a caller starts
to wait. What I did: nothing. A request still queued at its deadline is refused `:expired`
untouched, and one that starts late but runs past its caller's bound answers `{:unknown,
{:timeout, ms}}` from `Inlet`'s own call wrapper, never an exit. `Sessions`' ask at start waits
in the same queue (15 s), and a `Sessions` restarted while its `Inlet` runs a 60 s press stops
`{:inlet, {:unknown, {:timeout, 15000}}}` and is restarted by its supervisor. What the design
should change: decide whether a press's bound should cover one press queued ahead (twice the
press bound), or whether the `Inlet` should refuse a press while another runs.

### new-2. A stop's exit press waits under the stop's own bound

What happened: a stop or a main session's end of a row on a dialog presses "No, exit" (F-231)
and waits for that press before it reads the process. `Sessions.stop/3`'s call waits its
`timeout:` (30 000 by default) plus 30 000. At the default `see`, the press bound is now
60 040, so a press that runs to its every bound can outlast that call, which then answers
`{:unknown, {:timeout, 60000}}`. `Mains.end_main` passes its own fixed bound too. Why: the
brief's chain is the press calls (`answer_dialog/5`, `answer_menu/5`, `press_limit/3`) and
their callers; a stop is not one. What I did: nothing, to keep the unit to its concern. What
the design should change: derive a stop's call bound from `press_call/2` too, or let a stop's
own deadline cut the wait on its exit press (the press stays unknown, and the stop goes on to
SIGTERM).

### new-3. The library default bound grew from 20 s to 44 s

What happened: at the library's 2 s `see`, a press's bound was 20 000 and is now 44 040, and a
caller's 25 000 is now 49 040. Why: the old number fit two 2 s reads, not six tmux commands at
their own 6.5 s each. A press ends as soon as each read sees its keys, so the bound costs time
only when the host or tmux hangs. What I did: counted the commands as the brief asks. What the
design should change: nothing, unless a shorter bound is wanted; then lower the server's tmux
timeout, which the bound follows.

### new-4. `Shell`'s kills are not bounded

What happened: `Shell.bound/1` counts `Shell`'s two waits after a timeout, but not the two
runs of the local `kill` program (`System.cmd`, no timeout) that send TERM and KILL. Why: they
run on this machine and end at once. What I did: left them out and said so in the doc. What the
design should change: nothing, unless a local `kill` that hangs is a case to cover.
