# F-289: a Mailbox call answers within its bound under load

## What was asked

On a host at load average 20 to 45, `Mailbox` calls answered `{:unknown, {:timeout, 5000}}`:
`message` twice (its row was stored), and a start's `launch/4`, which then marked its row late
(F-284). F-284's new-3 found the cause: `Mailbox` is one process, and a settle pass read every
open row's transcript in it, so one pass held every call past a caller's 5 s. The unit measures
that, moves the reads out of the `Mailbox` process, models the change, and tests that
`message`, `launch/4` and `ended/3` answer within the bound while the rows still settle.

## What was built

- **A pass reads in a task** (`Mailbox.settle/2`, `reads/4`, `applied/2`). The snapshot is taken
  in `Mailbox`: the open rows, which rows to read, which addressees may be marked. A task then
  makes every call to another part: `Stream.read` (the first pass only), `Stream.rows`,
  `Acts.rows` for a forced end, each sending row's transcript (`Transcripts.read` and
  `Transcripts.text`), and for an addressee whose first row is due, `Intake.waiter?` and the
  turn read (`Registry`'s session files). Its answer comes back as a message. `Mailbox` applies
  it to its table as it is then: the forced ends, the settles, the replies and the marks.
- **A read decides only the row it was read of.** A `waiter`, `typed` or `launch` row settles by
  its transcript only while the row equals the snapshot the task read and no hand-on of it runs.
  A row written since was written by a call or an answer that asked for a pass of its own, which
  reads it again. A mark to an interactive addressee uses the pass's read of the session (its pid
  and session id must still match); one whose first row came due in this pass asks for another
  pass. The stale read costs at most a refusal: `Intake` and the `Inlet` read the waiter and the
  turn again in their own step, as before.
- **One pass at a time.** Cues that come while a pass reads make one more pass after it, not one
  each (`again`, true when it must read waiter rows).
- **The offset a mark keeps** is read by the hand-on itself, before its write, and written back
  with its answer (`sent/3`). Until then no settle reads the row; a restart before it leaves
  `from_byte` nil, so the transcript is read from its first byte, which is correct and only reads
  more.
- **A resume's `launch/4`** marks the row, then reads the session's offset from `Transcripts`'
  memory (`Transcripts.offset/4`, F-261, no file read) in a hand-on task. The task answers the
  caller and writes the offset back, only onto the row as marked and of the session it read.
  The read waits 2 s at most (`@offset`); with none by then, or for a session never read, the row
  is read from the transcript's first byte. A launch for a new session reads nothing, as before.
- **The time a take bound is judged by** is taken before the transcript read, not after it, and a
  fold's `now` before the fold is read (see new-5).
- F-284's `unstarted/2` and `launching?/2` keep their meaning: the snapshot skips a launching row
  until its start's deadline, and the apply decides `unstarted/2` before any evidence.

The model (`Delivery.tla`): the pass is two steps of its own, `Read(i)`, the reader's (the line
in the transcript, the process gone, nothing on its way), and `Apply(i)`, `Mailbox`'s, which
decides by SettleReplay's, SettleExit's and SettleSeen's rules on the values read, only for the
row as read and out of hand, and else decides nothing. A call (`CallIn`, `CallOut`) is answered
in a step of `Mailbox`'s own. `CallInBound` says no call waits through a read.
`Break_Delivery_ReadInMailbox` makes the read `Mailbox`'s step, as before this unit, and fails
`CallInBound`. `Break_Delivery_StaleEvidence` applies a read to a row a forced end settled since,
and fails `UnknownStays`. The variables are Delivery's own (`ev`, `asks`, `late`), so the specs
that instance it pass constants for them and settle in one step, as before. `Read` and `Apply`
are each weakly fair on their own: in the row steps' one fairness they would stand for the step
a row needs (TLC found that, a `Reclaim` never taken). Every Delivery configuration checks
`CallInBound`.

## Measured

`mailbox_load_test.exs`: 20 interactive rows sent by the waiter, each `sending` with no line, a
`tail` stand-in that sleeps 0.4 s before each read, and a transcripts cue. A `message` with a
120 s timeout shows how long a pass keeps a call; then `message`, `launch/4` for a new session,
`launch/4` for a resume and `ended/3`, each with its 5 s bound, during the next pass.

| | before (49a275f, load 35) | after (load 23 to 60) |
|---|---|---|
| a message behind the pass | 17 621 ms | 8 to 22 ms |
| `message` | 5 001 ms, `{:unknown, {:timeout, 5000}}` | 8 to 151 ms |
| `launch/4`, a new session | 5 002 ms, `{:unknown, {:timeout, 5000}}` | 14 to 99 ms |
| `launch/4`, a resume | not measured | 332 to 395 ms |
| `ended/3` | 5 001 ms, `{:unknown, {:timeout, 5000}}` | 8 to 52 ms |
| every row delivered after its line | not reached | 16.8 to 18.4 s |

A pass holds about 0.88 s per row there: two reads of 0.4 s each and the rest. The rows settle
as late as before; only the calls no longer wait (new-1).

The model, distinct states before and after (TLC times at load 30 to 75, so not comparable):
Delivery_Interactive and Delivery_Forced 248 008 to 802 532, Delivery_Wide 2 141 to 6 661.
With `TLC_WORKERS=2`, Delivery_Interactive and Delivery_Forced took 33 s and 36 s at load 13 to 14
on 12 cores, so the 30 s rule did not judge them. With TLC's own worker count they took 13 to 16 s
earlier (new-8).

## Tests

- `mailbox_load_test.exs` (new): the calls above answer within 5 s while a pass reads 20 slow
  transcripts, the resume's launch wrote the offset it read, and every row is delivered by its
  line.
- `mailbox_waiter_test.exs:283` and `delegates_interactive_test.exs`'s `message/3`: each waited
  in `await/2` on a `with` whose other answer, `{:ok, %{status: "sending"}}`, ended the wait at
  once. It passed only because the first call waited behind the start's settle. Each now waits
  for the status it names (new-6).
- `mover_test.exs:192` and `delegates_interactive_test.exs:672` each read a row once, right after
  a cue and a call. The second now waits for the mark. When the move reaches `resuming`, the first
  accepts `delivered` as well as `sending`, since the reader's own transcript cue may have settled
  the row. Its later checks still show the row delivered once and missing from the resume's
  arguments.

## Findings

### new-1. A pass is as long as before, and the rows settle as late

What happened: calls answer at once, but a pass still reads its rows one after another, about
0.9 s a row at a 0.4 s read, so 20 rows are delivered about 17 s after their lines. Under the
load seen (F-284), reads of seconds each would make it a minute.

Why: each row's transcript is one `Transcripts.read` and one `Transcripts.text`, and
`Transcripts` runs them in its own process, one at a time.

What I did: nothing more. The unit's ask was the calls.

What the design should change: measure a pass's length on the loaded host first. If it matters,
`Transcripts.read/3` takes many sessions in one call, so a pass could read all its rows' sessions
in one, and only then each one's text.

### new-2. `Transcripts` and `Stream` answer from memory only between their own reads

What happened: a resume's `launch/4` waits for `Transcripts.offset/4`, an answer from memory,
until `Transcripts` ends the read it is in: 332 to 395 ms with a 0.4 s read. The same holds for
`Stream.rows` behind a `Stream` read.

Why: both run their shell commands in their own process, the shape this unit removed from
`Mailbox`.

What I did: bounded the offset's wait at 2 s, past which the row is read from the transcript's
first byte, so the launch answers within its 5 s.

What the design should change: nothing yet, I recommend. If a launch is measured to wait the full
2 s on the loaded host, the same remedy applies to `Transcripts`: its reads in a task, its
memory answered at once.

### new-3. `reload_plugins/3` still reads in `Mailbox`'s step

What happened: the reload's check reads the turn (`Registry`'s session files, `Intake`) and passes
the waiter in `Mailbox`'s own step, so every call waits for one `Registry` read meanwhile.

Why: F-241 makes it so on purpose: no row may be handed to that waiter between the check and the
pass (`Reload.tla`).

What I did: nothing. It is the operator's act, and rare.

What the design should change: nothing, I recommend, unless a reload is seen to delay calls.

### new-4. A few writes stay in `Mailbox`'s step

What happened: a row too long to paste or to launch with is written to the agent's due directory
(`due_file/4`) in `Mailbox`'s step, and a forced end's settle records its row in `Acts`
(`Acts.record/6`, a call). Neither is a read, and neither was measured.

What the design should change: nothing, I recommend.

### new-5. The take bound was judged by a time taken after the read

What happened: a waiter row's evidence took `now` after its transcript read, and a stream row's
after the fold's. A line that came during the read, with the bound passing during it too, could
make a taken row due again.

What I did: the time is taken before each read, since the code moved there anyway.

What the design should change: nothing.

### new-6. A test's `await/2` on a `with` ends at the first answer of the wrong status

What happened: `with {:ok, %{status: "x"}} = ok <- call, do: ok` inside `await/2` returns the
call's other `{:ok, _}` answer, which `await/2` takes as done. Two tests relied on the call
waiting behind a settle, and broke when it no longer did.

What I did: both match the status in a `case` and answer nil otherwise.

What the design should change: nothing, I recommend; a reviewer can look for the shape.

### new-7. `subscribe_test.exs` can pick a port another process takes

What happened: one `gate-test` run failed `subscribe_test.exs:145` with `:eaddrinuse`.

Why: `free_port/0` opens a socket on port 0, reads its port and closes it. The tree binds the port
later, and on a busy host another test can take it in between.

What I did: nothing. The test is not this unit's.

What the design should change: I recommend that the tree bind port 0 itself and report the port
it got, so nothing can take it in between.

### new-8. Two Delivery configurations may pass 30 s with two workers

What happened: the pass's two steps tripled the state count of Delivery_Interactive and
Delivery_Forced, to about 833 000. With `TLC_WORKERS=2`, each took 33 to 36 s at load 13 to 14 on
12 cores, a load the 30 s rule does not judge.

What I did: nothing. With TLC's own worker count they took 13 to 16 s.

What the design should change: time them on an idle host with two workers. If they pass 30 s,
move one to `slow/` or shrink its constants.

## Gates

- `devenv test`: format, both compiles, `caf.deps` and every test passed. Its model step was
  ended from outside (`Terminated`, at Team_Services) while the host's CPU was needed, so the
  model ran again on its own.
- `TLC_WORKERS=2 bin/check-model`: passed. Every fast configuration is `ok`. Every `Break_`
  configuration's counterexample was found, among them `ReadInMailbox`, `StaleEvidence` and
  F-284's `LostMark`. Earlier, a scratch TLC run of mine took about 6 cores. I stopped it by its
  process id and ran every later check with two workers.
- `devenv shell -- gate-test`, three times: of the first three runs, one passed. The second
  failed `mover_test.exs:192` and `delegates_interactive_test.exs:672`. Each read a
  row right after a call it took as a barrier, the shape of new-6. The third run failed
  `subscribe_test.exs:145`, a port taken by another process (new-7). After the two test fixes,
  three more runs passed in a row.
- The names check found nothing.
