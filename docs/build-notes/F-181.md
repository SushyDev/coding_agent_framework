# F-181: usage limits; fresh readings, the login check and picking; the move modeled

The brief's six items, against `Limit.tla`. The unit ran past 1,000 lines by item 4, so, as the
brief says, it lands the model of the delegate's move and items 1 to 4, and stops before items
5 (the code unit's list in "Usage limits") and 6 (the move and the main's prompt in code). No
test started the real Claude Code; the measurements were made by hand, as the design says.

## What was built

- **The model first** (`model/tla/Limit.tla`): another account (`Others`), the main sessions
  (`Mains`), a reset more than `move_after` away (`far`, set at each `Close`) and another
  usable account with room (`room`, which changes on its own). `Away` decides before the reset,
  from an ended turn or Claude Code's own wait: a delegate's move is claimed on its row (`mv`,
  the act's row in `Acts`) once per limit; a main's `limit` prompt opens instead. `MoveAct` is
  the `Mover`'s one move: ok (the agent runs on the other account, where this one's window no
  longer reaches it), refused or failed (it stays limited and idle, a `limit` prompt), or
  unknown (it may have moved; a prompt, never a second move). A person's `AnswerMove` moves
  either kind. A restart that cuts a claimed move leaves it to a prompt (`Heal.tla`'s `Ask`).
  New invariants `NeverMovesMain` and `MoveOnce`; `GoOnOnce`, `NeverSpends` and every other
  property still hold. `Break_Limit_MoveMain` (a main moved as a delegate is) and
  `Break_Limit_MoveTwice` (the claim not checked: a failed move, a person's "go on", and a
  second move) fail as they should. Configurations `Limit_Move` (an interactive delegate),
  `Limit_MoveMain`, `Limit_MoveHeadless` (two limits) and `slow/Limit_MoveLarge` (a delegate
  and a main on one account). Every earlier configuration sets `Mains = {}` and
  `Others = FALSE`.
- **Fresh readings** (item 2, `caf_usage`):
  - `Usage.Room`, one rule of an account's room: each window `100 - used`, or the whole 100
    once its reset passed or where it is not known; the account's room the tighter of the two;
    spent while a window is full or its limit record is in force; credits never room.
  - No reading goes stale: `Reading.status/2` drops the 6-hour bound, and a window past its
    reset is whole with no session needed.
  - `Usage.Limit` parses a limit record (`quotaLimits`, `rate_limit_info`): a rejection's
    `limited_until`, window and `can_spend` (true only for `allowed` or `allowed_warning`).
  - `Usage` keeps on each account's row `limited_until`, `limit_window`, `can_spend`,
    `evidence` and `source` (`status_line` or `stream`); `take/4` takes a reader's fact (a
    record and windows, with its time and evidence), a later reset replacing the record and one
    read late never shortening it. On each `stream:` cue it reads the host's `Stream` rows and
    takes each headless session's latest `rate_limit_event` once, on the account its transcript
    is under: its windows (a headless session's reading) and its limit. Of the status line's and
    the stream's windows the newer wins.
  - `Usage.Allowance`, the `Scheduler`'s gate, reads `Usage.Room`, so `limited_until` is a spent
    window there (item 5's 6, built).
- **The login check** (item 3, new app `caf_logins`): `Logins` runs `claude auth status --json`
  through the app's `run:` (the surface builds it with `ClaudeCLI.auth_status/2`, the launch's
  own environment, and `Shell.run/4` under `env -i`), parses it (`Logins.Status`), keeps a
  logged-in or refused answer 10 minutes in `logins` and an answer that cannot tell for no time,
  and cues `logins:<host>` at a change of state. `Logins.usable/3` is prepared and logged in. A
  refused login is a `login` prompt in `Prompts` (`login:<account>`, no agent, answered `check`,
  which is `Logins.recheck/3`), from the first refusal until a check finds it logged in. Both
  stand-ins of Claude Code answer `auth status` logged in.
- **Picking** (item 4): `Usage.pick/4` takes `usable:` and answers the usable account with the
  most room, the first named at the same room; with none it refuses `{:none_usable, reasons}`,
  `{:allowance, retry_at}` (every usable one spent, the earliest reset) or `:no_account`.
  `Mains` (a start, and a resume that must leave its account), `Delegates` and the `Mover` take
  `usable:` (in the tree `Logins.usable/3`) in place of `Accounts.login/2`'s reading of which
  files exist, and give it to `Usage.pick/4`. A resume whose own account's login check cannot
  tell is `{:unknown, {:account, own, why}}`: it never leaves its account on an unknown.
- **The tree**: `Usage` after `Stream`, with `stream:`, in `reads/0`; `Logins` after it; the
  topic `logins` in `writes/0` and the cue table.
- **Docs**: "Usage limits" (what was measured, "The move", the remedies' row, what this unit
  built, what is still to measure), "Allowance", the parts and cue tables, the `limit` and
  `login` prompts, `Limit.tla` in "The model".

## What I ran into

- An endpoint answering with an API key gives no usage limit at all; the token makes Claude
  Code a subscriber, whose 429 with `unified-status: rejected` is the limit.
- The first two endpoints ran with no pid kept, and a hook refuses a kill by a pid found with
  `lsof`; they are two local Python listeners on ports 18431 and 18432, in my scratch directory.
  The later ones were started with their pids kept and ended by them.
- `Mains.resume_account/2` matched only `{:ok, _}` and `{:refused, _}`; a login check that
  cannot tell answers `{:unknown, _}`, now its own clause.

## Findings

### new-1. `StopFailure` carries no reset and fires at most sometimes headless

What happened: the design has `Usage` take the `StopFailure` hook's input as a limit record, an
exact fact. Its input has the error and the limit's text, no `resetsAt`, and headless it fired
once in nine runs. Why: the hook reports that a turn ended at an API error; the record is the
transcript's line and the stream's event. What I did: `Usage` takes no `StopFailure`; its
`take/4` takes a record from any reader that has one. What the design should change: name
`StopFailure` evidence of the agent's turn (`Sessions`' `limited`, beside the screen), never of
the account's reset; the account's record comes from the transcript's line (every mode) and the
stream's event (headless).

### new-2. The watchdog's bound needs no setting: the launch keeps its switch out

What happened: the code unit's list sets `CLAUDE_CODE_RETRY_WATCHDOG_MAX_USAGE_LIMIT_WAIT_MS` on
every headless launch so a request fails at once. Unset, a request already fails at once; the
variable bounds a wait only under `CLAUDE_CODE_RETRY_WATCHDOG`, and with that on a request
retries past its bound (`api_retry`, up to 300). Why: the wait is the retry watchdog's, off by
default. What I did: nothing; `ClaudeCLI`'s `env -i` keeps every `CLAUDE_CODE_*` the launch does
not name out. What the design should change: item 1 says the launch keeps
`CLAUDE_CODE_RETRY_WATCHDOG` out (it does) rather than setting the bound; or sets the bound to 0
as well, which changes nothing measured.

### new-3. Claude Code's own wait shows the input box under it

What happened: during the wait the input box is drawn, empty, as at an ended turn, with the
wait's two lines below it. Why: the wait is a notice, not a dialog. What I did: nothing; it is
item 5's `Screen` kind. What the design should change: `usage_limit_wait` is read from its
notice ("Continuing automatically at … · Esc to cancel"), and a screen with it is never an
ended turn: `Mailbox` would otherwise type into the wait. The margin past the reset is above
the 50 s measured.

### new-4. The menu's third option is not always credits

What happened: with credits off the third option is "Upgrade your plan". Why: Claude Code
offers what the account allows. What I did: nothing yet. What the design should change: the
menu's kind reads options by label, and the press of "Stop and wait for limit to reset" needs no
other; the label of the credits' option, where an account can spend, is still to measure.

### new-5. A `login` prompt is an account's, and is told to no parent

What happened: the `login` kind was `Sessions`', from a session whose login expired. The check
before a start or a resume finds it first, for an account, with no agent waiting. What I did:
`Logins` owns the row and the prompt has no agent, so no parent is told; the start or resume is
refused at once with the reason, `{:none_usable, …}` or `{:account, own, {:login, …}}`, and its
caller is told so. What the design should change: confirm; a `Sessions`-opened `login` prompt
(a session that meets Claude Code's own login screen) is the same kind with an agent.

### new-6. A check that cannot tell makes the account unusable for new work

What happened: `claude auth status` may time out or print what is not its JSON. Why: it is a
process, not a reading. What I did: such an answer is `{:unknown, …}`, kept for no time; the
pick passes over that account (it is not usable now), and a resume on its own account answers
`{:unknown, …}` and never moves the conversation. A cold check holds its caller up to the run's
30 s bound. What the design should change: nothing, unless a slow check under load shows; then a
check made ahead (at each account's cue) in place of at the start.

### new-7. The move from Claude Code's own wait

What happened: the operator's rule moves a delegate whose reset is more than `move_after`
away. A five-hour limit's reset is under 24 hours away, so Claude Code waits by itself. What I
did: the model's `Away` moves from the wait as from an ended turn: the `Mover` drains and ends
the process, and the wait with it. What the design should change: confirm that a delegate in
Claude Code's own wait with a reset past `move_after` moves (the model says it may), or keep it
waiting there; item 6 builds whichever is decided.
