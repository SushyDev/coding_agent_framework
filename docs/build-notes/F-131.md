# F-131: R11, `Cues`: the library routes what each part reads

R11 (`docs/design.md`, "The parts", "Order of the first units"; F-115's new-8, F-116, F-102's
new-3, F-127's new-1). The order in which the system settles was a table in the example app
(`@routes`), with a ticker that sent `Jobs` and `Services` a made-up topic and read `Stream`.
It is now the sum of the parts' own declarations, routed by a library part.

## What was built

- **`caf_cues`**, a base app that uses no other app, with one module, `Cues`: one process per
  instance whose only job is routing. It is started with `parts:`, each part's key to its
  declaration `%{topics: :all | patterns, cue: fun}`, and told each part's process with
  `started/3` as the tree starts it (a restart's new process replaces the last). A cue goes to
  every part whose declaration matches its topic, through that part's own `cue/2`. A pattern
  is words with `*` for any one, matched against the topic's first words, so `"stream"` takes
  `stream:<host>` and `"intake:*:*:waiters"` takes the waiters' topic but not Intake's rows'.
  A part's `cue` that raises is logged; the others still get the cue. `routes/2` is the pure
  lookup, with a doctest.
- **Declarations, as data.** `reads/0` in `Sessions` (`registry`, `stream`, `tmux`),
  `Delegates` (`stream`, `transcripts`, `sessions`, `mailbox`), `Mailbox` (`stream`,
  `sessions`, `delegates`, `transcripts`, `intake:*:*:waiters`), `Recoveries` (`tmux`; its
  `cue` now takes the cue as a second argument), the `Tracker` (`:all`) and the example app's
  `Ends` (`sessions`). No part depends on `caf_cues`: the embedding app hands the declarations
  over (`Example.reads/0`).
- **`Mailbox` reads a waiter row's lines only on `transcripts:` cues, the waiters' cue, the take
  bound and its start** (F-127's new-1). `cue/2` casts whether the cue is one of those two;
  any other cue settles the stream and call rows and delivers, and leaves a `sending` waiter
  row as it is.
- **`Jobs` and `Services` cue and settle on their own.** Each takes `notify:` (after each write
  of a row, `%{topic: "jobs" | "services", ids: [key]}`) and `interval:` (its settle of owed
  or cut rows, on `Reader`'s loop). `cue/2` stays, as "settle now"; they declare no topic.
- **`Stream` takes `interval:`** on `Reader`'s loop, so a delegate's `result` is read without
  a ticker, as the design's paragraph on the example app already said.
- **The example app** routes nothing: `@routes`, `route/2`, `tick/1` and `Ticker` are gone.
  `Cues` is its second child, after the reaper (so a restart of `Cues` restarts every part
  after it and each is named to the new one); every `notify:` is `Cues.cue/2` of the current
  `Cues`; `start_part/3` names each part to it.
- **Docs.** "The parts" in the design now holds the graph once, as a table of topic, sender and
  readers, and says why `Jobs`, `Services` and `Stream` read on an interval. README's both
  charts and Status.

Tests: `Cues`' own (routing by pattern, a restarted part, a raising part, no router); in
`caf_example`, "an app that wires only the parts' declarations hands each part every cue it
needs": `Cues` started with `Example.reads/0` and a stand-in per part, a cue of every topic a
part sends, and what each stand-in got against the needs written from the design's table. The
example's whole-tree tests run with no ticker and no hand routing; the late-end test now waits
on the suspended `Mailbox` holding `Jobs`' call, not on a cue the test sends. A `Jobs` test
shows an owed end handed again on the interval with no cue, and its `jobs` cue.

## What was measured

- Gates: `devenv test` passed every gate (gate-test 298 s, gate-model 760 s); `gate-test` three
  more times with no failure (312 s, 348 s, 275 s); `bin/check-model` passed (830 s).
- Lines changed against `main`, this note aside: 442 in `lib` and `mix.exs` (about 100 of them
  the new `Cues` module), 212 in tests, about 60 in the design and README. That is over the
  design's +200, −200 and the 500-line ideal for one unit; the extra is `Cues`' own tests, the
  routing test in the example, a `Jobs` interval test and the design's table.

## What I ran into

- `Jobs`' and `Services`' settles wait on hosts, and no reader samples their sockets (the
  sampler reads the agents' holder only), so "they cue" left open what makes them settle once
  the ticker goes (new-1).
- `Stream` had no `interval:` though the design's paragraph on the example app says it is read
  on the `Reader`'s interval (new-2).
- The waiters' topic shares its first word with Intake's rows' topic, which only the `Tracker`
  reads (new-3).
- The `jobs_test` and `services_test` suites call `cue/2` with a made-up topic to settle at a
  known moment; I kept `cue/2` for that rather than rewrite those suites around the interval.

## Findings

### new-1. `Jobs` and `Services` settle on an interval of their own

What happened: R11 says `Jobs` and `Services` cue and the ticker goes, but nothing cues them:
their jobs and services are held in their own directories on each host, which no reader
samples. Without the ticker an owed late end or a cut start would settle only at a restart.
Why: the ticker was standing in for a reader of those hosts.
What I did: both take `interval:` on `Reader`'s loop and settle on it; both cue their writes
(`jobs`, `services`), which today only the `Tracker` takes and drops. They declare no topic.
What the design should change: confirm it; "The parts" now says so. If a reader of the jobs' and
services' sockets is wanted later, they would declare its topic instead.

### new-2. `Stream` had no interval of its own

What happened: the design's paragraph on the example app says `Stream` is read on the
`Reader`'s interval (R2), but `Stream` read only on `read/2`; the ticker called it.
What I did: `Stream` takes `interval:`, as the readers do.
What the design should change: nothing.

### new-3. A topic pattern rather than a renamed waiters' topic

What happened: `Mailbox` reads `intake:<host>:<account>:waiters` and not
`intake:<host>:<account>`; a first-word rule cannot tell them apart.
What I did: kept the topic the design names, and made a declaration a word pattern with `*`.
What the design should change: either keep the pattern, or name the waiters' topic
`waiters:<host>:<account>` and match on the first word alone.

### new-4. The owner's end of services is still the app's own process

What happened: `Ends` (the call of `Services.owner_ended/3` on a `sessions:` cue) stays in the
example app, because `owner_ended/3` works in its caller's process (F-102's new-5) and so cannot
run inside `Services`' own cue. It now declares its topic like a part.
What the design should change: say whether `Services` should read `sessions:` itself (by
running the owner's end in a process of its own), so that an app wiring only the library's
declarations also ends a dead owner's services.

### new-5. A waiter row whose process went away waits for a transcript cue or its bound

What happened: before, any cue to `Mailbox` read the transcript of a `sending` waiter row, so a
row whose process was gone was made due again at the next cue of any kind. Now only a
`transcripts:` cue, a waiter's cue or the take bound (10 s) read it.
What the design should change: nothing; the bound already limits the wait.
