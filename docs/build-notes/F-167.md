# F-167: B1 part 3, `Prompt.tla`

A model unit: specs, configurations and the design's text; no Elixir. It is the third of B1's
five parts ("Order of the first units"): the `Prompts` box's contract, instancing `Hold` and
`Claim`, with `Dialog.tla`'s answer and `Team.tla`'s ask as its instances.

## What was built

- **`model/tla/Prompt.tla`** (626 lines, 93 of them its header; the design guessed about 300,
  for the row alone). Two parts in one module:
  - **The row**, which `Dialog.tla` and `Team.tla` instance: its status (`none`, `open`,
    `answering`, `answered`, `unknown`, `cut`, `expired`, `withdrawn`, `lost`), its state
    number, its kind and asker (the opener's, read only), and the answers' claims as
    `Claim.tla`'s. Its steps: `Open` (once per owner key), `Answer` (the row read open and the
    answerer rightful before the claim, then marked `answering`; the state number does not move
    while answering, so the claim stays current until the owner answers), `AnswerNow` (claim,
    hand and the owner's answer in one call), `Default` and `Expire` at a deadline, `Hand`,
    `LetGo` and `Refused` (the owner takes the answer, or refuses it), `Settle` (the owner's
    answer or, after a restart, its evidence), `Clear` (a person's), `Withdraw`, `Lose`, and the
    restarts `Cut`, `Readopt` and `CutAndReadopt`.
  - **Alone**, the owners the row hands answers to: Intake's holds as `Hold.tla`'s (a
    subagent's `PermissionRequest`, which the asker's parent may answer, and the `Stop` hold at
    Claude Code's cap); `Team.operate/4` for a `limit`, whose move's outcome may be known or
    not and whose default is the wait; the asker's supervisor for `restarts`. A whole restart
    and the asker's own (`RestartAgent`), which cuts its holds.
  - **The `stop_cap` kind** (decision 10). `blocks` is Claude Code's count of blocks in a row,
    reset by a tool call. Below the cap the app's callback decides a `Stop` hold, in one step;
    the hold whose block would be the last Claude Code honors is a `stop_cap` prompt for a
    person, whose "go on" is that block and whose "no" lets the turn end; past the cap the
    framework blocks no more. Claude Code's override of a block past the cap is the
    environment's act, and `NeverOverridden` says no block of the framework's meets it.
- **Properties**, each with a `Break_Prompt_*` that TLC fails:

  | Property | Says | Break | Configuration |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `OneAnswer` | an answer takes effect once | `AnswerTwice`, `RetryUnknown` | Owners |
  | `OnlyRightful` | every answer's claim is a person's, the app's for its kinds, the parent's for its kinds, or the framework's default | `AnyAnswerer` | Holds |
  | `NotAfterDeadline` | no answer is taken once the deadline passed | `LateAnswer` | Holds |
  | `DefaultOnlyDeclared` | the framework answers only by a default its kind declares | `DefaultAny` | Holds |
  | `KeptAcrossRestart` | a row once opened stays, through every restart | `InMemory` | Owners |
  | `NeverOverridden` | no `Stop` block of the framework's is overridden | `NoCap` | Holds |
  | `PromptsEnd` (liveness) | every prompt shown is answered, expired, withdrawn, or lost with its owner's request | `NoEvidence` | Owners |
  | `Escalated` (liveness) | a budget run out is a prompt, and its answer starts the agent process again | `SilentGiveUp` | Owners |

  Beside them, checked and not broken here (their Breaks are `Claim.tla`'s and `Hold.tla`'s):
  `OneClaimPerState`, `NoActOnCutOffClaim`, `ClaimsEnd`, `OneResponse`, `LateDropped`,
  `OnlyNamed`, `HoldsEnd`.
- **`Prompt_Holds.cfg`** (the permission prompt and the `stop_cap` prompt, `Cap` 2, one tool
  call, one restart of either kind) and **`Prompt_Owners.cfg`** (the limit and the restarts
  prompt, a budget of one, two restarts), and nine `Break_Prompt_*.cfg`.
- **`Dialog.tla`'s answer is an instance** (`P`). Its row's mark is the prompt's row: `pressing`
  is `answering`, `sent` is `answered`, `unknown` and `cut` are the prompt's, a cleared press is
  `open` again; the press tasks are the claims `P` holds (`pending` is `P`'s `acting`), and
  each press is the claim's act. The read opens the prompt, the take is `P!Answer` (by the person,
  or by the app for a kind in `Auto`), the press `P!Hand` or `P!LetGo`, the task's answer
  `P!Settle`, the restarts `P!Cut`, `P!Readopt` and `P!CutAndReadopt` with this spec's evidence
  (`Readopted`), a person's clear `P!Clear`. `AutoAnswer` and `RetryUnknown` are `P`'s
  `AnyAnswerer` and `RetryUnknown`. New here: `OnlyRightful` (`P`'s) and `KeptAcrossRestart`
  (`Boxes.tla`'s: a row on a dialog the pane shows has its prompt waiting), with
  **`Break_Dialog_PromptInMemory`** (`P`'s `InMemory`), which fails it.
- **`Team.tla`'s ask is an instance** (`Q`, kind `question`). `Ask` opens the prompt; `Answers`
  is `Q!AnswerNow`, so the prompt's status is the guard that lets the first answer in, where
  Mailbox's `AnswerRows(i) = {}` was; `AnswerTwice` is `Q`'s. New invariants in `Team_Asks`:
  `AskAnsweredRightly` and `AskClaimOnce` (`Q`'s `OnlyRightful` and `OneClaimPerState`). Two
  variables only, the row's status and the answers' claims: since an answer is claimed and
  acted on in one step, `Q`'s done and acted claims are read from the claims, and its state
  number from their count.
- **Headers**: `Prompt.tla`'s says how its properties imply `Boxes.tla`'s Prompts contract
  (below); `Dialog.tla`'s, `Team.tla`'s and `Boxes.tla`'s say what is now `Prompt.tla`'s.
- **The design**: "The model" gains a paragraph on `Prompt.tla`; B1 says its third part is built.

### How `Boxes.tla`'s Prompts contract is implied

`Boxes.tla` assumes of Prompts its `pr` (a dialog's prompt) and `rp` (a `restarts` prompt), and
checks `OneAnswer`, `KeptAcrossRestart` and `Escalated` on them. The mapping, argued in
`Prompt.tla`'s header and checked where a spec holds both sides:

- `pr`'s statuses are the row's, `Boxes.tla`'s `unknown` being the row's `cut` (a restart cut
  the press; a person clears it). `Boxes.tla`'s `Answer` is `Answer` then `Hand`, its `Told` and
  `SettlePrompt` are `Settle` and `Readopt` by the owner's evidence, its `Clear` is `Clear`.
- `OneAnswer`: `Prompt.tla`'s `Took <= 1` for owners whose evidence is exact; for the press,
  whose keys may be on their way, `Dialog.tla`'s `AtMostOnce` (presses sent, once more only
  after a person's clear) implies `Boxes.tla`'s presses landed, as F-163 argued.
- `KeptAcrossRestart`: `Prompt.tla`'s (no restart writes a row but to settle it) and, checked in
  `Dialog.tla` now, `Boxes.tla`'s own form, a row on a dialog the pane shows with its prompt
  waiting; `Boxes.tla`'s keys in flight are the press's `unknown` there.
- `Escalated`: `rp` is the `restarts` row waiting; the same property, checked in
  `Prompt_Owners`.

Where they differ: after a refused press `Boxes.tla` opens the prompt again, and `Dialog.tla`
now withdraws it, since the dialog never shows again (new-3). None of `Boxes.tla`'s properties
rests on it: each is about a row still on its dialog. I left `Boxes.tla`'s state as it is.

## What I ran into

- **`Prompt_Holds` passed on its first run**, so I checked the reach above before trusting it.
- **The hold steps lacked a guard on the held prompts**, and `Prompt_Owners`, which has none,
  threw on `Hold`'s functions; each now names `p \in HeldPs`.
- **`Dialog_Ask` did not end** (new-3): a refused press opened its prompt again, and each write
  moves the row's state number, so a person answering again and again made new states without
  end. TLC was past nine million states after ten minutes. The refused press now withdraws the
  prompt; with it the spec has 57,752 states.
- **The app's callback after a person's clear.** The old mark `cleared` kept the callback from
  answering a press a person had cleared; the prompt's status is `open` either way, so the
  callback's guard reads the row's `cleared` field as well.
- **`Team_AnswersTwo` is at the fast set's bound.** With six new variables (the row, its state
  number and four of `Claim`'s) it took 32 to 35 s against the baseline's 28 to 31 s under the
  same load; with the two it has now, 31 s against 28 to 33 s. Its 26 to 27 s on an idle machine
  (F-164) is about where it stands; I could not time it idle.

## Findings

### new-1. A person's "go on" at the cap is the last block Claude Code honors

What happened: decision 10 says the `Stop` hold, at Claude Code's cap of 8 blocks in a row,
hands the agent to a person as a `stop_cap` prompt, whose answer goes to Intake's hold: go on,
or let the turn end. A "go on" given to the hold is a block. If the prompt opens at the hold
past the cap, Claude Code overrides that block, so "go on" would do nothing.
Why: the cap counts blocks in a row with no tool call between; a block past it is overridden
whoever gives it.
What I did: modeled the reading that holds: the prompt is the hold whose block would be the
last one Claude Code honors (the 8th), so a person's "go on" is that block. If the agent stops
again with no tool call between, the framework blocks no more and the turn ends.
`NeverOverridden` checks it, and `NoCap` (the app's callback deciding every hold) fails it.
What the design should change: say that the `stop_cap` prompt is the hold of the cap's last
honored block, and that past it the hold passes and the turn ends, recorded `capped`; a person
who wants more sends a message, a new turn. The measurement F-162's new-11 left (that the cap
is 8, and whether a waiter's wakes count toward it) still comes before the `stop` hold is built.

### new-2. The design's statuses lack `unknown` and `cut`, and its liveness lacks `lost`

What happened: "Prompts" lists the statuses `open`, `answering`, `answered`, `expired`,
`withdrawn` and `lost`, and its rules name an `unknown` answer, kept and never sent again, and
a restart's settle "as a dialog's press is settled today", which is `cut`: once more after a
person's clear. Its liveness is that every prompt is answered, expired or withdrawn.
Why: the two unknowns differ. The owner's own unknown is never sent again; a press a restart
cut may be sent once more after a person who saw it did not land clears it. And a held request
whose holder died is `lost`, which nothing answers.
What I did: `Prompt.tla` has both, `unknown` (a person resolves it to answered or withdrawn)
and `cut` (a person's clear opens it again, or says it took), and `PromptsEnd` ends in
answered, expired, withdrawn or lost.
What the design should change: add `unknown` and `cut` to the statuses, and `lost` to the ends
of the liveness.

### new-3. An owner's refusal says whether its request still waits

What happened: "Prompts" says an owner's refusal sends the prompt back to `open`. In
`Dialog.tla` a press is refused only when the pane no longer shows its dialog, and it never
shows it again. Back to `open`, the prompt could be answered and refused without end: with the
row's state number moving at each write, TLC ran past ten minutes on `Dialog_Ask` (12,493
states before).
Why: "refused" covers two cases, an owner whose request still waits (answer again) and one
whose request is gone.
What I did: `Prompt.tla`'s `Settle` and `Refused` take the status the owner says: open again,
or `withdrawn`, `expired`, `lost`. `Dialog.tla`'s refused press withdraws its prompt.
`Boxes.tla` still opens its prompt again after a refused press; none of its properties rests on
it, and I left it.
What the design should change: "refused (back to open)" becomes "refused: back to `open` while
the owner's request still waits, else withdrawn". `Boxes.tla`'s `PressRefused` may follow when
it next changes.

### new-4. The rightful agent is the asker's parent, as the design says

What happened: the brief named the rightful answerer "a person or the app's decider, never
another agent outside the asker's subtree". The design says a person, through the app, for
every kind, and the asker's parent, through `steer`, for `permission`, `plan` and
`elicitation`.
Why: the parent is outside the asker's own subtree, so the brief's words read literally would
leave the parent out, or let in the asker's own children.
What I did: modeled the design: the asker's parent, only for those three kinds, and for a
subagent's request the parent of the session it runs in (the subagent is not an agent of the
tree); the app's decider for the kinds the app chose. `AnyAnswerer` lets a sibling answer a
permission prompt, or the parent a `stop_cap`, and fails `OnlyRightful`.
What the design should change: nothing, unless the parent's ancestors should answer too.

### new-5. `Team.tla`'s `AnswerClaimed` is not yet an instance

What happened: "Prompts" says `Team.tla`'s `Ask` and `AnswerClaimed` become `Prompt.tla`'s
instances; the brief named the ask only.
Why: a held request in `Team.tla` is an id in `held`, with no row of its own; making it a
prompt needs a row per request and the parent's answer as `Prompt`'s `Answer` under Grants'
claim on the child's state, a second claim beside the prompt's.
What I did: made the ask an instance and left `AnswerClaimed` as it is; `Team.tla`'s header and
B1 say so.
What the design should change: nothing; a later unit (with B6's `permission` kind) makes each
held request a `permission` prompt of `Q` and the parent's answer its `Answer`, with Grants'
claim kept as the edge check.
