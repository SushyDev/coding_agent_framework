# F-277: a `sending` row that never settles blocks every message behind it

## What was seen

An operator's message to a main session was typed by the `typed` transport. It was a short text,
framed and marked, so longer than `Transport.Typed.short/0`, and typed as a pointer to its due
file. Its row went `sending` with `sent_at` and `from_byte` set, and stayed so for 22 minutes.
Five later rows to that main stayed `queued`. After the app restarted, the row was settled
`delivered` within a second, and the next row was typed at once.

## Diagnosis

The row stayed `sending` because the typed settle waited for an ended turn, and no settle ever
read one.

- The evidence was there from the start. The transcript's user line with the pointer and the
  row's uuid was written 0.3 s after the write, after `from_byte`. So the confirm by text was not
  the problem: a pointer line carries the mark as a short line does, and `Waiter.seen/2` finds
  it.
- `Transport.Typed.settle/2` answered `:keep` for a seen row while the session file did not read
  idle (`ended: false`). Settles run only at cues and at the take bound's one timer. The timer
  fired 10 s after the write, inside the turn the line started.
- The app answers the session's Stop hook itself. Its intake log for the 20 minutes shows four
  Stops. Two were answered with a block at once, and the turn went on. Two were passed, and a
  prompt followed in the same second, or a cue came before the file read idle. In the idle gap
  of about 5 minutes after the last pass, nothing cued `Mailbox`.
- One row in flight per addressee (the design's rule) then held every row behind it. A waiter
  connected at each Stop, but `Mailbox` had no row it could hand on, so the app's own answer
  went instead, and the turn went on again.
- The restart did not cause it, and its rules held. At the start, `:read` arms each sending
  row's bound and settles at once. The session read idle then, so the row was delivered. The
  hand-on task died with the old node, and no row is left to it: the row is settled by its
  evidence, never typed again.

## What was built

- `Delivery.tla`: `SettleSeen` delivers a seen typed row at once, whether its turn has ended or
  not, as it does a waiter's and a launch's. A new `alone` step, `Continued`, under the constant
  `Continues`, lets the app answer a connected waiter itself so the turn goes on. The four
  instancing specs pass `Continues <- FALSE`.
- `Delivery_Continues.cfg`: an app that answers every turn's end, a long first row typed as a
  pointer, and every invariant and liveness property. 1 946 states, all hold.
- `Break_Delivery_SettleAtIdle.cfg`: the guard as the code had it. TLC finds a lasso in which
  row 1 is in the transcript, the turn ends, the waiter connects, the app answers it, and the
  turn goes on, for good: `SendingSettles` fails. That is this incident's shape.
- `Transport.Typed.settle/2` is `Transport.Waiter.settle/2`: the same evidence settles both.
  `Mailbox`'s `typed_seen/2`, the turn read that fed `ended`, is deleted. The doctest now
  shows a seen row delivered while its turn runs.
- `docs/design.md`'s transport table says when a typed row settles and why.
- A typed line is sized to the pane's real width in the `Inlet`'s write step, so a short
  message goes whole, not as a due file (new-4).

What holds the queue now: a typed row waits for its line until the take bound passes, 10 s
after the `Inlet`'s answer by default. With no line by then it is due again, first, under its
same uuid. The bound runs from `sent_at`, kept on the row, so a restart keeps it. The
exceptions are the design's own: a row whose line is only an `enqueue` is kept until the take,
and a transcript that cannot be read decides nothing (new-1).

## Tests

  user line delivers the row. The row behind it is typed once the file reads idle.
- "a typed row left sending by a restart is settled by its line": once the `Inlet`'s answer is

## Findings

### new-1. A transcript that cannot be read keeps a row with no bound

What happened: no case of it was seen. But `Typed.settle/2` and `Waiter.settle/2` answer
`:keep` for `seen: :unread`, past any bound. `:unread` is any failed read of the transcript:
the account's directory, `Transcripts.read/3` or `text/5` refused or timed out. A transcript
that stays unreadable keeps the row `sending`, and the queue behind it, for good.

Why: the design says a transcript that could not be read decides nothing. The row may have gone
in, so a retry could deliver it twice.

What I did: nothing. The brief asked for "unknown, then retried"; the design's typed and waiter
rules give due again past the take bound only when the transcript was read and shows no line.

What the design should change: past a stated bound of unread evidence (say the take bound
times 30, 5 minutes), settle the row `unknown` with reason `unread`, never resent, and tell its
sender as the wait bound does. That needs `UnknownWhy` to name the reason, and a Break where an
unread row waits for ever.

### new-2. A retry with no line is retried for ever

What happened: the brief says a row is retried once. The design says a row with no line past
the take bound is due again, first, under its same uuid, and sets no count. A row Claude Code
never takes is typed again every take bound, and holds every row behind it, since a row due
again goes first.

Why: `Delivery.tla`'s `Take` is strongly fair, so the model never meets a text that is never
taken.

What I did: nothing beyond the design's rule. The third test checks one retry, then the queue.

What the design should change: on the second bound with no line, `again` already 1, settle the
row `undelivered`, with reason `no_take`, and notice its sender as `WaitBound` does. Model it
with a `Take` that may never come, and a Break that retries for ever.

### new-3. An app's own Stop answer and a due row compete for the same waiter

What happened: at each Stop the app answered the waiter with its own block, because `Mailbox`
had nothing it could hand on.

Why: one row in flight per addressee. With the settle fixed, the next row is free at the next
cue, but an app that answers a waiter at once can still take the waiter first.

What I did: nothing.

What the design should change: say in "the app's Stop answer" which goes first when both a due
row and the app's answer want the same waiter. The recommendation is the due row, by asking
`Mailbox` before the app answers.

### new-4. A framed message of more than 3 characters was typed as a pointer

What happened: after the restart, the operator's 4-character message to the same main went as
a due file's pointer. So did the 13-character one before the restart.

Why: a typed line held at most a fixed 76 characters, the box of an 80-column pane, frame and
mark included. `From operator (message): ` is 25 characters and ` [delivery <uuid>]` is 48, so
73 were taken before the text. Any text over 3 characters went as a pointer. The pane is
seldom 80 columns: `Sessions` draws it at the `viewer` size, 200 by 50 by default.

What I did: the line is now sized to the pane's real width, in the `Inlet`'s own write step.
`Inlet.type/4` takes the line as a function of the limit it reads there, the pane's width less
four. `Mailbox` frames the text and the pointer line first, and the function types the row
whole when `Transport.Typed.fits?/3` says it fits, or else writes the due file and answers the
pointer. The file is written only when the pointer goes, and the width cannot change between
the choice and the keys. The fixed `Transport.Typed.short/0` is gone. Label and mark stay.

What this gives: at 200 columns, 123 characters of text from a 25-character frame; at 400,
323. Tests: "a 4-character message is typed whole into a 200-column pane"; "a 300-character
message is typed whole into a 400-column pane, a longer one by file", where the 400-character
row goes as a due file and is delivered by its pointer's user line.

What the design should change: "a few hundred characters always typed" holds only on a pane of
about 380 columns or more, with the app's `viewer` at `{400, 50}`, and only while no person
attaches at a narrower size. Two ways to widen it, for the main agent to pick:
set the `viewer` default to `{400, 50}` (a constant; 2.1.322 is read at 400 columns already,
`main-idle-400`), or capture 2.1.322's wrapped box with `bin/capture-screen` (a person at a
logged-in terminal) and teach `Screen` and the `Inlet`'s check to read it, which F-240's new-1
already asks for. The first is the smaller change; only the second holds at any width.

### new-5. A bracketed paste would type a row of any length, and settle it by its transcript line

What happened: the main agent reports that an embedding app once delivered every message into
the interactive session by bracketed paste, for any length and any number of lines: the text
loaded into a named tmux buffer from stdin (`load-buffer -b <name> -`), pasted bracketed with
its newlines kept and the buffer dropped (`paste-buffer -p -r -d`), then one Enter. Claude
Code draws it folded, "[Pasted text #1 +N lines]", but submits all of it, and the
transcript's user line carries the whole text and its `[delivery <uuid>]` mark. Not measured
here.

Why it matters: the typed line's limits (one line, the pane's width less four, no control
character) come from reading the text back in the box before Enter (F-132, F-113's new-1). A
paste is not read back as text: the box shows the fold. The confirm is the transcript's user
line with the mark, which is already what settles every typed row (`SettleSeen`), and is
firmer than the screen.

What I did: nothing. This unit keeps the typed line's sizing and the settle.

What the design should change, if the main agent takes it:

- "A session with no waiter": the `Inlet`'s write becomes the paste. Its checks before the
  keys stay (the turn by `turn:`, an idle screen with an empty box). The check after the keys
  becomes "the box shows a paste fold" in place of "the box shows exactly the text", which
  needs a captured 2.1.322 fixture of the fold and a `Screen` state for it. The limit and its
  refusals (`too_long`, `bad_text :control`) go, except a size bound on the buffer.
- The due file and its pointer stay only past that bound, a stated size such as the launch's
  `prompt_max` (F-155), so they serve very large rows only. `--add-dir` stays for those.
- The wake line (F-240) and `wait_bound` mostly go: they exist for a row the box cannot hold.
- `Delivery.tla`: `typed` and `Unknown` keep their shape, since the write is still one act
  whose answer is sent, refused or unknown, settled by the transcript. `NoLongTyped` and the
  `Wake` and `WaitBound` paths shrink to rows past the bound. A paste is one write in tmux but
  two commands (load, paste) and an Enter, so `Inlet.tla` and `Agent.tla`'s `WriteAsked` need
  the buffer named per write and dropped by the paste, with no other writer's paste between.
