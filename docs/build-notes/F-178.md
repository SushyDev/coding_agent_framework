# F-178: usage limits, modeled

A model unit: a spec, its configurations, its composition in `Boxes.tla` and the design's text;
no Elixir. It takes an account's usage limit as the account's state, with bounded remedies, and
lists what the code unit after it builds ("Usage limits" in the design).

## What was built

- **`model/tla/Limit.tla`**, with its header naming what it models: `Usage` (the account's
  record), the `Scheduler`'s gate on it, `Sessions`' turn state `limited` with the remedy's phase
  on the agent row and the checked press of the menu, `Mailbox`'s word to go on, `Intake`'s
  `StopFailure` and `quota_auto_resume_*` hooks (read as the turn and the screen they report),
  and `Screen`'s two kinds.
  - **The environment**: one account whose window closes up to `MaxLimits` times and resets
    (time reaching the reset is fair); agents, interactive or headless, whose running turns end
    at a closed window and write the record; Claude Code's own wait (shown when the reset is
    under 24 hours away and fewer than `AutoMax` continuations ran in a row; it goes on at the
    reset, or goes stale); the menu otherwise; a person at the viewer, bounded by `MaxOutside`
    (an Esc, a menu's option, a typed prompt, an exit) and a prompt's answer; a headless
    process that ends; another start asked for; a restart of the framework.
  - **The framework**: `Read` takes any agent's record as the account's (limited until its
    reset) and marks the agent `limited`; `Look` reads the screen; the `usage_credits` prompt
    where the account can spend, its default the stop; the checked `Press`, sent once, whose
    outcome may be unknown; `Unsure` reads where an unknown press went; `Abandoned` and
    `Overdue` watch Claude Code's own wait; at the reset `GoOn` writes on the agent row that a
    word is owed and counts it, `Send` stores it in `Mailbox` under a key of the agent and the
    limit, and `Sent` takes `Mailbox`'s answer; `Deliver` hands it into an ended turn, or by
    `launch` as a resume's prompt under a lease; `Continued` clears `limited` once a turn ran;
    past `Bound` go-ons in a row, a `limit` prompt.
- **Properties**, each with a `Break_Limit_*` that TLC fails:

  | Property | Says | Break |
  |---|---|---|
  | `NeverSpends` | the framework presses the credits only as a person answered | `DefaultSpends` |
  | `NoStartOnLimited` (action) | no lease is granted while the account's record says limited | `NoGate` |
  | `NoResumeOnLimited` | no word's resume is admitted while limited | the same gate as `NoStartOnLimited` |
  | `GoOnOnce` | at most one word per agent per limit, none where its own wait went on | `NoKey`, `IgnoreOwnWait` |
  | `MenuPressedOnce` | one press per menu shown, only on the menu, once more only after a person's clear | `RetryUnknown`, `Unchecked` |
  | `BoundedRetries` | the framework's go-ons in a row never pass `Bound` | `NoBound` |
  | `NoAgentLeftLimited` (liveness) | every `limited` agent goes on or is a person's prompt | `TrustOwnWait` |
  | `RecordRead` (liveness) | every limit record is read | |

- **Configurations**: `Limit_Interactive` (no credits, two limits, two outside acts, a
  restart), `Limit_Credits` (an account that can spend), `Limit_Headless` (two limits, an exit,
  a restart), the eight `Break_Limit_*`, and `slow/Limit_Large` (an interactive and a headless
  agent on one account that can spend).
- **`Boxes.tla`** composes the contracts that change: a constant `MaxLimits`, the account's
  record `acct` and a count `limits`; `HitLimit` (a running turn of the addressee ends at the
  limit and the record says limited) and `ResetLimit`; the `Scheduler`'s gate on `Start` and
  `Resume`; `Mailbox`'s mark and `Sessions`' hand into a `limited` turn only past its reset; a
  `limited` turn that neither ends by itself nor sticks, and may be stopped (a move at a limit).
  New `NoStartOnLimited` and `NoRowIntoLimit`, with `Break_Boxes_StartOnLimited` and
  `Break_Boxes_MarkIntoLimited`, in a new `Boxes_Limit`. Every other Boxes configuration sets
  `MaxLimits = 0`.
- **The design**: a section "Usage limits" (what Claude Code does, the evidence and its one
  reader, the account's state, the agent's state, the remedies and their bounds, what is a
  person's, the code unit's list, what is still to measure); the `Prompts` table's `limit` row
  and a new `usage_credits` row; the symptom `limited`; `Usage`'s row; B6's kinds; `Limit.tla`
  in "The model".

## What I ran into

TLC found five faults in my first drafts; each fix is the design's now.

- **The go-on's count after the store.** The word was stored first and the agent row counted it
  after; a restart between the two lost the count, and the agent went on once more than its
  bound. The agent row now says that the word is owed, and counts it, before `Mailbox` stores it
  (principle 2: the row before the act); a restart between the two sends it again, and the key
  stores nothing more.
- **The in-flight store named no limit.** `Mailbox`'s answer of a word for one limit, taken after
  the agent had hit the next one, moved the row past the new word, which was then never stored.
  The answer now carries the limit it is for.
- **Acts on a stale record.** Three counterexamples had one shape: the framework acted on the
  record it had read while a newer one was unread. A press decided for one menu landed on the
  next; a late read made an unknown press pressable again; and a word went at a reset to an
  agent whose own wait had already gone on and hit the next limit. Every act now requires every
  record of the agent read first (`Current`), and a late read keeps an unknown press unknown.
- **A word after the agent went on.** A person's prompt ran a turn between the decision and the
  store, and the word was stored anyway. `Send` now checks that no turn ran since the limit.
- **A person's answer to the `limit` prompt** after an unknown press is a clear, as a startup
  dialog's is: the menu may be pressed once more. `MenuPressedOnce` says so.
- The model's ghost of the go-ons in a row first counted at the store, where the row counts at
  the decision; a person's answer between them split the two. It counts at the decision now.
- **`slow/Limit_Large` with two outside acts** was past 2.8 million distinct states and growing
  after six minutes, with its liveness still to check, so I stopped it; with one outside act it
  takes 660 s.

## Findings

### new-1. Where the account can spend, the menu cannot be pressed at once

What happened: the brief asks the framework to press the menu's "stop and wait" once, and to
leave usage credits to a person through a prompt. The credits are an option of that menu: once
"stop and wait" is pressed, the menu is gone, and no documented command offers them again.
Why: Claude Code offers credits only on the menu it shows at the limit.
What I did: where the account's record says it cannot spend (`overageStatus` as measured here),
the framework presses at once. Where it can, it opens a `usage_credits` prompt first, whose
default at its deadline is the stop, and presses the answer. Credits come only from a person's
answer (`NeverSpends`; `Break_Limit_DefaultSpends`).
What the design should change: nothing more; the code unit sets the prompt's deadline (a
placeholder option, as the gates' thresholds are) and measures which `overageStatus` values
mean that credits are on, since only `rejected` was seen.

### new-2. The `limit` kind has two openers, one with a default and one without

What happened: "Prompts" gave the `limit` kind the default "wait", taken at its deadline. A
`limited` agent that needs a person (past its bound, a menu still shown after an unknown press,
a wait still shown past its reset) must stay a person's prompt: the default would leave it
limited with no one told, which `NoAgentLeftLimited` forbids.
Why: a deadline and a default are a prompt's, not its kind's.
What I did: `Sessions` opens its `limit` prompts with no deadline; the `Scheduler`'s, for a start
refused at a limit, keeps the wait as its default. The table says both.
What the design should change: B6's unit for the `limit` kind builds both openers;
`Prompt.tla`'s `Prompt_Owners` models the `Scheduler`'s and needs no change.

### new-3. Claude Code's own wait needs a margin, which is a timer

What happened: the framework cannot tell a wait that will go on late from one that never will
(`quota_auto_resume_stale` may not fire) except by time.
Why: Claude Code's wait is inside its process, and the only evidence is its screen and its hooks.
What I did: past the reset plus a margin, a wait still shown is a `limit` prompt, never a word
typed into it: the timer only asks a person (`Overdue`).
What the design should change: nothing; the code unit measures how long after the reset a
wait goes on (the notification's time against `resetsAt`) and sets the margin above it.

### new-4. `Boxes.tla` checks the limit for safety only

What happened: `Boxes_Liveness` takes 26 s under load, close to the fast bound, so the limit is
composed in a configuration of its own, `Boxes_Limit`, with the safety properties and
`NoStartOnLimited`.
Why: a `limited` turn adds states to every interleaving of the boxes.
What I did: `Limit.tla` checks the liveness of the remedies (`NoAgentLeftLimited`); `Boxes.tla`
checks that the gate and the mark hold against a dialog, a stop and the restarts.
What the design should change: if the composed liveness is wanted (a `limited` turn's row
delivered once its reset passed), a `slow/Boxes_LimitLiveness` configuration in the code unit.

### new-5. A headless request may block for hours and read as a running turn

What happened: with `CLAUDE_CODE_RETRY_WATCHDOG_MAX_USAGE_LIMIT_WAIT_MS` above the reset, a
headless request blocks until the reset, and its turn reads as working: no record, no
`limited`, and the lease held.
Why: the watchdog waits inside the request, and nothing is written while it waits.
What I did: the code unit's list sets it explicitly on every headless launch, to the value that
makes a request fail at once, which it measures first ("Still to measure").
What the design should change: nothing more, unless the measurement shows that no value makes
it fail at once; then the headless turn needs a reading of its own, which is a finding there.
