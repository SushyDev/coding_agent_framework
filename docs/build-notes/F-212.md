# F-212: a move's resume deadline bounds every unknown of the resume

The brief's two items: F-211's new-1 as the app decided it. No test started the real Claude
Code.

## What was built

- **The model first** (`Move.tla`). A variable `unlaunched`: the resume's launch answers
  unknown for now. That covers the app's `launch:`, `Mailbox.launch/4`'s mark and the own
  word's store, all as one.
  - `LaunchUnsure` turns it unknown, or known again, at any time, and may never turn it back
    (no fairness), only with `Unsure`, as `CheckUnsure` does for the check.
  - `Launches == ~unlaunched` guards `LaunchMarked`, `ResumedBare` and `OwnWord`: an unknown
    launch marks no row, stores no word and resumes nothing. `ResumedWith` is not guarded: the
    `Mover` asks the launch in the same step that marks the row, so a row it marked goes with
    the resume.
  - `ResumeRefused(a)`: the check's refusal or unknown past the deadline (`TakenAsRefused`, as
    in F-211), or the check takes `a` and the launch is unknown past the same deadline.
    `MoveBack` and `Stranded` take it. One deadline, `ResumeWaitEnds`, serves both.
  - Fairness: `LaunchMarked` joins the weak fairness on `Resumed \/ MoveBack \/ Stranded`. The
    `Mover` asks the launch in the same step as the check, so the mark is one of the resume's
    steps. Without it, an answer that toggles between asks could hold the resume.
  - `ResumeEnds` is unchanged and holds. **`Break_Move_FlagOnlyWait`** (new) keeps the deadline
    for the check's unknown only. TLC fails it: the launch stays unknown, the check takes the
    own account, and the resume waits for ever.
  - The header's `Mover` and app entries, and `Move_Unsure.cfg`'s comment, say it.
- **The code** (`Mover`):
  - Every unknown of the resume goes through `unanswered/3`. That means `usable:`'s, the app's
    `launch:`, `Mailbox.launch/4`'s, the own word's and `:no_first_line`. Before the deadline
    each is asked again at the step (5 s, one timer per row) and on each cue. Past the deadline
    it is `{:refused, {:unknown_past_deadline, why}}`, and the move goes back or the row is left
    down, as F-211 built it.
  - `why` names the unknown that ran out:
    - `{:usable, _}`: new. F-211 gave the check's reason bare.
    - `{:app_launch, _}`: new. `app_launch/2` tags the app's answer.
    - `{:launch, _}`: `Mailbox.launch/4`'s, as before.
    - `{:word, _}`: the own word's, as before.
    - `:no_first_line`: as before.
  - A row still `sending` (`{:sending, ids}`) is asked again the same way, but it is never taken
    as a refusal (see new-1).
  - Docs: the moduledoc's `resuming` step and failure list, `start_link/1`'s `launch:` and
    `resume_wait:`, the design's `resuming` paragraph, its failure list and `Move.tla`'s entry
    in "The model".
- **The tests** (`mover_test.exs`, and `test/support/mailbox_gate.ex`):
  - `MailboxGate` stands in front of the real `Mailbox` as the `Mover`'s `mailbox:`. It answers
    the requests a test names itself and passes the rest on.
  - A test for the app's launch, and one test each for `Mailbox.launch/4`, the own word and the
    first line.

## Findings

### new-1. A row still `sending` at the resume is never taken as a refusal

What happened: the brief says every unknown of the resume is taken as a refusal past the
deadline. `Mailbox.launch/4` answering `{:refused, {:sending, ids}}` (a launch row a restart cut,
say) is asked again at the step and on each cue, but never taken as a refusal.
Why: `Move.tla`'s `Stranded` waits for no row to be `sending`. A row left down while its first
row is in flight could take that row's turn in no process, or in the next start's. `Mailbox`
makes a launch row due again at its next settle once no process of the row runs
(`Transport.Launch.settle/2`, `gone`), so the wait is bounded by cues, not by a time. Its restart sets no deadline for a launch
row.
What I did: kept the wait and said why in `unanswered/3` and the design.
What the design should change: either nothing (say it is bounded by `Mailbox`'s settle at the
next cue), or give a launch row a deadline of its own in `Mailbox`, and say so in the design.
