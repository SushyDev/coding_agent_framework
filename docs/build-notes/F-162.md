# F-162: the framework as seven black boxes with small interfaces

A design unit. It changes `docs/design.md`, `README.md` and this note; no code and no spec. The
operator found the README's charts too complex for a framework meant to be simple principles
behind small interfaces, and asked for the outside to be designed: a few single-purpose boxes,
each a small interface over a modeled inside, and the smallest surface an agent sees, without
taking power from it. The remodel (F-115 to F-133) made each mechanism exist once inside; this
unit is the shape around them. An addendum to the brief added two more parts mid-unit:
idiomatic OTP (one process per agent, restarts that re-adopt, restart intensity as the healing
budget) and one inbox of prompts. A research survey of deterministic agent mechanisms (durable
execution, formal methods and runtime monitors, multi-agent failures and verifiers, harness
guidance) was read in its notes form; its final report had not landed when this note was
written, and nothing here names its sources.

## What was built

- **The design.** "The parts" now opens with the eight principles, the seven boxes (`Sessions`,
  `Mailbox`, `Prompts`, `Team`, `Scheduler`, `Hosts`, `Watch`) over a base that holds the
  `Ledger`, with each box's interface, the tables it owns, its contract and the apps it holds;
  the agent's surface (seven verbs and the intercepts); metrics and healing; extensions;
  processes and restarts; prompts; and the app's surface. The parts table and all that followed
  it are "Inside the boxes", unchanged. "Concepts" gains "Boxes and verbs". "Order of the first
  units" gains the path, B1 to B13 with F-157 among them, with sizes and what it buys. "The
  tools" says it is the list as built, which B5 serves as the verbs.
- **The README** opens with the eight rules; its overview chart is the seven boxes and their
  few arrows, with a table of their interfaces and a paragraph each on the agent's and the
  app's surface. The parts chart and the mechanisms' bullets move to "Inside the boxes", after
  "Status", and the apps chart is under it, with which box holds each app.

## What this replaces in the design

Everything else the design decided stays. Replaced, each once its unit is built:

- **"One writer per table"** becomes "one writer per row": the `agents` table's rows are each
  written by their own agent process (B4). Every other table keeps one writer, which is one
  writer per row too. A status list still lives once, in `Sessions.Agents`.
- **One `Sessions` process for every agent** becomes one agent process per agent under a
  dynamic supervisor, with per-host and per-account supervisors (B4). The rows, the start
  protocol and every spec's invariants stay; a restart of one agent is the whole restart's step
  for one agent.
- **The tools** ("Agents working together"): 25 tools become the seven verbs (B5), the
  operator's `end_main` and a main session's move and resume go to the app's surface, `run`
  goes after F-157, `reload` after new-2. Every check, claim and answer behind them stays.
- **Three answer functions** (`Sessions.answer_dialog/5` and `answer_startup`,
  `Mailbox.answer/4` for an ask, the operator's answer of a held request) become `Prompts`'
  one `answer/3` and the app's `answer` (B6). Their mechanisms stay where they are.
- **`Recoveries`** becomes the `Watch`'s healer (B8): it acts only by a remedy on the allow-list,
  through `Team.operate/4`, capped, and opens a `remedy` prompt past the cap. Its acts today (a
  lost viewer reopened, an orphan or a late stop ended by its record) are the default
  allow-list, so no behavior changes until the operator narrows it.
- **The `Tracker`** is the `Watch`'s `view/2`; `agents()` is `look(what: :tree)`.
- **Item 14's sentence** that a reviewer and a re-entry brief are an app's policies (new-6).
- **Item 15's library supervisor** is B3, from one config struct.
- **The `Ledger`** (`Acts` and `Cues`) was a box in this unit's first draft; with `Prompts`
  added it moved into the base, so the boxes stay seven. No agent sees it, and the app reads it
  through its own surface.
- **Item 16** in part: status with cost and activity is `look` and the `Watch`'s folds, and the
  urgent message is `message`'s `urgent`.
- **The README's charts**: the parts chart is no longer the overview; the seven boxes are.

## What I ran into

- **The brief asked both to remove resumes and moves from the agents' list and to keep an
  agent's full control of its subtree.** I read the first as the operator's resumes and moves
  (a main session's, which only the operator does, as decided) and kept a parent's resume,
  stop and move of its own delegates in `steer`. See new-8.
- **`pause` and `nudge`** were in the brief's list for `steer` with no mechanism behind them.
  A pause is an interrupt (the turn ends and the agent waits for its next row); a nudge is item
  16's urgent message (interrupt, then the message ahead of every due row, as one checked step),
  so it is `message`'s `urgent` option. Neither adds a mechanism.
- **`start_main`** became `delegate`'s `main:` option: the same verb hands work to another
  project's new main session, which gives no report.
- **Services** needed a verb of their own (`serve`): a native background command ends with its
  session and has no name, host, forward or owner to hand on, so intercepting it would not give
  what a service is.
- **Eight boxes, or seven.** The brief asks for at most seven, and `Prompts` made eight. The
  `Ledger` is the one every box writes and no agent sees, so it went into the base.
- **A per-agent restart budget.** A `DynamicSupervisor`'s intensity counts all its children
  together, so one crashing agent would spend every agent's budget. Each agent process runs under
  a small supervisor of its own, `:temporary` under the dynamic one, so its budget is its own and
  running out of it ends only its supervision.
- **What the research changed.** Variants are refused without a selector (sampling many and
  choosing by chance mostly multiplies cost), and share one budget equally; a reviewer checks the
  brief's objective and runs the checks itself (passing tests alone let wrong changes through);
  `fresh` re-enters from recorded state, not a prose summary; a hook that corrects a call is
  preferred to a bare deny, and hook context states facts, not orders; a `repeating` symptom
  from the hooks' rows, since repeated steps are the most common failure of long runs; a placement
  rule for where a rule goes (root instructions, a skill or brief, a hook's context, a guard);
  the allow-list widened only by a person and narrowed freely; and a crash of one agent modeled
  as a restart step of its own, since faults that show only under composition are the ones
  missed. It also showed that hooks fail open (new-9).

## Decided by the operator during this unit

From the research survey, the operator adopted four mechanisms, now in the design:

1. Every tool call is checked before it runs: policies as the app's data, a correction preferred
   to a deny, each decision a row ("Every tool call is checked before it runs"; built with
   B10).
2. The verbs guard every act by the model's transitions, refused with a reason ("The verbs are
   the model's monitor"; the legal acts per state with B5).
3. The `Ledger` is the only truth, and recorded real runs are replayed against new code in the
   gates (principle 2; B13).
4. `delegate(review: true)`: a fresh reviewer, read-only by its launch, against the brief's
   objective and an acceptance list stored as data (B11).

The brief says decisions 5 to 12 follow; none had arrived when this note was written, so the
design takes no position on them beyond what is above.

## Findings

### new-1. A heavy native command placed on another host has no `fetch`

What happened: the `run` tool's `fetch:` brings named paths back from a host's copy. F-157's
wrapper rewrites a native `Bash` call, whose input has no place for a fetch, and the path says
`run` leaves the list once the wrapper is built.
Why: a native command's outputs land in the worktree because it runs there; one placed on
another host runs in a copy.
What I did: kept `run` until F-157, and B5 leaves it in place.
What the design should change: F-157 decides it. My recommendation: a rewritten command runs on
another host only when the app's placement rule says its outputs are not needed back (a test
suite, a lint); one that writes outputs runs on the workstation's copy and its named outputs
come back as `run`'s do, by a list the app declares per command pattern.

### new-2. `reload` cannot go before a tool list change is measured

What happened: B5 takes `reload` off the agents' list, saying the framework reconnects an
agent's tools when its list changes. The endpoint keeps no MCP session, so it cannot push a
`tools/list_changed` notification, and a headless delegate has no screen for `/mcp reconnect`.
Why: whether Claude Code reads the list again on its own, and when, is not measured on the
pinned release.
What I did: B5 keeps `reload` hidden but served until this is measured.
What the design should change: measure on the pinned release whether a remote MCP server's
list is read again per request or per session; if per session, a list is fixed at an agent's
start (extensions scoped at start, which B12 already does) and `reload` goes only for
interactive sessions, through the `Inlet`'s `/mcp reconnect`.

### new-3. How a launch names an extension's skill is not checked

What happened: B12 writes an extension's skill into the agent's launch. The framework writes
only files of its own that a launch names by flag.
Why: the pinned release's way to load skills from a directory of the framework's own (a plugin
directory flag, or a directory added with `--add-dir` holding `.claude/skills`) is not checked.
What I did: the design says "into the agent's launch" and names no flag.
What the design should change: B12 starts with a check against the pinned release, as F-110
did for the launch's settings, and records the flag in "Rules learned".

### new-4. `updatedInput` changes a call's input, not its tool

What happened: the brief asked whether Claude Code's own `Agent` tool, for a long task, could
run as a tracked delegate. A `PreToolUse` answer can allow, deny, ask or rewrite the input; it
cannot turn an `Agent` call into a `delegate` call.
Why: the hook's decision is about the call it was given.
What I did: subagents stay native, read and counted; for types the app names, the hook denies
with a reason that names the `delegate` call to make, counted as a guard (B10).
What the design should change: nothing now. If Claude Code later lets a hook answer a call with
another tool's result, a delegate behind the `Agent` tool becomes possible, modeled first.

### new-5. Time in a state needs facts the tables do not keep

What happened: metrics fold acts and status writes, but `Store.update/4` changes a row in
place, so a table that keeps one row per thing (`agents`, `leases`, `messages`) has no history
of its statuses. Decision 3 (the `Ledger` the only truth, replayed) needs that history too.
Why: the remodel kept history only in `Acts`.
What I did: `Store` appends a status fact to the table's own history (`<table>_facts`) in the
transaction of each status write (B7), written by the row's own writer so one writer per row
holds, and the `Ledger`'s `query` reads the histories through their owners' metadata; the
`Ledger`'s own `facts` table holds only events from outside.
What the design should change: confirm. The histories keep every row, as `Acts` does; the hot
reads need an index by target and time, and a retention rule is owed once a home's size is
measured (a replay fixture is a run's histories cut out whole, so retention never breaks one).

### new-6. A reviewer and a re-entry brief were the app's; now they are `delegate`'s options

What happened: item 14 said a re-entry brief after a reset and a reviewer with its acceptance
list are an app's policies. The brief asks for them as `delegate`'s `fresh` and `review`, and
the operator decided `review` (decision 4).
Why: both need a protocol over time (the list fixed before the builder starts, a reviewer that
is not the builder, a report that carries the verdict; a start again from recorded state),
which is the framework's to model once.
What I did: item 14's sentence now says the mechanism is the framework's and what the list says
is the app's; B11 models both in `Delegate.tla` first.
What the design should change: nothing further; this replaces item 14's sentence.

### new-7. No apps merge

What happened: the brief allowed apps merged or made private.
Why: the umbrella's per-app dependencies and `gate-deps` keep the layers one way; a merge buys
fewer `mix.exs` files and costs every move of a test and a doc. What a box needs is that nothing
outside it calls in but through its module, which a gate can check across apps as well as
within one.
What I did: B2 adds one facade module per box and teaches `mix caf.deps` each app's box.
What the design should change: confirm. If B2 shows the gate cannot tell a facade call from an
internal one cheaply, merge the apps of a box instead, box by box.

### new-8. What leaves the agents' list

What happened: the brief named moves, ends, dialog answers, resumes and leases as the
operator's or the app's, and also asked that an agent keep full control of its subtree.
Why: a parent's resume, stop and move of its own delegate are on its subtree; a main session's
move, resume and end, and a startup dialog's answer, are the operator's (decided, F-133, F-140,
F-143); a lease is asked by `Team` and `Hosts` for every start and job, never by an agent.
What I did: `steer` keeps a parent's acts on its own subtree; `end_main` and a main session's
move and resume go to the app's surface, and a startup dialog is a prompt the app answers.
What the design should change: confirm, or name the acts the operator meant.

### new-9. Hooks fail open, so a policy enforced only by a hook lapses when the framework does

What happened: decision 1 checks every tool call in `PreToolUse`. The design keeps every hook
at `onFailure: "continue"` ("Intake"), so a framework that is down or slow lets every call
through unchecked.
Why: continuing is right for delivery and observation (a down framework never stops an agent's
work), and wrong for a rule that must never be broken.
What I did: the design says a must-never policy is also a permission deny rule in the role's
launch settings, which Claude Code enforces itself; a call that went on unanswered is the
`unenforced` fact; no supervised launch uses `--bare`, which skips hooks.
What the design should change: decide whether any hook should fail closed (`onFailure:
"block"`). My recommendation: none for now. Every must-never rule that can be written as a deny
rule is one, and the hook only corrects and counts; a rule that cannot be (a heavy command's
lease) is allowed to lapse while the framework is down, which `unenforced` shows. If one must
fail closed, it is modeled in `Intake.tla` as such first.

### new-10. Most prompt kinds need a reading the framework does not have yet

What happened: 3f lists plan approval, MCP elicitation, usage limit, login expired, Claude
Code's exit prompts and onboarding as prompt kinds. Today the framework reads permission
requests (a hook), asks (a row) and the trust and imports dialogs (the pane).
Why: each of the others reaches the framework another way, unmeasured: plan approval and
elicitation may be held hooks or only screens; a usage limit and an expired login show in a
headless log and on a pane differently; whether a subagent's `PermissionRequest` names the
subagent is not checked.
What I did: B6 builds the box with the three kinds read today and the `restarts` kind, and each
other kind is a small unit that starts with its reading checked against the pinned release, as
`Screen`'s fixtures are.
What the design should change: confirm the order; the app's `policy` kind needs no reading and
may come with B6.

### new-11. Claude Code overrides a `Stop` hook that blocks too often

What happened: the survey reports that Claude Code lets a turn end over a `Stop` hook's block
once it has blocked several times in a row with no tool call between. Item 14's `stop` hold for
the app, and any "keep going until" check, would be overridden silently.
Why: a guard of Claude Code's own against a loop.
What I did: the design says the `stop` hold counts its blocks and models the override as an
environment action.
What the design should change: measure the count and whether the waiter's `asyncRewake` wakes
count toward it on the pinned release, before the `stop` hold is built; refuse a block past the
count with a reason, or tell the parent, rather than let it be overridden.

### new-12. One writer per table becomes one writer per row

What happened: 3e asks for one process per agent owning its row and life. The design said one
writer per table, and `Sessions` was the writer of `agents`.
Why: an agent process that owns its life must write its own row, or every change goes back
through one process, which is the starvation 3e removes.
What I did: principle 4 is "one writer per row"; every table but `agents` has one writer for
all its rows, which still holds; `Sessions` keeps the schema and the read side
(`Sessions.Agents`), and the status list lives once there.
What the design should change: confirm. SQLite serializes the writes of several processes on
one file under its busy timeout; B4 measures the write rate of many agent processes on one
file under load before it lands.
