# F-20: Usage follows the "Allowance" section

## What was built

- `Usage.Reading.status/3` replaces `current?/2`: `{:current, used}`, `{:assumed, 0.0}` or
  `:unknown`, from the reading's `seen_at`, its `resets_at`, `now` (seconds) and `max_age`
  (seconds, default `Reading.max_age/0`, 6 hours).
- `Usage.pick/4` takes `max_age` after `now`. It picks the least share used among current and
  assumed readings, a current one before an assumed one at the same share, then the first name.
- `Tracker.UsageSource` rows carry `assumed`. Its `clock:` option (default the system's seconds)
  is the clock.
- Tests: the property over generated readings now covers the age bound (also a generated one),
  assumed readings and the preference; two tests state the bound and the preference exactly.

## Measured

- `gate-test`: all passed; `devenv test` exit 0.

## What I ran into

- `seen_at` is in ms and `now` in seconds. A reading is current while `seen_at + max_age >
  now`, so it expires at exactly 6 hours.

## Findings

### new-1. An assumed reading needs a recent write too

What happened: the design says a reading past its `resets_at` counts as 0 % used, and an older
one counts as unknown. Both can hold. Why: a week-old file is past its reset, and the first rule
alone would make every idle account look empty forever. What I did: the age bound wins, so a
reading older than 6 hours is unknown even after its reset. Design change: say so in
"Allowance", or decide that an idle account's reset reading stays assumed (then a view must
show its age, and `pick` may prefer fresh accounts).

### new-2. A tie among equal shares falls to name order

What happened: the design says "the first name"; the code takes the smallest name, not the first
in `among`. What I did: kept the earlier behavior. Design change: say which, or pass `among`
in priority order and take the first.
