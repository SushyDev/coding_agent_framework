# F-10: the `Tracker`'s core

## What was built

- **`Tracker`**: a `GenServer` the caller's tree starts, one per instance, with no name. It
  takes its sources as `{module, opts}` and reads each in full at start, so a restarted
  `Tracker` comes back from the tables alone. `cue/2` takes a reader's cue (a host app wires it
  to the reader's `notify:`) and re-reads the named rows from the table by id. A cue for a
  source still `:unread`, or one with no list of integer ids, re-reads the whole topic; a cue
  for an unknown topic, or of no known shape, is dropped. `view/2` and `behind/3` re-read each
  source's newest read before they answer (see new-1). A source that cannot open is shown with
  its problem; the `Tracker` still starts.
- **`Tracker.Fold`**: the pure fold. Per `{host, account}`: the sessions, newest first with a
  session with no time last, each with its liveness and its problems; each source's status
  (`:unread` or `:read`), `read_at`, age and problem; and the scope's `age`, the age of its
  newest read, or `:unread`. It is total: a row that is not a map, a column of the wrong type,
  a missing column or a nil timestamp folds into that row's own entry with the problem named.
  `behind/3` names each source `:unread` or older than the bound.
- **`Tracker.Source`**: the behavior a reader's table plugs in by (`open/1`, `describe/1`,
  `read/2`). `Tracker.RegistrySource` is the first: its topic is the one `Registry` cues on, and
  it drops a row of another `{host, account}` that a cue's id names, since ids are global to the
  table. `TmuxSampler`, `Holder`'s sockets, `Intake` and `Transcripts` will each add a module;
  the fold's contract does not change, and a kind other than `:sessions` is kept as raw rows
  until a unit gives it its entry.
- **Cues to views**: `notify` gets `%{topic: "tracker:<host>:<account>", changed: keys}`, keys
  being `{source_topic, id}` or `{source_topic, :source}`, at most once a second per topic by the
  `clock:` given. What changes in between is gathered into the next cue, which a timer sends;
  `timer: false` and `flush/2` let a test drive it with its own clock and no waiting.
- **`Registry.Table`**: `last_read/3` (the reads row alone, which `rows/3` now uses), and loading
  is total: an `extra` that is not a JSON object loads as nil, an unknown `alive` as `:unknown`.
  Before, one such row raised in every read of its `{host, account}`.
- **`Shell`**: after it kills a program past its timeout, it waits up to 1 s for that program's
  exit status before it closes the port (see "What I ran into").
- **Tests** (`test/tracker_test.exs`). Properties: over generated sequences of writes, malformed
  rows put straight into the table, cues delivered out of order, twice or never (a restarted
  `Tracker` loses those sent to the old one), cues with no rows, of unknown topics or shapes,
  restarts of the `Tracker` and of the writer's connection, and a clock that moves, the
  `Tracker`'s view equals the fold of the tables' current rows read in full, once every write's
  cue has arrived, and a source never written stays `:unread`; a view's cues per topic are at
  least a second apart by the clock, and together name every row written; the fold of good rows
  with rows of any term keeps every good row's entry. Examples: a real `Registry` read reaches a
  view through a cue and the view's cue, while a second instance on its own file reads and hears
  nothing; restarts of the `Registry` and the `Tracker` give the same view; freshness names an
  unread source, then one past the bound, and shows a source that cannot open with its problem.

## Measured

- `test/tracker_test.exs` (2 doctests, 3 properties, 2 tests) takes about 4 s under a load near
  30.
- With the `:unread` rule removed from `Tracker` (a cue for an unread source read only its
  ids), the first property failed after 12 runs: a malformed row put before the first read was
  missing from the view.

## What I ran into

- `test/registry_test.exs`'s timeout test failed once in four runs under load: the slow `ps`
  was killed at its timeout, and its exit status reached the `Registry`'s mailbox after `Shell`
  had closed the port, which crashed the `Registry` on an unexpected message. `Shell` now awaits
  the killed program's status before closing. Five runs after the change passed.
- One full `mix test` run under a load near 30 failed the `Registry` property "reads count and
  name every file" on its first generated case; my output filter dropped the details. Three
  more full runs and three runs of the `Registry` and `Tracker` tests alone passed. It may be
  a `Shell` run past its 10 s timeout while `bin/check-model all` runs in the same suite; it
  is not shown.
- A read whose rows did not change writes the reads row but sends no cue, so the `Tracker`'s
  freshness would age while the reader is current. See new-1.
- The unit is about 790 changed lines apart from this note: 460 of library code with its docs,
  290 of tests, 50 in `Table` and `Shell`. It could be split: the `Shell` and `Table` fixes (50
  lines) stand alone, and `Tracker.Fold` with its totality property (about 250) could come before
  the process and its sources.

## Findings

### new-1. A read that changes nothing sends no cue

What happened: `Registry` cues after a write that changed rows, or after its first read. A read
that finds the same rows updates `registry_reads.read_at` and says nothing.

Why: a cue names changed rows; a newer read with no change is not one. But freshness is the age
of the newest read, so a `Tracker` fed by cues alone would show a current reader as behind.

What I did: `view/2` and `behind/3` re-read each source's reads row (one row per source) before
they answer. A view's cue is not sent for a newer read alone.

What the design should change: rule 8 could say that freshness is read from the reader's table
when asked, not carried by cues, and that a view shows the age at its own pace.

### new-2. A cue lost between a write and its sending leaves a row stale

What happened: a reader writes its rows and then cues. A reader that stops between the two, or
a cue sent to a `Tracker` that is restarting, is lost; the next read of the same files changes
nothing and sends no cue, so the row stays stale in the `Tracker` until it changes again.

Why: a restarted `Tracker` reads every table in full, which covers its own restarts; a restarted
reader sends nothing.

What I did: nothing beyond the full read at the `Tracker`'s start. The property delivers every
write's cue in the end.

What the design should change: rule 1 could say that a reader cues its whole topic when it
starts (a cue with no ids, which the `Tracker` already reads as "the whole topic"), so a cue
lost to a reader's restart is made up.

### new-3. Readers' tables are read by other processes, so loading must be total

What happened: `Registry.Table` raised on a row whose `extra` was not JSON or whose `alive` was
not one of its four values, so one such row stopped every read of its `{host, account}`, for
the `Tracker` as well as the `Registry`.

What I did: loading is total, and the `Tracker` catches a source that raises and shows it as
the source's problem, keeping its last rows.

What the design should change: rule 4 could say that a table's loader is total too, not only
the fold, since the `Tracker` reads other parts' tables.

### new-4. The clock is wall time for both ages and the cue limit

What happened: `read_at` is written in wall time by each reader, so ages need wall time; the
cue limit uses the same clock to keep one option.

What I did: one `clock:`. A clock that steps back holds a view's cue until the timer sends it.

What the design should change: nothing now; a reader on another host writes `read_at` by that
host's clock, so ages across hosts need the hosts' clocks to agree, or the reader to record its
read in the framework node's time.
