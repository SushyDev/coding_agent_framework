# F-117: R1, `caf_store`, and the writers' tables on it

The remodel's first unit (`docs/design.md`, "Order of the first units", R1; F-37's new-3;
F-115's "Repeated code"). No behavior changes.

## What was built

1. **A new app, `caf_store`**, a base app beside `caf_shell` that uses only `exqlite`. Its one
   module, `CodingAgentFramework.Store`, is pure: it starts no process and owns no table.
   - `open(path, schema, opts)`: opens the file, sets the 5 s busy timeout and WAL, runs the
     part's schema, adds the columns a later version added (`added: [table: ["col TYPE", …]]`,
     "duplicate column name" taken as already added), then runs an optional statement that
     needs them (`then:`, `Mailbox`'s unique index on `reply_to`). It answers `{:ok, db}` or
     `{:refused, {:sqlite, reason}}`, as each part's `open/1` did, and closes the file it
     opened when a later step is refused.
   - `query/3` (prepare, bind, fetch, release: the 11 lines each writer had), `select/5`
     (rows as maps of the given columns), `rows_by_id/5` (an optional scope such as
     `host: host`, oldest first), `insert/4` (a row of fields, with a tail such as an
     `ON CONFLICT … RETURNING id`), `upsert/3` (`INSERT OR REPLACE`), `update/4` (a `SET` of
     fields on row `id`, a term other than text, an integer or nil stored as its `inspect`
     text), `transaction/2`, `marks/2`, `row/2`.
   - `load(bytes, fresh)`: a stored map loaded with the fields a newer build added taken from
     `fresh`, a field it does not know dropped and named, and bytes that are not a map or that
     `:safe` refuses loaded as `fresh`. This was `Stream.Table`'s; `Stream` now calls it and
     then `Fold.upgrade/1`.
2. **The eleven writers' tables on it**: `Sessions`, `Delegates`, `Stream`, `Mailbox`,
   `Intake`, `Jobs`, `Services`, `Copies`, `MCP`, `Claims` (in `caf_grants`) and
   `Recoveries`. Each keeps its own table, schema, statuses and functions; only the mechanics
   moved. Each app declares `caf_store`; `exqlite` stays only where an app still names it: in
   `caf_mcp`'s `lib` (the read-only open of `MCP.Table.caller/2`), and `only: :test` in the
   four apps whose tests open a file with `Exqlite` (`caf_stream`, `caf_mailbox`, `caf_jobs`,
   `caf_services`).
3. **`Tmux.Fake` moved** from `caf_tmux/lib` to `caf_tmux/test/support` (F-115's new-12),
   which `caf_tmux` compiles only for tests, as `caf_sessions` already does with its setup. The
   tests and doctests that use it (`caf_tmux`, `caf_control`, `caf_sampler`) are unchanged.
4. **Tests**: `StoreTest` (an older file gains its columns and a second open adds none; the
   `then:` statement sees them; a schema SQLite refuses is refused; WAL; `rows_by_id` with a
   scope; `update`'s inspect text; `upsert`; a transaction; `load`'s four cases) and four
   doctests. No existing test changed.
5. The README's status and the apps chart show `caf_store`.

Two places keep their own small rule, written once beside the call: `Sessions.Table.update/3`
stores an atom as its name (`"running"`), where the others store its `inspect` text
(`Recoveries`' `reason = ':repeating'` relies on that), so `Sessions` maps atoms before calling
`Store.update/4`; and `Claims` keeps its own `select`, which decodes its two term columns.

The readers (`Registry`, `Usage`, `Transcripts`, the sampler) keep their copies: they move onto
`Store` with `Reader` in R2.

## What was measured

- The eleven writers' table modules went from 1,729 lines to 1,329 (+194, −594 in their `lib`).
  `Store` is 209 lines with its docs, and its tests 79. Each writer's 11-line `query`, its
  `open`, its added-column loop, its `update` builder, its `rows_by_id` and its placeholder
  builder are gone: 11 copies of `query`, 11 of `open`, 6 of the added columns.
- The whole unit is +544, −608 lines, this note apart: about 1,150 changed lines, net −64.
  That is past the 500 a unit aims for, and above F-115's estimate for R1 (+250, −700),
  because `Store`'s docs and tests are larger than estimated and the writers' calls of `Store`
  are longer than calls of a local helper. See new-1.
- `mix test`: every app's tests passed in the first run, with 9 new in `caf_store` plus its 4
  doctests. `devenv test` passed with every gate run (`gate-model` took 1,219 s under load),
  and `gate-test` passed three times in a row, every app, nothing left running.

## What I ran into

- The first full `mix test`, which includes the model test, failed only `CheckModelTest`
  (TLC on every fast configuration), at its 300 s timeout. The load average was about 75 from
  other work. This unit changes no spec. `gate-test` leaves that test out, and `gate-model`
  runs TLC on its own.
- `Sessions.Table.update/3` stores an atom as its name, while `Delegates`, `Mailbox` and
  `Recoveries` store its `inspect` text, and `Recoveries` matches on the latter
  (`reason = ':repeating'`). `Store.update/4` keeps the second rule, and `Sessions` keeps its
  rule in one line before the call, so no stored value changed. See new-2.

## Findings

### new-1. R1 is past the 500-line aim

What happened: the unit is about 1,150 changed lines (+544, −608), more than twice the aim.
Why: R1 moves eleven modules at once, and each move deletes about 50 lines and writes about 20.
What I did: kept it one unit. The deletions are mechanical, and a half-moved set of writers
would leave two shapes of table code until a second unit landed.
What the design should change: a unit's size could count a mechanical move's deletions apart,
as fixtures are, or R-units that move many parts could say up front that they run past 500.

### new-2. Two rules for storing a term that is not text or a number

What happened: `Sessions` stores an atom as `"running"`, while the other writers store a term
as its `inspect` text (`":repeating"`).
Why: each copy of `update/3` was written on its own.
What I did: nothing changed. `Store.update/4` has the `inspect` rule, and `Sessions` turns
atoms into names before the call.
What the design should change: name one rule for a term in a column, likely the name for an
atom and no `inspect` text in any table, when R10 or R9 rewrites the rows that hold reasons.
