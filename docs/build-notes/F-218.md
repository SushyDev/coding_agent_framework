# F-218: the last unbounded waits, and call-local deadlines on the monotonic clock

F-217's survey left three things against the rule that every wait is bounded by the clock it
states: one `caf-run` ask had no bound (its new-3), `WaiterStandIn.run/2` waited for the hook with
no `after` (new-2), and some deadlines that live in one call were taken from OS time (new-1).
This unit closes all three.

## What was built

**1. The `caf-run` ask** (`caf_runs`). `prepare/5` writes one more variable into the wrapper,
`asked`: the plug's `:wait` and 5 s in seconds, as it writes `unanswered`. The wrapper's `curl`
takes it as `--max-time`. An ask that runs past it prints no answer, so it takes the path an ask
nobody answered always took: it counts as the first with none, and the wrapper asks again while
the no-answer bound (`:unanswered`, 30 s) lasts, then runs the command without a lease.

- `prepare/5`'s new option `:wait` (ms) is the `:wait` of the app's `Runs.Plug`. Its default is
  the plug's, `Runs.wait/0` (600 000), which the plug now reads from there, so the number has one
  source. A `:wait` that is not a positive integer is refused `:bad_wait`.
- The slack of 5 s is a module attribute of `Runs` (`@ask_slack`). It is for the plug's own end of
  a queued wait (`unknown no_answer` at `:wait`) to reach the wrapper, so only a stalled ask
  passes the bound, and a legitimate queued wait never does.
- `Runs`' doc states the bound and what it costs in the worst case; see new-1.

**2. `WaiterStandIn.run/2`** (`caf_test`). The `receive` has an `after` of 5 s, `@bound`, the
test's own await bound. After it, the aid sends KILL to the hook's process group (OTP starts a
port program with `setsid`, so the hook's pid is its group's id; `Shell.signal/3`, while the port
is open and its exit not taken, as `Shell` does), waits up to 1 s for the exit, closes the port
if it did not come, removes its directory and answers `{:timeout, 5_000}`. `turn_end/4`'s task
passes that answer on and writes nothing to the transcript. The doc says a test awaits the task
for longer than 5 s.

**3. Call-local deadlines.** Each is taken from `System.monotonic_time`; every deadline a row
keeps stays on OS time.

| deadline | now | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Holder.Process.end_held/5`'s grace and `@kill_wait` | monotonic | lives in the call |
| `Holder.Process.dead_by/3`'s `deadline` argument | monotonic (public, documented) | the two above pass it; it is a call's |
| `Services.wait/4`'s `timeout` and its rounds | monotonic (`mono/0`) | lives in the call |
| `Services`' `settle_row` read of a dead end | monotonic (`dead_by(…, mono())`) | a read now, not stored |
| `Jobs`' reads and retries within `run/6` (`retry`, `wait_end`) | monotonic (`bound`) | lives in the call |
| `Jobs`' `start_until` and `deadline`, `Services`' `start_until`, `end_until`, `Copies.Held.settle/5` | OS time, unchanged | stored in a row, outlive a restart |

`Jobs.run/6` takes two deadlines at its start: `deadline`, which the row keeps, and `bound`, the
same instant and span on the monotonic clock, which its own reads wait by. A step of the OS clock
moves the row's deadline, as it must for a restart to compare it, and no longer cuts or stretches
the call. `Services.dead_by`'s one other caller, `settle_row`, passed `now()` to mean "one read
now"; it passes the monotonic now.

**The `clock:` option.** None of these reads a part's `clock:` option: `Holder.Process`,
`Services` and `Jobs` take their time from their own `now/0` (`System.os_time`) and have no
`clock:`. So no test lost a way to drive time: those tests never drove it. The parts that have a
`clock:` (`Sessions`, `Delegates`, `Mains`, `Mover` and others) read it for the deadlines they
store in rows, which are unchanged.

**The design**: rule 5 has one line saying which is which (a deadline in one call is
monotonic, a row's is OS time or the part's `clock:`), and the `Runs` paragraph the ask's bound.
**The README** status line.

## The model

No spec in `model/tla/` changed. `Schedule.tla`'s shape `Native` has the wrapper ask and end; it
has no ask that stalls and is cut, which is the wrapper's no-answer path already modeled as an
unanswered ask. The monotonic deadlines change no protocol, only the clock a wait reads.

## Tests

- `runs_test.exs`: a stand-in intake that accepts every connection and never answers or closes
  it, `wait: 1_000`: the first ask is cut at 6 s, no sooner (the second accept is 6.9 to 10 s
  after the first, the cut plus the pause), the wrapper asks once more, and ends the run at 13 to
  16 s without a lease, with the no-answer line; two accepts, no third. Without `--max-time` the
  test fails: the wrapper hangs until `Shell`'s 40 s. And the written wrapper holds `asked` of
  605 s by default and 6.5 s for `wait: 1_500`; a `:wait` of 0 or 1.5 is refused `:bad_wait`.
- `stand_in_test.exs`: a waiter hook that records its pid, starts a child in its group and waits
  for it for ever: the task answers `{:timeout, 5_000}` in 5 to 8 s, the transcript is not
  written, and the hook and its child are both dead (waited for with `await`, as a `kill -0` of a
  zombie answers alive).
- `holder_process_test.exs`: `dead_by/3` on a live program with a monotonic deadline 1 s away
  answers `{:unknown, {:not_dead, live}}` no sooner than 1 s. Read as OS time, a monotonic
  reading has long passed and the wait would end at its first read.
  `stuck_backgrounds_test.exs` passes `dead_by/3` a monotonic deadline.

The slacks are stated wide (3 s over two asks, 3 s over the kill) for the same reason as F-217's:
a sleep, a `curl` and a `kill` each wake or start late under load.

## What I ran into

- The worktree's `deps` lagged the lock for `plug`; `mix deps.get` fixed it, as in F-217.
- The test aid's raw `Port.open` is why `WaiterStandIn` could not just call `Shell.run/4`: it
  reads the hook's stderr from a file, apart from its stdout. `Shell` merges the two. I kept the
  port and gave it `Shell`'s own end of a group.
- A test that cannot step the OS clock cannot show a step. The `dead_by/3` test shows the clock
  read instead: a monotonic deadline that an OS-time read would find already past.

## Findings

### new-1. A stalled intake holds the wrapper for two bounds, not one

What happened: an ask cut at `--max-time` counts as the wrapper's first ask with no answer, and
its no-answer bound (30 s) then starts, so the wrapper asks again, and the second ask may stall
for its whole bound as well. Why: the brief said a cut ask takes the path of `:unanswered` as it
is. With the defaults the wrapper runs its command within 1 241 s (605 s, 30 s, 605 s, a second's
pause) of the first stall, not 605 s. What I did: built it as the brief says and stated the sum
in `Runs`' doc. What the design should change: an ask cut at its bound could end the wrapper's
wait at once, since the framework accepted the connection and then did not answer a whole `:wait`
and 5 s, so another ask is unlikely to differ. That needs curl's own exit status (28), which the
wrapper's pipe into `while read` hides; the wrapper would take it through a status line (`-w`) or
a temporary file. It would halve the stall's worst case to one bound.

### new-2. The plug's `:wait` and the wrapper's are one number set in two places

What happened: `prepare/5`'s `:wait` must equal the `:wait` the app gives `Runs.Plug`. The surface
(`caf_surface`) sets neither today, so both are the default and agree. Why: the wrapper is written
at the start and the plug is configured separately, so nothing ties them. If an app raised the
plug's `:wait` and not the wrapper's, the wrapper would cut a legitimate queued ask at the
default and 5 s and run without the lease it was waiting for. What I did: gave both one default,
`Runs.wait/0`, and documented that an app sets both. What the design should change: when the
surface's `Config` exposes the wait, it is one key that feeds `Runs.prepare/5` and `Runs.Plug`.
