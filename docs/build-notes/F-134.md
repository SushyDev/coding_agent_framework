# F-134: `Schedule.tla`, and the `Scheduler` in the design

One lease ledger in front of every agent start, heavy run and landing, modeled before its code.
This unit changes specs, configurations and the design's text only; no Elixir.

## What was built

- **`model/tla/Schedule.tla`**. Durable `leases` (a sequence: a row's index is its id, so the
  next id is `Len(leases) + 1` and ids grow strictly, as a fencing token must). In memory: the
  queue per class, the rotation per class, a tick-down timer per queued request, the answer
  owed to each requester (`pending`), and the monitors on the holders. The requester's last
  answer (`ans`) and the holder's process (`proc`) outlive a restart. Environment: `pressure`
  (`ok`, `high`, `severe`) and `budgetOk` per account. `crashes` bounds the restarts.
- **Actions**: `Ask` (a first request) and `Reask` (the same id after a restart), both through
  `Receive`, which answers at once: the existing lease, a refusal, or queued. `Insert` is the
  grant transaction: capacity, gates, class order and the module rotation, one row with every
  unit the request needs. `Tell` hands the owed answer over, apart from `Insert`, so a restart
  between them is checked. `Tick`, `Expire` (the deadline, or a spent budget), `ProcessEnds`,
  `Release` (the monitor), `Reclaim` (after a restart: release if the process ended, monitor
  again if not), `Crash`, `Pressure`, `Budget`.
- **Properties**: invariants `CapacityHolds`, `NoDoubleGrant`, `ReleaseOnce` (with a release
  counter on each row), `GangAtomic`, `AnsweredByDeadline`, `RefusedIsNotFailed` (a refused
  request holds no lease); action properties `ClassOrder`, `NoStartUnderSevere`,
  `NoStartWithoutBudget`; liveness `QueuedIsAnswered`, `EndedIsReleased`.
- **Fairness**: weak on the scheduler's steps and on `Reask`, which is the requester's part of
  the protocol. None on `Ask`, `ProcessEnds`, pressure or budgets. `Crash` is never fair.
- **Configurations**: `Schedule_Safety` (a landing and two heavy runs from two modules, one
  slot and one lock, one restart), `Schedule_Mains` (a main session and a builder sharing one
  seat, one restart: the main session admitted under `severe`), `Schedule_Liveness` (a landing
  and a heavy run, one restart, every property). Thirteen `Break_Schedule_*`, at least one per
  property: `NoCapacityCheck` and `CountInMemory` (`CapacityHolds`), `NotIdempotent`
  (`NoDoubleGrant`), `NoCompareAndSet` and `ReclaimByTime` (`ReleaseOnce`), `Piecemeal`
  (`GangAtomic`), `NoDeadline` (`AnsweredByDeadline`), `RefuseBeforeLookup`
  (`RefusedIsNotFailed`), `NoClassOrder`, `NoSevereGate`, `NoBudgetGate`, `NoReask`
  (`QueuedIsAnswered`), `NoReclaim` (`EndedIsReleased`).
- **Neither `Claim` nor `Hold`** is instanced. A claim is one per state of a target and acted on
  once; a lease is units counted against a capacity, held for a process's life. A hold has a
  durable row and exactly one response, and settles lost or unknown after a cut; a queued request
  has no row, may be answered queued and then once more, and is asked again after a restart. The
  spec's header says so.
- **The design**: the `Scheduler`'s row in "The parts" with its `leases` table; "A lease" in
  the flows, with the operator's decisions written as decided (below); `Schedule.tla` in "The
  model"; item 14's admission replaced by the `Scheduler`; an entry for its code after R12.
  README: the `Scheduler` in the overview chart and its bullets, and in Status as next after
  R12. The apps chart is unchanged: no app is built.
- **Decided by the operator during the unit** (through the main session), written into the
  design: a person's main session is always admitted under `severe` (a rule in the spec, not a
  constant: the brief's "model both" became one value); the queue stays in memory, with
  re-requests by id; holding several accounts and moving work between them is allowed, so the
  account is a resource with several entries.

## What was measured

Times from `devenv shell -- bin/check-model <name>`, under a load average of 45 to 60 on 15
cores (other agents' runs), so the seconds are high; the distinct states do not depend on load.

| Configuration | Distinct states | Depth | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Schedule_Safety` | 503,364 | 20 | 18 s |
| `Schedule_Mains` | 16,050 | 15 | 5 s |
| `Schedule_Liveness` | 16,050 | 15 | 11 s |
| `Break_Schedule_*` (13) | 323 to 15,470 | 5 to 9 | 4 to 10 s each |

The first `Schedule_Safety` had four requests (a main session beside the landing and the two
heavy runs), two accounts and two restarts: it ran past 10 minutes and I stopped it. Two
restarts with three requests on one account gave 941,748 states in 38 s; one restart gave the
numbers above. The main session moved to its own small configuration.

`devenv test`, with every gate, passed (exit 0): the model gate checked every fast
configuration and parsed the slow ones. It ran under a load average near 99. The three Schedule
configurations took 4 to 15 s there. Eight older configurations (`Copy_Forward`, `Copy_Turns`,
`Delegate_Restarts`, `Start_SharedTree` and four of `Team`) took 30 to 133 s under that load.
That is F-128's new-1 again, and nothing this unit changed.

Reachability, each by a probe invariant TLC had to fail (a module in the scratch directory, not
kept): a main session granted under `severe` (`Schedule_Mains`); a tick of a queued timer; a
refusal with pressure `ok` and budget room (a full queue or a deadline); a turn of a class's
rotation; a re-request after a restart answered with the lease granted before it.

- The first safety configuration was far over the 30 s bound; see above.
- A probe's configuration file was written once into the worktree's root by a relative path;

## Findings

### new-1. A start's lease names a holder that does not exist yet

What happened: the design says a lease is reclaimed after a restart only when "its recorded
process has ended" by pid and start time. For an agent start, the process is started after the
grant, so the row has no record when it is inserted. The spec takes the requester's process as
the holder, alive from before it asks.
Why: the grant comes first by design. If the record were the framework's own calling process,
a restart of the framework would end it, and the reclaimer would free the lease of an agent
that dtach still holds: capacity overcommitted.
What I did: modeled the holder as a process that exists at the request and outlives the
scheduler's restart; the header names it as left abstract.
What the design should change: a lease names the row of the work it admits (the agent row, the
job row), whose `Holder.Process` record its owner keeps, and the reclaimer asks that owner
whether it ended. A row still starting counts as live until its owner settles it, as
`Start.tla` settles a starting row after a restart. The code unit should model that link before
it builds the reclaimer.

### new-2. A waiting landing's hold on the next free slot is not modeled

What happened: `ClassOrder` lets a lower class take a resource a higher waiting request
cannot use now. A landing that needs a slot and the lock can wait behind heavy runs that take
the slot one after another, and is refused at its deadline.
Why: the brief's `ClassOrder` is "no waiting request of a higher class fits". Holding the next
free slot for a waiting landing is a stronger rule that neither the brief nor the design
decided.
What I did: left it out; the header names it as abstract.
What the design should change: decide whether a waiting landing holds the next free unit of
each resource it needs. If so, `ClassOrder` becomes "a grant takes no unit a waiting request of
a higher class needs", with a `Break` for it, before the code.

### new-3. `high` pressure's bar on heavy runs has no property of its own

What happened: `NoStartUnderSevere` checks the `severe` gate; the `high` gate on heavy runs is
the same guard in `Admitted` but no property fails if it is removed.
Why: the brief's list names only the `severe` property.
What I did: the header says so.
What the design should change: if the `high` gate matters on its own, add `NoHeavyUnderHigh`
and its `Break` when the code unit next touches the spec.
