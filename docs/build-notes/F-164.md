# F-164: B1 part 2, the restart per agent

A model unit with one code fix: specs, configurations and the design's text, and
`Delegates.Work`'s answer for a refused row. It is the second of B1's five parts ("Order of the
first units"): the restart as a step per agent beside the whole one, with F-163's new-1 and
new-2 as decided.

## What was built

- **`RestartAgent`, one agent process's crash and its supervisor's restart, in one step**, in
  `Start.tla`, `Delegate.tla`, `Dialog.tla` and `Move.tla`, beside each spec's whole restart.
  It is named as in `Boxes.tla`; the design calls it `Restart(a)`. One `restarts` counter counts
  both kinds, so `MaxRestarts` bounds them together, as the design says, and every existing
  configuration now checks every interleaving of the two within its bound. The crash and the
  restart are one step because nothing reads the agent process's memory in between, and a cue
  lost in between is one the init gives again. What each loses and keeps:

  | Spec | Lost with the agent process | Runs on |
  |---|---|---|
  | `Start.tla` | that session's calls under way, the setup its reaper ends, its cue; the init cues its row | `Recoveries`' claims (the `Watch`'s), every other session's calls |
  | `Delegate.tla`, a delegate's | `Delegates`' call of it (answered unknown, so `Settled` decides), a Stop event not yet passed on (or not), `Sessions`' start or resume under way | `Delegates`' memory, `Mailbox`, the parent |
  | `Delegate.tla`, the parent's (`RestartParent`) | the hand of a report to it under way | its turn and process outside |
  | `Dialog.tla` | the press tasks and their answers; marks pressing read again as cut or unknown | `Prompts` (an answer on its way may reach the new process), the `Scheduler`, `Recoveries` |
  | `Move.tla` | the stop's idle read, the hand of a row to the session under way | the Mover's phase and `Sessions`' row (rows), `Mailbox`'s own memory |

  In `Delegate.tla` a cut start is adopted from its row: refused with nothing held, confirmed
  when its message went in with the hold (`SessionsDecides` and `Confirmed` still stand for
  both), or, for a headless start whose crash fell between the hold and the first line, an
  orphan (`sess` = `orphan`, a process with no line) that never confirms and that `Recoveries`
  ends (`OrphanEnded`, weakly fair). `Settled` waits on it, as the code does for an agent row
  that is neither running nor free.
- **F-163's new-2, decided.** `Delivery.tla`'s `AgentCut(a, went)`: the hand of a stream,
  waiter, typed or launch row to `a` is cut by `a`'s agent process's restart; its write went
  out before the crash or not (a stream line claimed, a waiter answered, keys typed, each only
  while the hand-on was possible; a launch's start that was made is `Launch`'s own step), and
  the mark is kept for the evidence. Alone, `AgentRestart(a)` takes it and loses `a`'s idle
  read. `Move.tla` and `Delegate.tla` call it for the session and the parent. `AtMostOnce` and
  `SendingSettles` hold under it in every `Delivery_*` configuration; **`Break_Delivery_
  AgentCutUndoes`** (the cut row made due again) fails `AtMostOnce`, by a trace through
  `AgentRestart`: the keys were typed, the crash cut the Inlet's answer, and the row, due again,
  was typed once more and taken twice.
- **F-163's new-1, decided (the code risk).** In `Delegate.tla`, where the lease owner's
  statuses are: `WorkEnded(d)` is what `Delegates.Work` answers (ended once the row never
  called or ended, or once it is refused, finished or failed and `Sessions`' row of it is free),
  and **`WorkEndedIsFree`**: work the owner says ended has no process. **`Break_Delegate_
  StatusEndsWork`** answers by the status alone and fails it: a finished delegate's process lives
  until the delegate ends. In the code, `Delegates.Work` no longer answers a `refused` row
  `:ended` at once: a refused row now takes the finished one's clause (a newer start owns its
  name, or `Sessions.Agents.free?/1` holds of its agent row). The new test in
  `delegates_test.exs`, "a refused start whose process still lives keeps its seat", writes a
  running delegate's row `refused` while its process lives (the state the race in new-1 below
  leaves), sees `:live`, kills the process, waits for its agent row to stop, and sees `:ended`.
  With the old clause put back it fails at the `:live` assert (`left: :ended`).
- **`Boxes.tla`** is refined in its header, not in its state: each effect of its `RestartAgent`
  is mapped to the step that now checks it against the mechanism (the press tasks to
  `Dialog.tla`'s, `CutCalls` and `UnknownUndoes` to `Delivery.tla`'s `AgentCut` and
  `AgentCutUndoes`, `stopRead` to `Move.tla`'s, no relaunch to `Start.tla`'s and
  `Delegate.tla`'s adoption, `Release`'s `WorkEnded` to `WorkEndedIsFree`). Its own state needs
  no change: its restart already is one step that keeps the mark and the rows, and none of the
  four specs found a behavior its contract leaves out. The budget stays its own: in the
  contract specs a restart is bounded, not budgeted.
- **Headers**: `Start.tla`, `Delegate.tla`, `Dialog.tla`, `Move.tla`, `Delivery.tla` and
  `Schedule.tla` say which restarts they have and what the owner's promise is checked by.
  `Start.tla`'s F-163 text said a refused agent row may hold a live process; it may not (new-2).
- **The design**: "How the specs meet OTP" says the step is built and `Delivery.tla` has the
  addressee's cut; "The model" gains a paragraph on the restart per agent; "A lease"'s end says
  why a refused row waits for its agent row; B1 says its second part is built.

## What I ran into

- **With one agent, the agent's restart adds no state.** `Start.tla`'s one-session
  configurations and `Dialog.tla`'s reach exactly the states they did: a crash and restart in
  one step loses what a whole restart then start loses, and the one thing it keeps, the
  `Watch`'s claims, a whole restart only cuts. So the per-agent coverage in `Start.tla` comes
  from `Start_SharedTree` (two sessions on one path) and the slow `Start_TwoSessions` and
  `Start_Large`; `Dialog.tla` has one agent, as a dialog is one agent's.
- **Most effects need no new branch.** A delegate's start cut before its hold is the adoption's
  refusal, and one cut after its hold and line is the hold then the crash; `SessionsDecides`
  already takes both, so the step changes `sess` only for the orphan. A hand cut after its write
  is `Handed` then the crash, which the whole restart already had; what `AgentCut` adds is the
  write and the cut in one step with the mark kept, which the Break needs to fail.
- **`Start_SharedTree` passed the fast set's 30 s.** It was 27 s before, the slowest of the fast
  set's own specs, and 30 s with each agent's restart in: TLC's 27 s (13 s of search, 10 s of
  temporal checking over 12 branches) and the JVM's start. I split its seven temporal properties
  over two configurations of the same constants, `Start_SharedTree` (every invariant, and
  `RunningOnlyIfLive`, `StartingSettles`, `UnknownSettles`, `RunningSettles`) and
  `Start_SharedTreeEnds` (`StoppingSettles`, `OrphanEnds`, `LateStopEnds`): nothing checked less,
  18 s and 17 s, about 5 s more in all. The next slowest are older (`Team_AnswersTwo` 27 s,
  `Team_OperatorMessage` 25 s, `Copy_Forward` 21 s).

## Findings

### new-1. A start may reach `Sessions` after `Delegates` refused its row

What happened: `Delegates` calls `Sessions.start/5` from a task, with a timeout. When `Sessions`
does not answer in time (one slow pane read delays every agent today, as F-153's run showed),
the call answers `{:unknown, {:timeout, wait}}` (`sessions.ex:483`), and the row waits for
`Settled`. At the row's deadline with no agent row written since the call, `Settled` refuses it
`interrupted`: "the call never reached `Sessions`" (`delegates.ex:782`). But the request is
still in `Sessions`' mailbox: `Sessions` takes it later, sets a deadline of its own from that
moment (`sessions.ex:593`), holds the process and writes the brief, which `Mailbox` marked
before the call. The process then runs under a refused row. `Delegates`' settle of a refused row
waits for its agent row to be free and never stops it, and `Recoveries` ends only orphans and
late stops, so as far as I can read nothing ends that process. I did not reproduce it.
Why: the call's give-up and the callee's take are not one decision; the model's `Called` takes
a call only while `Delegates` still holds it, so `Delegate.tla` never sees the late take, and
its `RefusedHoldsNothing` holds there while the code can break it.
What I did: the `Delegates.Work` fix keeps the lease of such a process (the seat it really
takes), and the test shows it. I built nothing else.
What the design should change: decide who ends a late start. A concrete recommendation:
`Sessions` refuses a start whose caller's deadline passed when it takes it (the call carries the
row's deadline; one clock serves both), so `Settled`'s reading "no agent row since the call"
becomes true; and `Delegate.tla` gains the request in `Sessions`' queue, which survives
`Delegates`' restart, under which `RefusedHoldsNothing` must still hold. After B4 the same
question stands for a call queued to an agent process.

### new-2. F-163's new-1 misread `Start.tla`: a refused agent row holds no process

What happened: F-163's new-1 said `Start.tla` lets an agent row be refused or orphan while its
process lives. `NoHeldWithoutRow` (checked in every `Start_*` configuration) says a live process
or socket has a row that has not ended, and `Ended` includes `refused`; `NotGivenUpEarly`'s
`refused` case is reached only under its Breaks. Only `orphan`, `unknown` and `starting` may hold
a live process.
Why: the gap new-1 found is real, but it is the delegate row's status against the agent row,
not the agent row's own.
What I did: corrected `Start.tla`'s header; the property is `Delegate.tla`'s, as decided.
What the design should change: nothing.

### new-3. F-163's new-3 came back: a `Mailbox` test races the cue it asserts after

What happened: the third `gate-test` failed `caf_mailbox`'s "a delegate that ended takes
nothing: its queued rows are settled ended" (`apps/caf_mailbox/test/mailbox_test.exs:71`, seed
977618): it asserts a brief is still `queued` after a cue, and `Mailbox` had settled it `ended`.
This branch does not change `Mailbox` or its tests. The next three runs passed.
Why: as F-163's new-3 says, whichever cue `Mailbox` handles first decides what the assert sees.
What I did: left the test as it is; it is a unit of its own.
What the design should change: nothing; the test should assert what the design says, a row to
an ended delegate settles `ended`, once an announced event says so.
