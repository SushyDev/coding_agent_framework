# F-191: the moves of a limited agent; an interactive delegate's limit; a start confirmed by its init

The brief's five items, from F-187's findings and the operator's decision of 21 Nov: the move of
a limited agent modeled in `Move.tla` first (item 1), the moves themselves (item 2), an
interactive delegate's limit (item 3), one word for every case (item 4), and a delegate's start
confirmed by its init (item 5). `Mover`'s `target/3` is left as it was, for F-190's clause. No
test started the real Claude Code, and nothing was measured by hand.

## What was built

- **The model first** (`Move.tla`, constant `Limited`), composed with `Limit.tla`'s `Away` and
  `MoveAct`, whose decision this move is. `HitLimit`: the turn ends at the limit, its rows given
  the limit's result, no waiter connected; `Mailbox` holds every row to the limited process, as
  to one stopping (`Held`). `ExitsAtLimit`: the process ends there (a headless one exits at
  once). `WordStored`: the healer's word to go on, stored first. `LimitMove`: the move once the
  word is stored, from a live process (drained, stopped: the move's stop alone reads the
  limited turn as ended) or from one gone (no drain, no stop). `Back`: a copy or resume refused
  leaves a limited session gone, phase `failed`. New properties, each with a break:
  `NoRowIntoLimit` (`Break_Move_LimitIdleForAll`: the limited turn ended for `Mailbox` too),
  `NoResumeIntoLimit` (`Break_Move_BackIntoLimit`: a failed move goes back as any move does),
  `LimitResumeCarriesRow` (`Break_Move_WordAfter`: the move begins before the word), and the
  liveness `LimitMoveSettles`. Configurations `Move_Limit` and `Move_LimitLiveness`; every
  earlier `Move_*` one sets `Limited = FALSE`. `Limit.tla`'s header names the code.
- **`Mover`**: `move/4` takes `act:`, a claim its caller made (the healer's, once per limit),
  marked acted and settled as its own, a refusal before the drain included. It moves a row
  whose process is gone at its limit (free, with a `usage_limit`), which skips the drain and the
  stop, and `Sessions.begin_move/4` takes such a row. A headless resume carries the first row
  due by stream as its first line (`Mailbox.launch/4` `via: :stream`, `Sessions.resume/6`
  `first:`), as `Delegates`' does. A limited row whose copy or resume is refused stays gone, its
  act failed, never resumed on its own account. `room/2`: the account a move would go to now,
  by the same pick, with nothing begun.
- **`Sessions`**: `stop/3` `for: :move` of an interactive session whose file does not read idle
  and whose row has a `usage_limit` reads its screen through the `Inlet`; Claude Code's own wait
  or its menu is taken as the turn's end and gets the SIGTERM. A delegate's start is confirmed
  by the init naming its session id when its process is gone after it, the record being this
  start's (a hold removes an earlier hold's record first); otherwise the reading is what it was.
- **`Limits`** (item 2): at `idle` or `own`, before the reset, a reset more than `move_after`
  away (option and config key, 2 h) and `room:` finding an account, once per limit (the row's
  `away`): a delegate's remedy `move`, kind `limit_far`, on the list and within its cap, claimed
  under `limit_far:<agent>:<until>`, the step `move` with its act on the row, the word stored and
  held (`go_on:` with `resume: false`, or `word:`), then `move:`. A main's is the `limit` prompt
  with `move_to`. A person's `move` answer is claimed by the healer with the person in the
  claim's state and detail, and the `Mover` is called as that person. The step `move` reads the
  act once the row no longer moves: verified is `moved`, anything else the `limit` prompt. A
  claim a restart cut is the prompt. A row stopped by its limit's move is still healed, so its
  word goes at the reset. The tree hands `room:` (`Mover.room/2`) and `move:` (`Mover.move/4`).
- **`Sessions.Limit`**: the steps `move` and `moved`; the `limit` prompt's answers `go_on`,
  `move` (where it names `move_to`) and `leave`; the answer `move` is a new act (`menu`), the
  go-ons in a row started again. `Prompts` says what `go_on` and `move` do in the prompt's text.
- **`Watch.default_remedies/0`**: `limit_far` by `move`, 20 per bound.
- **`Delegates`** (item 3): `go_on/5` serves an interactive delegate too, its limit read from
  its transcript (the last limit line, no turn after it); a gone one is resumed by launch with
  the word as its prompt. `resume: false` stores the word and resumes nothing (`{:ok, :stored}`).
  `settle_interactive` reads a turn whose transcript ends at the limit as neither a finish nor a
  kill, whether the process lives or not. The healer's word for every delegate is `go_on:`.
- **The word** (item 4): "Your turn stopped at a usage limit. Go on with your task from where it
  stopped."
- **Stand-ins**: `ScreenStandIn` with `--screen limit_wait` draws 2.1.322's own wait, its
  session file `busy`; `HeadlessStandIn` loses the `limit.fifo` wait, and F-187's test with it.
- **Docs**: "Usage limits" (how a limited agent's move goes, what F-191 built), "The delegate
  protocol" (an interactive delegate's limit), "Headless delegates" (the confirm), "How `Mover`
  does it" (the limited failure), "The model" (`Move.tla`'s `Limited`), and the parts table's
  `Limits` row.

## What I ran into

- `Move.tla`'s first `Move_Limit` failed `NoRowIntoLimit` as first written ("no turn of the
  limited process starts"): a row handed into the turn before the limit is Claude Code's to take,
  as a prompt typed before it is. The property is `Mailbox`'s: no row marked into a limited
  process. Then `NotCutByEnd` failed: a row's turn that ended at the limit had no result, so the
  move's SIGTERM read as cutting it. The hit gives its rows the limit's result, which is what
  Claude Code writes (the transcript's line, the stream's `result`).

- `caf.deps` refused `Delegates.Turn.word_uuid/1` in `MoverTest`; the test's word has a fixed
  uuid.
- `erl_child_setup: failed with error 32` printed once in `SessionsDialogTest` and once in
  `DelegatesInteractiveTest`, which passed, as F-190 saw in the `Mover` tests.

## Findings

### new-1. A live limited agent's failed move stays gone too, not only a gone one's

What happened: the brief's (b) says a gone row's failed move leaves the process gone. A live
row's move stops its process before the copy, so its failure meets the same choice: resume on
its own account, which is at its limit, or stay gone. Why: the `Mover`'s old rule (go back) was
for a move with room on both accounts. What I did: every move of a row with a `usage_limit`
that fails after the stop stays gone (`Move.tla`'s `Back` on `AtLimit`, live or gone), and its
word at the reset resumes it. A move given up before the stop (its drain or stop past the
deadline) still leaves the process running, limited. What the design should change: confirm
that a limited row's failed move never goes back, live or gone, as built.

### new-2. A headless move with no row due still resumes with no first line

What happened: the `Mover` now carries the first due row as a headless resume's first line. A
headless delegate moved with nothing due (an operator's move between turns) still resumes with
no line, and the real Claude Code writes its init only after one (F-152), so that resume would
never be confirmed. `MoverSetup` plays the stand-in `:at_start` for this reason. Why: only a
limited move has a word due by construction. What I did: nothing more. What the design should
change: a headless move with nothing due either waits for a row, or carries a word of its own
("you were moved to another account; go on"), stored under the move's act as the limit's word
is.

### new-3. The main's "wait" is the `go_on` answer

What happened: the brief says the main's prompt offers "move to <account>" beside "wait" and
"leave". Before the reset `go_on` reads the screen again and waits for the reset, which is the
wait; its choice id stays `go_on`, as `Prompts` already takes it, and the prompt's text says
what it does. Why: one answer set for every `limit` prompt. What I did: `go_on`, `move` and
`leave`, the text naming both. What the design should change: nothing, unless the choice should
be named `wait` where the prompt was opened before the reset.

### new-4. What the session file says during Claude Code's own wait is still unmeasured

What happened: the move's stop reads the screen only when the file does not read idle, so
either reading stops the move; the stand-in's file says `busy` so the test meets the screen
path. Why: F-181 captured the screens, not the file. What I did: nothing more. What the design
should change: measure it on the next limit by hand; if the file says `idle`, the screen read is
a second reading of the same end, and nothing changes.
