# F-242: a dialog press holds on a loaded host

## What was built

F-238's new-1, seen on the real program: in the real suite on 2026-11-24 (Claude Code 2.1.322,
load about 30 on 15 cores), two trust presses came back `{:unknown, {:still_shown, {:ok,
%{kind: :trust, selected: "No, exit", …}}}}`. The tree's `Inlet` read the screen back for the
library's 2 s `see:`, and the real program had not redrawn by then.

### The tree's `Inlet` takes its `see:` from `Config`

`Config` has a new field, `press_see` (ms, a positive integer, 10 000 by default). The tree
starts its `Inlet` with `see: c.press_see`. It hands the same value to `Sessions`
(`press_see:`) and to the inbox (`Prompts`' `press_see:`), and it sizes `Limits`' `press:`
call by it. The library's own default stays 2 s (`Inlet.see/0`), so a caller that starts an
`Inlet` or a `Sessions` with no option sees no change.

Why 10 s, from what was measured (below):

- the real program passed 2 s at load 30 on 15 cores, the brief's record;
- the stand-in's whole press (keys, marker read, Enter, leave read) took a median of about
  0.1 s, and its longest 2.7 s, at background priority with the load at 50 to 56;
- F-238's factor at that load is x4, and `scaled(2_000)` held there: 8 s.

10 s is five times the bound the real program passed, about four times the stand-in's longest
whole press, and over F-238's x4. A read ends as soon as the screen shows the keys, so the
bound costs time only on a press that fails. With item 3, the one common press that waited out
its whole read back ("No, exit") no longer does.

### `Sessions`' press bound derives from that `see`

`@press` (20 s) is gone. `Inlet.press_bound(see)` is the bound of one `choose/5`: two reads
of up to `see` each, plus `@commands`, 16 s for the tmux commands around them. 16 s is the part
of the old 20 s that two 2 s reads left, so the library's default bound is still 20 s; at 10 s
it is 36 s. `Sessions` keeps `[see: see, timeout: Inlet.press_bound(see)]` from its
`press_see:` and passes both with each press (a startup dialog's, a menu's, the usage limit
menu's). It passes `see:` itself, so the read and its bound come from one number whatever the
`Inlet`'s own default is.

A call that waits on a press (`answer_dialog/5`, `answer_menu/5`, `press_limit/4`) has a
client-side bound, which cannot read the instance's state. `Sessions.press_call(see)` is
`Inlet.press_bound(see)` plus `@read` (5 s, the wait for calls queued ahead). The default
`timeout:` is `press_call/1` of `Inlet.see/0`, 25 s, as before. The inbox's `@press` (25 s) is
now `press_call/1` of its `press_see:`, and the tree's `Limits` gets `press_limit/4` with
`timeout: press_call(c.press_see)`, 41 s at the default.

### A press whose pane closes is answered at once (F-238's new-2)

After the Enter (or the Escape), `Inlet`'s `left/6` read a failed capture as "says nothing"
and waited out its whole `see:`. Now a capture that `Tmux` refuses `:no_pane`, or
`:no_server` (the server ends with its last session), is the dialog left: `{:ok, :sent}`. tmux
never gives a pane's id to another pane, so a pane gone cannot still show the dialog. Any other
failed capture still says nothing.

`Dialog.tla` already allowed it: a press is `sent` only when it landed (`Lands`), and while a
press is under way only a landed press ends the process, since an end's SIGTERM waits for the
press's answer (`EndAfterPress`). The spec's comment above `Press` now says so; no step
changed (but see new-5).

### Tests

- `inlet_test.exs`, "a press whose redraw comes past 2 s is answered within the tree's see":
  the stand-in stopped (SIGSTOP) for 3 s as the press goes. At `see: 2_000` the press is
  `{:unknown, {:not_selected, _}}` and no Enter goes. At 10 000 it is `{:ok, :sent}`, after
  3 s or more, and the stand-in wrote `trusted`.
- `inlet_test.exs`, "a press whose pane closes is answered as soon as the pane is gone": "No,
  exit" at `see: 30_000` answers `{:ok, :sent}` in under 30 s, and the pane is gone. With the
  old `left/6` put back, the same test answered `{:unknown, {:still_shown, {:refused,
  :no_server}}}` after 30.5 s.
- `sessions_dialog_test.exs`, "a press reads back at the instance's press_see, past the Inlet's
  own see": a `Sessions` with `press_see: 10_000` over an `Inlet` left at 2 s, its held program
  stopped for 3 s. The answer is `{:ok, :sent}` after 3 s or more: `Sessions` passed its own
  `see:` and bound.
- The two "No, exit" tests in that file now assert `{:ok, :sent}`, a `verified` act and the
  mark `sent`, in under `scaled(2_000)`. Before, they took `{_, _}` and `verified` or
  `unknown`.
- The bounds derive: the doctests of `Inlet.press_bound/1` (20 000 at 2 s, 36 000 at 10 s) and
  `Sessions.press_call/1` (25 000, 41 000), and `Config`'s refusal of `press_see: 0`.
- The file's setup passes the same `scaled(2_000)` to its `Inlet` and to `Sessions`'
  `press_see:`, since `Sessions` now passes its own.

## Findings

### new-1. The real record reads as a slow exit, not a late marker

What happened: the brief reads the record as "the Down was sent, and the read back came before
the redraw". But only `left/6` answers `:still_shown`, after the Enter, so the marker had been
read back on the label. The screen left on the pane was the trust dialog with "No, exit"
selected. That fits a press of "No, exit" (an end's or a stop's exit press, F-231) whose
program had not exited 2 s after the Enter. It does not fit a press of "Yes, I trust this
folder" unless the program drew the dialog again, as new, after the Enter, which the stand-in
never does. Why: the record does not name the label pressed. What I did: built both fixes, since
either cause is covered (a later read back, and a pane gone answered at once). What the design
should change: the real suite should log a press's label beside its answer, so a record like
this one tells which it was.

### new-2. Two `see:`s must agree: the `Inlet`'s and `Sessions`'

What happened: `Sessions` now passes `see:` with each press, so the `Inlet`'s own `see:` only
reaches `type/4`, `reload_plugins/3` and callers that name none. Why: a press's bound must come
from the same number as its reads, and `Sessions` cannot read the `Inlet`'s. What I did: the tree
passes `Config.press_see` to both. A test or app that sets one and not the other gets the other
default. What the design should change: decide whether `Sessions` keeps its own `press_see:` or
asks its `Inlet` for its `see` once, at init.

### new-3. A caller's bound cannot read the instance's `press_see`

What happened: `answer_dialog/5`, `answer_menu/5` and `press_limit/4` bound their call on the
client side, where the instance's state is not known. Why: `GenServer.call/3` takes its timeout
from the caller. What I did: the default stays `press_call/1` of the library's 2 s, and the
tree's callers (the inbox, `Limits`) pass `press_call/1` of the config's value. An app that
calls `Sessions.answer_dialog/5` itself, on a `Sessions` with a longer `press_see:`, and passes
no `timeout:`, gets `{:unknown, {:timeout, 25000}}` while the press may still go on. What the
design should change: name this in the design's rule on bounds, or have `Sessions` answer a
press call at once and send the press's answer when it comes.

### new-4. `@commands` does not cover every tmux command at its own bound

What happened: a press makes six tmux commands outside its reads, each bounded by `Tmux.CLI`'s
5 s, so its worst case is 30 s, not 16 s. Why: 16 s is the old bound's part outside two 2 s
reads, kept so the library's default did not change. What I did: kept it. A press past its bound
is unknown, never hung. What the design should change: if a press should never be cut by its
own bound, derive `@commands` from the server's `timeout` too.

### new-5. A pane gone may be a person's own "No, exit"

What happened: a person at the viewer can press "No, exit" between the `Inlet`'s Enter and its
read. The `Inlet` then reads the pane gone and answers `sent`, though its own Enter may not have
landed. Why: a pane gone looks the same whoever ended it. `Dialog.tla`'s `ViewerPress` is an
atomic step, and its `Press` reads and answers in one step, so the spec does not show the window.
What I did: said so in the spec's comment. The row is stopped either way, and a press `sent` is
never pressed again, the same as an unknown one. What the design should change: nothing, unless a
press `sent` must mean its own keys landed. Then a pane gone should answer unknown, and the
person sees "unknown" for a press that did what they asked.

### new-6. tmux wakes a pane's own process that stops

What happened: the first late-redraw test sent SIGSTOP to the stand-in, and its press was still
answered at once. Why: tmux sends SIGCONT to a pane's own process when it stops for any signal
but SIGTTIN or SIGTTOU. F-238's probe stopped a program held by dtach, a step away from tmux.
What I did: the Inlet test runs the stand-in under `bash -c '"$0"; :'`, so the stopped process
is not the pane's own. What the design should change: nothing. A test aid that stops a program
to stand for load could hold it the same way.
