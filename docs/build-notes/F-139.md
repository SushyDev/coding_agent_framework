# F-139: the `Scheduler`, part 2: jobs, heavy slots and a landing's gang lease

`Jobs`' start asks the `Scheduler` for a lease first (`docs/design.md`, "A lease"), the heavy
slot is the first fenced resource, and a landing takes all it needs in one gang lease. F-135's
findings new-1, new-3 and new-4 were decided in the brief and are followed as decided: the
design's outcome shapes, an unread window has room, and the account stays picked by allowance.

## What was built

- **The model first** (`Schedule.tla`). The fence is a protocol: a start sent to a host may
  land after its owner read the host and found nothing, and then runs beside the next holder of
  its slot. A new shape, `Fence` (two heavy runs from two modules on one slot), adds the host:
  `run` (the wrapper: `sent`, `running`, `done`, `fenced`) and `fence` (`set`, `taken`,
  `revoked`). The owner writes the fence with the lease id, then sends the start
  (`WorkStarts`); the wrapper lands at any later step (`WrapperRuns`), takes the fence and runs,
  or finds it taken and exits; the owner settles live work ended only by its end read on the
  host, or as never run once it took the fence itself (`WorkEnds`). New invariants
  `NoRunUnleased` (a job runs only under its own unreleased lease) and `HostCapacity` (the
  running jobs never pass a capacity). Break `NoFence` (the wrapper runs without the fence, the
  owner settles a start that has not landed yet as never run) fails `NoRunUnleased`. The other
  shapes leave the host out (`Hosted`), so their state counts do not change.
- **`Jobs`**:
  - With `scheduler:`, a run takes `lease: %{class, module, units}` (class `:landing`,
    `:builder` or `:heavy`; without it the run is refused `{:bad, :lease}` before any row). It
    writes the row `asked`, then asks under request id `job:<id>`, holder `{"job", id}`.
    Queued, it waits in the caller's process for the one more answer, watching the
    `Scheduler`, at most `:lease_wait` (300 s). The row keeps the lease id (`lease`).
  - A refusal of any kind (the `Scheduler`'s four, or `scheduler_ended`, `unanswered`, an
    unknown answer) is the row `refused` with `reason` and `retry_at`, answered
    `{:refused, {:lease, {reason, retry_at}}}`. It is never owed or handed on as an end.
  - The fence: after the sync, `Jobs` writes the lease id to `<job>.exit.lease` on the host;
    the wrapper gets the id as an argument, takes `<job>.exit.fence` by `mkdir` before anything
    else, and runs only when it took it and the file holds its id. A start whose reply was cut
    is settled from the host after its deadline by taking the fence first (`cut_read/3`):
    taken by `Jobs`, the start is refused and nothing will run; taken by the wrapper, the job
    ran, and a dead socket with no exit file is `lost`, not `refused`. A job with no lease
    keeps the old path.
  - The row leaves `asked` for `starting` only while it is still `asked` (`{:advance, …}`), so
    a row a restart made `interrupted`, whose lease was then released, sends nothing.
  - `Jobs` watches each run's caller from its `asked` row: a caller that ends leaves a row still
    `asked` `interrupted` and owed, as a restart does, and one `starting` or `running` owed. So
    every leased row settles and its lease is released.
  - `Jobs.Work`, a pure module over a connection of its own: `:live` while the row is `asked`,
    `starting` or `running`; `:ended` once it holds an end or says nothing ran. Rows `starting`
    or `running` after a restart stay live until `Jobs` settles them by `Holder.Process`'s
    record.
  - `Jobs.Table` gains `lease`, `reason` and `retry_at`; an older file gains them on open.
- **`Scheduler`** reads the `jobs` topic.
- **`MCP`**: `run` takes `opts.class` (`heavy` by default, `builder`, `landing`), and with the
  app's `lease:` function, of the class and the caller's project, names the lease's units. That
  is the entry the app gives agents; the operator owns nothing, so it runs no job.
- **`caf_example`**: the `Scheduler` reads job rows through `Jobs.Work`; `Jobs` gets the
  `Scheduler`; `resources/1` adds a landing lock per project, `land:<project>`, capacity one,
  beside the config's `resources` (seat 4, slot 2); `job_units/3` answers each class's units
  from the config's new `job_lease` (a slot each), a landing's with its project's lock.
- **Docs**: the design's `Jobs` row, the cue table (`jobs` reaches the `Scheduler`), "A lease"
  (the fence, jobs and landings), the model's paragraph and the unit order; README's Status and
  the apps chart.

## What I ran into

- A heavy slot is counted, not numbered: "the current holder" of a slot is any of up to two
  leases. So the fence is per job, beside the job on its host, holding its lease id, not per
  slot (new-2).
- `Copy.tla` assumes a start never lands after its deadline (NeverLands); the fence makes the
  settle of a leased job's cut start sound without it, but `Copy.tla` still models the old
  read, and `Services`' start has no fence (new-1).
- Without the caller's watch, a run whose caller died while it waited for its lease, or after
  it was granted, left a row `asked` that only a restart of `Jobs` settles, and its lease held a
  slot until then (new-4).
- The wait for a queued lease runs before the job's timeout starts, so a `run` call can take
  `lease_wait` longer than its `timeout` (new-3).
- Running one app's tests from its own directory fails on the dependency check (`exqlite`'s
  `only:` and the lock); from the root, `mix test apps/<app>/test` works.

## Findings

### new-1. `Copy.tla` still assumes a late start never lands

What happened: `Schedule.tla`'s `Fence` shape checks that a leased job's start that lands late
runs nothing. `Copy.tla` models the same start settle with NeverLands (a command runs within
its deadline or never), and `Services`' start has no fence.
Why: the brief asked for the check in one spec; the fence lives in `Jobs`' wrapper only.
What I did: the fence for leased jobs, modeled in `Schedule.tla`; `Copy.tla` unchanged.
What the design should change: decide whether `Copy.tla` models the fence and drops
NeverLands for jobs, and whether a service's start gets the same fence once services ask for
leases.

### new-2. The fence is per holder, not per slot

What happened: "a resource carries the lease id and refuses a holder whose id is not current"
reads as one current holder; a slot of capacity two has up to two.
Why: the `Scheduler` counts units; it does not hand out numbered slots.
What I did: the fence is the job's own: its lease id beside it on its host, taken once by
either the wrapper or `Jobs`. Together with the count, no job runs outside its lease.
What the design should change: say that for a counted resource the fenced thing is the holder's
start, and keep numbered units for a lock that guards one shared thing (a lock file).

### new-3. A queued lease's wait comes before the job's timeout

What happened: a `run` waits up to `lease_wait` (300 s by default) for a queued lease, then runs
for up to its `timeout`. An agent's `run` can then outlast the timeout it named, and Claude
Code's own tool timeout.
Why: the design bounds the wait by the class's `max_wait`, and the job by its timeout; nothing
bounds their sum.
What I did: nothing beyond the bound; a refusal at the wait's end is a row like any other.
What the design should change: decide whether the job's `timeout` covers the queue's wait, or
whether `MCP` caps `max_wait` plus the timeout under the tool timeout, as `run_delegate` does.

### new-4. A run's caller ending settles its row

What happened: a run works in its caller's process; a caller that ended while its row was
`asked` (waiting for or holding a lease) left the row live until `Jobs` restarted, and the
lease held a slot until then.
Why: the lease lasts as long as its row's work, and nothing settled such a row.
What I did: `Jobs` watches the caller from the `asked` row: an `asked` row becomes
`interrupted` and owed, a `starting` or `running` one owed, as a restart does. This is a
mechanism the brief did not name; it was needed for the lease to be released.
What the design should change: confirm it, and say so in "Hosts" beside the restart's rule.

### new-5. The landing lock is per project, not per base branch

What happened: the brief names "a base-branch lock"; the example declares `land:<project>`.
Why: a project's landings all land on its trunk today; stacks may land on other bases.
What I did: one lock per project, capacity one, declared by the app, not the library.
What the design should change: when stacks come, decide whether a landing's lock is its base
branch's (`land:<project>:<base>`), which the app can declare the same way.

## Also in this unit

Its gate once failed F-138's ask test: `start_delegate` was refused `{:unknown_agent, "app-main"}`
right after the app started. `Sessions` wrote the app's declared main sessions in its
`handle_continue`, after `start_link` returned, so a tool call sent at once could reach `Grants`
before the row existed. They are now written in `init`, before `start_link` returns.
