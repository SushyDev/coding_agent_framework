# F-194: every answer and a person's interventions are rows

The brief's four items, toward native metrics an app can compare with its own baseline (B7):
every answer a row (item 1, F-179's new-4), a person's interventions as rows (item 2), one
reading of the metrics (item 3) and the design's metrics table (item 4). Items 1 and 2 came to
about 840 changed lines with their tests and docs (this note about 150 of them), and item 3 alone, a fold per metric with a
test each on fixed rows, would take the unit well past 1,000, so as the brief says, items 1 and
2 land first and items 3 and 4 are the next unit (new-1 says what each metric reads). Built on
`main` at F-193. No test started the real Claude Code, and nothing was measured by hand.

## What was built

- **Every answer is a row** (item 1). `Prompts.answer/5`, under `CodingAgentFramework.answer/4`,
  claims one row in `Acts` before the owner takes the answer (`Prompts.answering/4`) and settles
  it by the owner's answer (`Acts.answered/4`): actor who answered, target the prompt's agent
  (its id when it has none, a login's), act `answer`, no state (an answer the owner refused
  leaves the prompt open for the next), and in its `detail` the prompt's `prompt` id, `kind`,
  `choice` as given, `opened_at` and `subagent`. Its `claimed_at` is the answer's time. A choice
  the prompt does not offer, or a prompt not open, writes no row. A claim that does not go is
  the answer's refusal, so no answer goes unrecorded. `Prompts` takes `acts:`; the tree hands it
  its `Acts`.
- **`Acts.record/7`'s `state:`** keys a recorded fact, so a fact read again after a restart is
  written once: a second record of a target in that state is refused `{:already_claimed, by}`,
  as a claim is.
- **The transcript's interrupt lines** (`Transcripts.Fold`'s `interrupts`): each user line whose
  text is `[Request interrupted by user]` or `[Request interrupted by user for tool use]` (both
  checked by name in the 2.1.322 binary), with its `uuid`, `at` (ms), `tool_use`, and `turn_at`,
  the time of the prompt that opened the turn it ended. The line stays among the prompts, as
  before. `Fold.interrupt?/1` names the two texts. A fold an earlier build stored has other keys,
  so `Transcripts.Table.load_fold/1` gives a fresh one and the file is read again from its start.
- **`Intake.Table.events/4`**: a host's rows of one hook event past an id, oldest first.
- **`Interventions`** (item 2; `caf_recoveries`, one per host, after `hooks_silent` in the tree):
  a person's interventions as rows in `Acts`, actor `person` (or the config's
  `parts: %{interventions: %{person: name}}`), target the agent, each once under its state:
  - `typed` (`typed:<host>:<intake id>`): a `UserPromptSubmit` of an interactive agent's session
    (a main, or a delegate started `interactive: true`) whose prompt carries no mark of a row
    `Mailbox` handed that agent. Every text the framework puts into a session carries
    `[delivery <uuid>]`; the `Inlet` types nothing else into a prompt (Escape and a dialog's keys
    are keys). A headless delegate's prompts are never read: no person types there. A row whose
    session no agent row names waits `settle:` (a minute), then is passed by;
  - `interrupt` (`interrupt:<agent>:<line uuid>`): an interrupt line of an interactive agent's
    transcript on a turn the framework did not interrupt: no `interrupt` act on that agent in
    `Acts` (a parent's fix through `MCP`) claimed between the turn's prompt and the line, each
    `slack:` (5 s) wide.
  It reads at start, at each cue of `intake` and `transcripts`, and by `check/2`. A viewer
  attached is not read (new-2). The config's `parts` takes `interventions` `person` (a new type,
  `:name`), `settle` and `slack`.
- **Docs**: "Metrics and healing" (the answer rows and `Interventions`), "Prompts" (F-194's
  row), the cue table (`Interventions` on `intake` and `transcripts`), `answer/4`'s doc.

## What was measured

- `PromptsTest`: the permission test reads the one answer row (actor, target, prompt, kind,
  choice, its open time, settled `verified`), and no row for the refused answers; the ask test
  reads who answered an ask and who withdrew one (F-179's new-4), the withdrawal's choice
  `["withdraw", reason]`.
- `InterventionsTest`, three tests on real `Acts`, `Sessions`, `Intake`, `Mailbox` and
  `Transcripts` files: a marked prompt is the framework's, an unmarked one a person's typed
  input, a headless delegate's never read, and a restart writes none twice; a prompt of a
  session no row names holds the reader's place until `settle`, then is passed by; a person's
  Esc on one turn is a row, and the framework's interrupt of the next (a parent's fix claimed
  before its line) is not. Doctests of `Interventions`, `Acts.record/7`'s state,
  `Fold`'s interrupts and `Intake.Table.events/4`.
- `SurfaceTest` names `interventions` among the tree's parts and the readers of `intake` and
  `transcripts`; `ConfigTest` takes the `:name` type of a part's option.
- No spec changed. A record with a state is `Claim.tla`'s claim and settle in one step, once
  per state as every claim is; `Interventions` reads facts and acts on nothing; the answer row
  is written before the owner's act and settled after, as every act is (principle 2).

## What I ran into

- The interrupt line's two texts are in the pinned binary, but its JSON shape (a user entry
  whose content is one text block) is read from the shape Claude Code writes for a prompt with
  blocks, not from a run: a builder has no logged-in account to press Esc in (new-3).
- `after` is a reserved word in Elixir; `Intake.Table.events/4`'s place is `past`.

## Findings

### new-1. Items 3 and 4 are the next unit: each metric's rows, and four rules to decide

What happened: items 1 and 2 were about 840 lines; the reading of the metrics, a pure fold per
metric with a test each, would take the unit well past 1,000.
What I did: nothing of `metrics/2` or the design's table; I read what each metric can count from
today's rows.
What the design should change: build `CodingAgentFramework.metrics(caf, since:, until:, by:)` as
one unit, a pure module (in `caf_watch`, as the `Watch` box's `metrics/2`) over rows the surface
reads once, with these sources:

| metric | rows |
| --- | --- |
| builders started | `delegates` rows (one per start), `created_at` |
| reported, time to report | a row `finished` or `failed`; the report is `Mailbox`'s reply or notice to its `brief_uuid`, whose `created_at` less the row's `created_at` is the time |
| killed, restarted by a recovery | `delegates.resumes` and `resuming` (a resume after a kill); `Acts` rows of the healers' `end` and `resume`, and the operator's forced end (`detail.force`) |
| reports dropped | `Mailbox` rows of kind `reply` or `notice` to a brief settled `unknown`, or due again |
| usage-limit stalls, time past the reset | the `Watch`'s `limited` symptoms: `since`, the reset in `evidence`, `passed_at` when a turn ran; moves: `Limits`' `Acts` rows of act `move` |
| prompts answered or not, time to answer | `Intake` holds by their final `hold`, `Mailbox` asks with or without an answer row; the answer rows of F-194 for who and when |
| recoveries fired, failed, left for a person | `Acts` rows of the healers' actors by outcome; the `Watch`'s symptoms `asked` |
| a person's interventions | `Acts` rows of act `answer`, `typed` and `interrupt`; the operator's `stop` acts |

Four rules have no fact to read yet, and each wants a decision before that unit: what a
"stalled" builder is (no named symptom says it: `idle_untold` is not built, and `Delegates`'
`idle_bound` is off by default); what a "late" report is (a bound from the start, or a report
taken after its row ended); what "killed" counts when a process is gone with no act (a crash of
Claude Code itself, read only as `resuming`); and which day `by: :day` cuts at (UTC, or the
app's time zone given in the call). Without B7's status histories, a row's time in a status is
only its `created_at` and `updated_at`.

### new-2. A viewer attached has no fact: no part owns a tmux hook

What happened: the brief reads a viewer attached from tmux's `client-attached` "through the hook
the part that opens viewers already owns, if it has one". No part sets a tmux hook: nothing in
the libraries runs `set-hook`.
What I did: nothing; `Interventions` reads typed input and interrupts only.
What the design should change: one of two, decided first. Either `TmuxSampler` reads the
server's clients at each sample (`list-clients -F '#{client_tty} #{client_session}
#{client_created}'`), each client a fact by its tty and tmux's own creation time, so an
attach is a fact even across a restart of the framework; or `Tmux` sets a `client-attached` hook
on the framework's own server whose command posts to `Intake`, which a framework that is down
misses. The first needs no new protocol. Either way the framework's own opens (a viewer a person
asked for through the app, "Open in Ghostty") are the person's request already and need no
second row.

### new-3. The interrupt line is checked by name in the binary, not measured in a run

What happened: the brief asks for the interrupt line measured on the pinned release first. Its
two texts are constants in the 2.1.322 binary; no builder's run has a logged-in account to press
Esc in, and no test starts the real Claude Code.
What I did: the fold matches either text as the whole of a user line's text, a string or text
blocks, so either shape is read.
What the design should change: the next end-to-end run (`docs/e2e.md`) presses Esc once in a
main session and once during a tool call, and keeps the two transcript lines as fixtures for
`Fold`'s tests.

### new-4. An answer outside `answer/4` has no answer row, and a hold's open time is lost

What happened: only `answer/4` writes the answer row. A parent's `answer` through `MCP` is its
fix's row (act `answer`, actor the parent) with no prompt id or open time; a dialog the config's
`dialogs` answers, the app's `PreToolUse` or `Stop` callback's decision and a hold's default at
its deadline are rows of their owners with no time opened: `Intake` writes a held row's
`written_at` again when it settles, so its open time is gone once it is decided.
What I did: nothing.
What the design should change: `Intake` keeps `held_at` on a held row (a column added, written
once at the hold), and the parent's answer through `steer` goes through `answer/4` as the design
already decides ("Prompts", not yet built), so every answer's time to answer is read the same
way.

### new-5. A person's Esc within a turn the framework interrupted reads as the framework's

What happened: an Esc carries no mark, so the framework's own interrupt is told from a person's
only by its act's row in the turn the line ended. A person who presses Esc in the same turn the
framework interrupted, within that turn, is counted as the framework's.
What I did: the rule as above, with a slack of 5 s for the two clocks.
What the design should change: nothing, unless the metrics need that case: then `Inlet` and
`Stream` record the interrupt line's uuid on the act's row once the transcript shows it, and the
reader matches by uuid.
