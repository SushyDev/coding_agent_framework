# F-123: R5 part 2, the waiter

The second part of R5: the `waiter` kind of `Intake`'s one hold, with F-121's four findings
decided by the brief and folded into the design.

## What was built

- **The model first.** `Intake.tla` instances `Hold` a third time, as `HW`, for one session's
  waiters. A waiter's program can die unseen (`ProgramDies`), and a write to its connection may
  still succeed, so text written there is not read. A heartbeat to a dead connection fails and
  cuts the hold (`HeartbeatFails`, Hold's `Cut`). A second waiter waits behind the first and
  takes its place once the first's hold ends (`WaiterReplaced`). `Mailbox` stands in as the
  decider: one text in flight, handed only to the session's waiter while it is held, and told
  `sent`, `unknown` or nothing (refused). The deadline is hours and the heartbeat seconds, so
  `WaiterExpires` is guarded to come after a death is found.
  - Properties: `WaiterOneResponse` (Hold's `OneResponse`), `ReadOnce` (no text is read
    twice), `EmptyOnlyAtDeadline` (a waiter is turned away only by its deadline),
    `WaiterRequestsEnd` (Hold's `RequestsEnd`), `DeathFound` (a death is found before the
    deadline) and `WaiterTaken` (a waiter held becomes the session's, unless its hold ends
    first).
  - `Intake_Waiter.cfg`: three waiters, two texts, one restart; 32,118 distinct states, 26 s.
  - Three new breaks, each failing as it should: `NoHeartbeat` (`DeathFound`), `RefuseSecond`
    (`EmptyOnlyAtDeadline`) and `RefuseUnknown` (`ReadOnce`: text whose write a restart cut is
    told refused, handed again, and read twice).
  - Every other `Intake` configuration sets `MaxWaiters = 0` and `MaxTexts = 0`, so its state
    space is as before. `Hold.tla`'s header names the waiter.
- **`Intake`'s waiter kind.** `wait/4` holds a waiter's post (the plug's call): it has no row,
  its key is `{:waiter, n}`, and it is the session's waiter if the session has none. Otherwise
  it waits behind, and the session's waiter is sent `{ref, :beat}` at once. `deliver/5` hands
  text to the session's waiter: `{:ok, :sent}` once written, `{:refused, :no_waiter}` with none
  held or text already in flight, `{:refused, :empty_text}` for text that is only whitespace,
  and `{:unknown, {:request_closed, reason}}` when the connection closes before the write is
  confirmed. `waiter?/4` answers whether a session has one now. `closed/3` is the plug's word
  that a write failed. The hold uses the same drop, `:DOWN` lookup, deadline, decide, release
  and settle as the other kinds; a waiter's hold moves no row and sends no cue. When a session's
  waiter's hold ends, the newest waiter behind it takes its place. Options `waiter_deadline:`
  (7,080,000 ms) and `beat:` (5,000 ms).
- **The plug.** `POST /hooks/<account>/waiter` gets 200 at once and a chunked response: a
  newline every `beat` ms and at once on `{ref, :beat}`, then the text as the last chunk, or
  nothing more at the deadline. A failed write calls `Intake.closed/3`. A body that names no
  session gets 400; another path under `/hooks/<account>/` stays 404.
- **The hook program**, `Intake.waiter_hook/5`: the fragment `%{"Stop" => [...]}` with one
  `command` hook, `asyncRewake: true`, `timeout` 7,200 s. The command `exec`s `sh -c <script>`
  with fd 3 a here-document holding `header = "x-caf-secret: …"`; the script runs
  `curl -s -K /dev/fd/3 … --data-binary @- <url>`, strips leading whitespace, and writes a
  non-empty response to stderr with exit 2; otherwise exit 0. A secret outside
  `[A-Za-z0-9._~+/=-]` is refused, since it goes between double quotes in a curl config. Paths
  come in as `%{sh:, curl:}`, the shape `Programs.take/2` answers, so `caf_intake` gains no
  dependency.
- **`curl`** in `Programs`, devenv (`CAF_CURL`) and `TestHost`.
- **new-4.** A `PreToolUse` row is inserted with `data` empty, and the decision's move writes
  the input. `Table.move/7` takes `actor:` and `data:` as options.
- **The design**: "Delivery into a main session" takes new-2 and new-3, and a new paragraph
  "The waiter's connection" takes new-1; "Intake" takes the waiter's path, the kind's deadline
  and heartbeat, and new-4; `Programs` names curl; R5 says what is built.

Tests (`intake_waiter_test.exs`, ten): text as the last chunk once, and refused with no waiter;
the deadline; a body with no session; a second waiter behind the first, the first's heartbeat,
and the hand-over; a failed write while text is in flight (unknown) and the next waiter taking
over; a restart. Over a real Bandit socket: status, chunked heartbeats, then the text; a
connection closed unseen found by a heartbeat, and the next waiter taking over. The real hook
command, run as Claude Code runs it with a stand-in Stop input on stdin: text gives exit 2 and
the text on stderr; the secret is in no process's arguments while it waits (`ps -A`); the
deadline and a wrong secret give exit 0 and nothing on stderr.

## What was measured

- **When a closed connection is found**, on Bandit 1.14 over loopback, a raw client that posts
  and closes, 20 runs each: with a 100 ms beat, found after 202 to 462 ms (median about
  215 ms); with a 1 s beat, after 1,996 to 2,274 ms. The first write after the close succeeds
  and the second fails, every time: a death is found by the second heartbeat after it, about two
  periods, so 5 to 10 s at the default beat.
- **The heartbeat a second waiter triggers does not, alone, find a closed first.** With a 60 s
  beat, a first that closed, then a second 50 ms later: 500 ms on, both were still held and the
  first was still the session's. The triggered write is the first write after the close, which
  succeeds; the first's next regular heartbeat fails. See new-1.
- The intake tests run in about 3.3 s, the waiter's ten included.

## Findings

### new-1. One triggered heartbeat does not find a connection that just closed

What happened: the brief says a second waiter triggers a heartbeat to the first, which it
replaces once that write fails. Measured on Bandit over loopback, the first write after a close
always succeeds and the second fails. The triggered heartbeat is that first write, so it does
not end the first's hold; the first's next regular heartbeat does, up to one period (5 s) later.
In that time the session's waiter is the dead first, and text handed to it is written and
reported `{:ok, :sent}`, but not read.
Why: the client's close sends a FIN; the server's first write is accepted by its kernel, the
peer answers with a reset, and only a write after the reset fails.
What I did: built the brief's single heartbeat. Nothing is lost: `Intake.tla` already lets text
go to a dead connection unread, and the transcript settles it (`Mailbox` makes it due again).
The cost is a delay of up to one period and, rarely, a delivery that goes round once more.
What the design should change: either accept it as is (and say so), or have the trigger write
twice, a few milliseconds apart, so a closed first is found at the second waiter's arrival.

### new-2. A launch overlay with `hooks` replaces the account's hooks

What happened: `Accounts.overlay/4` merges an agent's settings over its account's, and every
key but `permissions` replaces the account's. A main session's overlay that carries the waiter
hook (`hooks.Stop`) would drop every Intake `http` hook of the account, the account's own
`Stop` included.
Why: item 11's overlay was built for permission rules, before any overlay carried hooks.
What I did: nothing; wiring the hook is R7's or `Mains'`. `Intake.waiter_hook/5` returns a
fragment for that merge.
What the design should change: the overlay's `hooks` add to the account's per event, as
`Accounts`' own `intake_hooks` does (`own ++ ours`), and `ClaudeCLI.Options` checks a `hooks`
key whose only allowed value is the framework's own waiter fragment (an app's own hooks stay
out, as now).

### new-3. A waiter's coming and going sends no cue

What happened: a waiter's hold has no row, so it sends no cue, and `waiter?` is answered from
`Intake`'s memory. A view that shows "a session idle with no waiter and something due" has
nothing to reload on.
Why: cues name row ids, and the waiter has none by design (its row is `Mailbox`'s).
What I did: no cue. `waiter?/4` is there to read.
What the design should change: `Intake` sends a cue on a session's waiter coming and going, on
its own topic (say `intake:<host>:<account>:waiters` with the session ids), for the view and for
`Mailbox`'s next try of a due row (R7), which otherwise retries `deliver` on a timer.

### new-4. `Services.stop` of a kept service failed once with EPERM from `kill`

What happened: in one of the gate's three `gate-test` runs, `ExampleTest`'s "a delegate's end
ends its services, and hands a kept one to its parent" failed:
`Services.stop(parts.services, "app-main", "kept", [])` answered
`{:unknown, {:end, {:signal, {:ok, %{output: "kill: ‘-24086’: Operation not permitted\n", status: 1}}}}}`.
The other two runs, and `devenv test`, passed. This unit changes nothing in `Services` or the
example app.
Why (not confirmed): on macOS, `kill` to a process group answers EPERM when the group's only
members are zombies, so a group whose processes had just exited, not yet reaped, gives EPERM
rather than "no such process".
What I did: nothing in the code; ran `gate-test` three more times.
What the design should change: `Services`' end reads EPERM on a group whose leader is gone as
the group gone (checked by `kill -0` on the leader, by pid and start time), or the test that
found it is examined by the unit that owns `Services`.
