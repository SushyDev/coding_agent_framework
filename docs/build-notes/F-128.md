# F-128: a failed end and a person's clear, in the model

F-126's new-3: `Start.tla`'s `Kill` always ended the process. So the end that fails was checked
only by tests: the process outlives the KILL, the act settles `unknown`, the row waits for a
person, and the end is tried once more after the person's clear (F-118's new-5). This unit
models it. It changes specs, configurations and the design's text only; no Elixir.

## What was built

- **Where**: in `Start.tla`, not `Agent.tla`. `Start.tla` already has the rows Recoveries ends
  (an orphan, a stop past its deadline), their records and the KILL. It needs only `Claim.tla`'s
  six variables and one more, `clears`. `Agent.tla` has `PersonClears`, but it has no row, no
  record and no end. Its one `Claim` instance numbers no states (a reading passes a loss). It
  would need all of `Start.tla`'s end, a second `Claim` instance with numbered states, and a
  place to keep the two kinds of target apart. The spec's header says so.
- **The claim**: `RC == INSTANCE Claim`, with targets the names and one actor, `recoveries`. A
  state is `<<rowProc[s], clears[s]>>`, the row's record and the person's clears of that name.
  The code's key is the same: the record's pid and start time, and the time of the last clear
  (`Recoveries.end_key/3`). A clear makes the state new, and nothing else does while the row
  waits.
- **The steps**: `EndClaimed(s)` claims the end of a row that owes one (`EndOwed`: late stopping
  or orphan, with a live process by its record). `EndRuns(s)` is the end itself: the KILL ends
  the process and cues, or the process outlives it and the act settles `unknown`. A KILL fails
  only while the name's clears are fewer than the new constant `MaxClears`, which bounds the
  failures. With `MaxClears = 0` every KILL ends its process, as before.
- **Two shapes, by the new constant `SplitEnds`**: with it TRUE the claim (`RC!Take`) and the end
  (`Kill(c)`, `RC!Act`) are two steps. `FrameworkDies` cuts a claim in memory (`RC!Restart`),
  `EndSettled(c)` settles it interrupted (`RC!Settle`), and a claim whose row no longer owes an
  end is let go (`RC!LetGo`). With it FALSE the claim and the end are one step, a new
  `Claim.tla` operator, `TakeAndAct`, as the code's one call takes them. Only
  `Start_FailedEnds` sets it TRUE. This is what `Agent.tla` does with `MaxLateViewers`: a
  configuration that does not study the race leaves it out. See "What was measured" for why.
- **Claims pass when the row stops**: `RunningSettled` and `StopSettled` (when it writes
  `stopped`) take a name's claims out (`RC!Passes`). The name's next start has a new record, so
  no later state can be one of them. Without this, rows of old starts made states that differ
  only in history.
- **The person**: `PersonClears(s)` happens when `WaitsForPerson(s)` holds: the row is orphan or
  late stopping, its process is alive by its record, and the claim on its current state is done
  (failed or interrupted). It adds one to `clears[s]`. People are not fair: a person may never
  clear.
- **Properties**: `OneEndPerState` (`RC!OneActPerState`) and `NoEndOnCutOffClaim`
  (`RC!NoActOnCutOffClaim`), invariants in every Start configuration. `OrphanEnds` and
  `LateStopEnds` now read "ends, or waits for a person". The weaker form is what the design
  says: a failed end waits for a person, and so does an end whose claim a restart cut (Claim's
  "ends interrupted, for a person"). Because `~>` holds at every point, after a clear the row
  must again end or wait. That is the check that the clear lets the end run once more.
- **Breaks**: `Break_Start_EndAgain` takes a second claim on a state whose end failed, with no
  clear (Claim's `TwoClaims`). TLC fails `OneEndPerState`: claim, KILL fails, claim, act again.
  `Break_Start_NoTryAfterClear` never ends a row a person cleared. TLC fails a temporal property:
  KILL fails, the person clears, and the row stays late stopping and neither ends nor waits.
- **Configurations**: `Start_FailedEnds.cfg` (new): one name, one start, one restart,
  `MaxClears = 2`, `SplitEnds = TRUE`. Every other Start configuration, fast, slow and Break,
  sets `MaxClears = 0` and `SplitEnds = FALSE`, except the two new Breaks, which set
  `MaxClears = 2`.
  The three fast design configurations and the two slow ones also check the two new invariants.
- **Text**: `Claim.tla`'s header names `Start.tla` among its instances. The design's table of
  specs (F-115) names the new properties and the failed end in `Start.tla`'s row.

## What was measured

Checked to be reachable in `Start_FailedEnds`, each by an invariant TLC had to fail (a probe
module in the scratch directory, not kept): two clears of one name; three ends, each on its own
state; a claim a restart cut off; a late stop waiting for a person.

Times from `devenv shell -- bin/check-model <name>`, on a machine whose load average was 13 to
34 on 15 cores the whole time (other agents' runs). Distinct states do not depend on the load.

| Configuration | `main` | First shape | Final |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Start_Restarts` | 40,593, 13 s | 260,925, 53 s | 43,671, 11 s |
| `Start_SharedTree` | 76,199, 36 s | 218,031, 82 s | 82,743, 28 s |
| `Start_Worktrees` | 41,152, 12 s | 258,534, 55 s | 43,754, 10 s |
| `Start_FailedEnds` (new) | — | 4,303, 3 s | 3,547, 3 s |
| `Break_Start_EndAgain` (new) | — | 587, 3 s | 320, 2 s |
| `Break_Start_NoTryAfterClear` (new) | — | 589, 3 s | 463, 3 s |

The first shape always split the claim from the end and kept every claim. Its full run passed
all 219 configurations, but the three Start design configurations took 53 to 82 s. I measured
two cuts on copies:

- Passing a name's claims at its next start took `Start_Restarts` to 62,631 states (12 s), but
  left `Start_SharedTree` (one start per name) at 218,031.
- The claim and the end in one step took `Start_SharedTree` to 106,799 states (38 s).
- Both, with the claims passing when the row stops, gave the final numbers above.

So the restart between claim and end, and the interrupted end it leaves, are checked only in
`Start_FailedEnds`. That configuration has one name, one start, a hard kill and a restart, and
it reaches an orphan and a late stop.

`devenv test`, with every gate, passed. Its model gate checked all 219 fast configurations and
parsed the slow ones. Under a load average of about 20, every configuration took under 30 s
except `Start_SharedTree`, which took 36 s, as it did on `main` under the same load (new-1). The
slow Start configurations were parsed, not run.

## What I ran into

- `Start_SharedTree` took 36 s before any change, over the 30 s bound. The load above explains
  most of it. See new-1.

## Findings

### new-1. The 30 s bound on a fast configuration depends on the machine's load

What happened: `Start_SharedTree` took 36 s on `main`, before this unit changed anything. The
machine's load average was about 20 on 15 cores.
Why: `bin/check-model` uses every core (`-workers auto`), and the gates of other agents' units
run on the same machine.
What I did: timed every Start configuration before and after, and give the distinct states, which
do not depend on the load. `Start_SharedTree` has 8.6% more states than on `main`.
What the design should change: give the bound under a stated load, or as distinct states (for
example, at most 100,000), which do not depend on the machine.

### new-2. An end whose claim a restart cut waits for a person, but `clear/2` refuses it

What happened: in the model, as `Claim.tla` says ("ends interrupted, for a person"), a restart
between Recoveries' claim of an end and its act leaves the row waiting for a person, whose clear
makes the state new. In the code, `Recoveries.waits?/1` takes only an `end` row that settled
`unknown`. An `end` row that `Acts.interrupt/3` closed as `interrupted` still holds its state
(the record and the last clear), so no later check claims it again. `clear/2` answers
`{:refused, :not_waiting}` for it. The orphan's process runs on, and nothing in Recoveries can
take it again.
Why: a lost viewer's interrupted claim does not get stuck, because the next loss has a new key
(the sample's generation). An end's key changes only with a clear. So only the end needs the
clear for an interrupted row.
What I did: nothing in the code. The model lets a person clear an interrupted end, which is what
the design means.
What the design should change: name the interrupted end in "The parts", `Recoveries`' row, as a
third state that waits for a person. `waits?/1` should take an `end` row that is `interrupted`,
as it takes one settled `unknown`.
