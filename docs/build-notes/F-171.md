# F-171: heavy commands taken transparently; the Stop count's reset; part C's rest

The brief had three parts: heavy commands under a lease through native `Bash` (F-157's part
B), the `Stop` count reset at a turn's end (F-157's new-1, measured by the main session), and
the rest of F-157's part C. All three are in this branch.

## What was built

**The model first.**

- `Schedule.tla` gains the shape `Native`: two heavy `Bash` commands on one slot, each under
  the wrapper on the workstation. The requester is the work: the wrapper's own process asks
  (`Ask`, `Reask` only while it lives), becomes the command once told granted (`WorkStarts`
  makes `run` "running"), exits after a refusal, and ends at any step, waiting or running
  (`WrapperEnds`: "done", an exit that posts its end, or "killed", which posts nothing). The
  owner reads that end from the process (`WorkEnds` only once `run` is "done" or "killed"),
  and in this shape the owner's read is fair, as the `Scheduler` asks on its interval. New
  property `GoneIsReleased` (every end of the wrapper's process releases its lease);
  `NoRunUnleased` and `HostCapacity` are checked here too. New Break `EndByPost`: the owner
  reads only the wrapper's own post, which a kill never sends, and TLC finds a wrapper killed
  after its grant whose lease is never released. Configurations `Schedule_Native` and
  `Break_Schedule_EndByPost`.
- `Intake.tla`: `NewTurn` resets Claude Code's count (no longer "may or may not"), and Intake
  resets its own on each `Stop` it passes (`StopTaken`'s pass, `CapAnswered`'s end,
  `StopDeadline`). `CountKept` holds while a turn runs (`turn # "ended" => fBlocks >=
  ccBlocks`): between Intake's reset at a pass and Claude Code's at the next turn, the count
  is not read, since a turn that ended holds no `Stop`. The three `Break_Intake_*` of the
  `Stop` hold still fail.

**Heavy commands (`caf_runs`, a new app in the `Hosts` box).**

- `Runs.pre_tool_use/2` is the policy, between `Mover`'s drain and the app's callback: the
  app answers first, and when it lets a `Bash` call run as it is (`:pass`, `:allow`, or a
  correction) and the command matches the first of the app's heavy commands (`heavy:` in the
  config, each a pattern and a class), the call is corrected with `updatedInput` into
  `'<home>/bin/caf-run' <class> <tool_use_id> <agent> -- '<command>'`. Every other field of
  the input stays (`run_in_background`, `timeout`), so Claude Code's own background command
  and its notification are unchanged. A deny or an ask stands, a call of no known agent is not
  rewritten, a wrapped command is not wrapped again, and a callback that raises is a pass.
- `Runs.prepare/4` writes the wrapper (mode 0700) and a curl config holding the secret (mode
  0600), so no secret is in any command line. The wrapper posts its request to `POST /runs` on
  the intake's port (`Runs.Plug`, beside `Intake.Plug` behind `CodingAgentFramework.Endpoint`)
  with its pid and start time. The request's process is the `Scheduler`'s requester:
  `%{id: "run:" <> tool_use_id, class, module: agent, account: nil, units, holder: {"run",
  "<pid> <start>"}}`, the units the class's `job_lease` (a landing's also takes its project's
  lock). Queued, the response is chunked: one `queued (position n)` line, which the wrapper
  prints, then a heartbeat newline every 5 s (a failed write ends the request, and the
  `Scheduler` drops it), then the answer. Granted, the wrapper `exec`s `sh -c <command>`:
  the pid stays, so the command's output and exit code are the wrapper's and the lease's holder
  is the command. Refused, it prints `caf-run: not run: the lease was refused (<reason>); try
  again after <UTC time>` and exits 75. A lease its id already has is answered only to the
  process that holds it, unreleased; any other asker is refused `used`, so a retry under one
  id takes no second slot. With no answer it asks again under the same id once a second, at
  most 30 times (`Schedule.tla`'s `Reask`, after a restart cut the request), then runs the
  command without a lease and says so in one line; `unknown` from the endpoint (no
  `Scheduler`) does the same at once.
- `Runs.state/2` is the owner of a `"run"` lease for the `Scheduler`'s `holders:`: live while
  that pid runs with that start time, ended once it does not (`Holder.Process.liveness/3`, one
  `ps`), unknown when `ps` cannot say. So the release comes on the `Scheduler`'s interval and
  after its restart, whatever ended the command.
- `run` leaves the agents' list: `MCP` keeps it in `@unlisted`, served and checked by name as
  before but in no `tools/list`. Its late end and its tests stay for the unit that takes it
  out (new-3).
- `Config` takes `heavy:` (checked by `Runs.heavy/1`, `{:refused, {:bad, :heavy}}`), and the
  example's config file `scheduler.heavy`.

**The Stop count's reset.** `Intake` resets a session's count when it releases a `Stop` hold
`passed` or `expired`: a pass of the callback, of a pause, of a person at the cap, past the
cap, and at the deadline.

**Part C's rest.**

- `Jobs.Table.meta/0` and `Copies.Table.meta/0` are `Tracker` sources, topics `jobs` and
  `copies`, in the tree's `Tracker`. `Reader.describe/2` takes `host_topic: false` (a writer
  that cues one topic for every host), and `Reader` an `order:` (a copy is keyed by three
  columns; its rows get the id `<host>/<project>/<name>`). `Copies` takes `notify:` and cues
  `%{topic: "copies"}` after each write, with no ids, so the source reads every row.
- The `Scheduler` cues `%{topic: "scheduler:<host>", pressure: level}` at each change of the
  level, and `pressure/2` answers `%{level, source, at, unread}`: the gates' level, the source
  (`:psi`, `:none`, or a `{:level, read}` source's `name:`), the last sample's time, and why the
  last sample could not be read. A sample that cannot be read changes nothing.
- On macOS the tree's default source is `sysctl -n kern.memorystatus_vm_pressure_level`
  (`Scheduler.Pressure.sysctl/1`): 1 `ok`, 2 `high`, 4 `severe`, anything else unknown.
  `sysctl` joins `Programs` and devenv (`CAF_SYSCTL`, procps' on both systems).
- A headless delegate's turn: `Stream.Fold` keeps `tasks`, added by a `system` event
  `task_started` and removed by `task_notification`, cleared at the process's exit and at a
  resume; `Sessions.Turn.stream/1` reads a fold whose turn ended with a task still running as
  `:running`.

**Tests.** `RunsTest`: the rewrite (fields kept, quoting, a wrapped command, the app's deny,
correction and crash, what is not rewritten), and the wrapper with the real `sh`, `curl` and
`ps` against a real `Scheduler` behind `Runs.Plug` on a real port: the command's output and
exit code and its release; the same id again refused `used`; queued with its one line, run
once the slot is free; refused by pressure, exit 75, nothing run; a `KILL` of the running
command released by the monitor; no `Scheduler`, run without a lease in one line; the
endpoint's refusals. `SurfaceTest`: the tree's wrapper granted by the tree's `Scheduler` on
the intake's port, the lease's units and holder, released. `IntakeStopTest`: a pass resets
the count and the next turn has the whole cap; a capped pass resets it. `SchedulerTest`: the
cue per change, `pressure/2`'s fields, an unknown value named and changing nothing.
`TurnTest`, `CopiesTest`, `JobsTest`, `ConsoleTest`, and doctests for `Runs`, `Runs.Plug`,
`Endpoint`, `Pressure.sysctl/1` and `Turn.stream/1`.

## What I ran into

- **`Scheduler.request/2` answers a released lease.** A request asked again under an id whose
  lease was released is answered `{:ok, lease}` with `released_at` set, by design ("the lease
  is looked up before any refusal"). The wrapper's endpoint therefore checks the lease's holder
  and `released_at` before it says granted, or a command copied and run again would run
  unleased under its old id.
- **The holder is a process, not a row.** A heavy command has no row of its own: the lease's
  holder key is the wrapper's pid and start time, and its owner is `ps`. A row per run would
  only copy what the lease row and the process already say, and its exit code is Claude
  Code's to show.
- **`sh -c` has no `$0` of the test's choosing.** The first tests named the gate as `$0`; the
  wrapper runs `sh -c <command>` with none.
- **Part C's tables.** `Jobs` cues one topic, `jobs`, for every host, and a copy has no `id`:
  `Reader` gained the two options above rather than a source of their own.

## Findings

### new-1. How Claude Code ends a `Bash` command it kills is not measured

What happened: the wrapper `exec`s the command, so the lease's holder is the command's own
process, and a kill of it releases the lease. A command that started children (a test suite's
VM) ends only when its process does; if Claude Code killed only the shell's pid on a timeout or
an interrupt, children could run on after the lease was released.
Why: Claude Code's kill of a `Bash` command (its signal, and whether it ends the whole tree or
group) is not recorded for the pinned release.
What I did: the holder is the process the command runs in; the doc says a process the command
leaves behind is unleased.
What the design should change: the release check records, for a foreground timeout and an
interrupt, which processes of a `Bash` command get which signal. If only the top one, the
wrapper should run the command in a process group of its own and the owner read the group
(`Holder.Process.signal/4`'s group check), at the cost of a second process.

### new-2. A queued wrapper waits inside the `Bash` call's timeout

What happened: a heavy command queued for a slot waits in its `Bash` call, whose default
timeout is two minutes; a foreground call that times out is killed by Claude Code, and its
request leaves the queue.
Why: the wait for a lease is the command's own time, as the design wants ("the agent learns
nothing new").
What I did: nothing beyond the `queued` line, which tells the agent why it waits.
What the design should change: decide whether the rewrite also raises a foreground call's
`timeout` by the class's `max_wait`, or leaves it, so that the agent may move a long one to
`run_in_background` itself.

### new-3. `run` is unlisted but still served

What happened: the brief says `run` leaves the agents' list. Its act, `MCP.caller/3`, the
tree's late-end routing and about ten tests (the call log, `place`, a lease's class, late
ends) are built on it.
Why: taking them out is about −200 lines with tests moved to `Jobs`' own, beside a unit
already over its size.
What I did: kept it in `MCP`'s `@unlisted`: no agent's `tools/list` names it, and a call by
name is served as before, as `reload` was kept.
What the design should change: a small unit removes `run`, its late end and `MCP.caller/3`, and
moves those tests to `Jobs` and the surface; or the design says whether the operator keeps a
`run` for landings, which today go through `Jobs` directly.

### new-4. The stream's task events are built on their names, not a recorded log

What happened: a headless delegate's fold keeps its background commands from `system` events
`task_started` and `task_notification` with a `task_id`, the names Claude Code's own types give
its stream messages.
Why: no log in this repository records a background command in a headless session, and the
brief names "the stream's task events" without their shape.
What I did: built on those names; the fold ignores any other shape, so a release that sends
another reads as before (idle after the `result`).
What the design should change: the release check records a headless session's background
command start and end on the pinned release, and the stand-in plays it.

### new-5. macOS's "urgent" has no value of its own in this reading

What happened: the brief says urgent or critical is severe. `kern.memorystatus_vm_pressure_level`
reports the dispatch levels 1, 2 and 4 (normal, warn, critical); as I read the kernel's
mapping, its urgent level reads as warn there. That is not measured here.
Why: the sysctl reports the level a process's memory-pressure source would see, not the
kernel's own four.
What I did: 4 is severe, 2 is high, 1 is ok, and any other value is unknown.
What the design should change: nothing, unless urgent must bar more than heavy runs; then the
source is the kernel's own level, which needs a reading of another interface.

### new-6. The queued wrapper test failed once and did not fail again

What happened: in the first of six `gate-test` runs, `RunsTest`'s "queued" test failed: the
first wrapper, holding the slot in `cat` on a FIFO until the test writes it, was cut by its
own 20 s `Shell.run` timeout (`{:unknown, {:timeout, 20000}}`), and `caf_runs` took 36 s in
place of its usual 1.5 s. The other apps took their usual times in that run, so it was not
load. Fifty more runs of `caf_runs`, ten of them with the failing seed, and five more gate
runs all passed.
Why: not found. The first wrapper's path to that point does not touch the `Scheduler`
after its grant, so the wait is in the test's steps before the FIFO is written: the second
wrapper's `queued` line, which needs `Scheduler.request/2` to answer while the same process
reads each `"run"` lease's owner by `ps` every 50 ms (`ps_timeout` 5 s), or the FIFO's open.
What I did: nothing in the code; the test stands as it is.
What the design should change: nothing yet. If it recurs, the test should time each step,
and the `Scheduler`'s owner reads should be checked for a `ps` that blocks its calls.
