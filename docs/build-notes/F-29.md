# F-29: `Team.tla`, the agents of one project in the model

## What was built

- **`model/tla/Team.tla`**, the model only, no code. The agents of one project (a parent and
  delegates), an agent of a second project that calls in, their messages, claims and services,
  and restarts of the framework. Its header names `Mailbox`, `Grants`, `Services` and `MCP` (none
  built), what it leaves abstract, its fairness and its defense in depth.
- Steps: `Prompted`, `TurnEnds`, `Faults` (stick or die), `DelegateEnds`; `Send`,
  `DeliveryAsked`, `DeliveryTyped` / `DeliveryRefused` (`Control`'s checked write),
  `DeliveryMarked`, `SendingSettled`, `Redirected`; `ClaimTaken`, `FixActs`, `FixRefused`,
  `ClaimSettled`; `ServiceStarts`, `ServiceHanded`, `ServiceOwnerEnded`; `FrameworkDies`,
  `FrameworkStarts`.
- Invariants: `DeliveredAtMostOnce`, `OnlyIntoEndedTurn`, `InOrder`, `OneClaimPerState`,
  `OneFixPerState`, `FixOnClaimedState`, `NoSelfFix`, `ParentNeverStopped`, `SameProjectOnly`,
  `OneOwner`, `TypeOK`. Liveness: `MessageDelivered`, `ClaimEnds`, `ServiceEndsWithOwner`.
- Thirteen `Break` values, each with a `Break_Team_*.cfg` TLC fails; four fast configurations
  in the gate's set; `slow/Team_Large.cfg`. `READING.md` gains a section on `Team.tla`.
  `bin/check-model` needed no change.

## What I ran into

- TLC explores each `\/` inside an action as its own branch, so `a \in Foreign \/ proc[a] # …`
  read `proc` of a foreign agent and stopped with an error. `Live` uses `IF` instead.
- Liveness took most of the time: the first fast configurations ran 85 to 277 s, against 5 to 7 s
  for their invariants alone. A person's prompt now reaches only the parent (a delegate's turns
  come from its brief, messages and respawns), `ClaimEnds` is one formula, and the configuration
  with two messages has no fault; `Team_Faults` checks two messages with a fault, safety only.
  `Team_Services` with one message took 35 s in the gate under load, so it has none.
- A first slow configuration that also had a service passed 1.9 million distinct states in 16
  minutes with its queue still growing, and each liveness pass took 4 minutes. Three agents with
  two messages and no service reached 1.6 million at depth 12, still growing. I stopped both by
  their pids. The slow configuration has two agents; three agents are checked in `Team_Claims`,
  without messages. Its 1,119 s ran beside other builders' TLC runs; most of it is the liveness
  check.

## Findings

### new-1. A message needs the same sending mark and settle as a report

What happened: "Messages" says a message is stored before it is sent and delivered once, but not
how a restart between the write and its record is settled. `Break_Team_RedeliverAfterRestart`
types a message twice in 12 steps.

What I did: the row says "sending" before `Control`'s write; after a restart a "sending" row with
no send under way is settled from the target's transcript, as `Delegate.tla` settles a report.

What the design should change: say under "Messages" that the row is marked before the write,
settled from the target's transcript after a restart, and carries a mark of its own the
transcript shows, the same as step 5 of the delegate protocol.

### new-2. A message is sent on to the parent only from a settled row

What happened: a row cut off while "sending" to a delegate that then ended may already be in the
delegate's transcript. Sending it on to the parent unsettled delivers it twice
(`Break_Team_RedirectUnsettled`).

What I did: `Redirected` takes only a "queued" row.

What the design should change: say a message to an ended delegate goes to its parent once its
row is settled, and only if the delegate's transcript does not hold it.

### new-3. "In order per sender" is per sender and addressee

What happened: order between messages to different agents means nothing, and a message sent on
to the parent may reach it after a later message the same sender wrote to the parent directly.

What I did: `InOrder` holds per sender and addressee, in each transcript. A message waits for
every earlier one from its sender to its addressee.

What the design should change: say "in order per sender and addressee"; a message sent on to the
parent keeps its order among its addressee's messages, and says so.

### new-4. Claims live in a table, and a call cut off by a restart is closed

What happened: `Grants` "holds a fix's claim", but not where. A claim held in memory is lost at a
restart, and a second fixer then fixes a state that was already fixed once
(`Break_Team_ClaimInMemory`), such as a second `C-c`.

What I did: claims are rows. After a restart a claim with no call under way is closed and its fix
never sent again, since it may have gone out.

What the design should change: say claims are rows of `Grants`' table, and a claim whose call a
restart cut off is closed as unknown, not retried.

### new-5. A fix acts only while the claimed state is current

What happened: a claim is for the target's current state, but nothing says the fix checks that
state again when it acts. A stop claimed on an idle delegate, delayed while the delegate died and
another agent respawned it, ends the new turn (`Break_Team_StaleFix`).

What I did: each state has a number that moves when the agent sticks, dies, is fixed or ends; a
claim carries it, and the fix acts only while it is current, else it is refused.

What the design should change: say a claim names the state's number and its fix answers
`{:refused, :state_changed}` when it moved. The number comes from the framework's own readings,
which can lag; the code unit should measure how far.

### new-6. Who a kept service goes to, and how one is handed on, is unclear

What happened: "unless it handed them to another agent with `service_start(…, keep: true)` or to
its parent" reads two ways, and no tool in the table hands a service to another agent.

What I did: `keep` gives the service to the owner's parent when the owner ends; `ServiceHanded`
is a call by which an owner hands a service to another agent of the project.

What the design should change: say `keep: true` hands a service to its owner's parent when the
owner ends, and add `service_hand(name, to)` to the tools, or drop "to another agent".

### new-7. "A message to a gone agent is refused" does not say what is gone

What happened: `agents()` lists "gone", a dead agent may be respawned, and an ended delegate's
messages go to its parent. Which of these refuse a message is not said.

What I did: a message to a dead agent waits for a respawn; one to an ended delegate goes to the
parent; the parent never ends. So in one project nothing is refused but a call across projects.

What the design should change: say a message is refused only for a name the project does not
hold, waits while its addressee is dead, and goes to the parent once a delegate has ended.

### new-8. "Released or verified" and "still claimed" are two things

What happened: liveness says a claim is released or its fix verified, and "Who may act" says a fix
that did not help shows as the same state still claimed.

What I did: the claim's call always ends (`ClaimEnds`); its row keeps the state claimed, so a
second fixer of a state whose fix did not help is still refused until the state moves.

What the design should change: say the call ends with the fix verified or not, and the row holds
that state until it moves; a person, not a second agent, takes over a fix that did not help.
