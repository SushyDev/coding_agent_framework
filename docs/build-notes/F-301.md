# F-301: a finished delegate does not hold a seat, and a full class says so

## What was built

- **A finished delegate frees its seat** (decided in the brief; `Delegate.tla`'s `seat`). Its
  lease now ends when its turn ends with the report delivered, not when its agent row is free.
  A delegate's row in `delegates` has a `seat`: NULL (held), `closing`, `free` or `asking`, with
  `seats` (the asks so far) and `asked_for` (the newest `Mailbox` row to it at the last ask).
  - At a settle, a finished row under a `Scheduler`, its process running, its report
    delivered, its turn ended by its kind's rule, no request held for a person and no row to it
    in `Mailbox`, is written `closing`. That is read again. Still quiet, it is written `free`;
    else held again.
  - `Delegates.Work.state/2` answers a finished row whose seat is `free` `:ended`, so the
    `Scheduler` releases its leases at its next cue of the row. `closing` and `asking` are live.
  - `Mailbox` takes a `seated:` function (`Delegates.Work.seated?/2`, on a connection of its
    own in the app's wiring). In the step that marks a row it marks none to a finished delegate
    whose seat is not held. The rows stay `queued`.
  - A row to a free delegate stored after its last ask writes `asking`, then asks the
    `Scheduler` for a lease under `delegate:<host>:<id>:<created>:<n>`, class `builder`, as a
    start does. Granted at once or later (queued, its one more answer as a message), the seat is
    held again and the row goes in at `Mailbox`'s next mark. Refused (or no answer), the seat
    is free again, the parent is told once by a notice under `seat:<host>:<id>:<n>`, and only a
    row stored after that ask asks again. A row left `asking` by a restart asks again under the
    same id; the `Scheduler` looks the lease up first.
  - A running, starting or waiting delegate holds its seat, as before. A main session's lease
    is not affected: a main is never `finished`, and `Mains.Work` is unchanged.
- **A full class** answers through the `Scheduler`'s queue. The observed `lease: unknown` was
  the `Scheduler`'s 5 s call timing out under load (`Scheduler.request/3`'s `{:unknown,
  {:timeout, 5000}}`, which `Delegates` refuses as `{:lease, {:unknown, …}}`), which F-299 is
  fixing. With the `Scheduler` answering, a full class answers a start with `wait: false`
  `{:ok, %{queued: n}}` at once, and a start that waits gets its lease once a seat frees. No
  `{:refused, {:cap, n}}` is built: see new-1.
- **`stop` on a finished, idle delegate** (new-3). The 15 s is `Delegates.fix_state/3`'s
  bound (`3 * @step`), the call `MCP` makes before its claim, so the stop never acted and the
  seat stayed held. The call waits in `Delegates`' mailbox behind a settle, and a settle read
  `Stream.rows/2` (each a copy of every row, bounded at 5 s) once for every finished
  delegate's own turns. The own turns are now read from one read of the rows per settle. The
  closing check, a status and a resume still read their own.

## The model

`Delegate.tla` gains `Seats` (the delegates whose seat frees) and `MaxMsgs` (rows stored to a
finished one), the seat's state, Mailbox's rows to a finished delegate, the `Scheduler`'s seat
asks, its grants and its release. The steps before F-301 leave the new state unchanged (`K`),
and a restart takes `Delegates`' wait for an answer (`SeatRestart`).

- `WorkEndedIsFree` now allows a finished delegate whose seat is free to have a process that
  idles, or that runs a turn nothing the framework wrote started (its own, a person's).
- `NoTurnWithoutGrant`: a turn a row to a finished delegate started runs only while a lease of
  its row is held and its owner says the work is live, so none is released under it.
- `FreeIsReleased`, `FreesOnceIdle` and `RowGoes` (liveness): a free seat's leases are released;
  a quiet finished delegate frees its seat; a row to it goes in, or waits only after its ask was
  refused.
- Breaks, each failing `NoTurnWithoutGrant`: `NoGate` (Mailbox marks while free), `NoClosing`
  (one read, the gate open between it and the write), `AskWhileFree` (the ask leaves the row
  free, so the grant is released before its answer is taken), `RefusedOpens` (a refusal opens
  the gate), `SeatInMemory` (a restart reads the seat held).
- A restart between the turn's end and the release releases once: the seat is durable, and the
  `Scheduler` releases by compare-and-set (`Schedule.tla`).

Every other `Delegate_*` configuration sets `Seats = {}` and `MaxMsgs = 0`; its state space is
unchanged (`Delegate_Queued` 14 134 distinct states, as F-170 measured).

On the stand-in (`delegates_test.exs`, "a finished delegate's seat (F-301)"), counted, not
timed:

- 2 seats, 2 delegates finish: both leases are released and both rows stay `finished`, their
  processes running; a third start is granted at once, not queued.
- 1 seat held by a running delegate: a message to a finished one makes it `asking` with one
  request in the `Scheduler`'s queue and its row still `queued`; once the running one finishes
  and frees its seat, the request (`…:1`) is granted, the row is delivered, its reply comes, and
  the seat is freed again (3 grants in all).
- an ask refused at its deadline: the seat is `free` again, the parent has one notice, the row
  is still `queued`, a cue asks nothing again, and a later message asks again (`seats` 2).
- a full class: `wait: false` answers `queued: 1`; a waiting start is answered its lease once
  the seats free (3 grants).
- `stop` of a finished, idle delegate answers `{:ok, :stopped}`, its lease is released, and a
  new start is granted.
- one settle with 3 finished delegates makes one `Stream.rows/2` call from `Delegates` (traced
  on `Stream`). Before the change, with 5 finished delegates, one cue of `Mailbox` and
  `Delegates` made 7 such calls in all; after it, 3.

## What I ran into

- The 15 s stop timeout did not reproduce on the stand-in: one settle with 5 finished
  delegates took well under a second. It needs a `Stream` slow under load, as F-299 saw.
- The test setup's `Mailbox` now has the child id `:mailbox`, so a test can stop it and start
  one that reads the seat.

## Findings

### new-1. `{:refused, {:cap, n}}` has no caller to answer

- **What happened:** the brief asks a full class to answer `{:refused, {:cap, n}}`, or `queued`
  when the caller waits. Every caller of `Scheduler.request/3` today waits in its queue: a
  start with `wait: false` is answered queued at once and its parent told later (F-170). The
  `unknown` seen came from the 5 s call timeout, not from a full class.
- **Why:** the `Scheduler` has no request that says it will not wait; it queues every request
  up to the class's bound, then refuses `queue_full`.
- **What I did:** tested the full class's answers that exist (queued at once, the lease after a
  wait), and built no new refusal.
- **What the design should change:** if an app wants a start that never waits, add a request
  shape for it (say `queue: false`), refused at once `{:full, %{resource => capacity}}` when it
  cannot be granted now, modeled in `Schedule.tla` as a request that never enters the queue,
  and a `start_delegate` option that asks it.

### new-2. A delegate's own turn and a person's turn run while its seat is free

- **What happened:** a background command of a finished delegate's own may wake it (F-155),
  and a person may type into an interactive one. Neither goes through `Mailbox`, so neither is
  gated, and either can run while the seat is free.
- **Why:** nothing the framework writes starts them; the gate is on rows.
- **What I did:** the model allows them (`WorkEndedIsFree`'s second case) and `NoTurnWithoutGrant`
  covers only the turns a row starts.
- **What the design should change:** decide whether a person's turn is admitted as a main
  session's is (always), and whether an own turn read while the seat is free asks for a lease
  after the fact, or holds the seat until such a command ends (a delegate with a background
  command still running would then keep its seat).

### new-3. A fix's call waits behind a settle that calls other parts once per row

- **What happened:** `fix_state/3` is bounded at 15 s, and runs only after the settle under way
  in `Delegates`. A settle called `Stream.rows/2` for every finished delegate, and still calls
  `Stream` and `Mailbox` for each running row and each finished row's seat, each up to 5 s.
  `fix_state/3` itself reads `Stream` twice, 5 s each.
- **Why:** `Delegates` is one process, and its settle makes its reads in that process.
- **What I did:** one read of the rows per settle for the own turns, which only tell notices.
  Not the reads that decide a status or a seat.
- **What the design should change:** read a settle's evidence in a task and apply it to the
  table as it is then, as `Mailbox`'s pass does (F-289, `Delivery.tla`'s `Read` and `Apply`),
  so a fix's call never waits behind the reads. That changes when a decision reads its
  evidence, so it is modeled first.

### new-4. A row may hold two leases for a moment

- **What happened:** when a row to a free delegate comes before the `Scheduler` has released
  its last lease, the new ask is granted beside it, and the row holds two seats until the next
  free.
- **Why:** the release is the `Scheduler`'s, at its next cue of the row; `Delegates` does not
  wait for it before it asks.
- **What I did:** nothing; the model allows it, and the class only has one seat fewer while it
  lasts.
- **What the design should change:** `Delegates` could ask only once the row's leases read
  released (`Scheduler.leases/2` or the table read-only), or a grant could release the
  holder's ended leases first.

### new-5. A row whose ask was refused waits, and its sender is not told

- **What happened:** after a refused ask the parent is told once, but the row stays `queued`
  until a newer row asks again or a stop settles it `ended`. A sender other than the parent
  (another delegate, the operator) is not told.
- **Why:** asking again on its own would be a hidden retry, and the brief keeps the delegate
  finished with its seat free.
- **What I did:** the parent's notice says a later message asks again.
- **What the design should change:** decide whether a refused ask settles the rows it was for
  `undelivered` with a notice to each sender, as `wait_bound` does for a typed row.
