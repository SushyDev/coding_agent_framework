# F-193: the limits' last pieces

The brief's six items, from F-191's new-2 and new-4, F-186's leftovers and F-192's findings:
a headless move's own word, modeled first (item 1); the `Scheduler`'s `limit` prompt (item 2,
not built: new-1); the `Watch`'s digest of `limited` by account (item 3); the runbook's usage
step (item 4); a claim's detail kept through its act and keyed by its symptom (item 5); and a
person's press `by` its claim's actor (item 6). Built on `main` at F-191, before F-192 landed:
items 5 and 6 touch the same claims F-192 gives `healer:`, so the two meet in a small merge.
No test started the real Claude Code, and nothing was measured by hand.

## What was built

- **The model first** (`Move.tla`, constant `Headless`, a headless delegate): a resume with no
  row due or in flight is not bare; the `Mover` stores a word of its own first (`OwnWord`, once
  per move, under the move's key), which the resume carries as its first line. New property
  `HeadlessResumeHasLine` (every headless resume carries a row marked by launch, the model's
  first line by stream) with `Break_Move_BareHeadless` (the bare resume allowed). Configurations
  `Move_Headless` (safety, a report, a message, a crash and a restart) and
  `Move_HeadlessLiveness` (`DrainedParentMoves`, `RowsArrive`). Every other `Move_*`
  configuration, `slow/Move_Large` among them, sets `Headless = FALSE`. The header names the
  code.
- **`Mover`** (item 1): a headless resume whose `Mailbox.launch/4` finds nothing due stores a
  notice from `framework` under `move:<agent>:<deadline>` ("This session was moved between
  Claude accounts. Go on with your task from where it stopped."), then marks it as the first
  line. A launch that still finds nothing is `{:unknown, :no_first_line}`, asked again at the
  next cue. **`MoverSetup`** plays the headless stand-in in the real release's shape (its init
  only after a first line), so its starts carry `first:` and every resume must.
- **`Watch.limited/2`** (item 3), a fold of the `limited` rows by `Watch.Limited.digest/2`: one
  reading per account limited now, its newest reset and that reset's window, and its agents,
  each with its own reset, `since` and `waited` by the `Watch`'s clock, longest first. Read
  through the app's `limited/1` and the console's `limited(caf)`.
- **The runbook** (item 4): the `usage-limit` record takes `limited(caf)`, the `limit_far`
  symptoms, and, while a main shows Claude Code's own wait, its session file, whole, with the
  time.
- **`Acts.acted/4`** (item 5) merges the act's `detail` into the claim's: both read as maps,
  the claim's keys kept, a text that is no JSON object the map's `text`. **Each healer's claim**
  keeps its symptom's state as `symptom` in its detail (`Recoveries`, `AbsentMains`,
  `StuckBackgrounds`, and `Limits`' four: `press_stop`, `go_on`, `move` by the healer and by a
  person), so a person's act is found from its answered prompt by one read of the rows. A
  `Recoveries` reopen writes its pane as `pane`.
- **`Limits`** (item 6): a press's `by` is the `limit` prompt's answerer, else the claim's
  actor: a person's `act`, or `framework` for the healer's own.
- **Docs**: "How `Mover` does it" (the headless word), "The model" (`Move.tla`'s `Headless`),
  "Usage limits" (what F-193 built and what it did not), "Prompts" (why the `Scheduler`'s
  prompt is not built), the runbook.

## Findings

### new-1. The `Scheduler` never sees a start refused at a limit, and a wait to a reset is not modeled

What happened: a start or a resume picks its account with `Usage.pick/4` inside `Delegates`
and `Mains`. When every usable account is limited the pick answers `{:refused, {:allowance,
retry_at}}` before the `Scheduler` is asked, and `Delegates` before it writes its row. The
`Scheduler` sees only requests that name an account, refuses a spent one at once, and keeps
its queue in memory with a `max_wait` of four minutes; `Limit.tla`'s other start (`req`) is
answered at once, granted or refused. So the "Scheduler's `limit` prompt" has no row to be,
and "the start queued to that reset under its lease" is a wait of hours across restarts that no
spec checks. The design also says two things: "Prompts" offers wait (its default at a
deadline) or move; the brief offers `wait` or `leave`. Why: the prompt was written into the
design before the pick moved into the starters (F-181). What I did: nothing; "Usage limits"
and "Prompts" say it is not built. What the design should change: the prompt is the start's
owner's row, not the `Scheduler`'s. A start or resume the pick refuses at a limit writes its
row (a delegate's `waiting`, a main's start row) with the soonest reset, the parent told as for
a queued start (F-170); `Prompts` reads that row as kind `limit`, id `limit:start:<row>`,
answered `wait` (its default at its deadline: at the reset plus the margin the owner picks
and asks for its lease again, under the row's own request id) or `leave` (the row refused,
`left`). Model it first in `Limit.tla` (`StartRefusedAtLimit`, `AnswerWait`, `AnswerLeave`,
`StartAtReset`, a restart keeping the row), with `NoStartOnLimited` still holding and the
liveness that a waiting start is granted or left after its reset. A unit of its own.

### new-2. A person's act is one read of the rows now, but the inbox does not show it

What happened: with item 5 a person's act is found from its prompt: the `Watch`'s row answered
`act` (target, state, `answered_by`) and the row in `Acts` of that target whose actor is
`answered_by` and whose detail's `symptom` is that state. `Prompts` reads no `Acts` file, and
when an answered prompt leaves the inbox is still not said (F-192's new-1). Why: the brief made
the inbox's part conditional on the rows. What I did: the rows, not the inbox. What the design
should change: `Prompts` takes `acts_db:` and the `acts` cue; a `remedy` prompt answered `act`
shows as `answering` (its word for an answer on its way to the owner) until that row ends, and
leaves the inbox then, its outcome the act's row (a fault in the fault ledger).

### new-3. The move's word is keyed by its deadline, not its act

What happened: the brief stores the word "under the move's act". A move that goes back to its
own account clears the act on the row (`Sessions.move_phase/5` with `act: nil`), and a copy
refused goes back before any resume, so its way back would find no act to key by. Why: the
act is cleared so `done/2` never verifies a failed act. What I did: the key is
`move:<agent>:<deadline>`, the move's deadline being on the row from its start to its end, so
one move stores one word whichever account its resume goes to. What the design should change:
nothing, unless the key must name the act.

### new-4. The move's word wakes a delegate whose report was taken

What happened: a headless delegate moved between turns has nothing due only when its turn
ended, so its report was taken. The word starts a turn, which `Delegates` tells the parent as a
turn of its own (F-155), not as a second report. Why: Claude Code headless needs a line before
its init (F-152), and the move has no other. What I did: the text says the session was moved
and to go on from where it stopped, which holds for a finished task too. What the design should
change: nothing, unless a moved delegate whose report was taken should not be woken; then the
move waits for a row instead, as F-191's new-2 offered.
