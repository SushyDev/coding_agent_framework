# F-132: R8, the `Inlet`: a checked typed write into a main session with no waiter

R8 (`docs/design.md`, "The parts", "A session with no waiter", "Delivery", R8), against
`model/tla/Delivery.tla`'s `typed` transport and `Agent.tla`'s one-command check, with F-113's
new-1, new-2 and new-4, F-116's rename, F-122's new-2, F-125's new-4, F-127's new-2 and F-130's
new-1 read for it.

## What was built

- **`caf_inlet`** in place of `caf_control`. `Inlet.type/4` is the checked typed write, one
  `Inlet` per host. In one step of its process it reads the turn again (the caller's `turn:`
  function, `:ok` or `{:refused, reason}`), captures the session's pane and reads it with
  `Screen` for the session's recorded release (idle, the box empty), checks the text, types it
  with no Enter, reads the screen until the box shows exactly that text (`see:`, 2 s by
  default), and then sends Enter as its own tmux command. A release that is nil or has no
  reading refuses the write. The pane is the one whose tag names the session, read from
  `Tmux.list_panes/1` in the same step; every write checks the tag again in its own tmux
  command. Answers: `{:ok, :sent}`; refused when nothing was typed (`{:bad_text, :empty |
  :control}`, `{:too_long, limit}`, `:no_release`, `{:unknown_release, r}`, `{:turn, why}`,
  `{:screen, reading}`, `Tmux`'s); unknown when keys may have gone in (`{:not_seen, reading}`
  with no Enter sent, `{:enter, why}`, `Tmux`'s). `Control`'s `open`, `resize`, `send`,
  `interrupt`, `stop` and `respawn` went: nothing called the last three, and the viewers' tmux
  use moved to their callers.
- **The limit.** A text with a control character (a newline among them) is refused, and so is
  one longer than the pane's width less four columns (the prompt, its space and two to spare):
  116 at 120 columns, 196 at `Sessions`' default 200. `Screen` reads a box of more than one line
  as unrecognized (F-113's new-1), so nothing longer is typed until a fixture shows a wrapped
  box.
- **`Tmux.capture/3`**: a pane's screen (`capture-pane -p`) and width, under the same tag check
  as a write, in one tmux command; the CLI, the fake (`Fake.set_screen/3`) and a contract test.
- **`Mailbox`'s `typed` transport** (`Transport.Typed`, beside `Waiter`, `Stream` and `Call`).
  With `inlet:` (and `registry:`, `transcripts:`, `accounts:`), a running interactive session
  whose waiter is known absent (`Intake.waiter?/4` answers false, or no `Intake` was given)
  gets its first due row typed: marked only once `Sessions.Turn.interactive/3` reads `:idle`
  (so a busy session's row is not marked and unmarked at every cue), then handed to the
  `Inlet` with a turn function that reads the agent row, `Registry`'s session files and the
  waiter again, inside the `Inlet`'s step, and refuses when the process is not the one marked.
  The text is one line: the row's text, a space and `[delivery <uuid>]`. Sent or unknown keeps
  the mark with `sent_at`; refused undoes it with the reason in `attempt`. A row the `Inlet`
  refused for its text (`too_long`, `bad_text`) is not typed again: it waits for a waiter.
  The settle reads the transcript from the offset kept at the mark, as the waiter's does: a user
  line carrying the mark is delivered once the turn reads idle or ended, or its process is gone;
  no line past the take bound, or the process gone with none, is due again, first, same uuid.
  The mark sets `sent_at` to its time plus the `Inlet`'s call timeout (10 s), the latest the
  keys can go in, and the answer to its own time, so a restart between them never makes the row
  due while its keys may still arrive. The take bound's timers are set again after a restart
  for `typed` rows too.
- **The release** (F-113's new-4). `Sessions` runs `<claude> --version` at an interactive start
  or resume, before the row, and records the first `x.y.z` it prints in the agent row's new
  `release` column (added to an older file on open); nil when it cannot be read. A headless
  session's is not read: nothing reads its screen.
- **Viewers.** `Sessions` takes `tmux:` in place of `control:`, and `Sessions.viewer/4` opens and
  grows a viewer with `Tmux.new_session/4` and `Tmux.resize_pane/4`. `Recoveries` reopens a lost
  viewer on its `server:`; its `control:` went.
- **Test aids.** `ScreenStandIn` in `caf_test`: a bash script that draws 2.1.322's
  `text-typed` screen with its box holding what was typed since the last Enter, or
  `turn-running`'s with `--screen running`, reads keys one at a time with no echo (`stty
  -echo -icanon`), appends each line taken at Enter to `<dir>/submitted`, writes its session
  file as `ClaudeStandIn` does when `Sessions` starts it, and answers `--version`.
  `ClaudeStandIn` answers `--version` too. devenv exports `CAF_STTY`.
  `SessionsSetup.type/3` types a line into a session's viewer for `SessionsTest`, in place of
  `Control.send/3`.
- **Wiring and docs.** The example app starts an `Inlet` in place of `Control` and hands it,
  with its `Registry`, to `Mailbox`. The design's state rule 2 ("one `Inlet` per host types
  into panes; `Sessions` and `Recoveries` open and size the viewers"), "A session with no
  waiter" (what the write checks and its limit) and R8; `Agent.tla`'s header names the `Inlet`;
  README's status, "Nothing typed to deliver" and "The apps" chart.

## What I ran into

- `capture-pane -p` inside `if-shell` prints to the client that ran the command, as
  `display-message -p` does, so a capture is one tmux command under the tag check. The width
  goes first, on a line of its own, so the screen after it is read whole.
- A stand-in that reads keys with `read -N1` alone echoes the keys that arrive between two
  reads, since bash restores the terminal after each; `stty -echo -icanon` once at its start
  keeps the screen to what it draws. Under the gate's load, a stand-in that redrew the whole
  screen at each key was still drawing a 116-key text past the 2 s `see:` bound, and the
  `Inlet` answered `{:not_seen, unrecognized}` (a capture mid-draw); it now draws once no more
  keys wait. How long 2.1.322 takes to draw a typed text under load is not measured (new-3).
- `Mailbox` is one process and waits for the `Inlet`'s answer, as it waits for `Intake`'s; a
  typed write takes up to `see:` plus a few tmux runs and a `Registry` read (new-2).
- A main session started before this build has no `release` on its row, so the `Inlet` refuses
  to type into it until it starts or resumes again.
- `Control`'s refusal `:no_control` had a `Recoveries` test (a viewer that fails to open); with
  no `Control` the test holds the agent with no agent row, so `Sessions` refuses its viewer
  command (`no_session`). tmux's own `session_exists` would not do: `Recoveries` counts it as
  a viewer opened since the samples.

## Findings

### new-1. A row the `Inlet` refuses for its text holds every later row to that session

What happened: rows go in order per addressee, one in flight, and the first due row is the one
tried. A text over the limit (a report is often longer than 196 columns, and has newlines) is
refused and stays first, so no later row to that session is typed until a waiter takes it.
Why: `InOrder`, and the limit of new-1 in F-113. What I did: such a row is not typed again
(`attempt` says why), so it costs nothing at each cue; it waits for a waiter.
What the design should change: capture a wrapped box and a multi-line one (F-113's new-1) so the
limit can grow, or say that a text the `Inlet` cannot type is for the waiter only, and whether
rows after it to the same session may go before it by `typed`.

### new-2. `Mailbox` waits on the `Inlet` for the whole typed write

What happened: the hand-on is a call from `Mailbox`'s one process: the `Inlet`'s reads, the
keys, the screen read until the box shows the text, and a `Registry` read inside the turn
function. While it runs, a `message/5` or `status/3` to `Mailbox` waits; their 5 s timeout can
answer unknown under load.
Why: the waiter's hand-on was already a call from the same process, and short.
What I did: bounded the call (10 s) and kept it in the process, so the mark and the hand-on stay
in order with every other write to the table.
What the design should change: say whether a transport's hand-on may block `Mailbox`, or runs
in a task whose answer comes back as a message, with the row `sending` meanwhile.

### new-3. The width limit is a guess at how 2.1.322 wraps

What happened: the limit is the pane's width less four columns; no fixture shows where the box
wraps, and a character drawn two columns wide counts as one. A text that wraps under the limit
is typed, never shows as one line, and is answered unknown with the text left in the box and no
Enter; the row is due again past the bound and then refused, since the box is not empty, until a
person clears it.
What I did: said so in `Inlet`'s doc.
What the design should change: the capture script records a text one column short of the width,
one at the width, and one with a wide character, and the limit comes from those; and the time
from the keys to the box showing them, which sets `see:` (2 s now, a guess).

### new-4. Only an interactive start records its release, and nothing checks it

What happened: `release` is read at an interactive start or resume only, and is whatever
`--version` printed; nothing compares it with the releases `Screen` reads, or with the release
devenv pins, until a typed write refuses it.
What I did: nothing more.
What the design should change: `Programs`' check at start (item 14) could refuse a claude whose
release has no `Screen` reading, so a session is never started that the `Inlet` cannot reach.
