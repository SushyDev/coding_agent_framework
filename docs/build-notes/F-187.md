# F-187: a limited delegate is never finished by its limit

The brief's item 1 (F-186's new-1) and item 3 (F-186's new-3 and new-4), against `Limit.tla`.
Item 2, the moves, is not built. It meets two questions the design does not settle (new-1):
whether a move may stop a `limited` agent, and what Claude Code's session file reads during its
own wait. No test started the real Claude Code, and nothing was measured by hand.

## What was built

- **The model first** (`Limit.tla`). `Report(a)` is `Delegates`' reading of a headless
  delegate's turn: the result of a turn that ended by itself is its report, and a result at a
  limit is not (`LimitNotReport`). `Break_Limit_LimitReport` takes the limit's result as the
  report. `reported` is the one new variable; every other action leaves it as it is
  (`Kept(A)`). `Read` keeps a `prompt` as it keeps an `unknown`, as the code already did
  (F-186's new-3). The header names the code this models (`Delegates.Turn`, `Delegates.go_on`).
- **`Delegates.Turn.next/2`** answers `:limited` when the result naming the turn's message has
  `api_error: "usage_limit_reached"`, whether the process lives or not. It also answers
  `:limited` when the turn's message is not yet written to this process while the stream's last
  result is a limit's: the word to go on before its resume. A delivered message with no result
  of its own is `:limited` too when the stream's last result is a limit's that names no message.
  F-181 kept no record of whether that result names its message, so both shapes are read.
  `Delegates` does nothing on `:limited`: it sends no report, and the exit is not a kill.
- **`Delegates.go_on/5`**, the word to go on after a limit, which the limit's healer calls at
  the reset. It is refused unless the row is `running`, headless, and its turn reads
  `:limited`. It stores the word with `Mailbox.go_on/4`, which puts it before every other row,
  under `Delegates.Turn.word_uuid/1` of the key `limit:<agent>:<until>`, so the word is stored
  once per limit. Then it writes the row's `resume_uuid`, so the turn is the word's. A live
  process takes the word by its stream (`{:ok, :live}`). A gone one is resumed with the word as
  its first line (F-152), under the start's lease, with the row's new status `going_on`; the
  caller waits for `Sessions`' answer. `resumes` does not count it. A refused or interrupted
  `going_on` puts the row back to `running`, still at its limit. `Delegates.Work` counts
  `going_on` as live work, and the table's lists of call and open statuses include it.
- **`Limits`**: a headless delegate's word goes through `go_on:` (`Delegates.go_on/5` in the
  tree) in place of `word:` and `resume:`. Its answer settles the `go_on` claim. A refusal or
  an unknown answer is the `limit` prompt.
- **`HeadlessStandIn`**: while `<dir>/limit` exists, every message hits a limit as F-181
  measured. The stand-in writes a `rate_limit_event` `rejected` with the file's reset, the
  assistant line with `api_error`, and a `result` with `is_error` and `api_error` naming the
  message, then exits 1.
- **Docs**: "The delegate protocol" (a turn at a usage limit is not the report; `going_on` in
  the lists of statuses and in the interrupted settle), "Metrics and healing" (F-186's new-4:
  a limit kind's ask is its `limit` prompt where `Limit.tla` names one), "Usage limits" (the
  table's headless row, and what F-187 built), "The model" (`Report`, `LimitNotReport`, and
  `Read`), and the parts table's `Delegates` row.

## What I ran into

- `Stream.Fold.outcome/2` gives a result's text, subtype and `is_error`, not its `api_error`.
  `Turn.next/2` reads the message's own kept result for that, so the outcome's shape and its
  doctests stay as they are.
- A word with a fresh uuid at each call would be stored twice if the healer ever asked twice.
  A name-based uuid of the key makes `Mailbox`'s one row per uuid the guard, as the key is for
  a notice.

## Findings

### new-1. A move of a limited agent meets two unsettled questions

What happened: item 2 moves a delegate that is in Claude Code's own wait, or headless between
turns, through the `Mover`. The `Mover` stops a running session with `Sessions.stop/3` `for:
:move`. That stops a delegate only once `Sessions.Turn.stream/1` reads its turn ended, and an
interactive session only once its session file reads `idle`. A limited row's turn reads
`:limited`, which the design says is never an ended turn (so `Mailbox` types nothing into it).
And what the session file says during Claude Code's own wait was never measured: F-181 captured
the screens, not the file. Two smaller gaps also block a move of a gone headless delegate:
`Mover.move/4` refuses a row that is not `running`, and its headless resume carries no first
line (`launch_first/2` answers nil for a headless row), which the real Claude Code needs before
it writes its init (F-152). And a failed copy or resume goes back by resuming on the agent's own
account, which for a gone delegate would resume it into its limit. Why: the `Mover` was built
for agents between turns, before a limit was a state. What I did: nothing of item 2. What the
design should change: (a) for `for: :move` only, a `limited` turn is ended. For an interactive
row, the stop reads the screen (`usage_limit_wait` or `usage_limit_menu`, through the `Inlet`)
in place of the session file, since F-181 measured that an exit ends the wait. Or measure the
file during the wait first, and decide from that. (b) A move of a row whose process is gone
skips the drain and the stop. Its resume carries the first row due by stream, as `Delegates`'
does. Its healer stores the word first (`Delegates.go_on/5` with no resume of its own). Its
failure leaves the process gone, never resumed on its own account. `Move.tla` models it before
the code.

### new-2. An interactive delegate's limit still ends or loses its report

What happened: an interactive delegate's report is the reply of the turn its brief began, read
from its transcript once a later user line closes that turn (`Transcripts.Fold.reply_to/2`). At
a limit the word is typed as a notice, a later user line, so the brief's turn closes with the
last reply before the limit, or none. And a gone interactive delegate with no reply is resumed
at once as a kill (`settle_interactive`), counted, into its limit. Why: F-186's new-1 named the
headless path, and this unit built that one. What I did: nothing for an interactive delegate;
its limit still shows on its row. What the design should change: an interactive delegate's word
at a limit is its word to go on, as a headless one's is (`go_on/5`, by launch for a gone
process, its `resume_uuid` the word's), and `settle_interactive` reads a turn whose transcript
ends with the limit's line as limited, not as a kill.

### new-3. The healer's word is the same text for every agent

What happened: `Limits`' word says the account's limit has reset. A moved delegate's word (item
2) would go on under the same key, `limit:<agent>:<until>`, so it is stored once per limit. If
the move fails and the word goes at the reset, it should still be true. What I did: nothing;
the text is F-186's. What the design should change: when the move is built, the word says only
that the turn stopped at a usage limit and to go on, so one text serves both.

### new-4. A headless start whose first turn hits a limit races its own confirm

What happened: in the second of three `gate-test` runs, the new `DelegatesTest` timed out at 60
s in `start_delegate`. The stand-in, as Claude Code does at a limit, wrote its init, the hit and
its result, and exited within milliseconds. `Sessions` confirms a delegate's start only while
its process lives (`confirm/3` needs `process: :live`), so a start whose first line hits a limit
may never be confirmed as running. Why: the confirm reads the init and a live process in one
step, and a limit on the first request leaves no time between the two. The start picks an
account that is not limited (`NoStartOnLimited`), so this needs the limit to close between the
pick and the first request. What I did: the stand-in waits on `<dir>/limit.fifo` before the
hit, so the test confirms the start first; the test then passed 10 runs of 10. I did not change
`Sessions`. What the design should change: a delegate's start is confirmed by the init naming
its session id whether its process still lives or not, as long as the record is this start's.
A process gone after its init is then a running row that exited, which the settle takes as any
exit, and `Delegates` reads its limited turn.
